## Cases Covered

### Dr. Wiita PC §1368 Competency Evaluation (Case #6)

The central case for this Citizen. Dr. Wiita conducted a PC §1368 competency evaluation via telehealth from South Carolina to California. No MC-350 form was generated — the California Judicial Council form required for reporting results of court-ordered competency evaluations to the court.

**Legal issues this Citizen identifies:**

**Issue 1 — Telehealth consent violation (BPC §2290.5(b)):**
Before delivery of any health care via telehealth, the provider must inform the patient and obtain verbal or written consent. If Michael Hartmann did not receive this advisement or did not consent to a telehealth evaluation, Dr. Wiita's evaluation was conducted in violation of BPC §2290.5. Violation = unprofessional conduct under §2290.5(d).

**Issue 2 — California professional standards apply to telehealth (BPC §2290.5(g)):**
All professional responsibility standards that apply to a licensed health care provider under their license apply while providing telehealth services. A South Carolina-licensed psychologist conducting a court-ordered California PC §1368 competency evaluation must comply with California standards for that evaluation — including the requirement to submit a proper report to the court under §1369(b)(1).

**Issue 3 — No MC-350 / §1369 report deficiency:**
PC §1369(b)(1) requires the examiner to submit a written report to the court addressing: (A) diagnosis; (B) whether defendant can understand the proceedings and assist counsel; (C) likelihood of attaining competency; (D) if requested, eligibility for §1001.36 diversion. Absence of MC-350 (or equivalent written report) means the §1369 evaluation was procedurally defective.

**Issue 4 — Competency presumption inverted:**
PC §1369(c)(3): defendant is PRESUMED competent. The burden to prove incompetency is on the party asserting it. A competency evaluation that was used to find incompetency without meeting the procedural requirements of §1368-§1369 — without proper judicial doubt on record, without compliant examiner, without proper report — is a due process violation actionable under §1983.

**Issue 5 — Proceedings not properly suspended:**
PC §1368(c): when a competency inquiry is commenced, ALL criminal proceedings SHALL be suspended. If proceedings continued without proper suspension and competency determination, the conviction or adverse order entered during that period is constitutionally infirm.

### Ward System / §5150 Holds

WIC §5150 holds may be the mechanism through which the ward system (documented in project_ward_system.md and project_conservatorship_breakthrough.md) was operationalized. Each hold requires:
- Probable cause that, as a result of a mental health disorder, the person is a danger to self/others or gravely disabled
- Written application stating the circumstances
- Advisement to the person at time of detention (§5150(g))
- Record of advisement kept by facility (§5150(h))

Holds conducted without probable cause, without proper advisement, or on the basis of false information submitted by a third party trigger civil liability under §5150(e): "the person shall be liable in a civil action for intentionally giving a statement that the person knows to be false."
