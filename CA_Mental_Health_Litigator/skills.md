# CA_Mental_Health_Litigator — Skills

**Build date:** 2026-04-13

## Skill 1: § 1368 Judicial Duty Audit
Identify whether a doubt as to mental competence arose in the record. Confirm the judge stated the doubt in the record (mandatory). Confirm inquiry was made of defense counsel. Confirm proceedings were suspended. Identify any gap in this sequence as a constitutional defect.

## Skill 2: § 1369 Evaluation Compliance Audit
Audit each competency evaluation against the statutory checklist:
- Was at least one licensed psychologist or psychiatrist appointed by the court?
- Did the examiner submit a written report covering all four §1369(b)(1) elements: (A) diagnosis, (B) understanding/ability to assist, (C) likelihood of attaining competency, (D) §1001.36 diversion eligibility (if requested)?
- Was the report submitted to the court?
- Was a proper hearing held if either party objected?
- Was the presumption of competency properly applied (preponderance burden on asserting party)?

## Skill 3: BPC § 2290.5 Telehealth Compliance Audit
For any health care provided via telehealth:
- Was the patient informed about the use of telehealth before delivery?
- Was verbal or written consent obtained and documented?
- Did the provider hold appropriate licensure for the type of service delivered?
- Were California professional standards applied to the evaluation/service?
- Were all confidentiality laws respected?
Identify failures as "unprofessional conduct" under §2290.5(d) — Medical Board complaint basis.

## Skill 4: Dusky Standard Application
Apply the two-prong Dusky test to any competency evaluation:
1. Does the defendant have sufficient present ability to consult with their lawyer with a reasonable degree of rational understanding?
2. Does the defendant have a rational as well as factual understanding of the proceedings against them?
Both prongs must be satisfied for competency. Identify evaluations that conflate historical history with present ability, or that fail to assess the specific Dusky prongs.

## Skill 5: § 5150 Hold Validity Analysis
Audit each §5150 hold:
- Was there probable cause that the person, as a result of a mental health disorder, was a danger to self/others or gravely disabled?
- Was the written application completed (§5150(e))?
- Was the advisement given at time of detention (§5150(g))?
- Was the advisement record kept by the facility (§5150(h))?
- Did any third party provide false information to trigger the hold (civil liability under §5150(e))?

## Skill 6: Civil False Statement Liability Under § 5150(e)
Identify third parties who provided information to trigger a §5150 hold. If the information was knowingly false — a person calling police with fabricated danger assertions — that person is civilly liable under §5150(e). Map to documented instances of orchestrated police calls and false reports.

## Skill 7: MC-350 Completeness Audit
The MC-350 (Judicial Council form) is the standard vehicle for reporting §1369 competency evaluation results to a California court. Identify:
- Whether an MC-350 (or equivalent written report) was submitted
- Whether all required §1369(b)(1) elements appear in the report
- Whether the examiner's credentials are documented on the form
- Whether the diagnosis, competency opinion, and treatment recommendation are all present
Missing or incomplete MC-350 = defective evaluation for court purposes.

## Skill 8: Cross-Jurisdiction Telehealth Evaluation Analysis
For any mental health evaluation delivered from outside California:
- The provider must comply with BPC §2290.5 regardless of their state of licensure
- Professional standards of both the originating state and California apply
- A South Carolina psychologist delivering a California court-ordered evaluation is subject to California standards under §2290.5(g)
- Identify licensing and professional conduct violations arising from cross-state delivery

## Skill 9: § 1368 Constitutional Defect Analysis for § 1983 Claim
Frame §1368/§1369 violations as due process claims:
- Mathews v. Eldridge balancing for procedural due process
- Pate v. Robinson (1966): conviction of legally incompetent defendant = due process violation
- Drope v. Missouri (1975): federal constitutional standard for when competency must be raised
Cross-ref to US_Federal_Civil_Rights_Litigator for §1983 drafting.

## Skill 10: Mental Health Record Access and Confidentiality Audit
Mental health records — including §5150 hold records and §1369 evaluation reports — are confidential under CMIA (CIV §56 et seq.) and HIPAA. Audit whether records were properly handled, whether disclosure to third parties was authorized, and whether the steward's requests for records have been fulfilled. Cross-ref to CA_Medical_Privacy_Officer.
