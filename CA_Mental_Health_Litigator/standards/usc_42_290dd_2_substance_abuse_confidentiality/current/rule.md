## Governing Provisions

- **42 U.S.C. § 290dd-2** — "Confidentiality of records" (substance use disorder patient records).
- **42 C.F.R. Part 2** — implementing regulations ("Part 2"), promulgated by SAMHSA.
- Amended materially by **CARES Act § 3221** (2020) and **SAMHSA final rule (effective 2024)** to align disclosure rules more closely with HIPAA — while retaining the heightened protection for SUD records against use in criminal, civil, administrative, or legislative proceedings absent a court order meeting § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C) and Part 2 Subpart E standards.

## Scope — What Records Are Covered

§ 290dd-2 and Part 2 cover **patient identifying information** from a "part 2 program" — a federally-assisted program that holds itself out as providing, and that actually provides, alcohol or drug abuse diagnosis, treatment, or referral for treatment. "Federally assisted" is broadly defined (any federal funding, tax exemption, federal licensure such as DEA registration, etc.), so the statute reaches most California SUD treatment programs.

Records are covered whether they are held by the program or have been **re-disclosed** to a downstream recipient — Part 2 imposes a redisclosure prohibition that "travels with" the record.

## The Rule — General Prohibition

Records of the identity, diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment of any patient that are maintained in connection with the performance of any program or activity relating to substance use disorder education, prevention, training, treatment, rehabilitation, or research that is conducted, regulated, or directly or indirectly assisted by any department or agency of the United States are **confidential**.

Disclosure is permitted **only**:

1. **With patient consent** meeting Part 2's consent-form requirements (specific identity of discloser, specific identity of recipient, specific purpose, specific records, signature, date, revocation rights).
2. **Under a court order** issued under § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C) **and** Part 2 Subpart E (heightened showing: good cause, including that public interest and need for disclosure outweigh the injury to the patient and to the physician-patient relationship; limitation to essential parts; protective measures).
3. **For medical emergencies** threatening the life or physical well-being of the patient.
4. **For qualified research, audits, or program evaluations** meeting Part 2 research-disclosure conditions.
5. **To the FDA** for adverse drug-event reports.
6. **To qualified service organizations** under Qualified Service Organization Agreements.
7. **Post-CARES/2024 final rule:** with a single consent covering all future uses and disclosures for treatment, payment, and health care operations (the HIPAA-harmonization reform), while retaining the **absolute bar on criminal/civil/administrative/legislative use** absent the § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C) court-ordered pathway.

## The Absolute Bar — No Use In Proceedings Without § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C) Court Order

§ 290dd-2(c) bars the use of Part 2 records or testimony relating to them in any **criminal, civil, administrative, or legislative proceeding** conducted by any federal, state, or local authority — unless authorized by a § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C) court order issued under Part 2 Subpart E.

This is stricter than California § 5328. A subpoena, discovery request, or ordinary judicial-access order is **not** a § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C) court order. The heightened-showing court order must be issued after an in-camera review with the specific good-cause, balancing, and limitation findings required by Part 2.

## Redisclosure Prohibition

Every disclosure authorized under Part 2 must carry the prescribed redisclosure notice:

> "This information has been disclosed to you from records protected by federal confidentiality rules (42 C.F.R. Part 2). The federal rules prohibit you from making any further disclosure of information in this record that identifies a patient as having or having had a substance use disorder either directly, by reference to publicly available information, or through verification of such identification by another person unless further disclosure is expressly permitted by the written consent of the individual whose information is being disclosed or as otherwise permitted by 42 C.F.R. Part 2. A general authorization for the release of medical or other information is NOT sufficient for this purpose (see § 2.32). The federal rules restrict any use of the information to investigate or prosecute with regard to a crime any patient with a substance use disorder, except as provided at §§ 2.12(c)(5) and 2.65."

Recipients who redisclose without consent are liable under § 290dd-2.

## Remedies

- **Civil action** — post-CARES, § 290dd-2(f) authorizes private right of action for willful violations.
- **Criminal penalty** — misdemeanor under § 290dd-2(f), fines up to specified amount per violation.
- **42 U.S.C. § 1983** — where state actors participated in the unauthorized disclosure under color of state law.
- **Injunctive relief** — federal district court authority under Part 2.

## The California Interaction

For California SUD treatment records:
- **WIC § 5328** applies if the records were generated during a Division 4/4.1/4.5/5/6/7 service. (§ 5250 IT certification addressing "impairment by chronic alcoholism" brings LPS-adjacent SUD records into § 5328.)
- **42 U.S.C. § 290dd-2 + Part 2** apply if the records were generated at or held by a federally-assisted SUD program.
- **CMIA (Civ. §§ 56.05 et seq.)** applies to all medical records.
- **The stricter rule controls.** For SUD records, § 290dd-2 is generally the strictest — court-ordered disclosure requires the § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C) + Part 2 Subpart E heightened showing, which is narrower than § 5328(a)(6) "as necessary to the administration of justice."

## What Must Exist on the Record

Every SUD-records disclosure audit must:
1. Identify whether the originating program is a "part 2 program" (federally assisted SUD program);
2. If yes, identify the specific Part 2 basis for each disclosure — consent form, court order meeting § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C), medical emergency, etc.;
3. Verify that every downstream recipient received the redisclosure notice;
4. Verify that any use in a legal proceeding was supported by a § 290dd-2(b)(2)(C) court order, not an ordinary subpoena or discovery process.

## Steward Relevance

Mental-health holds where "impairment by chronic alcoholism" or SUD diagnosis was part of the record — a common § 5150/§ 5250 co-diagnosis — have a **second** confidentiality regime layered on top of § 5328. Any disclosure of those records, whether in family-law, custody, CalVCB, criminal, or administrative proceedings, requires a § 290dd-2 compliance audit in addition to a § 5328 audit. This is an independent cause of action with a federal statutory damages framework.
