## Origin: Stats. 1967, Ch. 1667 — Lanterman-Petris-Short Act

§5150 was enacted as the LPS Act's primary tool for emergency involuntary detention. The LPS Act was sponsored by Assemblyman Frank Lanterman, Senator Nicholas Petris, and Assemblyman Alan Short — a bipartisan effort to reform California's mental health commitment system.

**The pre-LPS horror:** Before 1967, California's commitment system (dating to the 1870s) allowed indefinite institutionalization on vague grounds, without judicial review, without time limits, and without meaningful rights for the committed person. Thousands of Californians were institutionalized for years or decades based on complaints from family members, with no meaningful legal protection. The historical record includes deeply abusive practices — detailed in the CHRONICLE archive.

**The LPS reform:** The 72-hour, 14-day, 30-day, 180-day structure with mandatory judicial review at each stage was revolutionary. It ended indefinite commitment and created due process protections. The civil liability provision for false statements (§5150(e)) was part of the original Act — the sponsors understood that the involuntary hold power would be abused and built in civil accountability from the start.
