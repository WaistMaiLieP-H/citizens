# WIC § 5150 — Historical Chain: Origin

**Stage:** 01 — Origin
**Year:** 1967 — Lanterman-Petris-Short Act (LPS Act), Stats. 1967, Ch. 1667

---

## The Wound

Section 5150 was born from the opposite wound from what might be expected: before the LPS Act, California's mental health system operated with almost no procedural protections for involuntary civil commitment. People were committed for indefinite periods on the basis of a physician's certificate and a judge's order, with minimal evidentiary requirements and no requirement of dangerousness. Families could commit relatives. Physicians had broad authority. Commitments lasted years.

The wound was institutionalization as social control — not treatment as its primary function, but warehousing of people who were inconvenient, eccentric, or socially disruptive. California's state hospital population had grown to tens of thousands. Many patients did not meet any meaningful clinical threshold for dangerousness or incapacity.

---

## What the 1967 Legislature Did — The LPS Act

The Lanterman-Petris-Short Act fundamentally restructured California mental health commitment. It established:

1. **Defined criteria:** Only three pathways to involuntary commitment — danger to others, danger to self, or grave disability. Not general mental illness. Not family discomfort. Not social nonconformity.

2. **The probable cause standard:** Commitment authority tied to probable cause — the same constitutional threshold as arrest. This brought involuntary psychiatric detention within the framework of constitutional liberty protections.

3. **Time limits:** The 72-hour period was a radical departure from the prior system. People could not be committed indefinitely without further judicial process.

4. **Advisement rights:** The statutory advisement requirements ensured that detained persons knew why they were being held and how to access legal representation.

---

## The False Statement Provision — A Targeted Fix

The civil liability provision in §5150(e) for intentionally false third-party statements was a direct response to a documented abuse pattern: family members and others were calling police with fabricated danger allegations to use the §5150 mechanism for non-therapeutic purposes — getting a relative hospitalized to take control of their affairs, to punish a relationship partner, or to prevent them from participating in legal proceedings.

Making the false caller civilly liable was the Legislature's answer: the §5150 process is for mental health emergencies, not personal vendettas.

---

## The Promise

California promised in 1967 that no person would be involuntarily detained for psychiatric evaluation without probable cause tied to specific criteria, without a written documented basis, without advisement of their rights, and without a fixed time limit on the emergency detention.

That promise is violated every time the process is weaponized — used as a mechanism of control by people who know the statutory criteria are not met and the probable cause is manufactured.
