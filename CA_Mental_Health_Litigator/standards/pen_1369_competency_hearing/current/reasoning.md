# Pen. Code § 1369 — Competency Hearing — Reasoning

**Standard ID:** `pen_1369_competency_hearing`
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Date:** 2026-04-12

---

## Why Evaluator Qualifications Are the First Line of Defense

§1369(d)'s DSH training guidelines requirement reflects a hard-learned lesson: general clinicians performing forensic evaluations without specialized training produce unreliable results. Forensic psychiatry/psychology is a specialty. The clinical skills required to assess a patient in a therapeutic context are different from the forensic skills required to evaluate competency within a legal framework.

A general psychiatrist who examines a defendant for one hour via telehealth, without reviewing the case file, without forensic specialty training, and without using the mandatory court form, is not conducting a §1369 evaluation — they are performing a clinical impression. The court's appointment does not transform that impression into a legally valid competency determination.

## The Both-Prong Requirement as a Quality Gate

§1369(b)(1)(B) requires both Dusky prongs: understanding of proceedings AND ability to assist counsel. These are analytically distinct:
- A defendant can understand the proceedings intellectually (they know there is a judge, a prosecutor, a charge) but be unable to assist counsel rationally (delusions about their attorney, inability to process and retain legal advice, disorganized communication)
- A defendant can assist counsel in some functional way but lack genuine understanding of what the proceedings mean for them

An evaluator who addresses only one prong has answered the wrong question. Courts have vacated competency findings based on evaluations that collapsed the two prongs into one analysis. The audit question for the Dr. Wiita report: does the report separately analyze (i) understanding and (ii) counsel-assistance, or does it treat them as a single undifferentiated finding?

## The MC-350 As Institutional Accountability

The MC-350 is not bureaucratic formality — it is the instrument through which the court system monitors the quality of competency evaluations. The form structure forces the evaluator to:
1. Identify their qualifications and credentials
2. List the records reviewed
3. Address each statutory element separately
4. State their opinion on each element
5. Sign under penalty of perjury

When an evaluator bypasses MC-350, they bypass all of these accountability mechanisms. The absence of MC-350 is the document equivalent of a confession that the evaluation did not go through the normal accountability process. For an evaluation that resulted in a finding of incompetency (or was used to route a defendant into a mental health diversion), the absence of MC-350 is a significant procedural defect.

## The Presumption of Competence — Who Bears the Risk of Error

§1369(c)(3)'s presumption of competence means: if the evidence is uncertain, the defendant is found competent and the proceeding continues. The party claiming incompetence bears the risk of an equivocal evaluation. An evaluator who says "the defendant may have some difficulty" without clearly establishing incompetency by preponderance should result in a competency finding — not an incompetency finding.

A finding of incompetency based on an ambiguous or equivocal evaluation (especially one from an unqualified evaluator using a non-standard methodology) violates this presumption. The burden was not met. The finding is legally insupportable regardless of the clinical impression.
