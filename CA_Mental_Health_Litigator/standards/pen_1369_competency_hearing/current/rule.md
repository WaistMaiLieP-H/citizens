## Governing Provision

## The Rule — Four-Part Hearing Framework

### Part 1: Evaluator Appointment (§1369(a))

Court appoints **at least one** licensed psychologist or psychiatrist.
- If defense not seeking incompetence finding: two evaluators (one per side) upon request
- Developmental disability suspected: also appoint Regional Center director/designee

**§1369(d) DSH Training Guidelines — The Qualification Requirement:**
DSH adopted guidelines for education and training of appointed evaluators. Court SHALL appoint expert who meets those guidelines OR has **equivalent experience and skills**. Only if no qualified expert available may court appoint without qualification.

**Dr. Wiita Qualification Audit:**
1. Is Dr. Wiita licensed in California? (BPC §2290.5(g) + Medical Practice Act)
2. Does Dr. Wiita meet DSH training guidelines or have equivalent experience?
3. Was Dr. Wiita board-certified in forensic psychiatry (ABPN Forensic Psychiatry subspecialty)?

### Part 2: Mandatory Report Contents (§1369(b)(1))

The evaluator's report MUST address all four sub-elements:

| Required Finding | Sub-element | Dr. Wiita Status |
|---|---|---|
| (A) Diagnosis | Mental disorder, if any | Must appear in report |
| (B) Dusky both prongs | (i) Understand proceedings AND (ii) Assist counsel rationally | BOTH required — one alone is insufficient |
| (C) Restorability | Likelihood of attaining competency; antipsychotic medication response | Must address |
| (D) §1001.36 diversion (if defense requests) | Diversion eligibility opinion | Only if requested |

**The §1369(b)(1)(B) Both-Prong Requirement is the Critical Audit Point:**
A report that addresses only "understanding of proceedings" without addressing "ability to assist counsel in rational manner" is an incomplete report that does not satisfy §1369. Similarly, a report that addresses only counsel-assistance without addressing proceedings understanding is incomplete. BOTH prongs of Dusky must appear in the written report.

### Part 3: Presumption and Burden (§1369(c)(3))

Defendant is **presumed competent** to stand trial.
- Burden is on the party claiming incompetence
- Standard: **preponderance of the evidence**
- This means: if the evidence is equal, the defendant is found competent

If either party objects to the competency report, a hearing is held (§1369(c)(2)).
If no objection, court may determine competency based solely on the report (§1369(c)(1)).

### Part 4: Jury Trial Right (§1369(c)(4))

Competency determination is a jury trial right unless:
- Defense counsel WAIVES and prosecution CONSENTS, OR
- Probation/parole revocation proceeding (court decides)

Verdict must be **UNANIMOUS** — competency determination has the same unanimity requirement as a criminal verdict.

## The MC-350 Form — The Missing Audit Trail

Judicial Council Form MC-350 is the mandatory court-appointed expert evaluation form for competency evaluations. Its use is prescribed by court rules (Cal. Rules of Court). An evaluation that does not use MC-350 was not conducted within the court's prescribed procedural framework.

**If MC-350 was not used for Dr. Wiita's evaluation:**
- The evaluation may not have been properly completed under §1369's framework
- The absence of the form is independently documentable from the court file
- The absence is evidence that the evaluation was procedurally defective
- A procedurally defective evaluation that is then used to find incompetency may constitute structural error (In re Anthony H.)
