# WIC § 5250 — Case Law Anchors

**Layer:** 4 — Case Law
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## Primary Authority

### *Conservatorship of Roulet* (1979) 23 Cal.3d 219

Supreme Court of California held that beyond-a-reasonable-doubt and unanimous-jury standards apply to LPS conservatorship proceedings. While *Roulet* is directly about § 5350, its reasoning — that LPS commitment implicates the same liberty interest as criminal confinement — is the anchor for demanding a heightened standard at each preceding LPS gate, including § 5250 certification review. A § 5250 record that would not satisfy a reasonable trier of fact on the four cumulative conditions is, under *Roulet*'s logic, constitutionally infirm.

**Anchor use:** Cite *Roulet* to reject any argument that § 5250 certification is a mere administrative step. It is the statutory predicate for a liberty deprivation the Supreme Court has equated with criminal confinement.

### *Riese v. St. Mary's Hospital & Medical Center* (1987) 209 Cal.App.3d 1303

Court of Appeal held that persons detained under § 5150/§ 5250 retain the right to refuse antipsychotic medication absent an emergency or a judicial finding of incapacity. A § 5250 certification does not automatically carry medication-administration authority. The facility must separately establish incapacity to consent to the specific medication.

**Anchor use:** Forced medication during an IT window is its own § 5325 violation. *Riese* is the authority that prevents certification from being treated as a blanket consent proxy.

### *Conservatorship of Susan T.* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 1005

California Supreme Court addressed the evidentiary standards governing LPS conservatorship, reinforcing that continued involuntary commitment requires fresh findings at each statutory stage — a holding that structurally supports the view that § 5250 is an independent finding, not a continuation of § 5150.

**Anchor use:** Authority for the proposition that each LPS gate is an independent determination.
