# WIC § 5250 — Historical Chain / Historical Loss Layer

**Layer:** 3 — Historical Loss (what this provision was enacted to prevent)
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## Pre-LPS California Commitment Regime (pre-1967)

Before the Lanterman-Petris-Short Act (Stats. 1967, Ch. 1667), California civil commitment operated under the 1939 Welfare and Institutions Code framework (and its 19th-century predecessors). A person could be committed to a state hospital on a judicial finding of being "mentally ill and in need of supervision, care, or restraint" (former WIC § 5550). Commitment was **indefinite** — no automatic review, no fixed term. Release depended on the medical superintendent's discretion. California state hospital populations peaked around 1959 at more than 37,000 involuntarily committed persons.

## The Loss § 5250 Was Enacted To Prevent

Three specific pre-LPS harms drove the § 5250 design:

1. **Indefinite detention on a single finding.** Pre-LPS commitment orders had no sunset. A person committed at age 20 could still be institutionalized at 60 without any renewed judicial finding. § 5250 replaced this with a hard 14-day ceiling on continued post-§ 5150 detention.

2. **No clinical-independence requirement.** Pre-LPS commitment typically rested on the same petition or affidavit that triggered intake — there was no required step of independent clinical analysis by the treating facility. § 5250(a) installed that step.

3. **No family/friend safety valve.** Pre-LPS "inability to care for self" findings could be made without any inquiry into whether family or friends would provide support. § 5250(d), read with its explicit legislative-intent subdivision (d)(3), was the drafters' repudiation of this failure mode.

## Assembly Committee on Ways and Means (1967) — LPS Findings

The legislative findings accompanying LPS (codified at WIC § 5001) declared four purposes, of which three are directly tied to § 5250:

- To end the inappropriate, indefinite, and involuntary commitment of mentally disordered persons, persons with developmental disabilities, and persons impaired by chronic alcoholism;
- To provide prompt evaluation and treatment;
- To guarantee and protect public safety;
- To safeguard individual rights through judicial review.

"End the inappropriate, indefinite, and involuntary commitment" is the phrase that animates § 5250's four cumulative gates.

## Historical Loss Summary

§ 5250 exists because tens of thousands of Californians were detained for years without renewed findings, without independent clinical evaluation, and without any inquiry into less-restrictive alternatives. The four cumulative conditions of § 5250(a)–(d) are the drafters' direct answer to that history. A certification that short-circuits any of those four conditions replays the pre-LPS regime the statute was enacted to end.
