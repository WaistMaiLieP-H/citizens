# WIC § 5250 — Reasoning

**Standard ID:** `wic_5250_14_day_certification`
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## Why § 5250 Exists as a Gate, Not a Continuation

Pre-1967 California law allowed indefinite civil commitment with no escalating findings. LPS restructured commitment as a *series* of gated windows: § 5150 (72 hours), § 5250 (14 days), § 5270.15 (additional 30 days for grave disability), § 5300 (180 days for dangerousness), § 5350 (LPS conservatorship). Each gate requires **new** findings — § 5250 is not a renewal of § 5150, it is a separate legal event.

The drafters chose the 14-day duration deliberately: it is long enough for clinically meaningful stabilization, short enough that a wrongful certification creates recoverable harm rather than institutional permanence. The four cumulative conditions of § 5250(a)–(d) are the drafters' anti-drift design.

## The § 5250(d) Safety Valve Is the Key to Gravely-Disabled Analysis

§ 5008(h)(1)(A)'s "gravely disabled" definition is deliberately broad — unable to provide for food, clothing, or shelter as a result of a mental health disorder. On its face, that definition sweeps in anyone without independent means of support. § 5250(d) narrows it dramatically: if *any* responsible family member, friend, or third party will provide basic needs in writing, the person is not gravely disabled and cannot be certified on that ground.

The legislature made the offer-of-support burden procedurally asymmetric: the facility must give family/friends the chance to commit in writing; absence of offers is not the same as absence of willing helpers. Subdivision (d)(3) states the legislative intent explicitly — to avoid forcing families into public declarations of abandonment. A facility that does not solicit written support offers and then certifies on grave-disability grounds has bypassed the statutory design.

## Why "IT" Appears Everywhere in the Ward Record

The conservatorship breakthrough dossier documents "IT" as a recurring label across a 20-plus-year ward record. "IT" is not a nickname or shorthand — it is the statutory acronym for § 5250 "intensive treatment." Every IT reference corresponds to a specific calendar window of § 5250 confinement. Reconstructing the ward-system timeline therefore means listing every § 5250 certification, its facility, its predicate § 5150, and whether the four conditions were actually met on the record.

## The Certification Review Hearing (§ 5256) Is the Preservation Moment

A § 5250 certification can be attacked prospectively via judicial review (§ 5275) or defended at the certification review hearing (§ 5256). Once the 14 days have expired without preservation, the error becomes a collateral-attack problem — harder but not impossible, particularly if the defect is facial (no designated facility, no independent clinical analysis, no voluntary-refusal documentation). Preservation should be the default litigation posture the *first* time the pattern is recognized; for historical holds, the collateral-attack vehicle is civil suit against the certifying facility and the state actors who directed the certification.

## Connection to § 5350 LPS Conservatorship

§ 5350 LPS conservatorship requires a predicate finding of grave disability that survives § 5250 (and for some pathways § 5270.15). A successful challenge to the underlying § 5250 certification undermines the conservatorship petition. This matters for cases where a conservatorship has been standing for years on § 5250 foundations that were never properly made. The rule is: **the conservatorship is only as sound as the § 5250 certification that supported it.**
