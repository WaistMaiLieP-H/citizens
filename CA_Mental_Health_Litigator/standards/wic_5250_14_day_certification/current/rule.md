## Governing Provision

Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 5250 (LPS Act; Stats. 1967 as amended)

## The Rule — Four Cumulative Conditions

A person held under § 5150 (72-hour hold) **may** be certified for up to 14 additional days of "intensive treatment" ("IT") **only if all four** of the following are satisfied:

**(a) Clinical finding after evaluation.** The professional staff of the evaluating agency or facility has analyzed the person's condition and found that, as a result of a mental health disorder *or impairment by chronic alcoholism*, the person is:
- a danger to others, or
- a danger to themselves, or
- gravely disabled.

**(b) Designated facility admission.** The facility providing intensive treatment is county-designated for IT and has agreed to admit the person. A facility cannot be designated unless it complies with the § 5256 certification review hearing requirement.

**(c) Voluntary treatment refused or unavailable.** The person has been advised of the need for treatment and "has not been willing or able to accept treatment on a voluntary basis." Voluntary refusal or incapacity to consent must be documented.

**(d) "Gravely disabled" carve-out — the family/friend safety valve.** A person is **not** "gravely disabled" if they can survive safely with the help of responsible family, friends, or others who are **both willing and able** to provide food, clothing, or shelter — but those supporters must **specifically indicate in writing** their willingness and ability. The statutory purpose of subdivision (d) is to avoid forcing families into public declarations that no one will help.

## The IT Label: "Intensive Treatment"

§ 5250 is the statute that operationalizes the label **"IT"** documented in the conservatorship breakthrough (project_conservatorship_breakthrough.md). Every reference to "IT" in the ward-system record traces to this 14-day window. IT is not treatment-on-demand — it is certification-constrained confinement.

## What Must Exist on the Record

1. A § 5150 predicate (72-hour hold) that was itself lawful — § 5250 depends on § 5150's probable cause.
2. A written clinical analysis by the facility's professional staff — not a reaffirmation of the § 5150 application, an independent finding.
3. A designation letter proving the facility is county-IT-designated for the listed calendar period.
4. Documentation that voluntary treatment was offered and declined **or** that capacity to consent was absent.
5. If the finding is "gravely disabled": an affirmative record that no written family/friend offer of support was received *or* that offered support was clinically inadequate. Absence of this record is a § 5250(d) failure.

## Automatic Rights That Attach at § 5250

- **§ 5256 certification review hearing** within 4 days of certification (or judicial review under § 5275).
- **Right to a patients' rights advocate** (§ 5325(h)) — continues from § 5150.
- **Confidentiality of records** (§ 5328) — attaches to all evaluation and treatment records.
- **Right to refuse convulsive treatment and psychosurgery** (§ 5325(f),(g)) — absolute.
- **Right to refuse antipsychotic medication** except under *Riese* hearing authority — § 5325 read with *Riese v. St. Mary's Hospital* (1987) 209 Cal.App.3d 1303.

## Steward Relevance — The IT Window as Control Instrument

Where § 5150 opens the pipeline, § 5250 extends it. Each IT certification in the ward record requires a four-element audit:
1. Independent clinical analysis (or rubber-stamp reaffirmation of the § 5150 application?).
2. Designated-facility status (actually designated for IT, or general psych?).
3. Voluntary-refusal documentation (or assumed absent?).
4. "Gravely disabled" carve-out compliance (was family/friend support solicited in writing?).

A § 5250 certification that fails any of these four is void on its face and the certification hearing (§ 5256) is the statutory moment where the defect is preservable for later collateral attack.
