# PEN § 1369 — Competency Evaluation Procedure: Rule Summary

**Build date:** 2026-04-13

## The § 1369 Evaluation Checklist

When a §1368 competency doubt has been stated and inquiry commenced, §1369 governs the evaluation procedure. Every item on this checklist is mandatory.

### Examiner Qualification

**§1369(a)(1):** At least one licensed psychologist or psychiatrist must be appointed by the court. The court — not the parties — makes the appointment.

**§1369(d)(2):** The appointed expert must meet DSH training guidelines (adopted by July 1, 2017) or have equivalent experience and skills. An examiner who does not meet these standards may only be appointed if no reasonably available qualified expert exists.

**Application to Dr. Wiita:** A South Carolina-licensed psychologist delivering a California court-ordered §1369 evaluation must: (1) be appointed by the court, (2) be a licensed psychologist or psychiatrist, and (3) meet or have equivalent experience to DSH guidelines. The basis for Dr. Wiita's appointment must be documented. If the court appointed Dr. Wiita without confirming compliance with §1369(d)(2), the appointment was defective.

### Report Requirements — Four Mandatory Elements

**§1369(b)(1):** The written report to the court MUST include:

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **(A)** | Diagnosis of defendant's mental condition, if any |
| **(B)** | Whether defendant can understand the proceedings AND assist counsel in a rational manner (this is the Dusky test — both prongs) |
| **(C)** | Whether there is substantial likelihood of attaining competency in the foreseeable future, including antipsychotic medication response |
| **(D)** | If requested by defense: eligibility for PC §1001.36 mental health diversion |

A report missing ANY of elements (A)-(C) is an incomplete §1369 evaluation. A report that does not address whether the defendant can assist counsel in a rational manner (element B, second prong) has not evaluated the Dusky standard.

**The MC-350 connection:** The Judicial Council MC-350 form is structured to capture all four §1369(b)(1) elements. If no MC-350 was submitted, the question is whether an equivalent written report addressing all four elements was delivered to the court. If neither exists — the §1369 evaluation is procedurally void.

### The Competency Presumption — § 1369(c)(3)

**The defendant shall be presumed competent to stand trial unless it is proved by a preponderance of the evidence that the defendant is mentally incompetent.**

This presumption has three implications:
1. The burden is on the party asserting incompetency — not the defendant to prove competency
2. The standard is preponderance — a coin-flip burden, not clear and convincing
3. Absent a contested hearing with an objecting party, the court may rely on the evaluation report — but ONLY if the report satisfies §1369(b)(1)

A defective evaluation (missing elements, uncredentialed examiner, improper delivery) cannot overcome the presumption of competency. If an incompetency finding was made on the basis of Dr. Wiita's defective evaluation, the finding itself was made against the presumption and without a procedurally valid basis.

### The Unanimity Requirement — § 1369(c)(4)

If there is a jury determination of competency, the verdict must be UNANIMOUS. This mirrors the unanimity requirement for criminal convictions — competency determinations carry constitutional weight.

## The Dr. Wiita Evaluation — Full Defect Analysis

| Defect | Statutory basis | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Telehealth without consent | BPC §2290.5(b) | Unprofessional conduct; evaluation tainted from inception |
| SC-licensed examiner, CA professional standards not confirmed | BPC §2290.5(g) | Evaluation may not meet §1369(d)(2) guidelines |
| No MC-350 / missing written report | §1369(b)(1) | Evaluation procedurally void — no valid report submitted to court |
| Missing §1369(b)(1)(B) Dusky prongs | §1369(b)(1)(B) | Evaluation failed to assess the operative legal standard |
| Incompetency finding on defective evaluation | §1369(c)(3) | Finding made without overcoming presumption of competency |
| Proceedings continued without valid competency determination | §1368(c) | All subsequent proceedings constitutionally infirm |

Each defect is independently sufficient to challenge the validity of the competency determination. Together, they establish a comprehensive procedural breakdown that reaches constitutional dimension under Pate v. Robinson.
