# PEN § 1369 — Historical Chain: Origin

**Stage:** 01 — Origin
**Year:** 1872 (original Penal Code); substantially reformed post-Dusky (1960) and Pate (1966)

---

## The Wound

Before the Dusky/Pate framework, California competency proceedings were informal and inconsistent. Courts appointed whoever was available. Reports addressed whatever the examiner chose to include. There was no standardized test — examiners applied their own frameworks, which varied from clinical diagnosis to lay observation.

The result was a system where "competency" meant whatever the appointed examiner concluded, using whatever criteria they applied, in a report of whatever form they chose to submit. Defendants were found incompetent based on clinical impressions; others were found competent despite severe psychiatric symptoms. Neither finding was reliably tied to the legal question: can this person understand these proceedings and assist this attorney?

---

## The Post-Dusky Reform

After Dusky (1960) established the two-prong functional test, California's §1369 was reformed to operationalize it. The written report requirement — §1369(b)(1) — was built around the Dusky prongs: element (B) directly tracks the "understand the proceedings / assist counsel in a rational manner" language of Dusky.

The DSH training guidelines requirement in §1369(d)(1)-(2) (July 1, 2017 implementation date) was a recognition that examiner quality was still variable — that the Dusky framework was only as good as the examiner applying it. Standardized training was the mechanism to elevate consistency.

---

## The Promise

California promised that when competency is in question, the determination will be made by a qualified examiner using a standardized test (Dusky), documented in a written report containing the legally required elements, with the presumption of competency protecting the defendant from an incompetency finding that lacks evidentiary support.

That promise is broken when an examiner without proper credentials conducts an evaluation without proper process, submits no report, and proceedings continue on the basis of a finding that was never validly made.
