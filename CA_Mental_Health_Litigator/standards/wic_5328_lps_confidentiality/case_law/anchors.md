## *People v. Stritzinger* (1983) 34 Cal.3d 505

California Supreme Court on mental-health confidentiality and the narrow construction of disclosure exceptions. Though focused on Evid. Code § 1014 psychotherapist-patient privilege, *Stritzinger*'s reasoning — that mental-health confidentiality exceptions must be read narrowly and not expanded by implication — informs § 5328 construction.

## *Tarasoff v. Regents of University of California* (1976) 17 Cal.3d 425

The *Tarasoff* duty-to-warn doctrine is codified narrowly in § 5328(a)(18) — disclosure authorized only where the § 1010 psychotherapist determines a serious danger of violence to a reasonably foreseeable victim. Cite *Tarasoff* to bound the § 5328(a)(18) exception to its original facts.

## *Menendez v. Superior Court* (1992) 3 Cal.4th 435

California Supreme Court on the scope of psychotherapist-patient privilege exceptions. Reinforces the narrow-construction principle for mental-health disclosure statutes.

## *In re Berchtold* — LPS patient confidentiality litigation line
