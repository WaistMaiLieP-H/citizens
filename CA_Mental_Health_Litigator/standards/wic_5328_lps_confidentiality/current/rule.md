## Governing Provision

Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 5328

## The Rule — General Prohibition

**All information and records obtained in the course of providing services** under:
- Division 4 (§ 4000 et seq., developmental disabilities)
- Division 4.1 (§ 4400 et seq., regional centers)
- Division 4.5 (§ 4500 et seq., Lanterman Act)
- **Division 5 (§ 5000 et seq., LPS Act)** — the relevant division for mental-health commitments
- Division 6 (§ 6000 et seq., judicial commitment)
- Division 7 (§ 7100 et seq., state-hospital administration)

**to either voluntary or involuntary recipients of services are confidential.** Records from pre-1969 similar services are also confidential.

The prohibition is general. Disclosure is permitted **only** within the enumerated exceptions at § 5328(a)(1)–(28) and § 5328(b). Every disclosure must trace to a specific, numbered exception on the statute's face.

## The 28 Enumerated Exceptions (summary)

The exceptions are narrow and fact-specific. Grouped:

**Consent-based:**
- (a)(2) — patient designates recipient with approval of treating professional;
- (a)(4) — conservator/parent/legal guardian/GAL designation in writing for minor/conservatee;
- (a)(9) — disability/life insurance designation by applicant;
- (a)(10) — patient's attorney with release, or facility verification when patient cannot sign;
- (a)(13) — county patients' rights advocate with knowing voluntary authorization;
- (a)(17) — genetic counseling for blood relatives with consent.

**Provider-to-provider:**
- (a)(1)(A) — communications between qualified professionals in the provision of services or appropriate referrals or in the course of conservatorship proceedings (with patient/conservator/guardian consent to disclosure outside the facility);
- (a)(24) — emergency services and care communications among specified professionals.

**Governmental/judicial:**
- (a)(6) — to the courts, as necessary to the administration of justice;
- (a)(7) — to governmental law enforcement for federal/state elective constitutional officer protection;
- (a)(8) — to legislative Rules committees for authorized investigation;
- (a)(11) — to probation officer post-conviction with patient written agreement, for sentencing only, then sealed;
- (a)(16) — to county behavioral health director, law enforcement, or designee per §§ 5152.1, 5250.1;
- (a)(20) — to LEO with personally lodged arrest warrant for serious/violent felony — confined-or-not disclosure only;
- (a)(22) — employee adverse-action proceedings under Gov. Code § 19570 (strict protective-order conditions);
- (a)(26) — CVCB Forced/Involuntary Sterilization Compensation Program verification;
- (a)(27) — parties to judicial/administrative proceedings "as permitted by law" satisfying 45 C.F.R. § 164.512(e);
- (a)(28) — State Department of Health Care Services per § 4081.

**Research:**
- (a)(5) — with director regulations, institutional review board oversight, and signed oath of confidentiality.

**Dependency/child welfare:**
- (a)(1)(B) — ward/dependent of juvenile court removed from custody, to social worker/probation officer for services transition (use restricted in criminal/juvenile proceedings; § 827 preserved);
- (a)(4)(B) — minor dependent/ward with attorney/GAL written designation;
- (a)(12) — multidisciplinary personnel teams under § 18951(d) for child abuse/neglect;
- (a)(23) — developmental services decisionmaker under §§ 319/361/726.

**Elder abuse:**
- (a)(21) — multidisciplinary personnel teams under § 15610.55 for elder/dependent-adult abuse.

**Safety:**
- (a)(18) — *Tarasoff*-style disclosure to foreseeable victims and law enforcement when psychotherapist believes patient presents serious danger of violence;
- (a)(19) — HIV/AIDS exposure disclosure under Ryan White Act (with designated-officer confidentiality chain, personal liability under H&S § 120980).

**Insurance/benefits claims:**
- (a)(3) — to the extent necessary for recipient (or on recipient's behalf) to make a claim for aid, insurance, or medical assistance.

**Patient-rights/QI:**
- (a)(14) — to committee established under § 14725;
- (a)(15) — as described in § 7325.5 (only that information).

**HIPAA overlay:**
- (a)(25) — to business associate or for health care operations per 45 C.F.R. Parts 160/164.

**Prosecution/commitment proceedings:**
- (b) — to district attorneys as necessary for commitment/recommitment/release proceedings under Pen. Code §§ 1026, 1370, 1600, 2962, 2972 and WIC § 6600.

## The Operative Burden Is On The Discloser

Every disclosure requires the discloser to identify the statutory exception. Absence of an identifiable exception = unlawful disclosure. Listing the exception is not optional, and a post-hoc explanation is not a defense — the disclosure must have been statutorily authorized at the moment it occurred.

## Family Law / Custody / DVRO Admissibility Gate

§ 5328 is the admissibility gate for LPS records entering a family-law or probate proceeding. The most common mistakes on the family-law side:
- treating all § 5328(a)(6) disclosures "to the courts" as automatic — the exception is "as necessary to the administration of justice," not a blank check;
- relying on § 5328(a)(27) "parties to a judicial or administrative proceeding as permitted by law" without confirming HIPAA 45 C.F.R. § 164.512(e) conditions are also satisfied (qualified protective order or equivalent);
- assuming conservator written designation under § 5328(a)(4)(A) permits release beyond the designated persons — it does not.

## Remedies for Unauthorized Disclosure

- **Civil action** by the patient against the discloser (§ 5330 civil remedy framework).
- **Statutory damages** under related privacy frameworks (CMIA, constitutional privacy, privacy torts) where disclosure also violates those laws.
- **§ 1983** where the unauthorized discloser is a state actor.

## Steward Relevance — The Family-Law Audit Connection
