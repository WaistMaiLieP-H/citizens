# WIC § 5328 — Historical Loss Layer

**Layer:** 3 — Historical Loss
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## What § 5328 Was Enacted To Prevent

Pre-LPS California had no comprehensive mental-health confidentiality statute. State-hospital records were routinely accessible to:
- family members of committed persons (without patient consent);
- employers through informal contacts with medical superintendents;
- law enforcement through unrestricted cooperation arrangements;
- insurance companies through blanket medical release forms signed at admission.

The documented consequence was a one-way ratchet: once committed, a person's records circulated indefinitely with no patient control and no statutory constraint on redisclosure.

## The 1967 Original Version Versus Modern § 5328

The original 1967 § 5328 listed a smaller set of exceptions. Over the decades, the Legislature added exceptions for evolving circumstances — HIV/AIDS disclosure (Ryan White, 1990s), dependency/child-welfare handoffs, elder-abuse multidisciplinary teams, HIPAA harmonization, CVCB sterilization compensation verification, and others. Each addition was a narrow amendment responsive to a specific federal/state development.

The **general prohibition has remained stable.** What has evolved is the exception list. This matters for historical records: a pre-2000 disclosure must be tested against the version of § 5328 in effect at the time, not the current version. A disclosure that would fit a modern exception but did not fit the historical statute was unlawful when made.

## The 1970 Amendment (Paragraph (4), Subdivision (c))

§ 5328(c) memorializes that the 1970 amendment to paragraph (4) of subdivision (a) was declaratory of pre-existing law — meaning the Legislature viewed the 1970 change as clarifying, not altering, the conservator-designation framework. This drafting note is useful when litigating conservator-designation disclosures from the 1969-1970 period.
