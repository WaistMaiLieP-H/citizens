# BPC § 2290.5 — Telehealth Standards: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Mental_Health_Litigator / bpc_2290_5_telehealth
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## What § 2290.5 Requires

### Before Delivering Any Telehealth Service — § 2290.5(b)

The health care provider must:
1. **Inform the patient** about the use of telehealth — what it is, that services will be delivered remotely
2. **Obtain consent** — verbal OR written — for the use of telehealth as an acceptable mode of delivering health care
3. **Document the consent**

All three steps are required. Informing but not documenting consent is non-compliant. Obtaining undocumented verbal consent that cannot be confirmed is non-compliant. The documentation requirement is the audit trail.

### All Professional Standards Apply — § 2290.5(g)

A health care provider delivering services via telehealth is subject to:
- All laws governing professional responsibility
- All standards governing unprofessional conduct
- All standards of practice applicable to the provider's license

**The cross-jurisdiction implication:** A South Carolina-licensed psychologist delivering a court-ordered California PC §1369 competency evaluation via telehealth is delivering health care services in California. California's professional standards for that type of evaluation — including §1369's report requirements, DSH examiner qualifications, and the Dusky evaluation framework — apply to that provider while delivering the service.

§2290.5(g) does not exempt out-of-state providers from California standards. It applies to "health care providers" as defined in §2290.5(a)(3) — any person licensed under BPC Division 2. The provider's license may be from another state; the standards of practice that apply are California's when delivering services in California.

### Confidentiality — § 2290.5(f)

All laws regarding health care information confidentiality apply to telehealth interactions. A telehealth mental health evaluation is subject to the same CMIA and HIPAA protections as an in-person session. The telehealth format does not create a confidentiality exception.

### Scope of Practice — § 2290.5(e)

Telehealth does not expand scope of practice. If a type of service is not authorized for a provider in an in-person setting, it is not authorized via telehealth. A psychologist who lacks authority under their license to conduct court-ordered forensic evaluations cannot gain that authority through telehealth delivery.

### The Unprofessional Conduct Consequence — § 2290.5(d)

Failure to comply with §2290.5 constitutes **unprofessional conduct**. This is the language used in the BPC to trigger Medical Board (or Psychology Board) disciplinary proceedings. An unprofessional conduct finding can result in:
- License suspension
- License revocation
- Public reprimand
- Conditions on practice

The reference to §2314 not applying means that the §2290.5 violation does not require the degree of severity or harm typically required for Medical Board action — the non-compliance itself is sufficient.

### The Professional Accountability Track

If BPC §2290.5 was violated by Dr. Wiita:
1. **Medical Board / Psychology Board complaint** — unprofessional conduct under §2290.5(d)
2. **California and South Carolina bar/board cross-complaint** if SC licensure was used to deliver CA services without CA license
3. **§1369 evaluation challenge** — defective evaluation as basis for incompetency finding

### The § 2290.5(h) Correctional Exception

Section 2290.5 does NOT apply to patients under jurisdiction of the Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation or any other correctional facility. **Steward must confirm:** Was the Dr. Wiita evaluation conducted while Michael Hartmann was under CDCR jurisdiction? If yes, §2290.5 does not apply to that evaluation. If no — if the evaluation was conducted while he was a criminal defendant but not yet sentenced or not in custody — §2290.5 applies in full.
