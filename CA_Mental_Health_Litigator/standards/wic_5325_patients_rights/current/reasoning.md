# WIC § 5325 — Reasoning

**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## Why § 5325 Is Structured As A Floor, Not A Ceiling

§ 5325 enumerates **minimum** rights of LPS patients. The rights listed are the rights every detained person retains — they do not exhaust the rights a patient has under the California Constitution, the U.S. Constitution, common law, or other statutes. The statute's "Other rights, as specified by regulation" clause (§ 5325(i)) and the final paragraph's commitment to notification of "other constitutional and statutory rights... frequently misunderstood, ignored, or denied" expressly contemplate that the enumerated list is only the floor.

This matters when a facility argues that the list is exhaustive. It is not. A right not listed is not thereby waived.

## The Three Absolutes: ECT, Psychosurgery, Non-Waiver by Third Parties

§ 5325 contains three rights the statute treats as **absolute** — not subject to facility override, capacity finding, or third-party consent:

1. **§ 5325(f) — Refusal of convulsive treatment (ECT, insulin coma).** No capacity hearing can override. A facility that administers ECT over patient refusal is acting outside statutory authority, period.
2. **§ 5325(g) — Refusal of psychosurgery.** Same — absolute.
3. **Non-waiver clause.** The final paragraph: these rights may not be waived by the person's parent, guardian, or conservator.

Medication (antipsychotic) refusal is treated differently — it is subject to *Riese* capacity proceedings. The three absolutes above are not.

## Why § 5325(h) Advocate Independence Is the Accountability Hinge

Every other § 5325 right is directly exercised by the patient. § 5325(h) is the **structural** right — the right to meet with an independent person whose job is to carry forward the patient's concerns. The statute goes out of its way to define independence negatively: "no direct or indirect clinical or administrative responsibility." This language was drafted to foreclose the easy workaround of designating a clinical social worker or ward administrator as "advocate."

The § 5325(h) advocate is the statutory counterweight to the facility's institutional interest in continued confinement. Where advocate visits are missing, abbreviated, or performed by conflicted personnel, the enforcement mechanism for every other right is hollow.

## The Family-Law and Conservatorship Implication of Non-Waiver

The non-waiver clause is the provision most often misunderstood in adjacent proceedings. A conservator's signature on a "consent" to ECT, psychosurgery, or the rest of § 5325 is **void**. Family-law and probate orders that purport to delegate § 5325 authority exceed the delegating court's jurisdiction. This is the legal basis for attacking historical ECT or similar events that appear to have been authorized by a conservator or parent.

## The Handbook Duty As Its Own Cause of Action

"Immediately... given a copy" is a specific, auditable event. A facility that cannot produce a signed or witnessed handbook-receipt for a particular admission has violated § 5325 regardless of how well the substantive rights were honored during the stay. This is the single easiest § 5325 defect to document from records requests.
