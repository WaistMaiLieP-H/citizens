# WIC § 5325 — Patients' Bill of Rights — Rule

**Standard ID:** `wic_5325_patients_rights`
**Citizen:** CA_Mental_Health_Litigator
**Layer:** 1 — Current Operative Rule
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## Governing Provision

Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 5325

## Scope

§ 5325 applies to **every** person "involuntarily detained for evaluation or treatment" under LPS **and** to every voluntary psychiatric patient admitted to a health facility defined by H&S Code § 1250. The rights also apply to every person committed to a California state hospital.

## The Posting and Advisement Duty

A list of the § 5325 rights must be:
1. **Prominently posted** in the predominant languages of the community, **and**
2. **Explained** to each patient in a language or modality the patient can access, **and**
3. **Brought to attention** by any additional means the Director of Health Care Services or Director of State Hospitals designates.

Upon admission, the patient must **immediately** be given a copy of the prepared patients' rights handbook.

Posting plus explanation plus handbook are **three separate** duties. A facility that posts but does not explain has violated the advisement duty. A facility that explains but does not hand the handbook has violated the handbook duty. Each is independently actionable.

## The Enumerated Rights (§ 5325(a)–(i))

**(a)** Wear own clothes; keep personal possessions, toilet articles; keep/spend reasonable sum of own money for canteen purchases.
**(b)** Access to individual storage space for private use.
**(c)** See visitors each day.
**(d)** Reasonable access to telephones — both to make and receive **confidential** calls, or to have calls made for them.
**(e)** Ready access to letter-writing materials including stamps; mail and receive **unopened** correspondence.
**(f)** Refuse convulsive treatment — including ECT, any treatment depending on induced convulsion by any means, and insulin coma treatment. **Absolute right.**
**(g)** Refuse psychosurgery — lobotomy, psychiatric surgery, behavioral surgery, and all other brain surgery performed for the purpose of (1) modifying or controlling thoughts/feelings/actions/behavior rather than treating a known diagnosed physical brain disease, (2) modifying normal brain function/tissue to control thoughts/feelings/actions/behavior, or (3) treating abnormal brain function where the abnormality is not an established cause of the thoughts/feelings/actions/behavior. **Absolute right.** Prefrontal sonic treatment with no brain-tissue destruction is excluded from the definition of psychosurgery.
**(h)** See and receive the services of a **patient advocate** who has **no direct or indirect clinical or administrative responsibility** for the person receiving services.
**(i)** Other rights, as specified by regulation (see CCR Title 9 patient-rights regulations).

## The Non-Waiver Rule

The final paragraph of § 5325 is operational: **these rights may not be waived by the person's parent, guardian, or conservator.** Third-party waiver is statutorily void. A conservator who signs a purported waiver of ECT or psychosurgery rights has not achieved consent — the rights survive third-party signature attempts.

## Interaction With Medication Refusal (Riese)

§ 5325 does not expressly list a right to refuse antipsychotic medication, but that right exists at common law and constitutional law and is the subject of *Riese v. St. Mary's Hospital* (1987) 209 Cal.App.3d 1303, which requires a separate capacity determination before antipsychotic medication can be administered over objection. § 5325(f) and (g) rights are **absolute** and not subject to *Riese*-style override. Medication is different.

## Advocate Independence (§ 5325(h))

The § 5325(h) patient advocate must be structurally independent of the facility — "no direct or indirect clinical or administrative responsibility." A facility-employed "advocate" with any clinical or administrative role is not a statutory advocate. Identifying the person who functioned as advocate, and their actual reporting lines, is a threshold audit step.

## What Must Exist on the Record

1. Posting documentation — photo or sworn statement that the rights list was posted in the appropriate languages.
2. Advisement documentation — form or log entry recording the oral/signed explanation, including the patient's language/modality.
3. Handbook receipt — signed or witnessed receipt that the patient was *immediately* given the handbook upon admission.
4. Advocate visit log — § 5325(h) advocate visits with dates, durations, and the advocate's organizational affiliation (proving independence).
5. Treatment-refusal log — any ECT, convulsive treatment, or psychosurgery refusal, and the facility's documented response.

## Steward Relevance

§ 5325 is the enumerated backbone of every LPS-period rights claim. Each violation is:
- independently actionable in the § 5256 certification review hearing;
- a predicate for § 1983 state-actor claims when facility staff act under color of state law;
- a factual predicate for *Tarasoff*- and *Riese*-style common-law theories.

For the ward-system audit, § 5325 is the per-day checklist against which every hold-day is measured.
