# WIC § 5325 — Historical Loss Layer

**Layer:** 3 — Historical Loss
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## What § 5325 Was Enacted To Prevent

Pre-1967 California state-hospital conditions are the historical record § 5325 was drafted against. Documented pre-LPS practices in the state-hospital system included:

1. **Routine forced ECT and insulin coma treatment.** California state hospitals performed tens of thousands of ECT treatments without patient consent through the 1940s-60s. Insulin coma — a treatment now abandoned as ineffective and dangerous — was also administered without meaningful consent. § 5325(f) is the drafters' direct response.

2. **Lobotomies and other psychosurgery performed on involuntarily committed patients.** California state hospitals performed prefrontal lobotomies and related psychosurgeries through the 1950s, many on committed patients without the patient's meaningful consent. § 5325(g)'s detailed definition of psychosurgery — especially subparagraphs (g)(1)–(3), which reach surgery performed *for the purpose* of behavior modification — is the drafters' response to the documented pre-LPS practice.

3. **Opened mail and monitored calls.** Pre-LPS facilities routinely opened patient correspondence and monitored or blocked telephone calls. § 5325(d) (confidential calls) and § 5325(e) (unopened correspondence) address this directly.

4. **Confiscation of personal possessions and clothing.** Institutional uniforms and confiscated personal property were the norm. § 5325(a) and (b) restored basic dignity.

5. **No independent advocacy.** Pre-LPS patients had no access to an advocate independent of the facility. Complaints were routed through the medical superintendent. § 5325(h) created the structural counterweight.

6. **Third-party waiver.** Pre-LPS, guardians and committing family members were routinely treated as empowered to consent on the patient's behalf to virtually any treatment. The § 5325 non-waiver clause explicitly overrode that practice.

## The 1967 Legislative Findings (WIC § 5001)

The LPS Act's legislative findings, codified at § 5001, declared the Act's purposes included to "safeguard individual rights through judicial review" and to "provide individualized treatment, supervision, and placement services." § 5325 is the operational code for "individual rights" in the context of continued detention.
