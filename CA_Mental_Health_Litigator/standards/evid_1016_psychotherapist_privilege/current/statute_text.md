# Psychotherapist-Patient Privilege Exception — Statutory Text

**Source:** Cal. Evid. Code § 1016
**Retrieved:** 2026-04-12
**URL:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1016.&lawCode=EVID
**Verified:** true (VernenLegal MCP california_get_section)

## Text

There is no privilege under this article as to a communication relevant to an issue concerning the mental or emotional condition of the patient if such issue has been tendered by:

(a) The patient;

(b) Any party claiming through or under the patient;

(c) Any party claiming as a beneficiary of the patient through a contract to which the patient is or was a party; or

(d) The plaintiff in an action brought under Section 376 or 377 of the Code of Civil Procedure for damages for the injury or death of the patient.

---

## Context: The Psychotherapist-Patient Privilege (Evid. Code §§1010-1015)

§1016 is an exception to the psychotherapist-patient privilege established in Evid. Code §§1010-1015:

**§1012:** Subject to §1016 exceptions, the patient has a privilege to refuse to disclose and to prevent another from disclosing, a confidential communication between patient and psychotherapist.

**§1014:** The patient, not the therapist, holds the privilege. The therapist may claim privilege on behalf of the patient if the patient is absent or unable to assert it.

**§1016 exception trigger:** The privilege does NOT apply when the patient (or someone listed in (a)-(d)) has "tendered" — placed at issue — the patient's mental or emotional condition.
