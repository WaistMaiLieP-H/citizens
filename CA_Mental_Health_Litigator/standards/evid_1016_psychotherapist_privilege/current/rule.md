## Governing Provision

Cal. Evid. Code § 1016 (Stats. 1965, Ch. 299 — Evidence Code enacted; this section unchanged in operative form)

## The Rule — Patient-Tender Exception

The psychotherapist-patient privilege (Evid. Code §1012) is extinguished as to communications relevant to an issue concerning the mental or emotional condition of the patient, **when that issue has been tendered by**:

- **(a) The patient** — patient puts their own mental condition at issue
- **(b) A party claiming through or under the patient** — heirs, representatives, assignees
- **(c) A contract beneficiary** — party claiming through a contract to which the patient was a party
- **(d) CCP §376/377 plaintiff** — wrongful death or injury plaintiff

## What "Tendered" Means

"Tendering" an issue means affirmatively placing the mental or emotional condition at issue in the litigation. It is not enough that the condition is relevant — the party must have put it at issue as part of their own claims or defenses.

**Who can tender the issue:**
- The patient themselves (e.g., plaintiff in personal injury case claims emotional distress damages)
- A party claiming through the patient (e.g., estate representative)

**Who CANNOT tender the issue:**
- The opposing party alone — the defendant in a case cannot force disclosure of a plaintiff's psychotherapy records simply by arguing that mental condition is relevant
- A third party not listed in §1016(a)-(d)

## The Court-Ordered Evaluation Exception (Not §1016)

Important distinction: §1016 applies to **voluntarily acquired** psychotherapist-patient communications. Court-ordered evaluations under PC §1369 operate under a **different framework**:

- A court-ordered competency evaluation is not a confidential communication in the ordinary §1012 sense — it is ordered for the specific purpose of producing a written report for the court
- The defendant's communications during a court-ordered evaluation are not privileged to the same degree as voluntary therapy communications
- Evid. Code §1023 provides: no privilege exists as to a communication relevant to an issue of the patient's mental competency in a criminal proceeding in which the patient is a defendant

**§1023 is the more specific rule for criminal competency contexts.** §1016 covers the broader civil and criminal litigation landscape where a party tenders their mental condition.

## Strategic Analysis — Two Directions

### Direction 1: Protecting Mental Health Records from Opposing Discovery

When an opponent seeks to discover a party's mental health records:
- Does the opponent fall within §1016(a)-(d)? If not, the privilege is intact
- Has the patient tendered their mental condition? Filing a lawsuit claiming emotional distress damages tenders the condition
- The court must conduct an in camera review (Evid. Code §1040) before ordering disclosure

### Direction 2: Arguing §1016 Waiver When Privilege Has Been Asserted Incorrectly

If a party is asserting psychotherapist-patient privilege to block disclosure of records that relate to a mental condition the party has already put at issue:
- Identify the prior pleading or proceeding where the patient tendered the issue
- Demonstrate the relevance of the records to that tendered issue
- Argue §1016 exception applies — no privilege where issue has been tendered

## Steward Relevance

**Dr. Wiita evaluation records:**
- The §1369 competency evaluation is governed by §1023, not §1016 — it is court-ordered, not voluntary therapy; different confidentiality framework
- BUT: If Dr. Wiita reviewed any voluntary therapy records and those records are sought in subsequent litigation — §1016 analysis required

**Ward system / false §5150 holds:**
- If victims of false holds seek to introduce statements made during the hold evaluation in subsequent civil proceedings — the statements were made to mental health professionals in an evaluation context; §1016 analysis determines whether the privilege is waived if the victim's mental condition was tendered in the civil proceeding
- If the perpetrators of false holds argue that their communications with mental health professionals are privileged — they are not the "patient" in those communications; §1012 does not protect the false reporter

**Mental health records in discovery:**
- Any case involving mental health records requires §1016 analysis: who tendered the issue? Which communications are covered? Were they voluntary or court-ordered?
