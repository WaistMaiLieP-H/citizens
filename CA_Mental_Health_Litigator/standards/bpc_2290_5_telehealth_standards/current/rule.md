## Governing Provision

Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 2290.5 (Stats. 2011, Ch. 547; last amended Stats. 2021)

## The Rule — Five Critical Provisions

### § 2290.5(b) — Prior Informed Consent (Mandatory)

Before delivering health care via telehealth:
1. Provider **MUST inform** patient about the use of telehealth
2. Provider **MUST obtain verbal or written consent** for telehealth as an acceptable mode
3. Consent **SHALL be documented**

**For court-ordered evaluations:** The fact that the evaluation is court-ordered does not waive the consent requirement. §2290.5(b) applies to ALL delivery of health care via telehealth. The defendant must be informed that the evaluation is being conducted via telehealth and must consent to that modality (separate from consenting to the evaluation itself).

**Dr. Wiita audit:** Was a §2290.5(b) informed consent document created? Is it in the court file or the evaluator's file? If not → failure to comply = unprofessional conduct under §2290.5(d).

### § 2290.5(d) — Unprofessional Conduct

Failure to comply with §2290.5 **constitutes unprofessional conduct**. Unprofessional conduct in California:
- Is reportable to the relevant licensing board (Medical Board of CA for psychiatrists)
- Can result in license suspension, revocation, or disciplinary action
- Creates civil liability exposure for acts committed in the course of the unprofessional conduct

**Consequence:** If Dr. Wiita did not comply with §2290.5 — whether because Dr. Wiita lacked CA licensure, failed to obtain consent, or violated confidentiality — Dr. Wiita committed unprofessional conduct in the performance of the §1369 evaluation. The evaluation was conducted in violation of California professional standards.

### § 2290.5(e) — No Scope of Practice Expansion

Telehealth does NOT expand a provider's scope of practice. It does NOT authorize services not otherwise authorized by law.

**The CA Licensure Wall:** The California Medical Practice Act (BPC §2052) makes it unlawful to practice medicine in California without a CA license. BPC §2290.5(e) expressly preserves this requirement — telehealth from out of state is not authorized merely because the technology allows remote access. A South Carolina-licensed psychiatrist conducting a California court-ordered psychiatric evaluation via telehealth is:
- Providing medical services "in" California (the patient/defendant is in CA)
- Without California licensure
- BPC §2290.5(e) expressly states this is NOT authorized by the telehealth provision

### § 2290.5(f) — Confidentiality Laws Apply

All confidentiality laws apply to telehealth interactions — including CMIA, HIPAA, and psychotherapist-patient privilege (Evid. Code §1012). The remote modality does not relax any confidentiality obligation.

### § 2290.5(g) — Full Professional Standards Apply

**This is the most important provision for the Dr. Wiita analysis:**

> "All laws and regulations governing professional responsibility, unprofessional conduct, and standards of practice that apply to a health care provider under the health care provider's license shall apply to that health care provider while providing telehealth services."

This means: every requirement that applies to an in-person forensic psychiatric evaluation applies to a telehealth forensic psychiatric evaluation. The remote modality changes nothing about the substance of the professional obligations. Including:
- The DSH training guidelines for §1369 evaluators (Pen. Code §1369(d))
- The Judicial Council MC-350 form requirement
- The APA Specialty Guidelines for Forensic Psychology
- The AAPL Practice Guidelines for CST evaluations
- The California Medical Practice Act license requirement
