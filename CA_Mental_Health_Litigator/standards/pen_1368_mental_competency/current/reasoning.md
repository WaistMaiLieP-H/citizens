# Pen. Code § 1368 — Mental Competency — Reasoning

**Standard ID:** `pen_1368_mental_competency`
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Date:** 2026-04-12

---

## The Constitutional Foundation: Dusky v. United States (1960)

§1368 implements the federal constitutional competency requirement announced in Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S. 402 (1960): a defendant cannot be tried unless they have "sufficient present ability to consult with their lawyer with a reasonable degree of rational understanding" AND "a rational as well as factual understanding of the proceedings against them." Both prongs are required.

The Fourteenth Amendment due process clause prohibits trying an incompetent defendant. California's §1368 implements this requirement by creating a specific trigger (judicial doubt) and a mandatory inquiry process. The statute exists to protect defendants — but as with any protective mechanism, it can be inverted to harm the very person it protects.

## Why Defense Counsel's "May Be" Is the Critical Trigger

§1368(b)'s language "is or may be mentally incompetent" is deliberately broad. The Legislature recognized that at the moment of the §1368 inquiry, defense counsel may not be certain — they may only have a concern. Requiring certainty would defeat the purpose. So a hedged opinion ("I believe the defendant may be incompetent") mandatorily triggers the §1369 process.

This design decision creates the weaponization risk: if defense counsel is cooperative with the prosecution (or is ineffective), counsel's nominal "may be incompetent" statement triggers the mandatory §1369 referral regardless of the actual state of the defendant's competency. Courts have recognized this: an ineffective assistance claim can include defense counsel's failure to properly investigate competency before representing doubt to the court.

## The Suspension Mechanism as a Weapon

§1368(c)'s mandatory suspension of all criminal proceedings is the mechanism that enables abuse. Once doubt is triggered:
- Pre-trial motions are suspended
- Discovery is suspended
- Speedy trial rights (although they toll during §1368 proceedings) become complicated
- The defendant may be institutionalized for the restoration period
- Criminal case may remain suspended for months or years

In cases where a defendant was asserting important pre-trial rights — suppression motions, Brady material demands, challenging the legality of the arrest — a pretextual §1368 referral immediately suspends all those proceedings. This is the strategic value of the weaponized §1368.

## Documentation Standard: "Doubt in the Record"

§1368(a) requires the judge to **state that doubt in the record**. This is the audit trail provision. If the court did not state doubt in the record — if there is no minute order, no transcript entry, no recorded basis for the referral — the §1368 proceeding lacks its statutory predicate. This is not a harmless error in a case where the evaluation was used to stigmatize the defendant or route them through the mental health system.

For the Dr. Wiita case: review the transcript and minute orders from the court session where the §1368 referral was made. Is there a documented statement of doubt? Is the basis for the doubt specified? Or is the referral a bare order with no stated basis? The absence of a stated basis is evidence of pretextual referral.
