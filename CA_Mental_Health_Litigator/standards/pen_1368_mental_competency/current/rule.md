## Governing Provision

Cal. Pen. Code § 1368 (Stats. 1872; last amended Stats. 2016, Ch. 86)

## The Rule — Three-Stage Process

### Stage 1: Doubt Arises (§1368(a))

When **doubt arises in the mind of the judge** as to the defendant's mental competence:
- Judge SHALL state that doubt **in the record** (mandatory)
- Judge SHALL **inquire of defense counsel** whether the defendant is mentally competent
- If unrepresented: court SHALL appoint counsel first
- Upon request or sua sponte: court RECESS to permit counsel to confer with defendant

**The "doubt" standard:** Doubt must be genuine — based on observable behavior, counsel's representation, or other evidence. A pretextual §1368 referral (fabricated doubt to route a defendant into the mental health system without genuine incompetency concern) is a due process violation. Drope v. Missouri (1975): court must conduct a competency hearing whenever there is sufficient doubt about defendant's competency; ignoring behavioral evidence of incompetency is constitutional error.

### Stage 2: Counsel's Opinion (§1368(b))

| Counsel's Representation | Court's Obligation |
|---|---|
| Defendant IS or MAY BE mentally incompetent | Court SHALL order competency determination under §§1368.1 + 1369 |
| Defendant IS mentally competent | Court MAY STILL order determination on its own motion |

**Critical:** If counsel says defendant **may be** incompetent — even a hedged opinion — the court SHALL refer. The word "or may be" is disjunctive: counsel's doubt is sufficient to trigger mandatory referral.

### Stage 3: Suspension of Proceedings (§1368(c))

Once inquiry commences: **ALL criminal proceedings are suspended** until competency is determined.
- This is not discretionary — proceedings halt automatically
- Jury, if impaneled, is retained on call unless undue hardship; discharged if defendant found incompetent
- Suspension continues until competency is determined under §1369

## Weaponization Analysis — The Pretextual §1368 Referral

§1368 can be weaponized: a cooperative judge or prosecutor can trigger §1368 proceedings to:
1. Suspend criminal proceedings indefinitely while defendant is institutionalized
2. Route defendant through the mental health system without criminal conviction
3. Create a mental health record that can be used to dismiss future claims
4. Remove the defendant from the proceeding during a sensitive phase

**Indicators of pretextual referral:**
- No documented behavioral basis for judicial doubt
- Defense counsel did not raise competency — court raised it sua sponte
- Referral timed to coincide with defendant's assertion of rights or pre-trial motions
- Evaluator selected without competitive process or qualification verification
- No MC-350 form used — evaluation conducted outside procedural framework
