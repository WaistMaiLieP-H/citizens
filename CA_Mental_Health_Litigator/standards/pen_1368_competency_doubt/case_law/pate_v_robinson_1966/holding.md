# Pate v. Robinson (1966)
## § 1368 Case Law — Holding Summary

**Citation:** Pate v. Robinson, 383 U.S. 375 (1966)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Status:** VERIFIED — canonical Supreme Court precedent
**Statute version cited:** Federal due process; California §1368 implements this constitutional standard

---

## Holding

The conviction of a legally incompetent defendant violates due process. Where the evidence raises a bona fide doubt as to the defendant's competence to stand trial, the failure to conduct a competency hearing is a constitutional error requiring reversal. A trial court's failure to conduct a competency hearing when the evidence raises a sufficient doubt is reversible error without a showing of actual prejudice.

---

## Key Doctrine

1. **Bona fide doubt standard:** The question is whether the evidence before the trial court raised a genuine, real question about competency — not whether the defendant was actually incompetent. If the evidence raised the doubt and the court ignored it, constitutional error occurred.
2. **No harmless error:** Unlike many constitutional violations, failure to conduct a required competency hearing is not subject to harmless error analysis when the original record contains evidence sufficient to trigger the duty.
3. **Basis for §1368 mandatory language:** Pate is why California's §1368 uses "shall" rather than "may" — the judge has no discretion to ignore evidence of incompetency.
