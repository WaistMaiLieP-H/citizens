# Dusky v. United States (1960)
## § 1368 Case Law — Holding Summary

**Citation:** Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S. 402 (1960)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court (per curiam)
**Status:** VERIFIED — canonical Supreme Court precedent
**Statute version cited:** Federal competency standard; California §1368 implements this standard

---

## Holding

The test for competency to stand trial is "whether [the defendant] has sufficient present ability to consult with his lawyer with a reasonable degree of rational understanding — and whether he has a rational as well as factual understanding of the proceedings against him."

This two-prong test is the floor for all state competency determinations. California's §1368/§1369 framework implements Dusky.

---

## Key Doctrine

1. **Present ability:** Competency is assessed at the time of the proceedings, not historically. A history of mental illness does not establish incompetency; a current psychotic episode may.
2. **Rational understanding:** Factual knowledge of the proceedings is insufficient — the defendant must have rational understanding. Knowing one is on trial but being unable to rationally process that fact = incompetent.
3. **Consultation with counsel:** The defendant must be able to consult with their lawyer with a reasonable degree of rational understanding — not perfect consultation, but functional participation in the defense.
