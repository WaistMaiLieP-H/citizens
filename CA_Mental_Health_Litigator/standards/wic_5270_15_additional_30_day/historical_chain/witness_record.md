# WIC § 5270.15 — Historical Loss Layer

**Layer:** 3 — Historical Loss
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## What § 5270.15 Was Enacted To Prevent

Article 4.5 was added in the 1980s-era amendments to LPS to address a specific drift: after the original 1967 LPS Act, some counties were routinely moving gravely-disabled § 5250 patients **directly into § 5350 LPS conservatorship** at Day 15 with no intermediate certification. This had two effects the drafters wanted to stop:

1. **No clinical-interval review during the transition.** A person could sit in a facility for weeks between § 5250 expiration and § 5350 hearing with no required re-evaluation cadence.
2. **No family-notice protections.** Pre-§ 5270.15, there was no statutory 36-hour family-notice requirement for the extension phase; families learned of continued confinement after the fact.

Article 4.5, adopted as a county-opt-in framework because counties differed on cost/infrastructure, installed both protections — the 10-day interval reviews and the 36-hour family-notice rule — for any county that adopted it.

## The Historical Pattern of IT-to-Conservatorship Drift

In counties that did not adopt Article 4.5, the gap between § 5250 expiration and § 5350 hearing remained structurally unprotected. Reconstructing historical IT events in those counties requires mapping the person's actual location and clinical review cadence against the statutory minimum that would have applied had Article 4.5 been adopted — the argument being that the absence of opt-in does not excuse the minimum-care clinical standards that national mental-health standards recognized.
