# WIC § 5270.15 — Reasoning

**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## The 30-Day Gate Is the Second Staircase of LPS

LPS has two escalation staircases after § 5250. The **dangerousness staircase** runs § 5150 → § 5250 → § 5300 (180 days post-certification). The **grave-disability staircase** runs § 5150 → § 5250 → § 5270.15 (30 days) → § 5350 (conservatorship). § 5270.15 is the second step of the grave-disability staircase.

The drafters built two staircases because the *kind* of risk the statute is managing differs. Dangerousness is an acute, prove-it-happened-in-custody kind of risk (hence § 5300's requirement of a threatened or attempted violent act during the § 5250 period). Grave disability is a chronic inability-to-provide-basic-needs risk, which the drafters addressed by requiring each gate to re-find that the condition persists and that less-restrictive support is still unavailable.

## Why "Interval Not To Exceed 10 Days" Matters

Subdivision (b)(2) is the only LPS provision that imposes a fixed clinical-review cadence on staff during an extension window. It means that during a § 5270.15 certification, the record must contain *at least* three interval reviews (Day 10, Day 20, Day 30). Absence of any one of these reviews is a facial defect in the certification. This is the standard most easily audited from treatment records — either the interval notes exist, dated, signed, substantive, or they do not.

Daily treatment-plan monitoring under the same subdivision is the floor. A "Day 1 plan, no further notes" record is evidence that the § 5270.15 statutory standard was not met.

## The 36-Hour Family-Notice Provision and the Ward System

§ 5270.15(b)(1) establishes a **patient-controlled** family-notification rule. The default is family notification; suppression must be the patient's request, advised to the patient by the facility. In the ward-system context, this provision is doubly important: if the patient is told "your family doesn't want to hear from you" rather than "you have the right to suppress notification," the provision has been reversed. The record should show the *advisement* — not just the outcome.

## County Opt-In and Its Litigation Consequence

Article 4.5 (§§ 5270.10–5270.65) is county-opt-in. In California, major counties that have opted in include specific urban counties tracked in the Department of Health Care Services county listings. Where the county has **not** opted in, § 5270.15 simply does not apply and any 30-day extension purporting to rely on it is void on its face. The county-adoption audit is therefore a threshold question before litigating any § 5270.15 defect.

## Connection to § 5350 LPS Conservatorship

§ 5350 petitions routinely rely on the combined § 5250 + § 5270.15 record as evidence that grave disability persists. Collapsing the § 5270.15 certification collapses the § 5350 predicate in many (though not all) cases — the petitioner may then try to rebuild the record on other pathways (§ 5352.5 direct filing, etc.), but the reconstructed record will carry the prior defect as baggage.
