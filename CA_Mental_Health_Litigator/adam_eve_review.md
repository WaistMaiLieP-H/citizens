## Certified Legal Findings

### FINDING MENTAL-001: § 1368 Mandatory Duty — "Shall" Has No Exceptions

Cal. Pen. Code §1368(a)/(c) uses mandatory language throughout: the judge SHALL state the doubt, SHALL inquire of counsel, and proceedings SHALL be suspended. There is no exception based on judicial inconvenience or docket management. Any failure to comply with the mandatory three-step sequence is a statutory violation. If proceedings continued after a competency doubt was raised but before a valid competency determination was made, every subsequent proceeding is constitutionally infirm under Pate v. Robinson (383 U.S. 375, 1966).

**Reliance note:** §1368 text verified live 2026-04-13. Pate verified SCOTUS. Certified for pleading.

### FINDING MENTAL-002: § 1369 Evaluation Is Procedurally Void Without All Four Written Report Elements

Cal. Pen. Code §1369(b)(1) requires the written report to address: (A) diagnosis, (B) understanding the proceedings AND ability to assist counsel (the two Dusky prongs), (C) likelihood of attaining competency, and (D) §1001.36 diversion eligibility if requested. A report missing any of elements (A)-(C) is an incomplete §1369 evaluation. An incompetency finding based on an incomplete evaluation was made without the procedural foundation required by the statute. The presumption of competency (§1369(c)(3)) was not overcome by a compliant evaluation.

**Reliance note:** §1369 text verified live 2026-04-13. Dusky verified. Certified for pleading.

### FINDING MENTAL-003: § 1369 Competency Presumption — Burden on the Asserting Party

Cal. Pen. Code §1369(c)(3): the defendant is presumed competent to stand trial. The burden to overcome this presumption is on the party asserting incompetency, by a preponderance of the evidence. This presumption is affirmative protection — it cannot be overcome by a defective evaluation. An incompetency finding made on the basis of an evaluation that failed to meet §1369's procedural requirements was made against the presumption without legally sufficient evidence to overcome it.

**Reliance note:** §1369 text verified live 2026-04-13. Certified for pleading.

### FINDING MENTAL-004: BPC § 2290.5(b) Consent Is Required Before Every Telehealth Service

Before delivering any health care via telehealth, the provider must inform the patient and obtain consent — documented. This requirement applies to every service delivery, not just to the initiation of a treatment relationship. A court-ordered §1369 competency evaluation delivered via telehealth requires §2290.5(b) compliance for that specific evaluation. Failure is unprofessional conduct under §2290.5(d) — triggering Medical Board / Psychology Board disciplinary jurisdiction.

**Reliance note:** §2290.5 text verified live 2026-04-13. Certified for professional complaint filing.

### FINDING MENTAL-005: § 5150(e) — False Statement to Trigger Hold Is Civil Liability

Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code §5150(e): any person who intentionally gives a statement known to be false as the basis for probable cause for a §5150 hold is liable in a civil action. This provision targets the orchestrated police call — a person who fabricates danger allegations to weaponize the psychiatric hold process. Each such call, if intentionally false, is an independent basis for civil liability. The written application required by §5150(e) is the evidence that identifies who made the statement.

**Reliance note:** §5150 text verified live 2026-04-13. Certified for civil complaint drafting. Case law on intent standard: UNVERIFIED — steward must confirm before reliance in pleadings.
