# WIC § 5350 Origin: The Lanterman-Petris-Short Act (1967)

## The Wound: "Sneak-You-In" Psychiatric Commitment

Before the LPS Act, California's Mental Hygiene Law allowed indefinite civil commitment
to state psychiatric hospitals on minimal showing. Families could initiate commitment
without the proposed patient ever being heard in court. Commitment could last for life,
with no automatic review mechanism. State hospitals were warehouses — overcrowded,
under-resourced, with patients who had no legal rights and no path out.

The abuses were documented and extensive. The ACLU of Northern California's 1964 report
"The Dilemma of Mental Commitments in California" documented patients committed for
conditions that were not mental disorders, patients with no due process hearing, and
patients held for decades who had regained full capacity but had no mechanism to petition
for release.

## The LPS Act (1967) — Assembly Members Lanterman, Petris, and Short

The LPS Act, effective July 1, 1969, transformed California's psychiatric commitment
system:

1. **Ended indefinite commitment:** Any involuntary hold must begin with a limited period
   (72-hour evaluation under §5150) and can be extended only through specific, time-limited
   certifications with increasing procedural protections.

2. **Jury trial right:** §5350(d) guarantees the right to demand jury trial on the
   "gravely disabled" question. This was unprecedented — determining mental status
   before a jury, not just a judge.

3. **Time limits on conservatorship:** LPS conservatorship requires annual renewal
   proceedings — it does not run indefinitely like a Prob. Code conservatorship.

4. **County-initiated process:** Unlike Prob. Code conservatorship (family-petitioned),
   LPS conservatorship is initiated by the county mental health system or county counsel.
   This was intended to create a professional, not purely familial, gateway.

## The Bypass That Remained

Despite its reforms, LPS created the §5350(f) bypass: LPS investigation is not subject
to §1826 Prob. Code investigator requirements. The original justification was procedural
speed — LPS operates on emergency timelines. The result, sixty years later, is a
pathway where the most vulnerable individuals (those detained in psychiatric facilities
following a §5150) have the fewest pre-commitment procedural protections.

A 14-year-old subjected to a §5150 hold and then an LPS conservatorship petition would
have had no right to the §1826 mandatory interview and rights notification. The
LPS investigation — conducted by a county-designated officer, not an independent court
investigator using §1826 protocol — is the only pre-commitment procedural protection.

## The "Gravely Disabled Minor" Provision (§5350(a))

§5350(a) allows LPS conservatorship of a minor. This provision exists because children
can be gravely disabled due to severe mental health disorders — but it has no age floor.
A minor of any age can be placed under LPS conservatorship if found gravely disabled.
The Prob. Code §1800.3 limitation (married minor only) does not apply to LPS.
