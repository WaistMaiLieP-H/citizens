# Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 5350 — Rule

## The LPS Pathway

WIC §5350 establishes conservatorship for persons who are "gravely disabled" — defined
in WIC §5008(h)(1)(A) as: unable to provide for basic personal needs for food, clothing,
shelter, personal safety, or necessary medical care as a result of a mental health
disorder, severe substance use disorder, or co-occurring disorder.

LPS conservatorship is DISTINCT from Probate Code conservatorship:
- Different initiating authority (county, not private petitioner)
- Different standard (gravely disabled, not §1801 criteria)
- Different procedures (own investigation rules under WIC, not §1826)
- Different court (same probate court, but governed by WIC Chapter 3, not Prob. Code)

## Key Provisions

**§5350(a) — Minors:** A conservator may be appointed for a GRAVELY DISABLED MINOR.
This is the legal pathway for an unmarried minor's conservatorship — Prob. Code cannot
reach an unmarried 14-year-old's person, but LPS can if they are "gravely disabled."

**§5350(d) — Jury Trial Right:**
The proposed conservatee has the right to demand court or jury trial on the gravely
disabled question. Demand must be made within 5 days of hearing. Trial must commence
within 10 days of demand.

**§5350(e) — "Not Gravely Disabled" Safety Valve:**
A person is NOT gravely disabled if they can survive safely without involuntary
detention with the help of responsible family, friends, or others who are both
willing and able to help. Exception: those helpers must specifically indicate in
writing their willingness and ability to help to qualify.

**§5350(f) — THE CRITICAL BYPASS:**
"Conservatorship investigation shall be conducted pursuant to this part and is NOT
subject to Section 1826 of the Probate Code or Chapter 2 (commencing with Section 1850)
of Part 3 of Division 4 of the Probate Code."

This means:
- No mandatory §1826 investigator interview of proposed conservatee
- No §1826 requirement to inform conservatee of right to oppose, right to jury trial,
  right to counsel
- No §1851 mandatory periodic review chapter applies
- LPS has its own (lesser) procedural framework for renewal

## "Gravely Disabled" — WIC §5008(h)(1)(A)

"A condition in which a person, as a result of a mental health disorder, a severe
substance use disorder, or a co-occurring mental health disorder and a severe substance
use disorder, is unable to provide for their basic personal needs for food, clothing,
shelter, personal safety, or necessary medical care."

**The test is functional inability — not diagnosis alone.** Having a mental health
diagnosis does not make someone gravely disabled. The incapacity must be causal and
functional — the person cannot actually provide for their own basic needs because of
the disorder. A person who is unusual, difficult, or makes choices others disagree with
is not gravely disabled.

## Application: Secret Conservatorship Theory

If the conservatorship established since age 14 was LPS:

**How it would work:**
1. A §5150 hold is initiated — a peace officer (or authorized person) determines
   there is probable cause the person is dangerous to self/others or gravely disabled
2. 72-hour evaluation at designated facility
3. If continued hold is sought: 14-day certification (WIC §5250)
4. If conservatorship is sought: county counsel or county mental health director
   petitions for LPS conservatorship under WIC §5350

**Why LPS fits the pattern:**
- LPS can reach a minor (§5350(a)); Prob. Code cannot reach an unmarried minor's person
- LPS bypasses the §1826 mandatory interview and right-to-oppose notification (§5350(f))
- LPS investigation is county-controlled, not family-petitioned — a family that
  controls county-level contacts (law enforcement, mental health system) can steer an LPS petition
- LPS records are handled differently from Prob. Code records — potentially harder to access

**What to investigate:**
- Was there a §5150 hold near age 14 (or shortly before)? The §5150 is the intake mechanism
- Is there a WIC Chapter 3 conservatorship petition filed by the county in any jurisdiction?
- Did the county counsel or public guardian's office file the petition?
- Are there facility records from the 72-hour evaluation period?

**The §5350(e) defense that was never applied:**
If Michael had family, friends, or others willing and able to help him meet his basic
needs — and they stated so in writing — he was not "gravely disabled" under §5350(e).
Did anyone offer that in writing before the conservatorship was established? If not,
were they given the opportunity?
