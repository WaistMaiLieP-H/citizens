# Reasoning — Why WIC § 5350 Exists (and Why Its Safeguard Gaps Matter)

## The LPS Act's Promise

The Lanterman-Petris-Short Act (1967) was enacted in direct response to the widespread abuse of California's pre-1967 mental health commitment system. Before LPS:
- People could be indefinitely committed to state hospitals on a physician's certificate alone
- No time limits existed on involuntary commitment
- No independent review was required
- Families, employers, and political adversaries used commitment as a control mechanism — documented cases included LGBTQ+ individuals, political dissidents, and racial minorities being committed for expressing identity or challenging authority

LPS's central promise: due process before and during psychiatric intervention. Time limits on holds (72 hours, 14 days), required hearings, jury trial rights. The §5350 LPS conservatorship was supposed to be the narrow, court-supervised exception for people who genuinely could not function independently.

## The Gap LPS Created

When LPS excluded the Probate Code investigator safeguards (§5350(f)), it assumed the county mental health investigation process would be an adequate substitute. That assumption was wrong in practice:
- County LPS investigators have large caseloads
- The investigation is conducted by the same county department recommending the conservatorship
- No independent court investigator (like the §1826 Prob. Code system) provides a check on the county's recommendation
- The conservatee who is in a psychiatric facility has limited ability to independently challenge proceedings

## The Minor Pathway's Particular Danger

§5350(a)'s extension to minors creates an extraordinary vulnerability. A 14-year-old who was:
- Placed on a WIC §5150 hold (72 hours)
- Found to meet "gravely disabled" criteria in a county investigation (conducted without independent §1826 safeguards)
- Subject to an LPS conservatorship petition

...could have a conservatorship established without:
- Independent court investigator interview
- Meaningful right to retain independent counsel (if no one informed them)
- Jury trial (if the right was not communicated or exercised)
- Annual Probate Code review (§1851 excluded by §5350(f))

The wound from LPS §5350(f) is: the safeguards were excluded precisely for the population most at risk of an imposed psychiatric diagnosis being used as a control mechanism.
