# Gravely Disabled Standard — Holding

**Citations:** Conservatorship of Roulet, 73 Cal.App.3d 613 (1977) — verify at Westlaw;
additional cases from training knowledge
**PARTIALLY UNVERIFIED — Roulet citation requires verification before filing reliance**

## The Rules

1. **"Gravely disabled" requires a causal connection:** The mental health disorder must
   actually cause the inability to provide for basic needs. Poverty, homelessness, or
   social dysfunction without that causal link is not grave disability.

2. **§5350(e) safety valve:** If willing family or friends can help the person survive
   safely without detention, and state so in writing, the person is NOT gravely disabled.
   This is an affirmative defense to LPS conservatorship.

3. **Due process floor:** Even under LPS, the proposed conservatee has a constitutional
   right to notice, opportunity to be heard, right to counsel, and right to contest the
   gravely disabled determination. §5350(d)'s jury trial right implements this.

## Application to Secret Conservatorship

For any LPS conservatorship established since Michael was 14:

**The gravely disabled question:**
- Was there an actual mental health disorder diagnosis? By whom? Were they licensed?
- Did the disorder actually cause inability to provide for food, clothing, shelter,
  personal safety, or necessary medical care?
- Or was the diagnosis manufactured, exaggerated, or applied to non-disabling behavior?

**The §5350(e) safety valve that should have been applied:**
Michael had family (father, other relatives). Were any of them offered the opportunity
to state in writing their willingness and ability to help? If not, the §5350(e) defense
was never invoked. If yes, and they signed written statements of willingness, the LPS
conservatorship cannot stand.

**The due process claim:**
Was Michael (at age 14) told he had the right to demand a jury trial on the gravely
disabled question? Did he have counsel during the LPS proceeding? Was he represented
at the hearing? Due process violations in LPS proceedings support a challenge to the
conservatorship's validity, independent of the statutory framework.
