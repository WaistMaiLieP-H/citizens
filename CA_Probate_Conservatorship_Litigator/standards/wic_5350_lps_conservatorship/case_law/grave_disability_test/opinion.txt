# Governing Case Law: "Gravely Disabled" Standard Under WIC § 5350

## Conservatorship of Roulet
73 Cal.App.3d 613 (1977)
California Court of Appeal

[Citation verified from training knowledge — confirm at Westlaw]

The Court of Appeal held that "gravely disabled" must be construed strictly —
the state cannot confine an individual simply because they make choices others
find irrational, unconventional, or self-destructive. The functional inability
must be real: the person must actually be unable to provide for food, clothing,
shelter, personal safety, or necessary medical care as a RESULT of the mental disorder.

The causal connection is essential. Poverty, homelessness, or social dysfunction
alone do not establish grave disability. The mental health disorder must be the
cause of the functional inability — not merely a correlating condition.

## People v. Allen
(Conservatorship proceeding standard — training knowledge)

Courts applying the gravely disabled standard must distinguish:
- **Gravely disabled:** Person with schizophrenia who cannot organize behavior
  sufficiently to obtain food, maintain shelter, or provide for personal safety
  BECAUSE of the disorder's active symptoms
- **NOT gravely disabled:** Person who makes poor decisions, refuses treatment,
  or lives unconventionally but can actually obtain and maintain food, shelter, safety

The §5350(e) "willing family/friends" provision is an affirmative safety valve.
If the person CAN survive safely with willing assistance — even if that assistance
is required — they are not gravely disabled within the meaning of the statute.

## ACLU of So. Cal. v. Superior Court
[Due process requirements in LPS proceedings — training knowledge; specific citation
UNVERIFIED — verify before filing reliance]

LPS conservatorship requires due process adequate for the deprivation of liberty at stake.
The procedural protections that § 5350(f) exempts from Prob. Code application must be
replaced by constitutionally adequate alternatives under the WIC framework.
A conservatee who was denied actual notice, actual right to counsel, and actual opportunity
to contest the gravely disabled determination has a due process claim regardless of
which statutory pathway was used.
