# Cal. Prob. Code § 1800.3 — Rule

## Who May Be Conserved (§1800.3(a))

**Adults:** A conservator may be appointed for the person or estate of an adult (18+).
No marital status requirement. §1801 criteria must be met. §1826 investigation required.

**Married minors only:** A conservator of the PERSON may be appointed for a minor
ONLY IF the minor is married OR whose marriage has been dissolved.

**Consequence:** An UNMARRIED minor — including an unmarried 14-year-old — CANNOT
receive a conservator of the person under Prob. Code §1800.3(a)(2). The Prob. Code
pathway is categorically unavailable for conservatorship of an unmarried minor's person.

This is not ambiguous. The statute lists exactly who qualifies for minor conservatorship:
married or formerly married. No other category of minor qualifies under Prob. Code.

## The Least Restrictive Alternative Requirement (§1800.3(b))

**Mandatory express finding:** Before any conservatorship is granted, the court must
make an EXPRESS FINDING that conservatorship is the least restrictive alternative needed
for the conservatee's protection.

An "express finding" means it must appear in the court's order — not implied, not
presumed, not assumed. If the GC-340 order appointing the conservator does not contain
an express finding of least restrictive alternative, the order fails to comply with
§1800.3(b) and is procedurally defective.

## Alternatives the Court Must Consider (§1800.3(c))

Before finding conservatorship is the least restrictive alternative, the court must
consider:
- Supported decision-making agreements (WIC §21001)
- Powers of attorney
- Health care surrogate designation (Prob. Code §4711)
- Advance health care directives

A conservatorship order that was granted without the record showing these alternatives
were considered is subject to challenge — the §1800.3(c) analysis was not performed.

## Developmental Disability Track (§1800.3(d))

If the court becomes aware of developmental disability and the conservator is not
seeking §2356.5 authority, the court SHALL deem the proceeding a LIMITED conservatorship.
A conservatorship labeled "general" for a person with a developmental disability, where
§2356.5 authority was not sought, was incorrectly classified.

## Application to Case

**The Prob. Code pathway was not available for an unmarried 14-year-old:**

§1800.3(a)(2) forecloses it. This is the first legal determination any investigation
of the secret conservatorship must make: was Michael married or formerly married at age
14? If not, the Prob. Code conservatorship of the person could not have been established.

If there IS a Prob. Code conservatorship of person purportedly over Michael at age 14,
and he was unmarried, that order was entered without statutory authority. A court order
entered without subject matter jurisdiction is void — not voidable, void.

**The least restrictive alternative finding was required:**

In the GC-340 order, wherever it exists, there must be an express finding that
conservatorship was the least restrictive alternative. The absence of that finding
is a structural defect in the order.

**The alternatives that should have been considered:**

For a 14-year-old: were supported decision-making supports offered? Were relatives willing
and able to assist without conservatorship? The §1800.3(c) analysis must appear in the
record. If it does not, the conservatorship was granted without the required analysis.
