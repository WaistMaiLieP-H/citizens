# § 1826 Origin: The Court Investigator as Due Process Guardian

## The Wound: Conservatorship Without Investigation

Before 1981, California had no mandatory pre-appointment investigation requirement for
all conservatorship cases. The petition alone — filed by the family member seeking control
— was often sufficient for court appointment. The proposed conservatee had no guaranteed
right to be personally interviewed by a neutral party, to be informed of their rights,
or to receive a copy of any report about their own case.

The result was what reformers called "snap conservatorships": a family member files,
a hearing is scheduled, and the court appoints — sometimes with the conservatee present
in the courtroom but never having been told they had the right to oppose, demand a jury
trial, or have a lawyer appointed for them.

## § 1826's Response

The 1981 reform created the mandatory court investigator function specifically to break
the monopoly of the petitioner on the court's information. The court investigator:

- Is a neutral party employed by the court, not the petitioner
- Must interview the proposed conservatee PERSONALLY — not read a report about them
- Must inform the conservatee of every procedural right before the hearing
- Must report the conservatee's own expressed wishes to the court, including whether
  the conservatee WISHES TO OPPOSE

The §1826 report is explicitly confidential, with a CLETS/medical attachment that only
the conservatee and their attorney may receive. This prevents the petitioner from
weaponizing the investigation's findings against the conservatee.

## The LPS Gap That Remains

§5350(f) of the WIC exempts LPS conservatorship from §1826. The reason was practical:
LPS conservatorship operates on emergency timelines (72-hour holds leading to petition)
where the §1826 timeline (report filed 5 days before hearing) is difficult to meet.

But the practical result is that the most vulnerable proposed conservatees — those
in psychiatric facilities following a §5150 hold — have the fewest procedural protections.
The §1826 interview requirement, which ensures the conservatee is informed of their
rights and can express opposition before a neutral party, does not apply to LPS.

## What Every §1826 Compliance Gap Means

Each element of §1826 that was not complied with at establishment:
- Is an independent procedural violation
- Supports the finding that the conservatorship was not established with due process
- Strengthens a void ab initio argument (Freese v. Bragg) if combined with evidence of fraud
- Creates a basis for the court to scrutinize the conservatorship's entire history
