# Rule — Prob. Code § 1800 Conservatorship Legislative Intent

**Standard ID:** prob_1800_conservatorship_intent
**Authority:** Cal. Prob. Code § 1800

## Core Rule — Eight Legislative Mandates

Section 1800 establishes what every California conservatorship MUST achieve. These are not aspirational goals — they are the Legislature's express intent, enforceable as the framework against which any conservatorship is measured.

| Mandate | Subdivision | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Rights protection | §1800(a) | Rights of the conservatee are PROTECTED — not surrendered |
| Needs assessment | §1800(b) | Assessment of needs BEFORE and DURING conservatorship; goals for increasing functional abilities |
| Health/psychosocial needs met | §1800(c) | Health and psychosocial needs must actually be met — not ignored or subordinated |
| Least restrictive setting | §1800(d) | Community-based services FIRST; conservatorship only to the extent necessary |
| Periodic review | §1800(e) | Review must consider: (1) best interests AND expressed wishes; (2) whether conservatee could regain capacity; (3) whether conservatorship still needed |
| Basic needs | §1800(f) | Physical health, food, clothing, shelter — actual provision, not nominal oversight |
| Property protection | §1800(g) | Property is managed FOR the conservatee — not extracted |
| Informed choice | §1800(h) | Conservatee retains the ability to understand, make, and communicate their OWN choices TO THE GREATEST POSSIBLE EXTENT |

## The Eight Mandates as Litigation Checklist

Every § 1800 mandate that was not met in the conservatorship at issue is an independent ground for challenging the conservatorship's continued validity. An abusive conservatorship typically fails on:

- **§1800(a):** Rights were not protected — they were stripped
- **§1800(d):** No least-restrictive-alternative analysis; no community services offered
- **§1800(e):** No periodic review, or review conducted without genuine assessment of whether capacity was regained
- **§1800(h):** Conservatee's own choices were systematically overridden, suppressed, or not communicated

## "Rights Are Protected" — Not Surrendered

§1800(a) is the foundational mandate: rights are PROTECTED by conservatorship, not surrendered to it. A conservatorship that systematically deprives the conservatee of:
- The right to choose medical treatment
- The right to control real property
- The right to communicate freely
- The right to enter contracts
- The right to retain chosen counsel

...is operating CONTRARY to its statutory purpose. §1800(a) provides the litigation anchor to challenge any individual act of the conservator as outside the conservatorship's protective mandate.

## §1800(h) — Retained Choice Authority

The Legislature in 2014 amended §1800 to expressly add subdivision (h): the conservatee retains the ability to understand, make, and communicate their own choices to the greatest possible extent. This is not new law — it reflects the due process floor that existed before — but it is now explicit: a conservatorship that silences the conservatee's voice is operating outside the statute.
