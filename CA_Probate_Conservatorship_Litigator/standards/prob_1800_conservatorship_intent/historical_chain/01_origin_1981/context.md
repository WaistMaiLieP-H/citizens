# Historical Chain — § 1800 Origin (1981 Probate Reform)

## Pre-1981: The Ungoverned Conservatorship

California's pre-1981 conservatorship system had no meaningful safeguards against indefinite, unchallengeable control. Family members could petition for conservatorship of an elderly or disabled relative, receive an order from a probate court with minimal scrutiny, and exercise indefinite control with almost no court oversight.

The 1967 Lanterman-Petris-Short (LPS) Act had addressed involuntary psychiatric detention, but the Probate Code conservatorship system remained largely unreformed. Conservatees often did not know they had been conserved. Annual accounting was required for estate conservatorships but rarely audited. No investigator interview was required before appointment.

## The 1981 Probate Code Revision

California undertook a comprehensive recodification of probate law in 1981 (Stats. 1981, ch. 1051). The conservatorship provisions were substantially restructured:
- Mandatory court investigator pre-appointment report (§1826)
- Annual review with independent investigator (§1851)
- Clear and convincing evidence standard for establishment (§1801(e))
- Least restrictive alternative requirement (§1801, §1800.3)
- Legislative intent section (§1800) establishing the eight mandates as the governing framework

The 1981 reform was partly driven by disability rights advocacy following the Developmentally Disabled Assistance and Bill of Rights Act (federal, 1975) and the "normalization" movement in disability services that emphasized supported living over institutionalization.

## 2014 Amendment — §1800(h)

Senate Bill 1173 (2014) added §1800(h) expressly requiring that conservatees retain the ability to understand, make, and communicate their own informed choices. This amendment reflected ongoing advocacy by the ACLU Disability Rights Project, the California Disability Rights Network, and elder law advocates who documented persistent violations of conservatee autonomy.

## Current State

§1800 is the legislative intent section of the conservatorship chapter. Its mandates are enforceable in conservatorship proceedings as the standard against which all conservator conduct is measured. Courts apply §1800 in reviewing conservator accounts, in termination proceedings, and in any action to surcharge a conservator for breach of fiduciary duty.
