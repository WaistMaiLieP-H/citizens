# Conservatorship of Wendland — Holding

**Citation:** 26 Cal.4th 519 (2001)
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Standard:** Conservator's authority over medical decisions; limits of conservatorship power

## The Rule

1. A conservator of the person does NOT have unlimited authority over the conservatee's
   medical decisions. The conservatee's known wishes govern. Where wishes are unknown,
   the conservator must act in the conservatee's best interests.

2. Clear and convincing evidence of the conservatee's own expressed wishes is required
   before a conservator can make irreversible medical decisions (life-sustaining treatment
   withdrawal). Family inference about "what they would have wanted" is not sufficient.

3. A minimally conscious conservatee — not fully incapacitated — retains dignity interests
   and autonomy rights that constrain the conservator's authority.

## Application to the Secret Conservatorship Theory

Wendland establishes that even within a valid conservatorship, the conservator's power
is limited. Medical decisions made by a conservator that override the conservatee's
expressed wishes, without court authorization, are subject to challenge.

For the CA_Probate_Conservatorship_Litigator case context:

- **Spine surgery (Case #11):** Was the procedure authorized by a conservator? If so,
  was there clear and convincing evidence that Michael consented or that the procedure
  served his best interests? Did the conservator seek court authorization for the
  surgery as required under Prob. Code §2352?

- **SIRVA surgery (Case #20):** Same analysis — medical procedure on conservatee
  requires conservator authorization under court-approved powers; unauthorized medical
  intervention by a person claiming conservator authority is actionable.

- **§5150 holds as control mechanism:** Using psychiatric holds to control a conservatee
  who is not gravely disabled exceeds conservator authority and violates due process.

## Statute Version Cited

Cal. Prob. Code §§1801, 2352 as in effect 2001. No major amendments to these sections
on this issue since Wendland.
