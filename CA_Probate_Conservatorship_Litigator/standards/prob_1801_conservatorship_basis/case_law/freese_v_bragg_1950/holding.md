# Freese v. Bragg — Holding

**Citation:** 98 Cal.App.2d 478 (1950) — UNVERIFIED
**Court:** California Court of Appeal
**Standard:** Fraud in procurement of conservatorship order → void ab initio

## The Rule

A conservatorship order obtained through material misrepresentation to the court is
void ab initio — it has no legal effect from the beginning.

A void order can be:
- Attacked collaterally in any court, at any time, in any proceeding
- Raised as a defense to actions taken under the conservatorship's purported authority
- Used to invalidate all subsequent acts taken under the conservator's purported authority

No statute of limitations applies to a void judgment.

## Application to the Secret Conservatorship Theory

If the conservatorship established since age 14 was procured through fraud:
- False capacity declaration (GC-348) by a physician who did not examine Michael
- False investigator report (§1826) based on interviews that never occurred
- Misrepresentation of Michael's mental status or functional capacity to the court

**Then the order is void ab initio.** Every act taken under the conservator's authority
since establishment — property sales, medical decisions, financial transactions — was
taken without legal authority. The conservator had no court-granted powers because the
court's order was void.

This is the collateral attack theory: in the federal §1983 case, in the Honeysuckle
complaint, in the ERISA litigation — wherever the conservator's authority is invoked
as justification for conduct — the response is: the order is void, no authority existed.
