# Cal. Prob. Code § 1801 — Rule

## Subject Line: Subject to Section 1800.3

The opening phrase "Subject to Section 1800.3" is not boilerplate — it subordinates
ALL of §1801 to §1800.3's least-restrictive-alternative requirement and its constraints
on who may be conserved. §1800.3 must be read first.

## Grounds for Conservatorship

### (a) Conservator of Person
**Ground:** Unable to provide properly for personal needs — physical health, food, clothing, shelter.

**Test:** Inability to provide — not difficulty, not imperfect management. The person must
actually be unable to meet these core needs.

**Exception:** §1828.5(b)/(c) covers limited situations involving voluntary programs.

### (b) Conservator of Estate
**Ground:** Substantially unable to manage financial resources OR resist fraud or undue influence.

**Two pathways:**
1. Substantially unable to manage finances (inability to handle money, pay bills, manage accounts)
2. Unable to resist fraud or undue influence (vulnerable to financial predation)

**Hard limit:** "Substantial inability may not be proved solely by isolated incidents of
negligence or improvidence." One bad financial decision is not enough. One episode of
being deceived is not enough. The incapacity must be persistent and substantial.

### (c) Combined
Both person and estate conservatorship where both grounds are met.

### (d) Limited Conservatorship — Developmentally Disabled Adult
**The most protective framework:**
- Utilized ONLY AS NECESSARY — not as a default
- Designed to ENCOURAGE maximum self-reliance and independence
- Ordered ONLY to the extent of proven mental and adaptive limitations
- Conservatee is NOT PRESUMED INCOMPETENT
- Conservatee RETAINS ALL rights not specifically stripped by court order

This is Congress telling courts: limited conservatorship is a tool of last resort for a
specific population, not a mechanism of control. Every right retained by the conservatee
under §(d) is a right that was not stripped.

### (e) Standard of Proof
**CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE.**

This is the second-highest civil standard. It is not preponderance (more likely than not).
It requires the evidence to be highly and substantially more probable to be true than not.
Clear and convincing evidence of inability to manage — not just suspicion, not just family
concern, not just one professional opinion.

## Application to Case — The Secret Conservatorship

**For any conservatorship established under §1801:**

1. There must be a capacity declaration (GC-348) by a physician or psychologist documenting
   the grounds under (a), (b), or (d)
2. The §1826 court investigator must have interviewed the proposed conservatee
3. The clear and convincing evidence standard must have been applied

**For a conservatorship since age 14:**
§1801 applies to ADULTS. §1800.3(a)(2) limits conservatorship of a minor's person to
married minors. An unmarried 14-year-old cannot be conserved under §1801(a) — the statute
says "person" but §1800.3 constrains it to married minor or adult.

**If conservatorship exists over an unmarried minor:**
It was either:
- LPS (WIC §5350) — "gravely disabled" minor — which bypasses §1826 requirements (§5350(f))
- Guardianship (Prob. §1500 et seq.) — different statute, different process

**If LPS:** the clear and convincing evidence standard still applies but to "gravely disabled"
under WIC §5008(h)(1)(A) — unable to provide for basic needs due to mental health disorder.
