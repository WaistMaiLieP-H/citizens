# § 1801 Origin: The 1981 Probate Code Reform and The Wound It Answered

## The Wound: Custodial Control Without Due Process

Before California's 1981 Probate Code reform, conservatorship was administered under a
fractured system dating from the early 20th century. Courts could appoint conservators
with minimal evidence, minimal investigation, and no uniform standards. The result was
well-documented: elder Californians and disabled adults placed under conservatorships
with no effective right to oppose, no right to counsel unless they could afford it,
no guaranteed investigator visit before appointment, and no periodic review.

The conservatee — once placed — was often cut off from family, stripped of the right
to manage any finances, and unable to petition for termination without resources
the conservatorship itself had eliminated. The system was designed for administrative
efficiency, not for the protection of the conservatee.

## The 1981 Reform

California's comprehensive Probate Code reform (effective January 1, 1985) rewrote
the conservatorship framework from the ground up. The Legislature's stated intent
(§1800) was explicitly protective: assess needs, use community services first, provide
periodic review, ensure the conservatee retains maximum autonomy.

§1801's key reforms from prior law:

1. **"Subject to §1800.3"** — the least-restrictive-alternative requirement was new.
   Prior law had no such constraint. A conservatorship that is not the least restrictive
   alternative must not be granted — even if the person meets the §1801 criteria.

2. **"Substantial inability" standard for estate** — prior law was vague. The 1981
   reform required substantial inability, and explicitly excluded isolated incidents.
   One bad decision is not grounds. The incapacity must be persistent.

3. **Clear and convincing evidence (§1801(e))** — prior law applied a lower standard.
   The Legislature elevated the standard specifically because conservatorship strips
   fundamental civil rights.

4. **Limited conservatorship (§1801(d))** — the developmental disability track was
   developed in this era as an explicit departure from the "all or nothing" prior model.
   The intent: maximize independence, minimize the scope of court-ordered limitations.

## The LPS Gap

The 1981 reform did not unify the psychiatric pathway (LPS) with the Prob. Code pathway.
LPS conservatorships under WIC §5350 remained governed by different rules — and §5350(f)
explicitly exempted LPS from the §1826 investigator interview requirements added by
the 1981 reform. The promise of the 1981 reform — "you will be interviewed, informed,
represented, and heard" — did not reach LPS conservatees. That gap was intentional
and has been criticized but never fully closed.

## The Freese v. Bragg Line: Fraud Voids

California courts have long recognized that a conservatorship obtained by fraud is
void ab initio — not just voidable. Freese v. Bragg, 98 Cal.App.2d 478 (1950),
established this principle: if the petitioner obtained the court's order through
material misrepresentation, the order has no legal effect. A collateral attack on
the conservatorship is available at any time, in any proceeding, without the time
limits that apply to direct appeals.
