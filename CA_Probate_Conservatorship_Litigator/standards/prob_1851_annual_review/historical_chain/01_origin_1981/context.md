# § 1851 Origin: The Promise of Periodic Oversight

§1851 implements the Legislature's commitment in §1800(e): periodic review shall
consider the conservatee's best interests, expressed wishes, whether capacity has been
regained, and whether the conservatorship is still needed.

Before the 1981 reform, conservatorships had no mandatory review mechanism. A person
placed under conservatorship could remain under it indefinitely — even if their capacity
had been restored, even if their circumstances had changed, even if the conservator
was acting against their interests. The only mechanism for relief was a petition to
terminate, which required the conservatee to have enough capacity and resources to
initiate legal proceedings while under the control of the person they were trying to
remove.

§1851 broke this trap by making oversight mandatory and independent of the conservatee's
initiative. The court investigator visits without notice to the conservator — specifically
to prevent the conservator from staging the interaction. The investigator asks the
conservatee directly whether they want the conservatorship terminated. The answer goes
to the court, independent of the conservator.

A conservatorship that operated for years without §1851 reviews is a conservatorship
that operated without the judicial oversight California law requires. The absence of
review records is not a missing formality — it is the absence of the mechanism by which
the law checks whether the conservatee has been asking to be free.
