# Cal. Prob. Code § 1851 — Rule

## The Ongoing Obligation: Annual/Biennial Review

§1851 is the post-establishment counterpart to §1826. Once a conservatorship is
established, it does not run on autopilot. The court investigator must conduct periodic
reviews to determine whether the conservatorship is still necessary, still proper, and
still protecting the conservatee.

## The "Without Prior Notice to Conservator" Requirement

The review begins WITHOUT PRIOR NOTICE to the conservator (unless court orders otherwise
for necessity or to prevent harm to the conservatee).

This is not an accident — the Legislature understood that conservators who know a review
is coming can stage a favorable presentation. The no-notice rule ensures the investigator
sees the conservatee's actual living conditions, not a performance for inspection.

A conservator who manages to prevent the investigator's unannounced visit, or who
ensures the conservatee is not available for interview, is actively obstructing the
§1851 process. That obstruction is itself a violation.

## What the Investigator Must Determine

**(A)** Does the conservatee wish the court to TERMINATE the conservatorship?
**(B)** Does the conservatee wish the court to REMOVE the conservator?
**(C)(i)** Does the conservatee still meet §1801 criteria?
**(C)(ii)** Is the conservatorship still the LEAST RESTRICTIVE ALTERNATIVE?
**(D)** Is the conservator acting in the BEST INTERESTS of the conservatee?
    - Includes: placement, quality of care, physical and mental health treatment, finances
    - Conservator must make ALL books, records, receipts, expenditures available to investigator

**(E)** Voting capacity determination.

## Books and Records Access

"Upon request of the court investigator, the conservator shall make available to the
court investigator during the investigation for inspection and copying all books and
records, including receipts and any expenditures, of the conservatorship."

This is a mandatory disclosure obligation. A conservator who refuses access to financial
records during a §1851 review has violated this provision. The violation is subject to
court sanction.

## The Written Report

- Filed with court at least 15 days before review date
- Delivered to: conservatee, conservator, attorneys of record, spouse/first-degree relatives
- CONFIDENTIAL: only parties, persons who appeared, their attorneys, and court
- CLETS/medical in separate attachment — not shared with spouse/relatives

## §1851 Audit for Secret Conservatorship

If a conservatorship has been operating since Michael was 14, there should be a
§1851 review record for each review period since establishment. The review happens
as required by §1850 (at least every two years). For a conservatorship spanning decades:

**How many reviews should have occurred?** (Years of conservatorship ÷ 2) = minimum number of §1851 review files in the court record.

**What the absence of review records means:**
- The conservatorship operated without court oversight for the periods between reviews
- Each missed review is an independent violation of §1851
- Each missed review is a period where the conservatee's desire to terminate was never asked and never reported to the court

**What the investigator's report should show each review:**
- Personal visit to conservatee (without notice to conservator)
- Conservatee's expressed wish re: termination (or record that conservatee could not communicate)
- Finding that §1801 criteria are still met
- Finding that least restrictive alternative is still conservatorship
- Conservator's books and records reviewed

**If any of these are absent from the review record: that review was not conducted properly.**
