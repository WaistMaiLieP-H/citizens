# Rule — Prob. Code § 2580 Substituted Judgment

**Standard ID:** prob_2580_substituted_judgment
**Authority:** Cal. Prob. Code § 2580

## Core Rule — Court Authorization for Conservator Acts

Section 2580 authorizes the court — on petition by the conservator or any interested person — to ORDER the conservator to take specific acts that go BEYOND ordinary conservator management powers. These are acts that would normally require the conservatee's personal decision-making.

**Standard is "substituted judgment":** The court stands in the shoes of the conservatee and asks what the conservatee would have done if capable. The court's order "substitutes" for the conservatee's judgment.

## The Enumerated Acts — Asset Stripping Mechanisms

§2580(b) lists the specific acts the court can authorize. In the context of an abusive conservatorship, these provisions are the LEGAL MECHANISMS through which conservatee assets can be legitimately transferred OUT of the conservatee's estate:

| Act | Subdivision | Relevance to Cases |
|---|---|---|
| Making gifts from estate | §2580(b)(1) | Gifts to family members who are controlling the system |
| Conveying property interests | §2580(b)(2) | Conveying real property (Honeysuckle) — joint tenancy interests can be released |
| Creating trusts from estate | §2580(b)(5) | HILLBERGMANN UIT — conservatee's assets placed in trust controlled by family |
| Exercising insurance options | §2580(b)(8)(A)(B) | State Farm policy; Northern Trust annuity — change beneficiaries, surrender cash value |
| Exercising retirement plan options | §2580(b)(8)(D) | UA342 pension — elect benefit options, change beneficiaries, borrow |
| Making a will | §2580(b)(13) | Conservatee's testamentary intent can be substituted by court order |

## The Audit Question

For every major asset transfer in the cases at issue, the litigation question is:
1. Was there a §2580 petition filed and an order entered? (if yes, examine whether the conservatee was notified and could contest)
2. Was the act taken WITHOUT a §2580 order? (if yes, the act exceeded the conservator's authority — unauthorized and potentially void)
3. Was the §2580 petition filed with actual notice to the conservatee? (if no, the order was issued without due process)

An asset transfer from a conservatee's estate that was not authorized by a §2580 court order is a BREACH OF FIDUCIARY DUTY by the conservator and potentially conversion or elder financial abuse.

## Connection to Honeysuckle Sale

The sale of 2958 Honeysuckle ($605K, $465K equity unaccounted) — if the property was subject to a conservatorship — required either:
- Conservatee's informed consent (which requires the conservatee to have legal capacity to consent), OR
- A §2580 substituted judgment order from the court, OR
- A separate court order under Prob. Code §2540 (conservator's power to sell real property)

The 19 unsigned documents in the Honeysuckle transaction are evidence that neither consent nor proper court authorization was obtained.

## Connection to UA342 Pension

§2580(b)(8)(D) specifically authorizes conservator to exercise the conservatee's rights to "elect benefit or payment options, to terminate, to change beneficiaries or ownership" in retirement/profit sharing/employee welfare plans. The pension identity replacement and "retired at 44" designation could be the practical effect of:
- A §2580 order authorizing a pension benefit election on behalf of a conservatee, OR
- Unauthorized action by a conservator or their agent that mimicked what a §2580 order would authorize

Determining which track was used is the audit question for the ERISA case.
