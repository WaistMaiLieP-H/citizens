# Rule — Prob. Code § 1827 Conservatorship Jury Trial Right

**Standard ID:** prob_1827_jury_trial_right
**Authority:** Cal. Prob. Code § 1827 (Probate pathway); WIC § 5350(d) (LPS pathway)

## Core Rule — Due Process in Conservatorship Establishment

Conservatorship establishment is governed by civil action law and procedure, including:
- Rules of civil evidence
- Burden of proof at trial (clear and convincing evidence per §1801(e))
- RIGHT TO JURY TRIAL if demanded by the proposed conservatee

The right to jury trial is **the proposed conservatee's right** — not the petitioner's, not the county's. The conservatee demands it. The court must honor it.

## Two Parallel Jury Trial Rights

| Pathway | Statutory Basis | Demand Deadline | Trial Commencement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Probate Code conservatorship | Prob. §1827 | No specific statutory deadline (general civil rules apply) | Civil action timing |
| LPS conservatorship | WIC §5350(d) | **5 days following hearing** | **Within 10 days of demand** |
| LPS — failure to commence | WIC §5350(d)(2) | N/A | **Grounds for DISMISSAL** |

## What Jury Trial Decides

In a Probate Code conservatorship: whether the person meets the §1801 standard (unable to provide personal needs OR unable to resist fraud/undue influence; clear and convincing evidence).

In an LPS conservatorship: whether the person is "gravely disabled" within the meaning of WIC §5008(h).

The jury does not decide who the conservator will be. It decides whether any conservatorship is warranted at all.

## Due Process Foundation

The jury trial right in conservatorship proceedings is grounded in both California constitutional due process (Cal. Const. Art. I §7 and §16) and the federal Fourteenth Amendment. Loss of liberty and property rights through conservatorship without due process violates both.

In a secret conservatorship where the conservatee was never informed:
- The right to jury trial was never communicated
- The right was constructively waived without knowledge or consent
- Waiver of a constitutional due process right requires knowing, intelligent, and voluntary waiver — not silence
- A conservatorship established without any effort to communicate the jury trial right is procedurally defective

## The Constructive Waiver Problem

If the proposed conservatee was:
1. A minor (age 14) without independent representation
2. Not informed of the proceedings
3. Not informed of the right to demand jury trial
4. Placed in a facility where communication was controlled

...then any purported waiver of the jury trial right was not knowing and voluntary. The conservatorship was established without due process.

Application: Every year of conservatorship that persisted without re-notifying the conservatee of their right to seek termination (including jury trial on whether they remain "gravely disabled") is an ongoing due process deprivation.
