# Reasoning — Why the Jury Trial Right Matters in Conservatorship

## Liberty Interest in Conservatorship Proceedings

Conservatorship is not simply a civil property matter. It deprives the conservatee of:
- Control over personal decisions (where to live, medical treatment, daily activities)
- Control over financial assets (the conservatee cannot enter contracts, access accounts, or manage property without conservator approval)
- Freedom of movement (conservator can choose the conservatee's residence)

These are fundamental liberty interests. The California Supreme Court and the U.S. Supreme Court have consistently held that due process requires meaningful opportunity to be heard before such interests are taken.

The jury trial right in §1827 is the mechanism for that opportunity: the conservatee can demand that a jury of their peers — not a single judge, not a county official — decide whether the legal standard for conservatorship has been met.

## Why a Minor Would Never Exercise This Right

A 14-year-old who was:
- Not independently represented by counsel of their choosing
- In a facility controlled by the same family/county structure seeking the conservatorship
- Not informed of their right to demand a jury trial
- Dependent on the same system for basic needs

...has no practical ability to demand a jury trial even if they technically had the legal right. The formal right and the practical exercise of the right are not the same thing. This is the wound: due process protections that exist on paper but are inaccessible in practice are not adequate process.

The demand deadline in LPS proceedings (5 days after hearing; §5350(d)(1)) makes this even more acute: a person who was never notified of the hearing has no opportunity to make a demand within 5 days.
