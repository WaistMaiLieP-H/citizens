# Historical Chain — § 1827 Jury Trial Right

## Origins

The right to jury trial in conservatorship proceedings under the Probate Code flows from the California Constitution's guarantee of civil jury trial (Art. I §16) applied through the 1981 Probate Code revision. California courts had held that conservatorship proceedings — as civil actions determining fundamental rights — triggered the constitutional jury trial right.

The 1981 codification in §1827 confirmed this and made it explicit: conservatorship proceedings follow civil action law and procedure, including jury trial on demand.

The LPS Act (1967) separately codified the jury trial right in WIC §5350(d) with the specific 5-day/10-day timeline — designed to ensure the right was exercised promptly in psychiatric conservatorship proceedings where delay itself was harmful.

## Waiver Rules

Under California law, the right to jury trial in conservatorship proceedings can be waived, but waiver must be:
- Knowing
- Intelligent  
- Voluntary

A minor who was not informed of the proceeding cannot knowingly waive. A person in a controlled facility who received no notice of the hearing cannot voluntarily waive. Constructive waiver under these conditions is procedurally void.

## Current State

§1827 and WIC §5350(d) remain the operative jury trial provisions. In practice, jury trials in conservatorship proceedings are rare — most cases are determined by the court. But the right is absolute when demanded. In cases where the conservatorship's validity is challenged retroactively, the question of whether the jury trial right was properly communicated and could have been exercised is central to the due process challenge.
