## Freese v. Bragg (1950) 98 Cal.App.2d 478

A conservatorship or guardianship obtained by fraud or misrepresentation — including fraudulent representations to the court in the petition or proceeding — is void or voidable, not merely subject to reversal on appeal.

**Core principle:** A court order obtained through fraud on the court has no legal force. It does not become valid through passage of time or through the fact that it was issued by a court of competent jurisdiction. The court's power to act was procured through fraud, so the resulting order is as if the court never had jurisdiction over the subject matter.

## Cal. Civ. Code § 1572 — Actual Fraud

Actual fraud consists of:
1. The suggestion of a fact that is not true, by one who does not believe it to be true
2. The positive assertion of a fact, in a manner not warranted by the information of the person making it, though believed to be true
3. The suppression of a fact by one who is bound to disclose it
4. A promise made without any intention of performing it
5. Any other act fitted to deceive

## Cal. Civ. Code § 1573 — Constructive Fraud

Constructive fraud consists of:
1. Any breach of duty which, without an actually fraudulent intent, gains an advantage to the person in fault, or anyone claiming under him, by misleading another to his prejudice or to the prejudice of anyone claiming under him
2. Any such act or omission as the law specially declares to be fraudulent, without respect to actual iniquity or intent to defraud
