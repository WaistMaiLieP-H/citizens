# Context — 2024 SIM Swap Rules (FCC Second Report and Order)

## The wound that forced the rules

By 2020, the SIM swap fraud epidemic was documented and severe. The FCC received tens of thousands of consumer complaints about SIM swap fraud — typically resulting in complete account takeover, cryptocurrency theft, identity theft, and harassment enabled by phone-based 2FA bypass. The FTC documented SIM swap reports increasing from approximately 1,000 in 2018 to over 15,000 in 2021.

Major documented incidents: T-Mobile's 2020 breach ($86M settlement); AT&T's enabling of crypto theft ($65M settlement with affected victims through 2021-2022); multiple FCC consent decrees against all major carriers for failing to prevent SIM swap fraud.

Congress held hearings in 2022-2023 specifically on SIM swap fraud. The FCC's 2021 Notice of Proposed Rulemaking in WC Docket 21-441 stated explicitly: the existing 2007 CPNI rules were not written to address SIM swaps, creating a regulatory gap that carriers exploited.

## Why the 2024 rules matter for historical SIM swap cases

The 2024 rules are prospective — they set explicit requirements for SIM swap authentication going forward. However, they do NOT mean that carriers were free to ignore SIM swap security before 2024. The argument for pre-2024 cases runs through:
1. § 222(c)(1) general duty to protect CPNI (applies from 1996)
2. The 2007 rules' authentication requirements as applied to account modifications
3. The carriers' own CPNI policies (all major carriers had SIM swap policies predating 2024)
4. FCC enforcement actions against carriers for pre-2024 SIM swaps (T-Mobile 2022 consent decree; AT&T 2024 settlement)
5. The 2024 rules as evidence of the standard of care — they codify what adequate SIM swap authentication looks like, informing what "reasonable" required even before they took effect

## Diff from prior
Prior: No explicit SIM swap authentication requirement; carriers treated SIM swaps as outside CPNI rules. After: SIM swap authentication is explicitly governed; notification required before processing; single-factor authentication prohibited.
