# Historical Chain — 47 U.S.C. § 222 CPNI

## The wound

Before § 222, telecommunications carriers treated customer call records and account information as their own business data, sharing it freely with marketing partners, affiliates, and anyone willing to pay. The phone company knew every number you called, when, and how often. This data was commercially valuable — and entirely unprotected.

The specific wound that triggered § 222: carriers were selling CPNI to marketers and, worse, to private investigators and others who would then use it to stalk ex-partners, locate individuals, and enable harassment. The information was also vulnerable to inside-job theft by carrier employees selling records to third parties.

## Legislative origin — Telecommunications Act of 1996

Section 222 was enacted as part of the Telecommunications Act of 1996 (Pub. L. 104-104, § 702). The 1996 Act overhauled federal telecommunications law for the digital age, and § 222 was the privacy component — Congress's recognition that carriers' unique access to customer communication data required a specific privacy protection regime.

The 1996 enactment focused primarily on: (1) restricting carriers from using CPNI to market services beyond the existing relationship; (2) requiring customer consent for sharing CPNI with third parties; (3) establishing the FCC as the regulatory enforcement body.

## FCC implementation — 47 C.F.R. Part 64

The FCC implemented § 222 through regulations in 47 C.F.R. Part 64. Key developments:

**1998 — First CPNI rules:** FCC established the initial CPNI framework, requiring opt-in consent for sharing CPNI outside the service relationship.

**2007 — Authentication rules:** After documented instances of carriers providing CPNI to imposters (pretexting), the FCC required carriers to implement secure authentication before releasing CPNI. This was the direct regulatory precursor to SIM swap authentication requirements.

**2015 — AT&T $25 million enforcement:** FCC fined AT&T $25 million for employees selling customer records to third parties. Established that insider-facilitated CPNI leakage is an enforcement priority.

**2023-2024 — SIM swap rulemaking:** FCC proposed and adopted new rules specifically addressing SIM swap fraud, requiring:
- Enhanced authentication before processing SIM change requests
- Immediate customer notification of SIM change requests via a separate communication channel (not the ported number)
- Mandatory 24-hour delay option before SIM changes take effect
- Customer-configurable SIM lock features

The 2023-2024 rulemaking explicitly acknowledged that SIM swap fraud had become a major vector for identity theft, financial fraud, and domestic violence facilitation.

## The promise

Section 222 was enacted on the promise that a customer's call records, account information, and communication metadata belong to the customer — not the carrier — and that the carrier will protect that information from unauthorized access and disclosure. The FCC's 2023-2024 SIM swap rulemaking affirmed that this promise extends to protecting the customer's phone number itself from being hijacked.
