FCC REPORT AND ORDER: PROTECTING CONSUMERS FROM SIM-SWAPPING AND PORT-OUT FRAUD
WC Docket No. 21-341 (November 2023, effective February 2024)
Federal Communications Commission

---

BACKGROUND:

The FCC opened a rulemaking in 2021 (WC Docket No. 21-341) specifically addressing SIM swap and port-out fraud after documented consumer harm. The proceeding documented that SIM swap attacks had caused losses in the hundreds of millions of dollars, including to cryptocurrency accounts, financial accounts, and victims of domestic violence and stalking where the SIM swap enabled the abuser to monitor the victim's communications.

---

ORDER:

FCC BINDING AUTHENTICATION REQUIREMENTS (effective February 2024):

1. **Secure authentication before SIM changes.** Carriers must authenticate the account holder's identity through a secure method before processing any SIM change request. Acceptable methods include: multi-factor authentication; biometric verification; government ID verification at retail locations; account PIN combined with a secondary verification factor. Simple PINs or security questions alone are insufficient.

2. **Notification before and after SIM changes.** Carriers must send notification through a pre-established alternative contact (email or alternative number) when a SIM change is requested and when it is completed. This gives the account holder the opportunity to detect and block unauthorized changes.

3. **SIM lock options required.** Carriers must offer account holders the ability to lock their SIM against changes — no SIM change can be processed while the lock is active without the account holder first removing it through authenticated means.

4. **7-day waiting period for new number ports.** New customers who port a number must wait 7 days before that number can be ported again, preventing rapid cascading port-out attacks.

---

SIGNIFICANCE FOR CARRIER LIABILITY:

1. **Pre-2024 claims:** Before the 2024 effective date, the reasonable security standard was defined by the pre-existing § 222 regulations and FCC guidance. The 2023 Order's authentication standards reflect what was already required as a matter of reasonable security — the Order is confirmatory, not a new standard.

2. **Post-2024 claims:** Any SIM swap that occurred after the 2024 effective date using inadequate authentication is per se unreasonable under the FCC rules.

3. **California standard of care:** For § 1798.81.5 reasonable security claims, the FCC authentication standards define the baseline. A carrier whose SIM swap authentication did not meet the FCC's standards was below the reasonable security baseline.

4. **The domestic violence / stalking nexus:** The 2023 Order specifically acknowledged that SIM swap attacks are used in domestic violence and stalking contexts to monitor victims. This acknowledgment is directly relevant to cases #30-33 where the SIM swap was allegedly used to monitor the steward's communications.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

For cases #30-33 (SIM swap fraud):
1. Identify the specific carrier(s) involved and the approximate date of each SIM swap
2. The FCC authentication standards define the carrier's duty of care — failure to meet these standards is negligence per se
3. If any SIM change was processed without multi-factor authentication or secure verification, cite the FCC Order's standards as the applicable duty
4. The FCC's acknowledgment of the DV/stalking SIM swap pattern directly supports the theory that the carrier's authentication failure enabled stalking
5. File an FCC consumer complaint at FCC.gov specifically citing § 222 CPNI regulations and WC Docket No. 21-341
