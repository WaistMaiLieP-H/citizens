# FCC SIM Swap Order (2023-2024) — Carrier Authentication Requirements

**Citation:** FCC Report and Order, WC Docket No. 21-341, "Protecting Consumers from SIM-Swapping and Port-Out Fraud" (November 2023)
**Forum:** Federal Communications Commission
**Decided:** November 2023 (effective February 2024)
**Provisions construed:** 47 U.S.C. § 222 (CPNI); 47 C.F.R. Part 64; California Civil Code § 1798.81.5 (standard of care)

## Background

The FCC opened a rulemaking in 2021 (WC Docket No. 21-341) specifically addressing SIM swap and port-out fraud after documented consumer harm. The proceeding documented that SIM swap attacks had caused losses in the hundreds of millions of dollars, including to cryptocurrency accounts, financial accounts, and victims of domestic violence and stalking where the SIM swap enabled the abuser to monitor the victim's communications.

## The FCC Order (November 2023)

The FCC adopted binding rules requiring all covered carriers to:

1. **Implement secure authentication before SIM changes:** Carriers must authenticate the account holder's identity through a secure method before processing any SIM change request. Acceptable methods include:
   - Multi-factor authentication
   - Biometric verification
   - Government ID verification at retail locations
   - Account PIN combined with a secondary verification factor
   Simple PINs or security questions alone are insufficient.

2. **Notify the account holder immediately before and after SIM changes:** Carriers must send notification through a pre-established alternative contact (email or alternative number) when a SIM change is requested and when it is completed. This gives the account holder the opportunity to detect and block unauthorized changes.

3. **Implement SIM lock options:** Carriers must offer account holders the ability to lock their SIM against changes — no SIM change can be processed while the lock is active without the account holder first removing it through authenticated means.

4. **7-day waiting period for new number ports at new carriers:** New customers who port a number must wait 7 days before that number can be ported again, preventing rapid cascading port-out attacks.

## Significance for carrier liability

The 2023 FCC Order is the definitive statement of what constitutes "reasonable security" for SIM change authentication. For the steward's SIM swap cases:

1. **Pre-2024 claims:** Before the 2024 effective date, the reasonable security standard was defined by the pre-existing § 222 regulations and FCC guidance. The 2023 Order's authentication standards reflect what was already required as a matter of reasonable security — the Order is confirmatory, not a new standard.

2. **Post-2024 claims:** Any SIM swap that occurred after the 2024 effective date using inadequate authentication is per se unreasonable under the FCC rules.

3. **California standard:** For § 1798.81.5 reasonable security claims, the FCC authentication standards define the baseline. A carrier whose pre-2024 SIM swap authentication did not meet the FCC's 2024 standards was below the reasonable security baseline.

4. **The domestic violence / stalking nexus:** The FCC's 2023 Order specifically acknowledged that SIM swap attacks are used in domestic violence and stalking contexts to monitor victims. This acknowledgment is directly relevant to cases #30-33 where the SIM swap was allegedly used to monitor the steward's communications.

## Obtaining the Order

The FCC Report and Order is publicly available at FCC.gov, WC Docket No. 21-341. The Order can be cited in any civil complaint or FCC complaint filing.

## Authority status

FCC Report and Order (November 2023), effective February 2024. Establishes binding authentication requirements for SIM changes; defines the "reasonable security" standard for § 1798.81.5 claims; acknowledges DV/stalking SIM swap context. Non-judicial but authoritative on the standard of care.
