# Context — 2007 Amendment

## What changed and why

By 2006, several major breaches had exposed that the 2003 law had gaps. Two main problems:

**Problem 1 — Medical information wasn't covered.** The original law only protected financial identifiers (SSN, account numbers, credit card numbers). It did not cover medical diagnoses, health insurance information, or health identifiers — even though those were equally sensitive and equally valuable to identity thieves and extortionists.

**Problem 2 — Large companies were using "reasonable delay" as cover.** The original "expedient time possible" language allowed companies to argue that months of internal investigation were required before notification. The 2007 amendment made clear that notification must be "without unreasonable delay."

## Why this matters for the steward

Medical information about the steward (disability history, psychiatric evaluations, surgical records) was held by carriers and healthcare entities whose records were accessed via SIM-swap-enabled account takeover. The 2007 expansion of "personal information" to include medical information means that access to email or records containing medical information is a separate, additional § 1798.82 violation.
