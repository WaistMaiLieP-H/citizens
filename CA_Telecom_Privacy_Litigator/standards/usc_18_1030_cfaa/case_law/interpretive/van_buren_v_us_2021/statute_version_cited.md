# Statute Version Cited — Van Buren v. United States (2021)

## Evolution stage referenced

**Stage:** 06_current (post-2008, current CFAA framework)

*Van Buren v. United States*, 593 U.S. 374 (2021), interpreted § 1030(a)(2) as it reads in the current version of the statute. The Court analyzed the "exceeds authorized access" prong, which was introduced in the 1986 amendments and refined by the 1994 (civil remedy) and 1996 (protected computer) expansions.

## What the case decided and its impact on steward's claims

Van Buren NARROWED "exceeds authorized access" to mean accessing data or files that a person does not have permission to access — NOT merely using authorized access for an improper purpose (violating an employer's policy about *how* to use a system).

**Impact on steward's CFAA claims:** FAVORABLE. The steward's CFAA claims are based on § 1030(a)(2)(C) — "without authorization" not "exceeds authorized access." The SIM swap attackers and Ryan McClaran had ZERO authorization to access the steward's accounts. Van Buren's narrowing of the "exceeds" prong does not affect pure unauthorized-access cases. The narrowing helps defendants in inside-job cases; it does not help outside attackers who had no authorization at all.

## Applicable version note

Van Buren was decided under the current statute (post-2008). All analysis applies to the steward's cases, which arose primarily 2018–present.
