# Context — Civil Cause of Action Added (1994)

## The transformative amendment

The 1994 addition of § 1030(g) is the single most important amendment for civil CFAA litigation. Before 1994, CFAA was exclusively criminal — only the government could prosecute, and private victims had no independent right of action.

After 1994, private victims can sue. This is the provision that gives the steward the right to file a civil CFAA complaint directly against the attackers (Ryan McClaran, the SIM swap perpetrators, and potentially the carriers whose authentication failures enabled the attacks).

## The $5,000 threshold

§ 1030(c)(4)(A)(i)(I) requires $5,000 in "loss" in a one-year period. "Loss" is broadly construed by the courts to include:
- Investigation costs (retaining a forensic expert)
- Remediation costs (changing all credentials, recovering accounts)
- Losses resulting from account takeover (missing disability benefits, intercepted legal communications)
- The value of stolen data

For the steward, the aggregate SIM swap damage over multiple carriers and multiple years easily exceeds $5,000 in investigative and remediation costs alone.

## Diff from prior
Prior: Criminal only — government prosecution. After: Civil private right of action — steward can sue the perpetrators directly.
