# Context — CFAA 1986 Expansion

The 1986 Act introduced the "exceeds authorized access" concept — which becomes the central interpretive tension in CFAA law for 35 years, until Van Buren (2021). The question it poses: if someone has permission to access a system, but uses that access for an unauthorized purpose, are they "exceeding authorized access"?

For SIM swap cases: the SIM swap attacker was never authorized to access the carrier's system for the purpose of transferring the victim's phone number. The carrier employee who processed the unauthorized SIM swap "exceeded authorized access" — they had general authority to process SIM swaps, but not for fraudulent requests without proper authentication. This is the "inside job" prong of CFAA that applies where carrier employees are complicit in SIM swap fraud.

## Diff from prior
Prior: Only outsiders breaking in. After: Also insiders who exceed their authorization. This opened the door to prosecuting (and suing) carriers whose employees participated in unauthorized SIM swaps.
