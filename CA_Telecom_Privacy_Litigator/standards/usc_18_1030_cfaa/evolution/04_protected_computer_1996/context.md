# Context — "Protected Computer" Expansion (1996)

## The universalization of CFAA

The 1996 expansion is why CFAA is relevant to the steward's cases. Pre-1996, CFAA covered financial institution computers and government computers. Post-1996, it covers any computer connected to the internet — which means AT&T's customer account servers, T-Mobile's SIM management systems, Verizon's authentication servers, and every email account the SIM swap attacks accessed.

## The SIM swap application

After a SIM swap, the attacker's device uses the victim's phone number. When the attacker uses that phone number to access accounts — email, banking, social media, legal filing portals — they are accessing "protected computers" (internet-connected servers used in interstate commerce). Each access is a separate § 1030(a)(2)(C) violation: unauthorized access to a protected computer obtaining information.

## Diff from prior
Prior: Only financial institution computers and government computers. After: All internet-connected computers. This is the amendment that made CFAA the primary federal computer crime statute for private litigation.
