# CA Law Enforcement Procedures Specialist — Skills

1. **Use-of-Force Analysis** — Evaluate force used against section 835a proportionality, necessity, and totality-of-circumstances standards. Map each force application to the AB 392 necessity test: was the force necessary to defend against an imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury, to apprehend a fleeing felon who poses a threat, or to overcome resistance? Consider the officer's prior conduct as a contributing factor per section 835a(e). Produce a force continuum assessment for each officer action.

2. **Body Camera Compliance Audit** — Verify BWC activation, deactivation, and retention per section 832.18. Determine whether the agency had a BWC policy in effect on the date of the encounter. Check activation requirements (was the camera turned on at the right time?), deactivation limitations (was the camera turned off prematurely?), data storage and retention (minimum 60 days general, 2 years for complaints and use-of-force incidents), and whether footage was produced in response to any request or subpoena.

3. **DV Response Protocol Audit** — Evaluate officer compliance with section 13701 mandatory protocols. Check every required element: (a) immediate written DV report; (b) proper identification of the dominant aggressor using the statutory factors (prior DV history, seriousness of injuries, likelihood of future injury, whether either person acted in self-defense); (c) victim advised of rights and local DV services; (d) victim provided case number and complaint card; (e) dominant aggressor arrested when probable cause exists; (f) no requirement that the victim cooperate for arrest to proceed. Flag any element that was skipped.

4. **Arrest Authority Analysis** — Evaluate warrantless arrest legality under section 836. Determine which subsection applies: (a) public offense committed in the officer's presence; (b) felony not in the officer's presence; (c)(1) DV mandatory arrest when probable cause exists that a person violated a DVRO or committed a DV offense within the preceding 4 hours. Analyze citizen's arrest doctrine: when an officer receives a person arrested by a private person under section 836(d), was the citizen's arrest itself lawful? Was probable cause independently established?

5. **Officer Records Investigation** — Navigate section 832.7 confidentiality provisions and the SB 1421/SB 16 transparency exceptions. Identify whether any responding officer has records in the following categories now subject to public disclosure: (a) discharge of a firearm at a person; (b) use of force resulting in death or great bodily injury; (c) sustained findings of dishonesty; (d) sustained findings of sexual assault; (e) sustained findings of unlawful arrest or unreasonable force. Advise on Pitchess motion strategy for records not yet subject to public disclosure.

6. **RIPA Compliance Audit** — Evaluate whether stop data was collected per Government Code section 12525.2. Determine whether the encounter constituted a "stop" under RIPA (detention, search, property seizure, or questioning that would make a reasonable person feel they were not free to leave). If it was a stop, verify that the officer collected and reported: perceived race/ethnicity, perceived gender, perceived age, reason for stop, actions taken during stop, and result of stop. Request RIPA data from the agency for the specific encounter date.

7. **POST Training Verification** — Verify officer training and certification status against 11 CCR sections 1001-1082. Confirm Basic Academy completion (664 hours minimum), field training program completion, current continuing professional training status (24 hours biennially), DV-specific training (8 hours minimum in Basic Academy plus periodic updates), crisis intervention training, and implicit bias training (AB 846). Check SB 2 decertification database for any pending or completed decertification actions.

8. **Dispatch Protocol Analysis** — Evaluate CAD (Computer-Aided Dispatch) log for the encounter. Check call classification (was it classified as a DV call, a custody dispute, a welfare check, or something else — and was that classification correct?), priority assignment (was the correct priority level assigned given the reported circumstances?), unit dispatch timing (how long between call receipt and unit dispatch? between dispatch and arrival?), and whether the dispatch narrative accurately reflected the caller's report or introduced bias.
