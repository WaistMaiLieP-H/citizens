# Peace Officer Personnel Records — SB 1421 Disclosure Categories — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Pen. Code § 832.7
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** § 832.7(a) — Stats. 1978, Ch. 630; § 832.7(b) — January 1, 2019 (SB 1421); expanded January 1, 2022 (SB 16)

## The Rule

Peace officer and custodial officer personnel records are confidential by default (§ 832.7(a)). They cannot be disclosed in civil or criminal proceedings except through the Pitchess motion process (Evidence Code §§ 1043, 1046). This default rule protects officers from unsubstantiated complaint allegations being used against them in proceedings unrelated to the complaint.

The default is overridden for five categories of records (§ 832.7(b)), which must be disclosed to the public upon request under the California Public Records Act, regardless of the confidentiality default:

**(A) Use of Force Records:** Records of any incident involving discharge of a firearm at a person by a peace officer; use of force resulting in death or great bodily injury; sustained findings of unreasonable or excessive force; sustained findings of failure to intervene against another officer using clearly unreasonable force.

**(B) Sexual Assault Records:** Any record of a sustained finding that a peace officer engaged in sexual assault involving a member of the public (includes acts committed while on duty, propositioning, or coercion under color of authority).

**(C) Dishonesty Records:** Any record of a sustained finding of dishonesty by a peace officer directly relating to the reporting, investigation, or prosecution of a crime — or relating to reporting or investigation of another officer's misconduct. Includes false statements, false reports, destruction or falsification of evidence, and perjury.

**(D) Prejudice and Discrimination Records:** Any record of a sustained finding of conduct involving prejudice or discrimination on the basis of race, sex, gender, national origin, disability, age, sexual orientation, or military/veteran status.

**(E) Unlawful Arrest or Search Records:** Any record of a sustained finding that a peace officer made an unlawful arrest or conducted an unlawful search.

Disclosure includes the full investigative file: reports, audio/video evidence, interview transcripts, materials presented for criminal charging decisions, findings, disciplinary records, and letters of intent to impose discipline. Records must be produced within 45 days of request. Agencies may delay (up to 60 days for active criminal investigation; 180 days for administrative investigation). Agencies shall redact only for: personal data (home address, family members), victim/witness anonymity, and confidential medical/financial information prohibited by federal law.

## Key Elements

1. **Default confidentiality** (§ 832.7(a)): Personnel records are confidential; Pitchess motion is the only judicial pathway in a pending case.
2. **Five mandatory disclosure categories** (§ 832.7(b)): Use of force/death, sexual assault, dishonesty, prejudice/discrimination, unlawful arrest/search — MUST be disclosed publicly, notwithstanding § 832.7(a) and § 7923.600 of the Government Code.
3. **45-day production deadline** (§ 832.7(b)(11)): Agencies cannot indefinitely delay — outside active investigation windows, 45 days is the hard limit.
4. **Narrow redaction authority** (§ 832.7(b)(6)-(7)): Agencies may redact only for the enumerated purposes; general "personnel privacy" is not a valid basis for redaction of records that fall within the five mandatory categories.

## What This Means in Practice

A member of the public who wants to know whether an officer who shot someone had prior sustained findings of excessive force can submit a CPRA request. Under § 832.7(b)(1)(A), the agency must produce those records within 45 days. The officer's personnel file is no longer the shield it was before 2019. But for records outside the five categories — ordinary performance reviews, training records, complaints that were not sustained — § 832.7(a) still applies.
