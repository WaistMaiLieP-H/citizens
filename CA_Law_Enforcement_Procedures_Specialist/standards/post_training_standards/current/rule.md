# Peace Officer Standards and Training — Minimum Qualification Standards — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Gov. Code § 1031; 11 CCR § 1001 et seq. (POST Regulations)
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** Gov. Code § 1031 — Stats. 1961 (minimum standards); POST created Stats. 1959, Ch. 1778

## The Rule

Every person appointed as a peace officer in California must meet all of the following minimum standards before exercising peace officer authority (Gov. Code § 1031):

1. **Work authorization:** Legally authorized to work in the United States under federal law.
2. **Age:** 18 years of age or older.
3. **Fingerprinting:** Fingerprinted for search of local, state (CAL-DOJ), and national (FBI) fingerprint files to disclose any criminal record.
4. **Moral character:** Good moral character, as determined by a thorough background investigation. This is a comprehensive inquiry into character, fitness, and history — not a checkbox.
5. **Education:** High school graduate or equivalent (GED, California High School Proficiency Examination, or two-year, four-year, or advanced degree from an accredited college or university).
6. **Physical and psychological fitness:** Free from any physical, emotional, or mental condition — including bias against race or ethnicity, gender, nationality, religion, disability, or sexual orientation — that might adversely affect the exercise of peace officer powers. Physical evaluation by a licensed physician and surgeon. Psychological/emotional evaluation by a licensed psychiatrist (minimum 5 years experience in mental/emotional disorders, including 3 years post-residency) or a licensed psychologist (minimum 5 years experience, including 3 years post-doctorate).

Beyond § 1031, the Commission on Peace Officer Standards and Training (POST) — established by Gov. Code § 13500 et seq. — administers training certification requirements under 11 CCR § 1001 et seq. POST Basic Training (approximately 664 hours of mandated curriculum) must be completed before a peace officer may work without direct supervision. POST training covers arrest procedures, use of force, evidence handling, report writing, community relations, emergency vehicle operations, and first aid, among other required topics.

Agencies may adopt additional or higher standards than § 1031 requires (§ 1031(g)).

## Key Elements

1. **Bias evaluation explicitly required** (§ 1031(f)): The psychological evaluation must assess for bias against enumerated characteristics. Bias is not a soft concern — it is a disqualifying condition if it would adversely affect the exercise of peace officer powers.
2. **Background investigation** (§ 1031(d)): "Thorough" — agencies cannot satisfy this with a cursory check. Documented background deficiencies that an agency ignored are relevant to hiring liability.
3. **POST Basic Training (~664 hours)**: Minimum mandatory training before independent duty. An officer who exercises peace officer authority without completing POST-required training is acting outside their certification — relevant to liability analysis.
4. **Floor, not ceiling** (§ 1031(g)): § 1031 is the minimum. Agencies that adopt higher standards are not in conflict with the statute.

## What This Means in Practice

When an officer commits misconduct, one of the first questions is whether the agency met its minimum statutory obligations before putting that officer on the street. If the background investigation was not thorough, if the psychological evaluation was skipped or cursory, or if the officer never completed POST Basic Training, the agency has a direct statutory compliance failure — separate from and in addition to any vicarious liability for the officer's conduct.
