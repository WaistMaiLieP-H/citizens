CALIFORNIA GOVERNMENT CODE § 12525.5
Racial and Identity Profiling Act — RIPA Data Collection
As enacted by AB 953 (Stats. 2015, Ch. 466) and amended by AB 2067 (2017),
SB 978 (2019), and AB 175 (2019)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(a) For purposes of this section, the following definitions apply:

    (1) "Law enforcement agency" means a state or local agency that employs
        peace officers.

    (2) "Pedestrian stop" means an interaction between a peace officer and
        an individual who is being detained but who is not the subject of a
        traffic stop.

    (3) "Stop" means any detention by a peace officer of a person or any
        interaction with a person in which the officer requests or demands
        information or requests or demands that the person identify themselves
        or requests the person to provide other information in the context of
        a law enforcement encounter.

    (4) "Traffic stop" means a stop of a motor vehicle by a peace officer.

(b) Commencing July 1, 2018, each state and local agency that employs peace
officers shall collect data on all stops conducted by officers of that
agency. The data shall include, at a minimum, all of the following:

    (1) The perceived race or ethnicity of the person stopped.

    (2) The perceived gender of the person stopped.

    (3) Whether the person stopped appeared to be 17 years of age or
        younger.

    (4) The reason for the stop.

    (5) The result of the stop, including, but not limited to, whether a
        warning, citation, infraction, or arrest was issued or made.

    (6) If the person stopped was asked to consent to a search, whether
        consent was given and whether the search resulted in discovery of
        evidence of a crime.

    (7) Whether the officer used force against the person.

    (8) Whether the person was transported to a hospital or other medical
        facility.

(c) Each agency subject to subdivision (b) shall annually report to the
Attorney General the data collected pursuant to subdivision (b). The
Attorney General shall make the data publicly available and shall publish
an annual report analyzing the data.

    The Attorney General shall:

    (1) Post the data to a publicly accessible internet website.

    (2) Analyze and report on the data, including analysis by the race,
        ethnicity, and gender of the persons stopped.

    (3) Develop a racially and identity neutral methodology to analyze
        collected data to determine whether law enforcement agencies are
        engaging in racial or identity profiling.

(d) The data collected pursuant to this section shall not include personally
identifiable information of the persons stopped or of the peace officers
making the stops. However, each law enforcement agency shall maintain a
copy of the data that contains the personally identifiable information of
the peace officer who made the stop, and shall make those records available
for inspection and audit by the Attorney General.

(e) Each agency subject to subdivision (b) shall maintain the data collected
pursuant to subdivision (b) for a minimum of five years.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

RIPA BOARD (Government Code § 12525.6):

A nine-member Racial and Identity Profiling Advisory (RIPA) Board was
established to oversee implementation. The Board is chaired by the Attorney
General and includes representatives from law enforcement, community
advocacy, civil rights organizations, and academia.
