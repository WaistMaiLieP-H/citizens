# Penal Code § 13701 — Domestic Violence Response Policies: Plain-English Context

## What This Law Does

Every California law enforcement agency is required to have written policies governing
how officers respond to domestic violence calls. Those policies must reflect that DV
is serious criminal conduct, must encourage arrests and prosecutions, and must cover
specific categories: when to make felony arrests, how to handle dual-arrest situations,
what information to give victims, and how to write the incident report.

Three obligations are absolute — they apply regardless of circumstances:

1. **A written incident report must be completed on every DV call**, whether or not
   an arrest is made, whether or not the victim cooperates, and whether or not the
   officer observed any act of violence. There is no discretion here. A DV call
   equals a report. No exceptions.

2. **A victim information card or equivalent must be provided**, listing available
   resources, civil and criminal remedies, and information about mandatory arrest.

3. **The policies themselves must exist in writing**. Not assumed, not verbal, not
   left to officer discretion — written, adopted, implemented. Failure to have a
   written policy is itself a violation.

## Why the Mandatory Report Matters

The mandatory report requirement exists because DV victims often do not cooperate with
prosecution at the scene. An officer who responds to a call, decides no arrest is
needed, and writes no report has eliminated the record. If the victim later wants to
pursue charges, or if the encounter is reviewed in litigation, there is no
contemporaneous documentation.

Section 13701(c) eliminates officer discretion to skip the report. The report must be
written. What it says may reflect the officer's perspective — but it must exist. And
when it doesn't, that absence is itself evidence of a § 13701 violation.

## What the Report Must Capture

The § 13701 form requirements include:
- Physical symptoms of abuse visible to the officer
- Demeanor of both parties
- Names and ages of any minor children present
- Description of weapons on the premises
- Any threats made by the suspect

This is a standardized information-capture requirement. If the report for June 16,
2023 omits required fields — particularly the demeanor of both parties, the presence
of children (Cole), or the basis for treating the encounter as a DV call — each
omission is a violation of the mandatory form requirement.

## The DV Framing Problem

Section 13701's mandatory arrest and reporting provisions assume that the DV call
represents an actual domestic violence incident. When a call is framed as DV by a
third-party dispatcher directing law enforcement but no DV occurred, the § 13701
machinery is still triggered — but it should produce a report accurately reflecting
what was found at the scene.

If officers arrived on June 16, 2023 framing the encounter as DV and that framing
was driven by pre-positioning (Christina on scene) and third-party direction (mother
directing APD dispatch), then the § 13701 report is a forensic record of whether
officers documented reality or imported the framing they arrived with.

operative on the responding officers. The audit checklist:

**Report existence:**
- Was a § 13701 incident report completed for the June 16, 2023 call? If not, that

**Report completeness:**

**The absence test:**
- If the report omits required fields, compare what was omitted against what would
  have been unfavorable to the DV framing. Selective omission in a mandatory form
  is not accidental — it is evidence of a conscious choice to not document
  what the form required.

**Victim information card:**
- Was Michael Hartmann provided a victims information card? Under § 13701, the
  obligation to provide the card is not limited to the person designated as the
  "victim" by the responding officer. If Michael was a victim of the coordinated
  dispatch and his rights under § 13701 were not provided, that is a separate failure.

  If an arrest was made of Michael without probable cause, that is a § 836 violation.
  Both outcomes are auditable against the written report.

obtainable via PRA request. Once obtained:
