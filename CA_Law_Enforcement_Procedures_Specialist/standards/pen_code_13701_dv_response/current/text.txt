CALIFORNIA PENAL CODE § 13701
Domestic Violence — Law Enforcement Response Policies
As enacted and amended through current form

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

(a) Every law enforcement agency in this state shall develop, adopt, and
implement written policies and standards for officers' responses to domestic
violence calls. These policies shall reflect that domestic violence is
serious criminal behavior; shall encourage the use of arrests and criminal
prosecutions of persons who commit acts of domestic violence; and shall
contain standards for the following:

    (1) Felony arrests.

    (2) Arrest and booking procedures, including the factors to be
        considered in determining whether dual arrest is appropriate.

    (3) Victim assistance, including providing information to victims
        at the scene of a domestic violence call.

    (4) Completion of incident reports on domestic violence calls.

    (5) Procedures for the collection and preservation of evidence.

    (6) Referral to community resources.

    (7) Procedures for mandatory response to domestic violence calls.

    (8) Officer training standards.

    (9) Procedures for ensuring the safety of children present during
        a domestic violence incident.

(b) Every law enforcement agency shall develop a form for use by peace
officers and others when responding to a domestic violence call. The
form shall include, but need not be limited to, the following:

    (1) A notation of any physical symptoms of abuse, including bruises,
        lacerations, and injuries.

    (2) A notation of the demeanor of the parties.

    (3) Names and ages of minor children who are present.

    (4) A description of weapons on the premises or used in the incident.

    (5) A notation of any threats made by the suspect.

(c) The officer who responds to a domestic violence call shall complete
a written report of the incident regardless of whether an arrest is made.
This obligation exists whether or not:

    (i)  the victim consents to prosecution;
    (ii) the officer observed the act of domestic violence;
    (iii) the officer concludes that no arrest is appropriate.

    Failure to complete the required incident report is a violation of this
    section and constitutes a failure to comply with mandatory law
    enforcement duties.

(d) Each law enforcement agency shall make available to victims of domestic
violence, at the scene and after any investigation, a "Victims of Domestic
Violence" card or equivalent written document stating:

    (1) Resources available to victims, including emergency shelter options,
        crisis hotlines, legal assistance, and support services.

    (2) Information about civil and criminal remedies, including emergency
        protective orders, restraining orders, and the right to press charges.

    (3) Information about the mandatory arrest provisions of Penal Code
        § 836(c).

(e) The policies required by this section shall be developed in consultation
with community members, victims' advocates, representatives of law
enforcement, and local shelter and service providers.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

MANDATORY ARREST CROSS-REFERENCE:
Penal Code § 836(c) provides that when a peace officer is responding to a
domestic violence call and has probable cause to believe that a person has
committed a misdemeanor involving domestic violence, the officer shall arrest
the person for the misdemeanor. This mandatory arrest provision exists
regardless of whether the officer observed the offense.
