# Reasoning — Cal. Penal Code § 13701 (Domestic Violence Response — Mandatory Policies)

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_13701_DV_RESPONSE
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why This Standard Exists

Section 13701 was enacted in 1984 as part of California's first comprehensive domestic violence response legislation. The Legislature found that law enforcement agencies across California were treating DV calls as private family matters outside the scope of criminal law enforcement — officers would respond, observe the situation, tell the parties to "work it out," and leave without making arrests or writing reports. This practice left victims unprotected and created no evidentiary record.

Section 13701 abolished officer discretion to not report. The mandatory report requirement was designed to create an irrefutable evidentiary trail for every DV call, regardless of what officers believed about the incident's merits.

---

## Why the Mandatory Report Requirement Cannot Be Excused

The "shall" in § 13701(c) reflects a legislative determination that officer judgment about whether a call "really" involved domestic violence cannot be the gating condition for documentation. Officers who arrive at a call, decide it was a misunderstanding, and write no report have used their subjective assessment to eliminate the record — exactly the practice the Legislature intended to stop.

The report must be written even when:
- No violence is observed by the officer
- The victim refuses to cooperate
- The caller recants or claims the call was a mistake
- The officer believes the call was fabricated

This last scenario is particularly important: if a DV call is fabricated — dispatched by a third party who was not present and who directed officers to a scene where no DV occurred — the § 13701 report, when completed accurately, becomes the documentary proof that no DV was found. When the report is not completed, or is completed with imported framing rather than observed facts, the absence or distortion itself becomes evidence of the violation.

---

## Why Required Report Fields Are Not Optional

The six mandatory content fields of § 13701(c) are a structured evidence-capture protocol. Each field exists because the Legislature identified a specific category of information that prior officer discretion had resulted in systematically not capturing:

- **Demeanor of both parties**: Officers leaving notes that said "complainant reported DV" without documenting what they observed — creating one-sided records.
- **Minor children present**: Children were routinely ignored in DV reports, leaving no record of their exposure or condition.
- **Weapons on premises**: Weapons presence is a risk-stratification factor; its omission left future responders uninformed.
- **Threats made**: The pattern of threats over time — not captured in individual reports — is the escalation evidence courts need.

When a § 13701 report omits required fields, the auditable question is not whether the officer forgot. The question is which required fields are missing and whether their absence was convenient to a particular narrative.

---

## Why a Fabricated DV Call Still Triggers § 13701

Section 13701's obligations are triggered by the DV-coded call, not by the underlying truth of the DV allegation. If an agency's dispatch system codes a call as DV, officers responding to that call are operating under § 13701 from the moment they arrive. Their obligation to document what they found — accurately — is not contingent on the DV allegation being true. It is contingent on the call having been made.

This means: if a call is pre-framed as DV by a third-party dispatcher, officers arrive with § 13701 obligations, and the report they write is the official record of what the § 13701 investigation found. A report that imports the dispatcher's framing without documenting observed facts is not a § 13701-compliant report — it is a fabricated one.

---

## Application in Practice

For any DV-coded call:
1. Confirm the agency has a written § 13701 policy (obtain via PRA if needed).
2. Confirm a report was completed. Absence = standalone violation.
3. Audit the report against the six mandatory content fields. Each omission = auditable failure.
4. Evaluate whether the report documents observed facts or imported framing.
5. Confirm a victim information card was provided to the appropriate party.
6. Compare officer conduct to the agency's written § 13701 policy.
