# Domestic Violence Law Enforcement Response — Mandatory Policies — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Pen. Code § 13701
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** Original enactment 1984; major amendments 1994, 2000

## The Rule

Every law enforcement agency in California shall develop, adopt, and implement written policies and standards for officer responses to domestic violence calls (§ 13701(a)). These policies are not optional and not aspirational — they are mandatory legal obligations enforceable by the Department of Justice.

**Mandatory written policies must include (§ 13701(a)):**
- Felony arrest requirements when probable cause exists for felony DV
- Guidance on dual-arrest situations (when both parties claim to be victims)
- Mandatory information provision to victims
- Reporting requirements for every DV call

**Mandatory incident report (§ 13701(c)):** A written report shall be completed on every domestic violence call, regardless of:
- Whether an arrest was made
- Whether the victim cooperates or requests no action
- Whether the officer observed any act of violence
- Whether the call was ultimately determined to be domestic violence

The report must be completed. There is no officer discretion to skip it.

**Mandatory report contents (§ 13701(c)):** The incident report must document:
1. Physical symptoms of abuse or injury visible to the officer
2. Demeanor of both parties at time of officer arrival
3. Names and ages of minor children present
4. Description of any weapons on the premises
5. Any threats made by either party
6. Actions taken by responding officers

**Mandatory victim information (§ 13701(b)):** Every DV response must include provision of a written statement to the victim containing:
- Available civil and criminal remedies
- Available law enforcement options
- Shelter and support services
- Whether the suspect may be released before arraignment

**DV arrest policy (§ 13701(a)(1)):** Written policies shall reflect the legislative determination that DV is criminal conduct that is not to be excused or ignored and shall encourage officers to make arrests when probable cause exists.

## Key Elements

1. **Written policies are mandatory** — their absence is a § 13701 violation independent of any individual officer conduct.
2. **Report on every call** — no discretion, no exceptions; the absence of a report for a DV-coded call is per se a violation.
3. **Mandatory report contents** — the six required fields must appear; selective omission of required fields is an auditable failure, not mere oversight.
4. **Victim information card** — required regardless of whether the victim is the caller or is the person who called law enforcement.

## What This Means in Practice

The mandatory report requirement is the most operationally significant element of § 13701. When a DV-coded call generates no report, the statute has been violated. When a report omits required fields — particularly the demeanor of both parties or the presence of minor children — each omission is a documented statutory failure. The pattern of omissions tells an auditor what the officer chose not to document, which is often more revealing than what was documented.
