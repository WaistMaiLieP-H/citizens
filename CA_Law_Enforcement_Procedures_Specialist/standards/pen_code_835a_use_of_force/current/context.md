# Penal Code § 835a — Use of Force: Plain-English Context

## What This Law Does

Before 2020, California officers could use force as long as it was "reasonable" — the
federal Graham v. Connor standard. That standard was judged after the fact: would a
reasonable officer in the same situation have acted the same way? It was almost
impossible to fail because officers could articulate a subjective belief about threat,
and courts deferred.

AB 392, effective January 1, 2020, replaced "reasonable" with **necessary**. That
one-word change transformed the analysis. Force is necessary only if there was no
reasonable alternative available that would have accomplished the same lawful objective
with less force. If de-escalation, verbal commands, or doing nothing would have achieved
the same result, force was not necessary.

The second major change: **the officer's own pre-force conduct counts**. Under § 835a(d)(3),
"totality of the circumstances" includes the conduct of the officer leading up to the
use of force. An officer who escalates a situation — through aggressive approach, failure
to de-escalate, drawing a weapon unnecessarily — cannot then claim that force was
necessary because the situation had become dangerous. The officer's contribution to the
danger is weighed against the claimed necessity.

## The Three Questions Every Force Encounter Must Answer

1. Was force used at all? (Includes physical contact against a person's will —
   grabbing, restraint, handcuffing when not under arrest, separation of parent from child.)

2. If force was used, was it necessary under the totality of circumstances, or would
   a reasonable alternative have accomplished the same lawful objective?

3. Did the officer's own conduct contribute to the need for force? If so, that
   contribution is weighed against the claimed necessity.

## What Counts as Force

Under § 835a(d)(1), "deadly force" means any use of force that creates a substantial
risk of death or serious bodily injury, including discharge of a firearm. But the
statute also governs non-deadly force — any physical contact that restrains, moves,
or controls a person against their will.

Physically separating a child from a parent is a use of force. Holding a person's arm
while escorting them is a use of force. Handcuffing a person who is not under arrest
is a use of force. The necessity standard applies to all of it.

## Steward Relevance: June 16, 2023 APD Response

Section 835a had been in effect for three and a half years on June 16, 2023. APD was
required to have a compliant written use-of-force policy for two and a half years under
SB 230 (effective January 1, 2021).

**Concrete audit questions for case #04-23-01959:**

- Were officers wearing body cameras, and were those cameras active throughout the
  encounter? (See also § 832.18 — body camera standards.)

- Was any physical contact made with Michael Hartmann during the encounter? Was he
  physically moved, restrained, or prevented from approaching Cole?

- Was Cole physically removed from Michael's custody or presence by officer action?
  If so, that is a use of force subject to the necessity standard.

- Per the June 16, 2023 timeline reconstruction: Christina was pre-positioned; the
  mother directed APD; the arrival took 20 minutes. The officers' conduct upon arrival —
  how they approached, what they said, whether they attempted to determine context before
  intervening — is part of the totality of circumstances.

- Did officers draw weapons during the encounter? Did they approach in a manner that
  escalated rather than de-escalated?

- If force was used, was it necessary given the totality of circumstances? Two adults
  present, child not in danger, no imminent threat of death or serious bodily injury.
  Under those facts, physical intervention would not satisfy the necessity standard.

**The adverse inference path:** If APD had body cameras active (as required) and the
footage was not preserved (see § 832.18 — minimum 60-day retention, longer for
complaint-related footage), the failure to preserve creates an adverse inference.
The footage presumably showed what was available to preserve — which means it
presumably showed what happened. Failure to retain after a complaint is departmental
admission by silence.

**APD Policy Compliance:** APD was required to adopt a § 835a-compliant use-of-force
policy by January 1, 2021. Obtaining that written policy via PRA request establishes
the standard APD set for itself. Comparing officer conduct on June 16, 2023 against
APD's own written policy is the audit. If officers deviated from the policy, that
deviation is per se evidence of the violation.
