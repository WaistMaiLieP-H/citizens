# Glenn v. Washington County, 673 F.3d 864 (9th Cir. 2011)

## The Case

A section 1983 excessive force case in the Ninth Circuit. Officers used significant force during an arrest, and the court evaluated the totality of circumstances, including events leading up to the use of force.

## What the Court Held

The Ninth Circuit:

1. **Totality of circumstances includes pre-force conduct**: The court held that the totality of circumstances used to evaluate force includes the events leading up to the force application. Officers cannot create a dangerous situation through their own conduct and then use that danger to justify force.

2. **Tactical choices matter**: If officers had reasonable alternatives available — such as waiting for backup, maintaining distance, or using verbal de-escalation — and chose not to use them, that is relevant to whether the force that followed was reasonable.

3. **The "created the need" doctrine**: When officers' own pre-seizure conduct is an "integral part of the totality of circumstances," it must be considered. Officers who unnecessarily escalate bear responsibility for the escalation.

## The Rule

**The totality of circumstances for evaluating force includes the officer's own tactical decisions and conduct leading up to the force. Officers who create the need for force through their own actions cannot then claim the force was justified by the danger they created.**

## Why This Case Matters

Glenn v. Washington County is a Ninth Circuit case — it is binding federal authority in California. The "created the need" doctrine it articulates was later codified in California state law by AB 392, which explicitly provides in section 835a(d) that the officer's conduct leading up to the use of force is part of the totality of circumstances analysis.

For the steward's case: If APD officers approached the June 16, 2023 situation in a way that escalated it — aggressive posture, weapons drawn, refusal to listen, immediate physical intervention without verbal de-escalation — their own conduct is part of the force analysis. The Glenn doctrine, now codified in California statute, means they cannot blame the situation they created.
