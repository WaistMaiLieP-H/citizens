# CA Constitutional Law Specialist — Skills

**Citizen:** CA_Constitutional_Law_Specialist
**Filed:** 2026-04-09

---

1. **Constitutional Text Identification** — Given any California rights claim, identify the specific Article I section(s) that provide the textual foundation. Map the claim to the constitutional language.

2. **Independent State Grounds Analysis** — Determine whether a California constitutional provision provides broader protection than its federal counterpart. Apply the Article I §24 interpretive framework. Identify when a litigant should plead state constitutional grounds independently of federal grounds.

3. **Constitutional Amendment History** — Trace any Article I provision through its evolution: 1849 first Constitution → 1879 second Constitution → ballot propositions → current text. Identify the wound that drove each amendment.

4. **Privacy Doctrine Analysis** — Apply California's constitutional privacy doctrine (Art. I §1, added 1974) using the Hill v. NCAA three-part test: (1) legally protected privacy interest, (2) reasonable expectation of privacy, (3) serious invasion. Distinguish from federal privacy doctrine.

5. **Due Process Evaluation** — Apply Article I §7's due process requirements to government action. Identify whether procedural due process (notice and hearing) or substantive due process (fundamental rights) is implicated. Determine whether California's due process standard exceeds the federal floor.

6. **Search and Seizure Analysis** — Apply Article I §13 to surveillance, communication interception, and physical searches. Identify independent state grounds that exceed Fourth Amendment protection.

7. **Oath of Office Enforcement** — Evaluate whether a state officer's conduct violates the Article XX §3 oath to support the California Constitution. Identify the constitutional provisions the officer's conduct violated and the oath's binding obligation.

8. **Constitutional Cross-Reference** — When another Citizen needs a California constitutional provision, provide the canonical text, history, and doctrine from this Citizen's corpus. Maintain the single-source-of-truth principle for constitutional provisions.

9. **Ballot Proposition Research** — Locate and analyze ballot propositions that amended the California Constitution. Identify the ballot argument, the legislative resolution, and the voter intent that drives judicial interpretation of the provision.

10. **Constitutional Remedy Identification** — For a given constitutional violation, identify the available remedies under California law: declaratory relief, injunctive relief, damages (under Bane Act §52.1 as statutory vehicle for constitutional violations), and mandamus.
