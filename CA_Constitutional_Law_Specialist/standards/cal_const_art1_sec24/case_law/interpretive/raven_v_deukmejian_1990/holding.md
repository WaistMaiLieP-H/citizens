# Raven v. Deukmejian, 52 Cal. 3d 336 (1990)

## The Case

After Proposition 115 (the Crime Victims Justice Reform Act of 1990) was passed by voters, it was challenged as an unconstitutional revision of the California Constitution rather than a permissible amendment. Article I, Section 24 provides that rights guaranteed by the California Constitution are not dependent on the U.S. Constitution — California can provide broader protections. Proposition 115 attempted to limit California courts to federal constitutional standards in criminal cases.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **Proposition 115's provision requiring California courts to follow federal constitutional standards in criminal cases was a permissible amendment, not a revision, of the California Constitution.** A revision requires a constitutional convention or legislative proposal; an amendment can be enacted by initiative.

2. **However, Article I, Section 24 preserves the principle that the California Constitution is an independent source of rights.** Section 24 states: "Rights guaranteed by this Constitution are not dependent on those guaranteed by the United States Constitution." This independence survives Proposition 115 — the Legislature and the people can define California rights, and those rights are independently enforceable.

3. **The independent state grounds doctrine has constitutional status in California through Section 24.** California courts are not bound by federal constitutional floors — they can provide broader protection under the state constitution.

## The Rule

**Article I, Section 24 establishes California constitutional rights as independent of federal rights. California courts may interpret the state constitution to provide broader protections than the federal Constitution, and that independence has constitutional rank.**
