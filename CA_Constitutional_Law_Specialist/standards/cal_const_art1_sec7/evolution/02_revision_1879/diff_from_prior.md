# 1879 Revision — Diff from 1849

## What Changed

1. **Section number changed:** Due process moved from Section 8 (1849) to **Section 13** (1879). The entire Article I was reorganized and expanded.

2. **Takings clause separated:** The 1849 Section 8 bundled due process with the takings clause ("nor shall private property be taken for public use without just compensation"). The 1879 Constitution separated the takings clause into its own section (Section 14). Due process now stands with only double jeopardy and self-incrimination.

3. **Equal protection still absent:** The 1879 revision still does not contain a standalone equal protection clause, despite the Fourteenth Amendment having been ratified in 1868. California would not add an explicit equal protection provision until 1974.

4. **Privileges and immunities added elsewhere:** The 1879 Constitution added a privileges and immunities provision (not in the 1849 version), but it was placed in a different section, not combined with due process.

## What Did NOT Change

The due process language itself is nearly identical: "nor be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law." The only difference is the removal of the comma after "property" — a punctuation change with no legal significance.

## Structural Significance

The 1879 revision gave due process more room to breathe by separating it from the takings clause. But it still bundled due process with double jeopardy and self-incrimination in a single section — a bundling that would persist until 1974, when Prop. 7 finally gave due process its own standalone section (Section 7) and moved the criminal-defendant protections to Section 15.
