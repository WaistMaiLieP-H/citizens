# 1974 Proposition 7 — Diff from 1879

## What Changed — Everything

Proposition 7 was a comprehensive revision of Article I. It did not amend Section 13 — it **replaced the entire Article I** with a modernized version. The changes to the due process provision were structural, not merely cosmetic:

1. **Section number changed again:** Due process moved from Section 13 (1879) to **Section 7** (1974). This was part of a complete renumbering.

2. **Due process finally separated from criminal-defendant protections.** For the first time since 1849, the due process clause is NOT bundled with double jeopardy and self-incrimination. Those criminal-defendant protections moved to Section 15. Section 7 is now solely about due process and equal protection.

3. **Equal protection ADDED.** For the first time in California constitutional history, the due process section explicitly includes an equal protection guarantee: "or denied equal protection of the laws." This language tracks the Fourteenth Amendment's Equal Protection Clause. Before 1974, California had due process but no explicit equal protection.

4. **Privileges and immunities clause added as subdivision (b).** "A citizen or class of citizens may not be granted privileges or immunities not granted on the same terms to all citizens." This creates a standalone equal-citizenship guarantee within the same section as due process.

5. **Language modernized:** "No person shall be... deprived" became "A person may not be deprived." "Men" became "person" throughout Article I. The passive-prohibition framing was replaced with active-prohibition framing.

## What This Means

The 1974 revision created the modern California due process and equal protection framework. For the first time:
- Due process has its own section
- Equal protection is explicit
- The privileges and immunities clause prevents class-based discrimination
- The language is gender-neutral and modernized

This is the version the California Supreme Court interpreted for five years before the 1979 busing amendment narrowed subdivision (a) for the specific purpose of school desegregation remedies.
