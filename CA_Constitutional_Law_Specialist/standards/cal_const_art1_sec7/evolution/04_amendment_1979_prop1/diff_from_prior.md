# 1979 Proposition 1 — Diff from 1974

## What Changed

Proposition 1 (November 6, 1979, Res.Ch. 18, 1979) amended **only subdivision (a)** of Section 7. Subdivision (b) was not touched.

The amendment added all text after the first sentence of subdivision (a). The first sentence — "A person may not be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law or denied equal protection of the laws" — remained unchanged. Everything after that is the 1979 addition.

## The Busing Limitation

The 1979 amendment was a direct response to California Supreme Court decisions requiring school busing remedies that went beyond what the federal Fourteenth Amendment required. The California court had interpreted the pre-1979 equal protection clause as providing independent state grounds for mandatory school desegregation — meaning California courts could order busing even in districts where no federal court would do so.

The amendment limits state equal protection obligations **for pupil school assignment and transportation only** to whatever the federal Equal Protection Clause requires. This is a lockstep provision — but only for busing.

## What Did NOT Change

- The first sentence of (a): due process and equal protection guarantees remain fully intact
- Subdivision (b): the privileges and immunities clause is unchanged
- **For any purpose OTHER than pupil school assignment/transportation**, Section 7(a) provides the same independent state grounds it has since 1974
- Due process is not affected by the busing limitation at all — it applies only to equal protection in the school context

## This Is the Current Text

Section 7 has not been amended since 1979. The text as amended by Prop. 1 is the current text on leginfo.
