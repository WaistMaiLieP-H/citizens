# Raven v. Deukmejian, 52 Cal.3d 336 (1990)

## The Case

Proposition 115 (the Crime Victims Justice Reform Act) was approved by voters in June 1990. Among many provisions, it amended Article I, Section 24 to require that criminal defendants' rights be construed consistently with the U.S. Constitution — the lockstep provision. Opponents challenged the proposition as an unconstitutional revision (requiring a constitutional convention or legislative supermajority) rather than a mere amendment (permissible by initiative).

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court held that **most of Proposition 115 was a valid amendment**, not a revision, because it did not alter the fundamental structure of California government. However, the Court struck down one provision that would have eliminated the independent state constitutional right to due process for criminal defendants beyond what the federal Constitution provides — holding that this specific provision went too far and constituted a revision.

The critical distinction: Proposition 115 could require lockstep interpretation of enumerated criminal defendant rights (which it did via the §24 amendment), but it could not eliminate the underlying state constitutional right to due process itself. The right exists independently under Section 7; the interpretive methodology for criminal cases changed under Section 24, but the right is still there.

## The Rule Raven Stands For

**The California Constitution's due process guarantee in Article I, Section 7 is a structural feature of the Constitution that cannot be eliminated by initiative amendment.** The voters can change how courts interpret specific rights in criminal cases (the §24 lockstep), but they cannot remove the due process guarantee itself. Section 7's due process clause is part of California's constitutional architecture.

For the steward's case: Raven confirms that Section 7 due process is a foundational, structural right — not merely a policy preference that can be voted away. Any government action that deprives the steward of life, liberty, or property without due process violates this structural guarantee.
