# Article I, Section 7 — Historical Chain

## The Standard That Says the Government Cannot Take Without Process

---

### I. The Wound

Due process exists because governments took things from people without asking. Liberty. Property. Children. The history of due process is the history of governments acting first and explaining never. Every procedural protection in the law exists because someone was harmed by its absence.

### II. The Promise (1974)

Section 7 was added by Proposition 7 in 1974 as part of the comprehensive Article I revision. Subdivision (a) states the due process and equal protection guarantee: "A person may not be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law or denied equal protection of the laws."

This is California's Fourteenth Amendment. But it is not the Fourteenth Amendment. It is an independent California constitutional provision that exists on its own terms.

### III. The Busing Amendment (1979 — Prop. 1)

The 1979 amendment to subdivision (a) was driven by the school busing controversy. The California Supreme Court had interpreted the pre-1979 equal protection clause to require school desegregation remedies broader than what the federal Fourteenth Amendment required. Proposition 1 amended Section 7(a) to limit state equal protection obligations for pupil assignment and transportation to the federal floor.

This is important because of what it does NOT do: the busing limitation applies ONLY to pupil school assignment and transportation. For every other purpose — including due process in conservatorship proceedings, equal protection in custody proceedings, property rights, liberty interests — Section 7 retains its full independent force. The 1979 amendment was surgical, not general.

### IV. Subdivision (b) — The Privileges and Immunities Clause

"A citizen or class of citizens may not be granted privileges or immunities not granted on the same terms to all citizens." This is California's equal-citizenship guarantee. It prohibits the government from creating classes of citizens with different rights — which is precisely what a secret conservatorship does. If one person is placed under a conservatorship they do not know about, they are a member of a class (conservatees) with diminished rights, while everyone else retains full citizenship. Subdivision (b) says the government cannot do this unless it grants the same treatment on the same terms to all.

### V. For the Steward's Case

Due process under Section 7(a) requires notice and hearing before the government deprives a person of life, liberty, or property. A conservatorship is a deprivation of liberty. If a conservatorship was established without adequate notice to the steward, without a hearing at which he was present and represented, or without a capacity determination that meets the requirements of Probate Code §811, then the conservatorship was established in violation of Section 7(a)'s due process guarantee.

The property deprivations documented in the steward's case — house sale, financial fraud, pension freeze — each implicate Section 7(a) if they were accomplished through state action (court orders, recording chain manipulation, government-facilitated transfers) without adequate procedural protections.

Section 7 is the California constitutional foundation for every procedural challenge the steward brings. It does not depend on the Fourteenth Amendment. It stands on its own.
