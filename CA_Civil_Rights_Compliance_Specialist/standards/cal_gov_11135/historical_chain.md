# Historical Chain — Cal. Gov. Code § 11135
## Nondiscrimination in State-Funded Programs — Stats. 1977, Ch. 972

---

## The wound

Federal civil rights law created a floor in 1964. Title VI of the Civil Rights Act prohibited discrimination based on race, color, and national origin in any program or activity receiving federal financial assistance. Title IX (1972) extended the prohibition to sex discrimination in federally funded education programs. But both statutes were tied to federal funding. A California agency, program, or institution that received state money but not federal money was outside their reach. And even where federal funding was present, enforcement depended on federal agency action — a mechanism that was slow, politically sensitive, and often did not reach the specific discrimination at issue.

Throughout the early 1970s, individuals in California who were denied services, excluded from programs, or treated unequally by state-funded entities on the basis of sex, race, disability, or other characteristics had no direct California statutory remedy. They could attempt a constitutional claim under the equal protection clause, but constitutional claims require state action and intent — a demanding standard. They had no statute that said, plainly, that a state-funded program cannot discriminate.

---

## The legislative response

In 1977, the California Legislature enacted Government Code Article 9.5 (Stats. 1977, Ch. 972), adding §§ 11135–11139 to the Government Code. Section 11135 stated the rule directly: no person in California shall, on the basis of protected characteristics, be denied full and equal access to the benefits of, or subjected to discrimination under, any program or activity conducted, operated, or administered by the state, funded directly by the state, or receiving any state financial assistance.

California modeled the structure on Title VI — a broadly applicable nondiscrimination rule tied to the funding relationship — but extended it to characteristics that federal law then did not cover, including age, mental disability, physical disability, medical condition, marital status, and sexual orientation (added over successive amendments). The 2016 amendment (SB 1442, Stats. 2016, Ch. 870) was the most recent, expanding protected bases and clarifying coverage of the California State University system.

The disability subdivision (§ 11135(b)) directly incorporates ADA Title II protections and provides that stronger California protections supersede. The perception and association subdivision (§ 11135(d)) extends protection to people discriminated against based on a perceived protected characteristic or their association with a protected person.

---

## The current state

Section 11135 binds every state agency, every state-funded program, and every entity receiving state financial assistance — including county courts, social services agencies, public health programs, school districts, and any institution that takes a dollar of state money.

Protected-basis definitions are imported from the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) under Government Code § 12926, which means the protections track FEHA's definitions and are interpreted in light of FEHA case law.

For the CA Civil Rights Compliance Specialist, § 11135 is the primary vehicle for challenging discrimination in state-funded programs when federal Title VI or Title IX does not reach the funding source or the protected basis. It is directly relevant where state agencies discriminated against individuals on the basis of disability (including perceived disability), or where state-funded services were delivered unequally based on a protected characteristic.
