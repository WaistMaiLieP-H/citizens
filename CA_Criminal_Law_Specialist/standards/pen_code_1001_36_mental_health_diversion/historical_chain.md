# Historical Chain — Pen. Code § 1001.36

## Origin

Section 1001.36 was enacted in **2018** (Stats. 2018, ch. 1005 (AB 1810), § 2, eff. June 27, 2018). It created the first comprehensive statewide mental health pretrial diversion program applicable to both misdemeanor and felony charges.

**Context:** Prior to § 1001.36, mental health diversion was available only in counties that had established collaborative courts (mental health courts) under § 1001.01. AB 1810 standardized and expanded access statewide, making diversion a statutory right triggered by eligibility rather than a program-dependent privilege.

**Legislative history:** The Legislature found that mental health diversion reduces recidivism, is more cost-effective than incarceration, and better addresses the root causes of criminal behavior in defendants with mental illness. The statute reflects the Legislature's express policy preference for treatment over punishment for eligible defendants.

## Key subsequent developments

**2018 — Emergency implementation:** Because AB 1810 was chaptered as an urgency statute, it took effect June 27, 2018, immediately upon signing.

**2019 — AB 718 amendments:** Minor technical amendments clarified the role of the public guardian and the interface between § 1001.36 diversion and conservatorship proceedings (§ 5350 WIC).

**2020 — People v. Frahs (9 Cal.5th 618):** The California Supreme Court held § 1001.36 applies retroactively to all non-final judgments under *In re Estrada*. This dramatically expanded the statute's reach to include defendants whose cases were pending on appeal at the time of enactment.

**2022 — SB 1223:** Amended subdivision (b)(2) to strengthen the pro-defendant presumption: the court now SHALL find the mental disorder was a significant factor unless the prosecution proves by clear and convincing evidence it was not. Before 2022, the burden allocation was less explicit.

**2023–present:** The statute has been substantively stable. The interaction with § 1370 (competency) and § 1001.9 (record restriction) has been clarified by subsequent Court of Appeal decisions.

## Relationship to other diversion statutes

| Statute | Scope |
|---|---|
| § 1000 | Drug diversion (deferred entry of judgment) |
| § 1001.20–1001.33 | Military/veteran diversion |
| § 1001.36 | Mental health diversion (this statute) |
| § 1001.80 | Military diversion (separate) |
| § 1001.95 | Misdemeanor diversion (general, 2021) |

Section 1001.36 is the broadest diversion statute — applicable to both misdemeanor and felony charges (with enumerated exclusions) and to any qualifying mental health diagnosis, not just substance use.
