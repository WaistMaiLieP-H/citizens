# Pen. Code § 1001.36 — Mental Health Diversion: Context and Elements

## What this statute does

Section 1001.36 (enacted 2018) creates a pretrial diversion program for defendants with diagnosed mental disorders when the disorder was a significant factor in the offense. Upon successful completion of treatment, the criminal charges are dismissed and the arrest is deemed never to have occurred.

This statute is the primary vehicle for case #9 (Solano M25-00758, "Diversion").

## Eligibility: The two-part test

**Part 1 — Eligibility (subd. (b)):**

A. The defendant must have a diagnosed mental disorder per the current DSM, including (but not limited to):
   - Bipolar disorder
   - Schizophrenia / schizoaffective disorder
   - PTSD
   - Depression (if sufficiently severe)
   - Anxiety disorders
   - Other qualifying conditions
   
   **Excluded:** Antisocial personality disorder and pedophilia.
   
   Evidence must include: diagnosis or treatment by a qualified mental health expert within the last 5 years.

B. The mental disorder must have been a **significant factor** in the commission of the charged offense. 
   - **Presumption:** Once a qualifying diagnosis is shown, the court shall find the disorder was a significant factor **unless** the prosecution shows by clear and convincing evidence it was not a motivating, causal, or contributing factor.
   - This is a pro-defendant presumption. The prosecution bears the burden of rebutting it.

**Part 2 — Suitability (subd. (c)):**

Even if eligible, the defendant must also be suitable:
1. A qualified mental health expert opines the symptoms would respond to treatment
2. The defendant consents and waives speedy trial (or meets the incompetency-track criteria)
3. The defendant agrees to comply with treatment
4. The defendant does not pose an unreasonable risk of danger to public safety (§ 1170.18 definition)

## Excluded offenses (subd. (d)) — § 1001.36 diversion is NOT available for:

- Murder or voluntary manslaughter
- Sex registration offenses (§ 290) except indecent exposure (§ 314)
- Rape
- Lewd/lascivious acts on child under 14 (§ 288)
- Assault with intent to commit rape, sodomy, or oral copulation (§ 220)
- Rape in concert (§ 264.1)
- Continuous sexual abuse of a child (§ 288.5)
- Specified WMD violations (§ 11418(b)/(c))

**Critical point for case #9:** If the Solano charge falls within any of these excluded categories, § 1001.36 is unavailable. If it does not (e.g., a non-violent misdemeanor or a qualifying felony not in the excluded list), § 1001.36 is available.

## Process and timeline

1. **Petition:** Defense files motion for § 1001.36 diversion, supported by qualified mental health expert declaration or report.
2. **Prima facie hearing:** Court may require prima facie showing before full hearing (informal; hearsay and offers of proof permitted).
3. **Full hearing:** Court considers prosecution's position, defense expert evidence, treatment plan, danger-to-public-safety assessment.
4. **Diversion period:**
   - Felony: up to 2 years
   - Misdemeanor: up to 1 year
5. **Completion/dismissal:** If defendant performs satisfactorily (substantial compliance with treatment, no significant new violations), court dismisses charges. Arrest deemed never to have occurred.
6. **Failure/reinstatement:** If defendant fails (new felony, new violent misdemeanor, unsatisfactory performance, grave disability), court holds hearing to reinstate proceedings, modify treatment, or pursue conservatorship.

## Post-completion benefits

Upon successful completion:
- Charges dismissed with prejudice
- Arrest deemed never to have occurred (§ 1001.9 records restriction)
- No obligation to disclose the arrest or diversion on employment applications (except peace officer positions and specific direct-question contexts)
- Records cannot be used to deny employment, benefit, license, or certificate
- Prior diversion completion may be considered by the court on a subsequent § 1001.36 petition (subd. (k))

## Retroactivity: Frahs rule

In **People v. Frahs** (2020) 9 Cal.5th 618, the California Supreme Court held that § 1001.36 applies retroactively to cases not yet final on appeal at the time of its enactment (under *In re Estrada* (1965) 63 Cal.2d 740). A defendant whose case was pending on appeal when § 1001.36 became effective in 2018 is entitled to remand for a diversion eligibility hearing if the record shows a prima facie basis for diversion.

This means:
- **If M25-00758 results in conviction:** A timely appeal + diagnosed mental disorder may support a *Frahs* remand if § 1001.36 was not considered at trial.
- **If § 1001.36 is not raised at trial:** It may constitute ineffective assistance of counsel (IAC) if the record supports eligibility.

## Defense strategy for case #9 (Solano M25-00758)

1. **Obtain charging document immediately.** Determine the specific charge and verify it is not in the § 1001.36(d) excluded list.
2. **Obtain or obtain referral for mental health evaluation.** A DSM diagnosis from a qualified mental health expert within the last 5 years is the threshold requirement. Given the steward's documented medical history (spine surgery 2021/2022, SIRVA, documented physical and psychological trauma from the 16-year orchestration), a qualifying diagnosis may be provable.
3. **Draft the significant-factor argument.** Once a diagnosis is established, the presumption under subd. (b)(2) shifts the burden to the prosecution to rebut by clear and convincing evidence.
4. **Propose a treatment program.** The court must approve the treatment plan; have an outpatient program identified.
5. **File promptly.** Diversion can be sought at any point "until adjudication." Filing before preliminary hearing or arraignment is optimal.
6. **Preserve the *Frahs* argument.** If diversion is denied at trial level, ensure it is preserved for appeal.
