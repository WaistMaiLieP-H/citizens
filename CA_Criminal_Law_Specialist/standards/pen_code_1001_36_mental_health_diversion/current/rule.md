# Cal. Penal Code § 1001.36 — Pretrial Mental Health Diversion

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_1001_36_MENTAL_HEALTH_DIVERSION
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current — operative provisions)

**(a)** On an accusatory pleading alleging a misdemeanor or felony not set forth in subdivision (d), the court may, in its discretion and after considering the positions of the defense and prosecution, grant pretrial diversion if the defendant satisfies the eligibility requirements in subdivision (b) and the court determines the defendant is suitable under the factors in subdivision (c).

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 1001.36; operative as of 2018; amended 2021, 2022)*

---

## Eligibility Requirements — § 1001.36(b)

The defendant must satisfy both:

1. **Mental disorder diagnosis** — Diagnosed with a mental disorder identified in the current DSM (including bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, PTSD, but **excluding** antisocial personality disorder and pedophilia). Evidence must include a diagnosis or treatment within the past five years by a qualified mental health expert.

2. **Mental disorder was a significant factor in the offense** — If the defendant has a qualifying diagnosis, the court shall find the disorder was a significant factor unless there is clear and convincing evidence it was not a motivating, causal, or contributing factor.

---

## Suitability Factors — § 1001.36(c)

The defendant must also satisfy all of:
1. A qualified mental health expert opines the defendant's symptoms would respond to mental health treatment;
2. Defendant consents to diversion and waives the right to a speedy trial;
3. Defendant agrees to comply with treatment conditions;
4. Defendant will not pose an unreasonable risk of danger to public safety (§ 1170.18 definition).

---

## Excluded Offenses — § 1001.36(d)

Diversion is **not available** for:
- Murder or voluntary manslaughter;
- Sex offenses requiring § 290 registration (except § 314);
- Rape;
- Lewd or lascivious act on child under 14 (§ 288);
- Assault with intent to commit rape/sodomy/oral copulation (§ 220);
- Rape or penetration in concert (§ 264.1);
- Continuous sexual abuse of child (§ 288.5);
- Specified WMD violations (§ 11418(b)/(c)).

---

## Diversion Period and Outcome — §§ 1001.36(f), (h)

- **Maximum diversion period:** 2 years (felony); 1 year (misdemeanor)
- **Successful completion:** Court shall dismiss the charges. The arrest shall be deemed never to have occurred and records shall be restricted per § 1001.9.
- **Record seal:** The restricted record shall not be used for employment, benefit, license, or certificate purposes without the defendant's consent.
- **Failure to comply:** Court may terminate diversion and resume criminal proceedings after a hearing.

---

## Plain English

If you are charged with a crime and have a diagnosable mental disorder that contributed to the offense, you may be able to have prosecution postponed while you complete mental health treatment. If you complete the treatment program successfully, the charges are dismissed and the arrest is treated as if it never happened. The court has discretion to grant diversion — it is not automatic — and serious violent and sex offenses are categorically excluded. The law is designed to route people with treatable mental health conditions out of the criminal justice system and into treatment.
