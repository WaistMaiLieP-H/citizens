# People v. Toledo (2001) 26 Cal.4th 221

**Citation:** People v. Toledo (2001) 26 Cal.4th 221, 109 Cal.Rptr.2d 315, 26 P.3d 1051
**Court:** Supreme Court of California
**Decided:** 2001
**Statutes construed:** Pen. Code § 422

## Facts

Toledo made a threatening statement to his wife during a domestic dispute. The statement was arguably conditional — it contained language that made it less than an absolute unconditional threat. Toledo was convicted of making criminal threats under § 422. The primary appellate issue was whether a conditional threat can constitute a "criminal threat" under § 422, given the statute's requirement that the threat be "so unequivocal, unconditional, immediate, and specific" as to convey gravity of purpose.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **The "UUIS" test (unequivocal, unconditional, immediate, and specific) is the core standard for § 422.** The statute requires that the threat, on its face and under the circumstances in which it is made, convey a gravity of purpose and an immediate prospect of execution. Each of the four adjectives — unequivocal, unconditional, immediate, and specific — must be assessed in context.

2. **Conditional threats can satisfy § 422.** The word "unconditional" in § 422 does not absolutely bar all conditional threats from the statute's reach. A threat may be conditional in form yet still satisfy the UUIS test where:
   - The condition is not a genuine limiting condition but merely a linguistic formulation
   - The overall circumstances, including tone, context, and relationship between the parties, make the threat unequivocal and specific
   - The condition could not be avoided by the victim (e.g., the condition has already occurred or is inevitable)

3. **"Sustained fear" requires more than a transitory emotional response.** The victim's fear must be more than momentary or fleeting to qualify as "sustained." Courts look at: whether the victim took protective measures (changed locks, called police, left the area); whether the fear continued after the immediate communication; and whether the fear was objectively reasonable given the circumstances and the relationship between the parties.

4. **Context of the relationship is highly relevant.** A threat made in the context of an ongoing pattern of violence or intimidation carries far greater weight than an isolated outburst between strangers. Prior acts of violence by the defendant toward the victim make it more likely that the victim's fear was reasonable and sustained.

5. **The defendant need not intend to carry out the threat.** Section 422 expressly states that the crime is complete "even if there is no intent of actually carrying it out." The specific intent required is the intent that the statement be taken as a threat — not the intent to execute it.

## Why this is load-bearing

*Toledo* is the California Supreme Court's definitive statement on § 422 elements. It:
- Establishes that conditional threats can qualify
- Clarifies the "sustained fear" analysis
- Confirms that context and relationship history are relevant
- Reaffirms that no intent to carry out is required

For the defense against a § 422 charge, *Toledo* is used to argue that the allegedly threatening statement did not satisfy the UUIS test in context — not just in isolation.

## Relevance to the steward's cases

If the steward faces a § 422 charge arising from the orchestrated scheme:
- *Toledo* establishes that the court looks at context, not just the words. If the statement was made in a context of documented conflict, the prosecution will argue context supports the charge; the defense argues context shows the statement was not a true threat.
- *Toledo*'s "sustained fear" requirement can be challenged by showing the alleged victim did not act as if in sustained fear (continued normal activity, did not call police, did not seek a restraining order until much later).

If the steward is the complainant:
- *Toledo* supports a § 422 complaint for threats communicated during the orchestration, if the threats were unequivocal, specific, and caused sustained fear.
