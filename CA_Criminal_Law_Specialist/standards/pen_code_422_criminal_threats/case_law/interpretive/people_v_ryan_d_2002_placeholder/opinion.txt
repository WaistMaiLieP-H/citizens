IN RE RYAN D.
100 Cal.App.4th 854 (2002)
California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District
Decided: 2002

---

FACTS:

Ryan D., a minor, made threatening statements at school that were alleged to constitute criminal threats under § 422. The primary issue on appeal was whether the statements caused the alleged victim to be in "sustained fear" as required by the statute.

---

HOLDING:

§ 422 "SUSTAINED FEAR" ELEMENT:

1. **"Sustained fear" requires more than momentary, fleeting, or transitory fear.** To be "sustained" within the meaning of § 422, the victim's fear must persist for a period of time longer than a brief or passing emotional reaction. Courts contrast "sustained" fear with fear lasting only a moment.

2. **Several minutes of fear can be sufficient.** The statute does not require the fear to last hours or days — even fear lasting a matter of minutes has been found to constitute "sustained" fear in some circumstances, depending on the severity of the threat and the victim's circumstances.

3. **The reasonableness of the fear is assessed objectively.** The fear must be such that a reasonable person in the victim's position would have experienced sustained fear. This objective test prevents liability where the alleged victim's fear was idiosyncratic or unreasonably prolonged.

4. **Evidence of protective behavior supports the sustained fear finding.** Where the victim called police, sought a protective order, changed routines, or otherwise took protective action in response to the threat, these behaviors support the inference that the fear was genuine and sustained.

5. **Context of a prior relationship matters.** Where the defendant and victim have a history of conflict, violence, or intimidation, the victim's fear is more likely to be both genuine and sustained than where the parties are strangers with no prior history.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

DEFENSIVE — If the steward faces a § 422 charge, attack the sustained fear element by showing:
- The alleged victim continued normal activities without apparent disruption after the alleged threat
- No protective measures were taken until much later (no police report, no restraining order sought promptly)
- The alleged fear was not objectively reasonable given the circumstances and relationship

COMPLAINANT — If the steward is filing a § 422 complaint for threats received:
- Document all protective measures taken after the threat (changed locks, moved, changed phone numbers, sought legal advice) — these are evidence of sustained fear
- Document the ongoing pattern of fear — not just the moment of the threat but the continuing impact
- Establish the prior history of the parties (the 16-year orchestration) to show the objective reasonableness and duration of fear
