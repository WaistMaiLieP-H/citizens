# In re Ryan D. (2002) 100 Cal.App.4th 854

**Citation:** In re Ryan D. (2002) 100 Cal.App.4th 854, 123 Cal.Rptr.2d 193
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District
**Decided:** 2002
**Statutes construed:** Pen. Code § 422 — "sustained fear" element

## Facts

Ryan D., a minor, made threatening statements at school that were alleged to constitute criminal threats under § 422. The primary issue on appeal was whether the statements caused the alleged victim to be in "sustained fear" as required by the statute.

## Holding

The Court of Appeal held:

1. **"Sustained fear" requires more than momentary, fleeting, or transitory fear.** To be "sustained" within the meaning of § 422, the victim's fear must persist for a period of time longer than a brief or passing emotional reaction. Courts contrast "sustained" fear with fear that lasts only a moment.

2. **Several minutes of fear can be sufficient.** The court acknowledged that the statute does not require the fear to last for hours or days — even fear lasting a matter of minutes has been found to constitute "sustained" fear in some circumstances, depending on the severity of the threat and the victim's circumstances.

3. **The reasonableness of the fear is assessed objectively.** The fear must be such that a reasonable person in the victim's position would have experienced sustained fear. This objective test prevents liability where the alleged victim's fear was idiosyncratic or unreasonably prolonged.

4. **Evidence of protective behavior supports the sustained fear finding.** Where the victim called police, sought a protective order, changed routines, or otherwise took protective action in response to the threat, these behaviors support the inference that the fear was genuine and sustained.

5. **Context of a prior relationship matters.** Where the defendant and the victim have a history of conflict, violence, or intimidation, the victim's fear is more likely to be both genuine and sustained than where the parties are strangers with no prior history.

## Why this is load-bearing

*In re Ryan D.* is the leading appellate authority on the "sustained fear" element. It establishes that:
- The element has a real duration requirement (not momentary)
- But the duration is relatively short — minutes can suffice in the right circumstances
- Objective reasonableness is the test
- Protective behavior is evidence of sustained fear

## Defense use

If the steward faces a § 422 charge, attack the sustained fear element by showing:
- The alleged victim continued normal activities without apparent disruption
- No protective measures were taken (no police report, no restraining order sought) until much later
- The alleged fear was not objectively reasonable given the circumstances

## Complainant use

If the steward is filing a § 422 complaint for threats received:
- Document all protective measures taken after the threat (changed locks, moved, changed phone numbers, sought legal advice)
- Document the ongoing pattern of fear — not just the moment of the threat but the continuing fear it caused
- Establish the prior history of the parties (the 16-year orchestration) to show the reasonableness and duration of fear
