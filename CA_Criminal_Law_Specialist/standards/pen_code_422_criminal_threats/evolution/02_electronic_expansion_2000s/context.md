# Context — Electronic Communications Expansion

The electronic expansion of § 422 reflects the transformation of threat behavior itself. By 2000, harassment and threats had moved substantially online — email, early social media, and cell phone text messages. The 1988 statute's "verbally, in writing" language was not obviously inclusive of email. Courts needed clarity.

The amendment's practical significance is that modern threat cases — including any case where a party claims they received threatening messages — are analyzed under the same four-part test regardless of medium. The medium (text, email, in person) is legally irrelevant. What matters is: was the threat unequivocal? Unconditional? Did it convey an immediate prospect of execution? Was it specific enough to constitute a credible threat of death or GBI?

## Diff from prior
Prior: Verbal and written threats only. After: All electronic communications covered. The four-part test is medium-neutral.
