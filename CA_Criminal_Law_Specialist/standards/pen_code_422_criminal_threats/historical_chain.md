# Historical Chain — Penal Code § 422 (Criminal Threats)

## Origin

**1988 — § 422 enacted as "terrorist threats" statute**
California Legislature enacted the original § 422 as part of a wave of anti-terrorism legislation. The original statute targeted explicit unconditional threats to commit violent crimes against specific persons. The offense was framed narrowly — directed at stereotyped "terrorist" conduct but reaching far short of the modern scope.

**Historical loss driving enactment:** Legislative history reflects concern about rising political violence and targeted intimidation campaigns. The term "terrorist threats" reflected the political climate of the late 1980s, not a limitation to international terrorism.

## Material Amendments

**1994 — Legislature broadens to cover "electronic communication"**
Early amendment added electronic communication as a medium by which a threat could be conveyed, anticipating the rise of internet and digital communications. This amendment confirmed that § 422 covers threats delivered by email, text message, and later social media platforms — not just in-person or telephone threats.

**2000 — "Terrorist threats" renamed "criminal threats"**
The Legislature renamed § 422 from "terrorist threats" to "criminal threats" to avoid confusion with federal anti-terrorism law and to reflect the statute's ordinary-crime function. This was a title change only — no substantive amendment to the elements.

**2001 — People v. Toledo (2001) 26 Cal.4th 221 (definitive judicial construction)**
The California Supreme Court definitively construed all five elements of § 422 in *Toledo*, establishing the UUIS test (unconditional, unequivocal, immediate, specific) for the "so unequivocal, unconditional, immediate, and specific" element. *Toledo* remains the controlling authority on every element of § 422.

**2002 — In re Ryan D. (2002) 100 Cal.App.4th 854 (sustained fear duration)**
The Court of Appeal clarified the "sustained fear" element, establishing that fear must persist beyond a momentary reaction but that even minutes can suffice depending on circumstances. *Ryan D.* is the leading authority on the temporal component of sustained fear.

**2006 — Subd. (b) added: public official aggravating factor**
The Legislature added subdivision (b), which elevates the offense to a felony (no wobbler treatment) where the threat is directed at a public official in the performance of official duties. This amendment did not change the elements of the offense under subd. (a).

**2010s — Digital communications expansion in practice**
Courts applied § 422 to threats delivered via social media, text messages, voicemails, and internet postings, confirming that the "electronic communication" language encompasses all digital media. The underlying element analysis from *Toledo* applies equally regardless of medium.

## Current Statute Version

The "criminal threats" statute as currently codified (2025) reflects the 2006 text. All five elements from the 2001 *Toledo* construction remain valid and controlling. No substantive amendment since 2006.

**Consistency check:** *Toledo* (2001) and *Ryan D.* (2002) were decided under the pre-2006 statute, but the 2006 amendment (adding subd. (b) for public officials) did not modify the subd. (a) elements they construed. Both cases apply directly to current § 422(a) litigation.

## Historical Loss Anchors

| Year | Event | Loss Pattern |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 1988 | Enactment | Legislature responding to targeted intimidation with no criminal remedy |
| 1994 | Digital amendment | Threats delivered by electronic means evaded older statutes |
| 2000 | Rename | "Terrorist" label caused jury confusion in ordinary stalking/domestic threat cases |
| 2001 | *Toledo* | Courts applying inconsistent tests to conditional threats; Supreme Court settled the framework |
| 2002 | *Ryan D.* | Acquittals in school threat cases where fear was brief; appellate court set minimum duration standard |
| 2006 | Public official amendment | Officials receiving threats had no elevated remedy; Legislature created felony track |
