# Cal. Penal Code § 422 — Criminal Threats

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_422_CRIMINAL_THREATS
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** Any person who **willfully threatens** to commit a crime which will result in death or great bodily injury to another person, with the **specific intent** that the statement, made verbally, in writing, or by means of an **electronic communication device**, is to be taken as a threat, even if there is no intent of actually carrying it out, which, on its face and under the circumstances in which it is made, is so **unequivocal, unconditional, immediate, and specific** as to convey to the person threatened, a gravity of purpose and an immediate prospect of execution of the threat, and thereby causes that person reasonably to be in **sustained fear** for their own safety or for their immediate family's safety, shall be punished by imprisonment in the county jail not to exceed one year, or by imprisonment in the state prison.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 422; "wobbler")*

---

## Elements — *People v. Toledo*, 26 Cal.4th 221 (2001)

1. The defendant willfully **threatened** to commit a crime that would result in death or great bodily injury;
2. The defendant made the threat with the **specific intent** that it be taken as a threat;
3. The threat, on its face and under the circumstances, was so **unequivocal, unconditional, immediate, and specific** that it conveyed a gravity of purpose and immediate prospect of execution;
4. The threat caused the victim to be in **sustained fear** for their own safety or that of their immediate family;
5. The victim's fear was **reasonable** under the circumstances.

---

## Specific Intent Requirement

The defendant must specifically intend the statement to be received as a threat. A statement made in a heated argument that objectively sounds threatening but was not intended as such may not satisfy § 422. However, the defendant need not intend to carry out the threat — only to communicate it as one.

---

## "Unequivocal, Unconditional, Immediate, Specific"

Courts apply this standard to determine whether the communication, viewed objectively, was sufficiently threatening:
- **Conditional threats** ("If you do X, I'll kill you") may or may not qualify depending on context — true conditions vs. threatening ultimatums;
- **Vague threats** ("You'll pay for this") generally do not qualify — the threat must be specific enough that the victim knows what is threatened and can reasonably fear it;
- Courts look at the totality of circumstances: prior threats, the parties' relationship, accompanying actions.

---

## "Sustained Fear"

The victim's fear must be sustained — more than momentary. A fleeting moment of alarm is not sufficient. Courts assess:
- How long the victim experienced fear;
- Whether the victim changed their behavior (installed security, sought shelter, called police) in response;
- The reasonableness of the fear given the circumstances.

---

## Electronic Communication Included

Threats made by phone, text, email, social media, or any electronic communication device are explicitly covered. The method of delivery does not affect culpability.

---

## Punishment — Wobbler

- **Misdemeanor:** County jail up to 1 year
- **Felony:** State prison

The decision to charge as a felony or misdemeanor typically depends on: the specificity of the threat, the defendant's criminal history, the relationship between the parties, and whether accompanying conduct suggested a genuine intent to carry out the threat.

---

## Plain English

Threatening to kill or seriously injure someone is a crime — even if you never intended to follow through — if the threat was specific enough, you meant it to be taken as a threat, the person reasonably believed it, and they were genuinely afraid. Texts, emails, and social media posts all count. The fear must last more than a moment. A vague "you'll regret this" doesn't meet the standard; "I will kill you on Friday when you leave work" likely does.
