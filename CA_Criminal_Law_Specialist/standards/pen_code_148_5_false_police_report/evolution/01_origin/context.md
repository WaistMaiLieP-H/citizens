# Context — § 148.5 Origin

## The wound

False crime reports divert law enforcement resources from real crimes and can cause innocent people to be investigated, arrested, or detained. The early 20th-century California legislature recognized this harm after documented cases of people filing false robbery or assault reports to fraudulently collect insurance, to harm business competitors, or to retaliate against neighbors.

## The knowledge-of-falsity requirement

The specific intent element — "knowing the report to be false" — is the central defense element in § 148.5 prosecutions. A person who sincerely believes a crime occurred, even if mistaken, cannot be convicted under § 148.5. This is not a general intent crime. The prosecution must prove the person knew, at the time of the report, that no crime had actually occurred.

## Steward relevance

§ 148.5 is the criminal offense applicable to fabricated police reports — specifically, reports where the reporter knew no crime had actually occurred. In Case #7's context, the OPD forensic audit finding (6/2/2009 report missing, 6/11/2009 OPD report fabrication finding) is directly relevant to § 148.5 analysis of counter-claims. § 148.5 is a defensive weapon: if the person who filed a report against the steward knew the report was false, they committed § 148.5.
