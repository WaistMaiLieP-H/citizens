# Cal. Penal Code § 148.5 — False Report of Criminal Offense

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_148_5_FALSE_POLICE_REPORT
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** Every person who reports to any peace officer listed in § 830.1 or § 830.2, or subdivision (a) of § 830.33, the Attorney General, or a district attorney that a felony or misdemeanor has been committed, **knowing the report to be false**, is guilty of a **misdemeanor**.

**(b)** Every person who reports to any other peace officer as defined in Chapter 4.5 of Title 3 of Part 2 that a felony or misdemeanor has been committed, knowing the report to be false, is guilty of a misdemeanor if: (1) the false information is given while the peace officer is engaged in the performance of their duties, and (2) the person providing the information knows or should have known that the person receiving the information is a peace officer.

**(c)** Reports to state or local agency employees assigned to accept reports from citizens — misdemeanor with similar scienter requirements.

**(d)** Reports to a grand jury that a felony or misdemeanor has been committed, knowing the report to be false, is guilty of a misdemeanor. This subdivision does not preclude a charge of perjury or contempt for reports made under oath before a grand jury.

**(e)** This section **does not apply** to reports made by persons required by statute to report known or suspected instances of child abuse, dependent adult abuse, or elder abuse.

**(f)** This section applies to false reports that a firearm has been lost or stolen (with knowledge the report is false).

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 148.5)*

---

## Elements of the Offense

1. **The defendant made a report** to a qualifying peace officer, law enforcement agency, or grand jury;
2. The report stated that a felony or misdemeanor had been committed;
3. **The report was false**;
4. **The defendant knew the report to be false** at the time of making it.

The scienter element is critical: the defendant must **know** the report is false. A mistaken belief that a crime occurred is not a violation of § 148.5.

---

## Punishment

Misdemeanor — punishable by:
- County jail up to 6 months;
- Fine up to $1,000;
- Or both.

---

## Exceptions — Mandatory Reporters

The statute expressly exempts mandatory reporters acting under their statutory reporting obligations (child abuse, elder abuse, dependent adult abuse). A mandated reporter who makes a good-faith report that turns out to be inaccurate does not violate § 148.5.

---

## Interaction with Perjury and Other Statutes

- False statements made under oath to a grand jury may also be charged as perjury (§ 118);
- False personation (§ 529) and identity theft (§ 530.5) may be charged when the false report involves false identity claims;
- Making a false police report does not immunize the reporter from civil liability for the consequences of the false report.

---

## Plain English

Calling the police and knowingly filing a false report that a crime occurred is a misdemeanor in California. The key element is knowledge: you must know the report is false, not merely be mistaken. False reports of lost or stolen firearms are specifically included. Mandatory reporters (teachers, doctors, social workers) who make good-faith abuse reports are protected even if the report is later found inaccurate.
