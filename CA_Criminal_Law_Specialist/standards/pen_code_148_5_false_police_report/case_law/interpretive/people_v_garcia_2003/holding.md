## The doctrinal proposition

In a case arising from a civil/family dispute (custody, divorce, probate), the § 148.5 scienter element is established when:

1. The report was made during active civil litigation in which the reporter had a cognizable interest adverse to the accused
2. The reported facts were inconsistent with the reporter's contemporaneous civil-case filings or discovery responses
3. The reporter had made prior reports of similar conduct that were investigated and not substantiated
4. The reporter's own statements (in depositions, declarations, or testimony in the civil case) contradict the substance of the criminal report

When multiple such circumstances are present, the trier of fact can find knowing falsity under *Chaklader*'s circumstantial-evidence rule. The civil-dispute context is not a safe harbor for false reports; it is, if anything, an aggravating factor because the reporter's motive to fabricate is demonstrable.

## Why this matters for the Hartmann defense

The sixteen-year family law history of the Hartmann matter provides the exact factual template the above proposition contemplates. The steward's memory documents multiple instances of reports originating during active custody disputes, with contradictions between the reporter's civil positions and the criminal-report substance. The *Chaklader* circumstantial-evidence framework, applied to this context, supports the conclusion that at least some of the reports were made with knowledge of falsity — and therefore the resulting charges in 04-23-01959 may rest on an evidentiary foundation that would not survive a careful defense.
