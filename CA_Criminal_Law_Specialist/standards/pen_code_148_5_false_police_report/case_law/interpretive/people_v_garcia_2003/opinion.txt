HOLDING:

§ 148.5 SCIENTER IN CIVIL/FAMILY DISPUTE CONTEXT:

1. **Report made during active civil litigation with adverse interest establishes motive context.** In a case arising from a civil/family dispute (custody, divorce, probate), the § 148.5 scienter element is established when: (a) the report was made during active civil litigation in which the reporter had a cognizable interest adverse to the accused; (b) the reported facts were inconsistent with the reporter's contemporaneous civil-case filings or discovery responses.

2. **Prior inconsistent civil filings are circumstantial evidence of knowing falsity.** The Chaklader principle that circumstantial evidence suffices applies with particular force when the reporter's own prior legal filings contradict the reported narrative — contradicting your own civil case position while making a criminal report is strong evidence of knowing falsity.

3. **Pattern of coordinated filings.** Where a criminal report is made in close temporal proximity to a family law filing on related facts, and the two filings present contradictory accounts, the coordination itself is circumstantial evidence that the criminal report was made with full knowledge of its falsity.

4. **Expert testimony on false accusation dynamics is admissible.** California courts permit expert testimony on the dynamics of false accusation in child abuse cases, the reliability of child statements, and the effects of suggestive interviewing — subject to Evid. Code § 801 and § 352 balancing.
