# Pen. Code § 1054.1 — Prosecution's Discovery Obligations

## What this statute does

Section 1054.1 is California's statutory codification of the prosecution's pre-trial discovery obligations, enacted in 1990 as part of Proposition 115. It requires the prosecution to disclose six categories of material to the defense. It also incorporates — but does not supersede — the constitutional Brady/Giglio obligations from federal law.

## The six categories (§ 1054.1(a)-(f))

### (a) Witness list
Names and addresses of all persons the prosecution **intends to call** at trial. This is prospective — the prosecution must update the list as new witnesses are added.

**Enforcement:** If the prosecution fails to timely disclose witnesses, the defense may seek a continuance, exclusion of the witness, or other sanctions under § 1054.5.

### (b) Defendant's statements
All statements made by the defendant — written, recorded, or oral — that are in the prosecution's or investigating agencies' possession. This includes:
- Written confessions or admissions
- Recorded police interviews
- Oral statements reported by officers in their reports
- Statements to third parties if known to the prosecution

**Relevance for case #7:** Any statements attributed to the steward in the APD incident report for 6/16/2023 must be disclosed. Review the report carefully — any oral statements attributed to the steward that are not accurate must be challenged.

### (c) Real evidence
All **relevant** real evidence seized or obtained during the investigation. Key word: "relevant." The prosecution controls what it deems relevant, but material exculpatory evidence cannot be withheld as "irrelevant" — Brady obligations apply independently.

**Relevance for case #7:** Physical evidence: photographs of the alleged injury (if any), any forensic evidence, the scene documentation.

### (d) Material witness prior convictions
The existence (not the specifics) of any felony conviction of a **material witness** whose credibility is likely to be critical. The prosecution need not disclose minor convictions or convictions of witnesses whose credibility is not critical.

**Relevance for case #7:** If the complaining witness (Christina or another) has a prior felony conviction, and her credibility is central, that conviction must be disclosed.

### (e) Exculpatory evidence — the Brady trigger
Section 1054.1(e) codifies the **Brady obligation** in California state law. "Any exculpatory evidence" must be disclosed.

**Brady v. Maryland (1963) 373 U.S. 83** established the constitutional baseline: the prosecution must disclose all material evidence favorable to the accused. "Material" means there is a reasonable probability that disclosure would have changed the outcome.

California's § 1054.1(e) uses the word "any" exculpatory evidence — arguably broader than Brady's "material" standard. Courts have sometimes held California's standard is at least as protective as Brady.

**Exculpatory evidence in case #7 includes:**
- Any police notes or CPS notes reflecting inconsistencies in the child's account
- Any prior reports involving the same parties where similar allegations were made and withdrawn or found unsubstantiated
- Any evidence that the initial report was motivated by the family court proceedings
- Any evidence of coordination between the reporting party and Christina's family law attorney
- APD internal communications about the case

### (f) Witness statements and expert reports
All relevant written or recorded statements of witnesses the prosecution **intends to call**, including expert reports, examination results, and test results.

**Relevance for case #7:**
- The child's forensic interview transcript and recording — must be disclosed if prosecution intends to call the child
- Any medical expert report on the alleged injury
- Any CPS assessment reports

## Brady + Giglio: the constitutional overlay

Section 1054.1 is the California statutory framework. Layered on top are the federal constitutional obligations:

**Brady v. Maryland (1963) 373 U.S. 83:**
- Prosecution must disclose all material evidence favorable to the accused
- Applies even if the defense does not specifically request it
- "Material" = reasonable probability that disclosure would have changed the outcome
- Applies to exculpatory AND impeachment evidence

**Giglio v. United States (1972) 405 U.S. 150:**
- Extends Brady to impeachment evidence
- If a prosecution witness has received a promise of leniency or other benefit in exchange for testimony, that must be disclosed
- Applies to agreements, whether formal or informal

**United States v. Agurs (1976) 427 U.S. 97:**
- Clarified materiality standard: the test is whether the omitted evidence creates a reasonable doubt that otherwise would not exist
- Prosecution cannot hide behind a failure-to-request defense once Brady evidence is known to exist

## The Pitchess motion — officer misconduct records

For cases involving police officer testimony, the defense's discovery rights extend beyond § 1054.1 to the Pitchess motion procedure (Evid. Code §§ 1043-1047). A Pitchess motion allows the defense to access otherwise confidential peace officer personnel records showing prior complaints of dishonesty, excessive force, or other misconduct.

**For case #7:** If APD officers testified at the preliminary hearing or will testify at trial, a Pitchess motion should be filed. Given the documented APD forensic audit findings (10 violations), there is a basis for the motion.

## Enforcement — § 1054.5

If the prosecution violates § 1054.1, the court may:
1. Order immediate disclosure
2. Grant a continuance
3. Prohibit the prosecution from presenting the evidence or witness
4. In egregious cases, dismiss the charges

A Brady violation — if it was "material" — can support a new trial motion (Pen. Code § 1181) or a habeas corpus petition.

## Affirmative defense duty — what the defense must do

**Defense must make a demand.** Section 1054.1 runs to the prosecution, but to be safe:
- File a formal written demand for discovery under § 1054.1(a)-(f) early in the case
- Specifically demand: (1) the child's forensic interview recording and transcript; (2) all CPS/DCFS reports and referral documents; (3) all APD incident reports and call logs for 6/16/2023; (4) any prior reports involving the same parties; (5) any expert reports; (6) all statements attributed to the defendant

**Document everything.** If the prosecution provides discovery late or incompletely, document the timeline for a potential § 1054.5 sanction motion.
