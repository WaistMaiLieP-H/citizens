# Cal. Penal Code § 1054.1 — Prosecutorial Discovery Obligations

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_1054_1_DISCOVERY
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

The prosecuting attorney **shall disclose** to the defendant or his or her attorney all of the following materials and information, **if it is in the possession of the prosecuting attorney or if the prosecuting attorney knows it to be in the possession of the investigating agencies**:

**(a)** The names and addresses of persons the prosecutor intends to call as witnesses at trial.

**(b)** Statements of all defendants.

**(c)** All relevant real evidence seized or obtained as a part of the investigation of the offenses charged.

**(d)** The existence of a felony conviction of any material witness whose credibility is likely to be critical to the outcome of the trial.

**(e)** Any exculpatory evidence.

**(f)** Relevant written or recorded statements of witnesses or reports of the statements of witnesses whom the prosecutor intends to call at trial, including any reports or statements of experts made in conjunction with the case, including the results of physical or mental examinations, scientific tests, experiments, or comparisons which the prosecutor intends to offer in evidence at the trial.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 1054.1)*

---

## Scope — Constructive Possession

The duty extends to materials in the possession of the prosecuting attorney AND materials the prosecutor **knows** are in the possession of investigating agencies. A prosecutor cannot escape disclosure obligations by remaining willfully ignorant of what investigating agencies possess.

---

## Mandatory Nature

The statute uses the word "shall" — disclosure is not discretionary. The prosecution must produce all listed categories without a defense motion unless the court has ordered protective measures.

---

## Relationship to Brady and Pitchess

**Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963):** Federal constitutional disclosure requirement — the prosecution must disclose any material exculpatory evidence (evidence that is favorable to the accused and material to guilt or punishment). Brady applies independently of § 1054.1 and cannot be contracted around by state statute.

**Pitchess motions (Evid. Code §§ 1043–1047):** Law enforcement personnel records (complaints against officers) are confidential and must be obtained through a Pitchess motion — not through § 1054.1 discovery. The statutory discovery framework does not override Pitchess confidentiality.

---

## Plain English

In a California criminal case, the prosecution must give the defense: the names of all witnesses it plans to call, defendant statements, physical evidence from the investigation, records of any key witness's felony convictions, all exculpatory evidence, and witness statements from witnesses the prosecutor plans to call. The duty covers anything the DA knows about, including materials sitting with investigating police agencies. Missing or withholding exculpatory evidence can violate both this statute and the Constitution (Brady). Enforcement is handled under § 1054.5.
