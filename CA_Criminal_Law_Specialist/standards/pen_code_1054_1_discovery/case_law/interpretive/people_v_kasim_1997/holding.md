## The doctrinal framework: § 1054.5 sanctions for § 1054.1 violations

When the prosecution fails to comply with § 1054.1, the defense must act promptly to enforce the obligation. Section 1054.5 provides:

### The hierarchy of sanctions

California courts have held that sanctions for § 1054.1 violations should be proportional to the prejudice caused:

1. **Continuance (least severe):** If the violation is discovered before trial, a continuance to allow time to review the late-disclosed evidence is the typical first remedy. This remedies most violations without affecting the outcome.

2. **Exclusion of the evidence or witness:** If the prosecution failed to disclose a witness or evidence and the violation was willful or the late disclosure cannot be cured by continuance, the court may exclude the evidence or bar the witness.

3. **Mistrial (if discovered mid-trial):** If the violation is discovered during trial and cannot be cured, a mistrial may be appropriate.

4. **Dismissal (rarest sanction):** Where the violation is willful, the suppressed evidence is critical to the defense, and a lesser remedy cannot cure the prejudice, the court may dismiss the charges. Dismissal is reserved for the most egregious Brady/Giglio violations.

### The "willful" vs. "inadvertent" distinction

The prosecution's intent affects the remedy analysis:
- **Inadvertent:** Typically remedied by continuance and disclosure. Courts are reluctant to impose serious sanctions for genuine mistakes.
- **Willful:** Courts are much more likely to impose serious sanctions — exclusion, mistrial, or dismissal — where the prosecution deliberately withheld favorable evidence.

### What constitutes "prejudice"

For a § 1054.1 violation to require reversal (rather than harmless error), the defense must show the evidence was material — *Brady*'s reasonable-probability-of-different-outcome standard. California courts apply this materiality standard to § 1054.1 violations as well.

### Defense demand is not required but is essential strategically

Although the statutory obligation is affirmative and does not technically require a defense demand, making a specific written demand:
- Creates a record of the prosecution's knowledge of the obligation
- Strengthens any subsequent claim that noncompliance was willful
- Establishes the timeline for any sanction motion
- Identifies specific categories of Brady material the prosecution must search for
