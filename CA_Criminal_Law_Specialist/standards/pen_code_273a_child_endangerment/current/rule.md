# Cal. Penal Code § 273a — Child Endangerment

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_273A_CHILD_ENDANGERMENT
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** Any person who, under **circumstances or conditions likely to produce great bodily harm or death**, willfully causes or permits any child to suffer, or inflicts thereon unjustifiable physical pain or mental suffering, or having the care or custody of any child, willfully causes or permits the person or health of that child to be injured, or willfully causes or permits that child to be placed in a situation where his or her person or health is endangered, shall be punished by imprisonment in a county jail not exceeding one year, or in the state prison for **2, 4, or 6 years**.

**(b)** Any person who, under **circumstances or conditions other than those likely to produce great bodily harm or death**, willfully causes or permits any child to suffer, or inflicts thereon unjustifiable physical pain or mental suffering, or having the care or custody of any child, willfully causes or permits the person or health of that child to be injured, or willfully causes or permits that child to be placed in a situation where his or her person or health may be endangered, is guilty of a **misdemeanor**.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 273a)*

---

## Two Tiers — Same Conduct, Different Risk Level

| Tier | Risk Level | Punishment |
|------|------------|-----------|
| § 273a(a) | Circumstances likely to produce great bodily harm or death | Felony: 2/4/6 years state prison OR misdemeanor county jail ≤1 year |
| § 273a(b) | Circumstances other than likely to produce GBH/death | Misdemeanor only |

The key distinction is **objective risk level** — not whether the child was actually harmed. A child left in a hot car (likely GBH/death risk) is a § 273a(a) case; a child left alone briefly in a safe environment (lower risk) may be § 273a(b) or no crime.

---

## Elements

1. A person **willfully** caused or permitted a child to suffer or be injured, or placed the child in a dangerous situation;
2. The child's person or health was endangered or the defendant had custody and allowed injury;
3. **Under circumstances likely (or not likely) to produce great bodily harm or death**.

The "willfully" element does not require intent to harm the child — it requires a voluntary act or failure to act where there was a duty.

---

## § 273a(c) — Mandatory Probation Conditions

If probation is granted:
- Minimum 48 months probation;
- Criminal court protective order;
- Mandatory child abuser treatment counseling;
- Abstinence from drugs/alcohol if drug-related;
- Waiver of conditions only on record findings.

---

## Common Contexts

- Child left in hot vehicle (§ 273a(a) — GBH/death circumstances);
- Child exposed to domestic violence in the home;
- Child left in care of known drug user or violent person;
- Child present during drug manufacturing or sales;
- Caretaker failing to obtain necessary medical care;
- Emotional or psychological abuse by caretaker.

---

## Interaction with § 273d (Corporal Punishment)

Section 273a covers endangerment and non-corporal abuse (placing a child at risk, permitting suffering). Section 273d covers willful infliction of cruel or inhuman corporal punishment resulting in a traumatic condition. Both may be charged when physical abuse results in injury.

---

## Plain English

Child endangerment under § 273a is a "wobbler" (felony or misdemeanor) when the circumstances were likely to cause serious harm or death, and a misdemeanor when the risk was lower. You don't have to actually hurt a child — putting a child in a dangerous situation is enough. The more dangerous the circumstances, the more serious the charge. Parents and caregivers have affirmative duties to protect children in their care; failing to act when a child is endangered is as culpable as acting to endanger them.
