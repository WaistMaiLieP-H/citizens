# Reasoning — Cal. Penal Code § 273a (Child Endangerment)

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_273A_CHILD_ENDANGERMENT
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why This Standard Exists

Section 273a reflects the Legislature's recognition that children in the care and custody of adults are uniquely vulnerable — they cannot protect themselves, remove themselves from dangerous situations, or report abuse to authorities without adult assistance. The duty of a caretaker is affirmative: not only to refrain from harming the child but to actively protect the child from harm.

The statute's design — covering both active endangerment ("causes or permits any child to suffer") and passive failure to protect ("willfully causes or permits that child to be placed in a situation where his or her person or health is endangered") — reflects this affirmative duty. A parent who knowingly leaves a child in the care of a person they know to be violent is as culpable under § 273a as a person who directly endangers the child.

---

## The Two-Tier Structure — Risk-Based, Not Harm-Based

The misdemeanor (§ 273a(b)) / felony (§ 273a(a)) distinction is based on the objective risk level of the circumstances — not on whether actual harm occurred. This risk-based approach reflects two legislative judgments:

1. **Prevention:** Imposing criminal consequences for dangerous situations before harm occurs creates incentives for caretakers to remove children from dangerous environments before a tragedy occurs.

2. **Evidence:** Many serious child endangerment situations are detected before the child is actually injured. If criminal liability required proof of actual harm, many genuine endangerment situations would go uncharged.

The "circumstances likely to produce great bodily harm or death" standard is objective — what would a reasonable person conclude about the risk, not what the defendant believed.

---

## Why Domestic Violence Exposure Is Charged Under § 273a

Exposing children to domestic violence — even when the child is not physically targeted — is charged under § 273a because the Legislature recognized that:
- Children witnessing domestic violence suffer serious psychological harm;
- Children present during DV episodes are at risk of being accidentally injured;
- Using children as leverage or weapons in domestic disputes endangers the child's emotional and psychological health.

A child who witnesses a parent being beaten is "permitted to suffer" within the meaning of § 273a even if the child is not physically touched.

---

## Application in Practice

Section 273a is frequently charged in cases involving:
- Children left in hot vehicles (§ 273a(a) — likely GBH/death circumstances);
- Children exposed to parental methamphetamine use or manufacturing;
- Children present during or witnessing domestic violence;
- Children with unmet medical needs (failure to provide necessary medical care);
- Children left with caretakers the defendant knew to be abusive or dangerous.

Section 273a is often charged alongside § 273d (corporal punishment) when physical abuse causes injury AND the child was also placed in an endangering situation. Both charges may be sustained by the same underlying conduct if they address different aspects of the harm.
