PEOPLE v. WETZEL
11 Cal.3d 104 (1974)
California Supreme Court
Decided: 1974

---

FACTS:

A citizen's arrest waiver was filed in connection with an arrest. The question was whether the citizen's arrest under Pen. Code § 837, subd. 1 was valid — specifically whether the predicate public offense had actually been committed in the arrestor's presence, as required by the statute.

---

HOLDING:

§ 837 GROUND 1 — ACTUAL OFFENSE, ACTUAL PRESENCE:

1. **A citizen's arrest under § 837 subd. 1 requires that a public offense was actually committed or attempted in the arrestor's presence.** The arrestor's subjective belief that an offense occurred is insufficient — the offense must have actually taken place.

2. **"Actual presence" is a strict requirement.** It is not enough that the arrestor believed the offense occurred; the offense must have in fact occurred in the arrestor's direct sensory range. This is the same strict standard applied by the Court of Appeal in People v. Hurtado (1981).

3. **The standard is stricter than for peace officers.** Peace officers may arrest on probable cause — a reasonable belief standard. Private citizens exercising § 837 authority do NOT have the benefit of the probable cause standard. The risk of an invalid citizen's arrest falls entirely on the arrestor.

4. **An invalid citizen's arrest is false imprisonment.** If no offense actually occurred in the arrestor's presence, the citizen's arrest is invalid from inception and constitutes an unlawful restraint of liberty.

---

WETZEL AS SUPREME COURT FOUNDATION:

Wetzel is the California Supreme Court authority confirming the strict validity requirements for citizen's arrests. The Court of Appeal cases (Hurtado, Snyder) elaborate on specific elements; Wetzel provides the Supreme Court imprimatur for the strict standards framework as a whole.

The combined doctrine:
- Peace officers: probable cause standard (more flexible)
- Private citizens under § 837: actual offense + actual presence (or actual commission for Grounds 2/3) (strictly required)
- Good faith is no defense to an invalid citizen's arrest
- The invalid citizen's arrest is false imprisonment

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

For the June 16, 2023 incident (APD Case #23-004494), a citizen's arrest waiver was filed. Under Wetzel:
1. Was a public offense actually committed in the arrestor's presence? If the answer is no — if no crime actually occurred, or if the arrestor did not personally witness it — the citizen's arrest was invalid from inception
2. An invalid citizen's arrest is false imprisonment under § 236
3. The persons who induced the invalid citizen's arrest through false reports are liable for the resulting false imprisonment as co-conspirators or aiders and abettors under § 182(a)(2) and § 31

Combined with the Fourth Amendment unlawful seizure claim (§ 1983), the Wetzel invalid-citizen's-arrest doctrine provides parallel state criminal and civil liability for the individuals who orchestrated the June 16, 2023 event.
