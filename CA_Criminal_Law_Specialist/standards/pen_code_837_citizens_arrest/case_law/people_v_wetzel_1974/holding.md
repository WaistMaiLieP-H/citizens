# People v. Wetzel, 11 Cal.3d 104 (1974)

## Citation
People v. Wetzel, 11 Cal.3d 104 (1974)

## Court
Supreme Court of California

## Holding

A citizen's arrest under Penal Code section 837, subdivision 1, requires that a public offense was actually committed or attempted in the arrestor's presence. The arrestor's subjective belief that an offense occurred is insufficient — the offense must have actually taken place. If no offense was in fact committed in the arrestor's presence, the citizen's arrest is invalid, and the arrest itself constitutes an unlawful restraint of liberty.

## Key Principles

1. For a misdemeanor citizen's arrest (Ground 1), the offense must have actually been committed in the arrestor's presence. Reasonable belief is not enough.
2. This standard is stricter than the standard for peace officers, who may arrest on probable cause.
3. The risk of an invalid citizen's arrest falls on the arrestor, not the arrestee.

## Steward Relevance

On June 16, 2023 (APD Case #23-004494), a citizen's arrest waiver was filed. Under Wetzel, the critical question is: was a public offense actually committed in the arrestor's presence? If the answer is no — if no crime actually occurred, or if the arrestor did not personally witness it — the citizen's arrest was invalid from inception. An invalid citizen's arrest is false imprisonment.
