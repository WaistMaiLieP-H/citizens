# People v. Hurtado (1981) 124 Cal.App.3d 321

**Citation:** People v. Hurtado (1981) 124 Cal.App.3d 321, 177 Cal.Rptr. 401
**Court:** California Court of Appeal
**Decided:** 1981
**Statutes construed:** Penal Code § 837 — citizen's arrest; "in presence" requirement; validity of arrest; Ground 1 analysis

## Facts

A private person effected an arrest of the defendant based on an alleged offense the arrestor had not personally witnessed. The defendant challenged the legality of the citizen's arrest, arguing that the "in presence" requirement of § 837, Ground 1 was not satisfied and that the arrest was therefore unlawful.

## Holding

The Court of Appeal addressed the "in presence" requirement for citizen's arrest under § 837:

1. **"In presence" means personal sensory perception.** A citizen's arrest under Ground 1 requires that the arrestor personally perceived the offense through their own senses — sight, sound, smell, or touch. An arrest based on what the arrestor was told by others does not satisfy the "in presence" requirement.

2. **Hearsay or secondary information does not establish presence.** A private person who rushes to a location because someone called them and reported a crime in progress does not "witness" the crime in the § 837 sense. They were not present when the offense occurred.

3. **The offense must have actually occurred.** Ground 1, like all three grounds, requires that the offense have actually been committed or attempted. A reasonable belief that an offense occurred is not sufficient for Ground 1 — the arrestor must have perceived it directly.

4. **Consequence of invalid arrest is false imprisonment.** An arrest that does not satisfy any of the three § 837 grounds is an unlawful detention and constitutes false imprisonment under § 236. The unlawfulness is not excused by good faith.

## The doctrinal proposition

Hurtado establishes the strict interpretation of the "in presence" element:

- **Personal presence required** — not constructive presence, not presence by proxy
- **Direct sensory perception** — the arrestor must have seen, heard, or otherwise directly perceived the offense
- **No hearsay substitute** — being told about an offense by the victim or a witness does not create § 837 Ground 1 authority to arrest
- **Good faith irrelevant** — an arrest that violates § 837 is unlawful even if made in good faith

## Application to the Hartmann matter

For the June 16, 2023 incident, if any private person participated in or directed the arrest of the steward:

1. Did that person personally witness the alleged offense, or did they act on information from others?
2. If based on information from others (Christina's account, the child's account, a 911 caller), Ground 1 was not satisfied for any private-person arrest authority
3. If no actual offense was committed, all three grounds fail for all private persons
4. The persons who orchestrated the false accusation to induce the arrest are liable under § 182(a)(2) regardless of whether they personally effected the arrest

## Authority status

Published California Court of Appeal decision (1981). Establishes the strict "in presence" requirement for § 837 Ground 1 citizen's arrests. Applied in false imprisonment and unlawful arrest civil and criminal proceedings.
