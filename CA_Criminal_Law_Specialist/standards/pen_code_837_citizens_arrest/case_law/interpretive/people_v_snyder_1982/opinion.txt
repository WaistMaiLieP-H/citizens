PEOPLE v. SNYDER
136 Cal.App.3d 608 (1982)
California Court of Appeal
Decided: 1982

---

FACTS:

A private person effected a citizen's arrest under § 837 Ground 2 or Ground 3, asserting that the defendant had committed a felony. The defendant challenged the arrest on the grounds that the felony had not actually been committed, defeating both Grounds 2 and 3 as a matter of law.

---

HOLDING:

§ 837 GROUNDS 2 AND 3 — "ACTUALLY COMMITTED" REQUIREMENT:

1. **Both Ground 2 and Ground 3 require that a felony was in fact committed.** This is an objective historical fact, not a matter of the arrestor's belief. If no felony was in fact committed, neither Ground 2 nor Ground 3 is available — regardless of how sincerely the arrestor believed otherwise.

2. **Good faith belief cannot substitute for actual commission.** Ground 2 ("has been committed" by the person arrested) and Ground 3 ("has in fact been committed" with reasonable cause) both require the felony to have occurred. The arrestor's subjective good faith is legally irrelevant.

3. **Ground 3 provides greater protection than Ground 2 but still requires the felony to have occurred.** Ground 3 adds a reasonable cause element — useful where the arrestor cannot be certain whether this particular person committed the felony — but it does not remove the underlying requirement that a felony actually happened.

4. **An arrest made on a false belief that a felony occurred is unlawful.** If someone falsely reports a felony to induce an arrest, and the person who makes the arrest acts on that false report, the arrest is unlawful — and the person who made the false report may be separately liable for causing the unlawful arrest.

---

THE SNYDER ELEMENT TABLE:

| Ground | Felony Required? | Reasonable Cause Required? | Presence Required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground 1 | Not necessarily | No | Yes (for any public offense) |
| Ground 2 | Yes — by THIS person | No | No |
| Ground 3 | Yes — by SOMEONE | Yes | No |

---

THE FALSE ACCUSATION CONSEQUENCE:

If a person falsely represents that a felony occurred to induce another to make a citizen's arrest (or to induce police to arrest), and no felony was in fact committed:
- Grounds 2 and 3 are unavailable as a matter of law
- The arrest is unlawful
- The false accuser is liable under § 182(a)(2) (conspiracy to procure false arrest) and § 236 (false imprisonment as a co-conspirator or aider and abettor)
- The false accuser is liable under § 148.5 if the false report was made to police

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

For the June 16, 2023 incident (04-23-01959):
1. If the predicate conduct alleged did not in fact occur — i.e., no public offense was actually committed — then all three § 837 grounds fail for any private-person arrest authority
2. Any peace officer arrest derived from the false accusation was based on manufactured probable cause, not actual probable cause
3. The persons who made and coordinated the false accusation face § 182(a)(2) conspiracy liability regardless of whether the arrest was by a private person or a peace officer
4. The manufactured probable cause provides the foundation for the § 1983 Fourth Amendment claim against the APD officers who acted on the false information
