Cal. Penal Code § 837 — Origin (1872 Codification)

§ 837 was enacted in the 1872 California Penal Code codification, codifying the common law citizen's arrest doctrine:

§ 837 (1872, substantially current):
"A private person may arrest another:
1. For a public offense committed or attempted in his presence.
2. When the person arrested has committed a felony, although not in his presence.
3. When a felony has been in fact committed, and he has reasonable cause for believing the person arrested to have committed it."

The common law citizen's arrest doctrine predates the statute by centuries — California codified existing common law practice, not a new legal innovation.

Key structural features:
1. Misdemeanor arrest: ONLY if committed in the private person's PRESENCE
2. Felony arrest: may be based on reasonable cause to believe, even without direct observation
3. "Has been committed" requirement for felony: the felony must have actually occurred — a citizen cannot arrest for a felony that did not actually take place, even with reasonable cause

Limitations established in 1872 (still current):
- No authority to use force beyond what is necessary to effect the arrest
- Must deliver the arrested person to law enforcement promptly
- Unlawful citizen's arrest = potential criminal liability (battery, false imprisonment)

Source: California Penal Code (1872 Codification).
