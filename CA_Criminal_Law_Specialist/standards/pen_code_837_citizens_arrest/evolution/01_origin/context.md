# Context — § 837 Origin (1872)

## The wound / the right

Citizen's arrest doctrine originated in English common law because there was no professional police force — law enforcement depended on the general public. The doctrine gave private individuals the authority to detain someone actively committing a crime and hold them for the constable.

California codified this in 1872 as part of the original Penal Code. By 1872, California had professional law enforcement, but the doctrine remained useful: crimes in progress where no officer was present, felonies where the perpetrator might flee before officers arrived.

## The 2021 reform context

Following the 2020 Ahmaud Arbery murder (a citizen's arrest gone fatally wrong) and documented California incidents of racially motivated citizen's arrest claims, California significantly restricted the doctrine in 2021 (AB 1775). The 2021 amendments narrowed the circumstances under which a private person may lawfully arrest — limiting the felony arrest authority and adding restrictions on force.

**This means the current § 837 (post-2021) is materially different from the 1872 original.** A two-stage evolution is needed: 01_origin (1872 common-law codification) → 02_amendment_2021 (AB 1775 restrictions) → current.
