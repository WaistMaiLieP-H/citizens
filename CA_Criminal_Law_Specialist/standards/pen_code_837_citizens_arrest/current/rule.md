# Cal. Penal Code § 837 — Arrest by Private Person (Citizen's Arrest)

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_837_CITIZENS_ARREST
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

A private person may arrest another:

**1.** For a public offense committed or attempted **in his or her presence**;

**2.** When the person arrested has committed a **felony**, although not in his or her presence;

**3.** When a felony **has in fact been committed**, and he or she has **reasonable cause** for believing the person arrested to have committed it.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 837; enacted 1872; amended Stats. 2018, Ch. 36, §1 — gender-neutral language)*

---

## Three Grounds for Private Arrest

### Ground 1: Public Offense in Presence

- A public offense (felony or misdemeanor) was committed or attempted;
- The offense occurred **in the arrestor's presence** — the arrestor personally witnessed it;
- The arrestor is a private person (not acting in official law enforcement capacity).

"Presence" means the arrestor personally observed the offense — through any sense, not just sight. The offense must be substantially contemporaneous.

### Ground 2: Felony Not in Presence

- The person arrested has **in fact committed a felony** — the felony must have actually occurred;
- The felony need not have occurred in the arrestor's presence;
- **No "reasonable cause" escape:** If the felony did not actually occur, this ground fails even if the arrestor had reasonable grounds to believe it did. The actual commission is an element, not the belief.

### Ground 3: Felony Believed Committed

- A felony **has in fact been committed** (objective requirement — the felony must exist);
- The arrestor has **reasonable cause** to believe the specific person arrested committed it;
- Combines objective predicate (felony occurred) with subjective standard (reasonable belief as to actor).

---

## The Risk of Wrongful Private Arrest

If a private person makes an arrest that is not legally justified under § 837, the arrestor may face:
- **Criminal liability:** False imprisonment (§ 236), battery (§ 242), kidnapping (§ 207) depending on the circumstances;
- **Civil liability:** § 1983 (if acting under color of law or in coordination with government) or state tort claims.

The second and third grounds both require that a felony has "in fact" occurred — reasonable belief that a felony occurred is not sufficient for Ground 2 if no felony actually occurred.

---

## Delivery After Arrest — § 847

After making a private arrest, the arrestor must without unnecessary delay take the arrested person before a magistrate or deliver them to a peace officer. Prolonged detention after a private arrest without delivery to law enforcement is unlawful.

---

## Modern Context — Security Guards and Merchants

Section 837 is frequently applied to merchant (shopkeeper) detentions of suspected shoplifters and to security guard detentions. Cal. Pen. Code § 490.5 provides merchant privilege — a merchant or their employee may detain a person for a reasonable time to investigate suspected shoplifting, using reasonable non-deadly force.

---

## Plain English

A private citizen can arrest someone without a police officer present in three situations: (1) you personally witnessed the offense, (2) the person actually committed a felony (whether you saw it or not), or (3) a felony actually occurred and you have reasonable grounds to believe this specific person did it. The arrest must be turned over to law enforcement immediately — this is not a right to imprison someone privately. Getting it wrong exposes you to criminal charges (false imprisonment) and civil liability.
