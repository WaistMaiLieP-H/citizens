# Cal. Pen. Code § 837 — Arrest by Private Person

## Verbatim Text

A private person may arrest another:

1. For a public offense committed or attempted in his or her presence.

2. When the person arrested has committed a felony, although not in his or her presence.

3. When a felony has been in fact committed, and he or she has reasonable cause for believing the person arrested to have committed it.

## Source

California Penal Code, Part 2, Title 7, Chapter 5, Section 837

Enacted 1872. Amended by Stats. 2018, Ch. 36, Sec. 1 (gender-neutral language).

## Elements

### Ground 1: Public offense in presence
- (a) A public offense was committed or attempted
- (b) The offense occurred in the arrestor's presence
- (c) The arrestor is a private person (not a peace officer acting in official capacity)

### Ground 2: Felony committed
- (a) The person arrested has in fact committed a felony
- (b) The arrestor is a private person
- Note: The felony need not have occurred in the arrestor's presence, but it must have actually been committed — reasonable belief is not sufficient under this ground

### Ground 3: Felony with reasonable cause
- (a) A felony has in fact been committed (by someone)
- (b) The arrestor has reasonable cause to believe the person arrested committed it
- (c) The arrestor is a private person

## Key Distinctions

- Ground 1 covers any public offense (felony or misdemeanor) but requires the arrestor to have personally witnessed it
- Ground 2 requires an actual felony — if no felony was committed, the arrest is invalid regardless of the arrestor's belief
- Ground 3 requires both an actual felony AND reasonable cause — the felony must have happened, and the arrestor must have reasonable grounds to believe this particular person did it
- For misdemeanors, ONLY Ground 1 applies — the arrestor must have personally witnessed the offense

## Consequence of Invalid Citizen's Arrest

If none of the three grounds are satisfied, the arrest is unlawful. An unlawful arrest by a private person constitutes false imprisonment under Cal. Pen. Code § 236. The person arrested may use reasonable force to resist an unlawful citizen's arrest.
