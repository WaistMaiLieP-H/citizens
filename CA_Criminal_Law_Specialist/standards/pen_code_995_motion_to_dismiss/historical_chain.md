# Historical Chain — Pen. Code § 995

## Origin

Section 995 is one of the original provisions of the California Penal Code, enacted in **1872** as part of the initial codification. The section's core structure — allowing a defendant to move to set aside an indictment or information on specified grounds — has remained substantially unchanged since 1872.

**Context:** The § 995 motion is part of California's two-track preliminary proceeding system. In the indictment track, a grand jury screens charges before trial. In the information track (far more common), a magistrate conducts a preliminary hearing and decides whether to hold the defendant to answer. Section 995 provides the trial court with authority to review the magistrate's probable cause determination.

## Key developments

**1872:** Enacted as part of the original Penal Code. The core "reasonable or probable cause" standard dates to 1872.

**People v. Uhlemann (1973) 9 Cal.3d 662:** The California Supreme Court clarified that on a § 995 motion, the superior court sits as a reviewing court applying the same substantial evidence standard — it does not re-weigh evidence or make credibility determinations. This remains the controlling framework.

**People v. Jennings (1988) 46 Cal.3d 963:** Further refined the scope of review, confirming that the superior court and the Court of Appeal both apply the same standard: whether any substantial evidence supports the magistrate's probable cause finding.

**1990s — Proposition 115 (1990):** Proposition 115 amended the California Constitution to restrict the defendant's right to cross-examine witnesses at preliminary hearings. Post-Proposition 115, some hearsay evidence became admissible at preliminary hearings via qualified law enforcement officer testimony. This change affected the quality of evidence that could support a magistrate's probable cause finding, and correspondingly affected the § 995 calculus.

**1995 — § 995a added:** The § 995a "alternative procedure" was added, allowing the prosecution to reopen the preliminary hearing rather than having the information dismissed outright. This legislative change significantly reduced the practical impact of § 995 motions by giving the prosecution a "second bite."

**Present:** Section 995 remains the standard vehicle for pre-trial challenge to the sufficiency of the probable cause finding. Its practical utility has been somewhat reduced by Proposition 115 and § 995a, but it remains essential where the preliminary hearing record is clearly deficient.
