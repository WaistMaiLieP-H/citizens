# Context — § 995 Modernization

The mid-20th century modernization of § 995 expanded the motion's scope from purely procedural (was the preliminary hearing held?) to substantive (was the probable cause finding supported by competent evidence?). This shift aligned § 995 with the California Supreme Court's developing jurisprudence on the standard of review at preliminary hearings.

The practical effect: a defendant can now challenge the information not only because the magistrate skipped the hearing, but because the magistrate committed legal error at the hearing — e.g., admitted evidence that was unconstitutionally obtained, applied the wrong legal standard, or found probable cause on evidence that legally could not support it.

## Diff from prior
Prior: § 995 = procedural only (was the process followed?). After: § 995 = substantive (was the probable cause finding legally sufficient?). This made § 995 one of California's most powerful pre-trial motions.
