# Context — § 995 Origin (1872)

## The wound

The 1872 codification answered a 19th-century wound: in early California courts, prosecutors could file criminal charges without any judicial review of probable cause. An accusation by the DA was sufficient to send a defendant to trial. This created obvious opportunities for political prosecution and abuse of the charging power.

The preliminary hearing (and the § 995 motion challenging it) were the structural guarantees against arbitrary prosecution. The principle: before you can try someone, a neutral magistrate must find that the evidence supports the charge. If no such finding was made — or if the magistrate committed legal error — the defendant can challenge the information before trial.

## The structural function

§ 995 is not a trial-stage motion. It is pre-trial: the defendant moves to set aside the information (or indictment) on the ground that it was improperly filed — either because there was no preliminary hearing, or because the magistrate's probable-cause finding was legally insufficient (evidence was obtained illegally, elements were not shown, or the commitment was legally defective).

## Why § 995 is alive in 2026

The § 995 motion is one of the most important pre-trial tools in California criminal defense. A successful § 995 motion dismisses the case before trial. Even if the case is later refiled, a § 995 victory creates an evidentiary record of the defects in the prosecution's case and potentially limits what charges can be brought.
