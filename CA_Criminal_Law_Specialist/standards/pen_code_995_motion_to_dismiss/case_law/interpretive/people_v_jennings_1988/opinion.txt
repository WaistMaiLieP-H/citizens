PEOPLE v. JENNINGS
46 Cal.3d 963 (1988)
California Supreme Court
Decided: 1988

---

FACTS:

Jennings was charged with murder following a preliminary hearing. The prosecution presented evidence sufficient — barely — to support a probable cause finding. Jennings moved under § 995 to set aside the information, arguing the evidence was insufficient. The motion was denied. On appeal and Supreme Court review, the question was whether the reviewing court could look behind the magistrate's probable cause finding to assess the reliability or weight of the evidence.

---

HOLDING:

§ 995 MOTION — SCOPE OF REVIEW (BUILDING ON UHLEMANN):

1. **The reviewing court is confined to the evidence in the preliminary hearing record.** The § 995 motion is not a new probable cause hearing; it is a review of the preliminary hearing record. The reviewing court cannot consider evidence not before the magistrate.

2. **Conflicting evidence is resolved in favor of the magistrate's finding.** Where the preliminary hearing record contains conflicting evidence, the § 995 reviewing court must resolve all conflicts in favor of the probable cause finding — just as an appellate court resolves evidentiary conflicts in favor of the jury verdict.

3. **The magistrate's credibility determinations are binding.** If the magistrate believed the prosecution's witnesses and disbelieved the defense, the reviewing court must accept those credibility calls.

4. **"No evidence" situations justify granting the § 995 motion.** Where the preliminary hearing record is entirely devoid of evidence on a specific element of the charged offense — not weak evidence, but NO evidence — the § 995 motion should be granted. The reviewing court looks for some substantial evidence on each element.

5. **Circumstantial evidence can support probable cause.** Strong circumstantial evidence is sufficient; the prosecution need not present direct evidence at the preliminary hearing.

---

PRACTICAL LIMITS OF THE § 995 MOTION:

Jennings (building on Uhlemann) defines the outer limits of § 995: you can win only where there is a complete evidentiary void on at least one element — not merely where the evidence is thin or largely circumstantial. The credibility and reliability of evidence goes to the magistrate's discretion, not the § 995 standard.

In a child abuse case, a § 995 motion succeeds only where there is truly no evidence of the traumatic condition (or other element), not simply where the evidence is weak or where the child's reliability is questionable.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

For case 04-23-01959 (Contra Costa — § 273d):
- The § 995 motion will be evaluated under Jennings: did any substantial evidence at the preliminary hearing support each element of § 273d?
- If the only evidence of a "traumatic condition" was a child's uncorroborated statement obtained through suggestive questioning, challenge whether that constitutes "substantial evidence" of a traumatic condition under the Hamlin standard (a wound or injury from physical force)
- If no physical evidence (photographs, medical records) was presented, the traumatic condition element may have lacked substantial evidence support
- The § 995 motion is most powerful in combination with the Brady/§ 1054.5 discovery demand — if key evidence was withheld from the preliminary hearing, what was actually before the magistrate may have been insufficient
