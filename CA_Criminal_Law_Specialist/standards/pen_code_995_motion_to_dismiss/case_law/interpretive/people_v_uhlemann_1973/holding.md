# People v. Uhlemann (1973) 9 Cal.3d 662
## [Filed in people_v_jenkins_1975 folder — folder name is legacy; case is Uhlemann]

**Citation:** People v. Uhlemann (1973) 9 Cal.3d 662, 108 Cal.Rptr. 657, 511 P.2d 609
**Court:** Supreme Court of California
**Decided:** 1973
**Statutes construed:** Pen. Code § 995

## Facts

Uhlemann was charged by information following a preliminary hearing. He moved under § 995 to set aside the information, arguing the evidence at the preliminary hearing was insufficient to establish probable cause for the charged offense. The superior court denied the motion; Uhlemann appealed.

The California Supreme Court granted review to resolve the standard of review applicable to § 995 motions at both the superior court and appellate levels.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **The standard for a § 995 motion is whether the magistrate had "reasonable or probable cause" to hold the defendant to answer.** This is the same standard that applies at the preliminary hearing itself: whether any substantial evidence supports a rational belief that the defendant committed the charged offense.

2. **The superior court does not weigh evidence or make credibility determinations on a § 995 motion.** The superior court sits as a reviewing court, not a fact-finder. Its role is to determine whether the evidence in the preliminary hearing record, taken as a whole, supports the magistrate's conclusion. If substantial evidence supports the probable cause finding, the motion must be denied even if the court would have ruled differently as the trier of fact.

3. **"Substantial evidence" in the § 995 context means any competent evidence that a reasonable person of ordinary caution would conscientiously entertain a strong suspicion the defendant committed the offense.** This is below a preponderance of the evidence and well below proof beyond a reasonable doubt. It is more than mere possibility or conjecture.

4. **The appellate court applies the same substantial evidence standard.** On appeal from a § 995 ruling, the Court of Appeal does not independently re-examine the preliminary hearing record from scratch — it reviews whether the superior court properly applied the substantial evidence standard.

5. **The prosecution need not prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt at the preliminary hearing.** Probable cause requires less. Even weak or conflicting evidence may satisfy the standard if a rational person could entertain suspicion.

## Why this is load-bearing

*Uhlemann* is the controlling standard for every § 995 motion in California. It defines:
- The scope of the superior court's review (reviewing, not fact-finding)
- The applicable standard (substantial evidence to support reasonable or probable cause)
- The appellate standard (same as the trial court)

This means a § 995 motion is hardest to win where the preliminary hearing had any competent evidence supporting each element — even weak evidence. The motion is most viable when the preliminary hearing record is entirely void of evidence on a specific element, or where all the evidence supporting an element was inadmissible.
