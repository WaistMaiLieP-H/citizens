# Cal. Penal Code § 529 — False Personation

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_529_FALSE_PERSONATION
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** Every person who **falsely personates another** in either his or her private or official capacity, and in that assumed character does any of the following, is punishable pursuant to subdivision (b):

1. Becomes bail or surety for any party in any proceeding whatever, before any court or officer authorized to take that bail or surety;
2. Verifies, publishes, acknowledges, or proves, in the name of another person, any written instrument, with intent that the same may be recorded, delivered, or used as true;
3. Does any other act whereby, if done by the person falsely personated, he might, in any event, become liable to any suit or prosecution, or to pay any sum of money, or to incur any charge, forfeiture, or penalty, or whereby any benefit might accrue to the party personating, or to any other person.

**(b)** Punishment: Fine not exceeding **$10,000**, or imprisonment in a county jail not exceeding **one year**, or imprisonment pursuant to subdivision (h) of Section 1170, or by both that fine and imprisonment.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 529; wobbler)*

---

## Elements

1. The defendant **falsely personated** (impersonated) another person — either in that person's private or official capacity;
2. While acting in that assumed identity, the defendant committed one of the three specified acts: (a) became bail/surety; (b) verified, published, acknowledged, or proved a written instrument in the other person's name; or (c) did any act exposing the personated person to liability, suit, or penalty, or conferring a benefit on the impersonator.

---

## The Breadth of Subsection (a)(3)

Subsection (a)(3) is extremely broad: any act that would expose the real person to liability — civil or criminal — or that benefits the impersonator constitutes false personation if done while falsely claiming to be that person. This covers:
- Using another person's identity to sign contracts;
- Impersonating another person in court proceedings;
- Signing legal documents in another's name;
- Claiming another person's identity to receive government benefits.

---

## Distinction from Identity Theft (§ 530.5)

| Feature | § 529 (False Personation) | § 530.5 (Identity Theft) |
|---------|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| Focus | Impersonating a specific person + committing an act | Using personal identifying information for unlawful purpose |
| Act requirement | Yes — must do a qualifying act while impersonating | Use of information is the offense |
| Scope | Historical — requires actual impersonation of real person | Modern — covers electronic theft of information |
| Punishment | Wobbler | Public offense (variable) |

Both statutes may be charged when a defendant steals someone's identity and then acts as that person in a legal or financial proceeding.

---

## Official Capacity Impersonation

Impersonating a person in their "official capacity" covers impersonating government officials, law enforcement officers, or others acting in an official role. Separately, Cal. Pen. Code § 146a specifically addresses impersonating a peace officer.

---

## Plain English

False personation is pretending to be someone else and then doing something that either benefits you or creates liability for the real person. Signing a contract in someone else's name, posting bail as if you were that person, or acknowledging a legal document in their name all qualify. It's a wobbler (misdemeanor or felony) and frequently charged alongside identity theft (§ 530.5) when someone steals an identity and then uses it in legal or financial transactions.
