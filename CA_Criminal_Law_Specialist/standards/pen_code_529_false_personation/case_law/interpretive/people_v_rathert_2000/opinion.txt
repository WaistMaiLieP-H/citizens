PEOPLE v. RATHERT
24 Cal.4th 200 (2000)
California Supreme Court
Decided: September 2000

---

FACTS:

The defendant was prosecuted under Pen. Code § 529 after falsely representing himself as another person and engaging in acts in the assumed identity. The prosecution charged under § 529(a)(3), the catchall provision. The defendant argued that the catchall required proof that the personated person was actually exposed to specific liability, and that mere abstract possibility was insufficient.

---

HOLDING:

§ 529(a)(3) CATCHALL — BREADTH AND OPERATION:

1. **The § 529(a)(3) catchall requires a nexus beyond the mere act of false personation.** It is not enough that the defendant pretended to be another person. The prosecution must prove the defendant also engaged in an additional act that could result in the impersonated person's liability or the impersonator's benefit.

2. **The "might, in any event, become liable" phrase is disjunctive and applies a potentiality standard.** The prosecution need not prove that liability actually attached to the impersonated person. It is sufficient that the impersonator's act could have resulted in such liability under the circumstances. This is a potentiality standard, not a result standard.

3. **"Benefit" to the impersonator is independently sufficient.** The second branch of § 529(a)(3) — "whereby any benefit might accrue to the party personating" — is an independent basis for prosecution. The impersonator can be convicted even if no actual liability attached to the impersonated person, as long as the impersonator obtained or sought to obtain a benefit.

4. **"Benefit" is not limited to economic gain.** The term includes any advantage the impersonator sought — social standing, avoidance of detection, evasion of legal process, or any other personal benefit. This interpretation is consistent with the 1872 drafters' intent to reach a broad range of deceptive acts.

---

WHY RATHERT IS LOAD-BEARING:

Rathert is the leading California Supreme Court interpretation of § 529(a)(3), establishing the operational reach of the catchall:
- Potentiality standard (not actual liability required) — makes prosecution easier
- Disjunctive structure (liability OR benefit) — two independent prosecution paths
- Broad construction of "benefit" — reaches any personal advantage

Almost any meaningful act done in a false identity — signing a document, applying for services, checking in somewhere, even representing oneself socially — can potentially satisfy the catchall under Rathert's standard.

---

§ 529 vs. § 530.5 CHARGING DECISION:

| Statute | Focus | Key Element |
|---|---|---|
| § 529(a)(3) | Identity impersonation | False personation + any act exposing impersonated person to potential liability OR benefit to impersonator |
| § 530.5(a) | PII theft | Willful acquisition of PII + use for any unlawful purpose |

Section 530.5 tends to be charged when the theft is PII-focused (SSN, credit card, account); § 529 tends to be charged when the impersonation is identity-focused (showing up as someone else, signing as someone else, representing oneself as someone else in a transaction). Prosecutors often charge both alternatively.
