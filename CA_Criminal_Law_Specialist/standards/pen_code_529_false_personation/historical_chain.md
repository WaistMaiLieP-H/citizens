# Historical Chain — Pen. Code § 529 (False Personation)

| Year | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1872 | Original Penal Code enacts § 529 | One of the oldest still-operative PC sections |
| 1900s-1990s | Minor amendments; substantive stability | Long-term stability |
| 1997 | § 530.5 identity theft statute enacted | Modern parallel; does not replace § 529 |
| 2010s | § 529 current form | Stable, archaic but broadly applicable |
| 2026 | In force | Active prosecution tool |

The 1872 origin matters: § 529 was drafted to address 19th-century concerns about courtroom impersonation, forged bail, and false signatures on legal instruments. The catchall in subd. (a)(3) was included to future-proof the statute, and it has worked — modern courts apply the 1872 language to 21st-century digital impersonation patterns without needing legislative amendment.

Section 529 is the clearest example in the Criminal Law Specialist's corpus of a statute whose 150-year textual stability is a feature, not a bug. The catchall language does the work that would otherwise require ongoing amendment.
