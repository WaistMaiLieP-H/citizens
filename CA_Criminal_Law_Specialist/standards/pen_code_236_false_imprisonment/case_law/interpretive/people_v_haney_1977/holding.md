# People v. Haney (1977) — § 236 General Elements Framework

**Court:** California Court of Appeal
**Decided:** 1977
**Statutes construed:** Penal Code § 236 — elements of false imprisonment; restraint requirement; intent

## Holding

## The doctrinal proposition

The elements of false imprisonment under § 236 have been elaborated by California case law into the following framework:

1. **Act of restraint** — any intentional act that confines the victim or compels the victim to remain in a place. The restraint can be:
   - Physical (locking a door, blocking movement, using force)
   - Verbal (orders or threats that compel submission)
   - Deceptive (false representations inducing the victim to submit)
   - By color of law (pretending to be a peace officer)

2. **Confinement** — the restraint must confine the victim in some meaningful way. A trivial obstruction or momentary contact is insufficient. The confinement can be to a specific location (a room) or a specific space (a vehicle), or it can be a restriction on the victim's freedom to move (holding the victim in place).

3. **Without consent** — the victim must not have consented to the restraint. Consent procured by fraud or coercion is not valid consent. Apparent consent by a person who is unconscious, intoxicated, or otherwise unable to consent is not valid.

4. **Unlawfulness** — the restraint must lack lawful authority. Lawful authority includes peace officer arrest authority, § 837 citizen's arrest, self-defense, defense of others, parental authority over minor children, and emergency necessity.

5. **General intent** — the defendant must have intended to commit the restraining act, though not necessarily to confine unlawfully. Accidental contact or inadvertent blocking is not false imprisonment.

## Distinction from kidnapping

Kidnapping (§ 207) is the more serious offense and requires asportation — movement of the victim a substantial distance. False imprisonment does not require movement. A person who locks someone in a closet commits false imprisonment; a person who drags someone into a vehicle and drives away commits kidnapping.

Asportation distance is measured by:
- Actual distance moved
- Whether the movement increased the risk of harm
- Whether the movement was incidental to another crime or independent

When in doubt, prosecutors charge both § 236 and § 207 alternatively, and the jury decides which applies.

## Civil and federal parallels

State tort of false imprisonment has substantially similar elements but is proved by a preponderance of the evidence rather than beyond a reasonable doubt. The civil tort does not require the "unlawful" element in the same way — a private citizen who falsely imprisons another can be civilly liable even without proving criminal intent.

Federal § 1983 claims for unlawful seizure apply when the restraint was effected by state actors under color of state law. The Fourth Amendment provides the constitutional floor: seizure must be reasonable, supported by probable cause or reasonable suspicion, and effected with proportional force.
