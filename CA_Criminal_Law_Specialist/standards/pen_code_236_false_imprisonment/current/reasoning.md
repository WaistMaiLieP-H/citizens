# Reasoning — Cal. Penal Code § 236 (False Imprisonment)

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_236_FALSE_IMPRISONMENT
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why This Standard Exists

False imprisonment addresses one of the most fundamental violations of personal liberty: preventing a person from exercising their right to move freely. Unlike assault (which threatens harm) or battery (which inflicts harm), false imprisonment is the deprivation of liberty itself — the freedom to go where you choose. This right is so fundamental that its unlawful violation constitutes a distinct criminal offense.

The statute's extreme brevity — "unlawful violation of the personal liberty of another" — reflects that the concept is simple: you cannot keep someone somewhere they do not want to be without legal justification. The brevity also reflects that courts have developed the elements through common law, making the statutory text a framework rather than an exhaustive definition.

---

## Why Force/Violence/Menace/Fraud Elevates to Felony

The felony tier (§ 237) for false imprisonment accomplished by violence, menace, fraud, or deceit reflects the qualitatively different character of coercive confinement. A parent who locks a child's room and a kidnapper who uses threats to keep a victim in a vehicle both violate the victim's liberty, but the mechanism matters for culpability and victim harm.

Violence and menace involve direct coercion — the victim is aware they are being held against their will by force or fear. Fraud and deceit involve deception — the victim may not even know they are imprisoned because they believe they are free to leave when in fact they are not. Both are more culpable than simple restraint because they add an additional element of wrongfulness: force, threat, or deception.

---

## Why No Movement Is Required (Distinction from Kidnapping)

The choice to not require asportation (movement) for false imprisonment reflects the legislative judgment that restraining a person's liberty in place is as violating of their autonomy as moving them. A kidnapper who takes a victim from one location to another exercises control over the victim's movement in addition to their liberty — the additional element of movement is what elevates the offense to kidnapping. But restraint without movement is still a complete violation of personal liberty warranting criminal consequences.

---

## False Imprisonment by Government Actors

When law enforcement officers restrain persons without legal justification — unlawful detention without reasonable suspicion, arrest without probable cause, prolonged detention without booking — the conduct constitutes false imprisonment in addition to potential Fourth Amendment violations actionable under § 1983. The overlap between the criminal statute and the constitutional tort is significant for victims of government misconduct.

---

## Application in Practice

False imprisonment is charged in:
- Domestic violence contexts where a partner is physically prevented from leaving during a dispute;
- Human trafficking cases (often the base charge alongside more specific human trafficking statutes);
- Unlawful detention by security guards, bouncers, or private citizens exceeding their § 837 citizen's arrest authority;
- Custody cases where a child is unlawfully kept from a custodial parent (sometimes charged alongside § 278.5);
- Detention of employees by employers in wage-related disputes;
- Medical facility involuntary holds that do not comply with § 5150 WIC procedures.

The misdemeanor/felony distinction in § 237 — based on the presence or absence of violence, menace, fraud, or deceit — is decided by the charging decision and may be reduced in plea negotiations.
