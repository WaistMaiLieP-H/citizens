# Cal. Penal Code § 236 — False Imprisonment

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_236_FALSE_IMPRISONMENT
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

False imprisonment is the **unlawful violation of the personal liberty of another**.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 236)*

---

## Punishment — § 237

**Misdemeanor (base):** Fine and/or county jail if accomplished without violence, menace, fraud, or deceit.

**Felony:** If false imprisonment is "effected by violence, menace, fraud, or deceit," it is punishable as a felony under § 1170(h) — state prison 16 months, 2 years, or 3 years.

---

## Elements of False Imprisonment

1. **Intentional restraint or confinement** of the victim's freedom of movement;
2. **Without lawful privilege** to do so;
3. **Against the victim's will** — the victim did not consent.

The confinement need not be physical — restraint through threats, fraud, deceit, or assertion of authority that the victim reasonably believes compels compliance is sufficient. The confinement need not be for any particular length of time.

---

## The Violence / Menace / Fraud / Deceit Enhancement (Felony)

For felony false imprisonment, the restraint must be accomplished by:
- **Violence** — physical force used to confine;
- **Menace** — express or implied threat of physical harm creating fear;
- **Fraud** — deception used to induce the victim to remain confined;
- **Deceit** — misrepresentation inducing the victim to believe they cannot leave.

---

## Distinction from Kidnapping (§ 207)

False imprisonment differs from kidnapping in one critical element: **asportation** (movement). Kidnapping requires moving the victim a substantial distance. False imprisonment does not require movement — only restraint.

When the victim is moved, even a short distance, the distinction becomes legally significant: prosecutors may charge both kidnapping and false imprisonment, or charge the more serious kidnapping offense.

---

## False Imprisonment by a Public Official

When false imprisonment is committed by a law enforcement officer or other government official acting under color of authority without legal justification, it may also give rise to a § 1983 civil rights claim (Fourth Amendment unlawful seizure) in addition to potential criminal liability.

---

## Interaction with Domestic Violence and Custody Cases

False imprisonment is frequently charged in domestic violence contexts where an intimate partner is prevented from leaving. In the custody context, unlawfully preventing a child from leaving with a lawful custodian may be charged as false imprisonment in addition to § 278.5 (child custody deprivation).

---

## Plain English

False imprisonment is restraining someone against their will without legal justification. It's a misdemeanor if done without force — a felony if accomplished through violence, threats, fraud, or deception. Unlike kidnapping, no movement of the victim is required — just keeping them somewhere they don't want to be. Holding a person in a room, preventing them from leaving through threats, or locking a door to trap someone all qualify. The charge is commonly seen in domestic violence cases, unlawful detention by private citizens, and human trafficking.
