# Diff from Prior Stage — § 166 1989 DVPA Amendment

## Prior stage: 01_origin (1872)

**1872 § 166:** Simple criminal contempt misdemeanor for six categories of in-court or judicial-process misconduct:
- Disorderly conduct in court
- Breach of court peace
- Willful disobedience of lawful process or court order
- Resistance of lawful order or process
- Publication of out-of-court contempts
- Other specified acts

**Key limitation of 1872 text:** "Willful disobedience of lawful process or court order" was present but no specific DVPA enforcement mechanism. No mandatory minimum. No domestic violence context. No notice-in-court provision. Criminal contempt was a general misdemeanor with ordinary discretionary sentencing.

---

## What changed in 1989–1990

**ADDED: § 166(c)(1)** — New subdivision creating a separate criminal enforcement track for violations of DVPA protective orders:

- Violation of a protective order issued pursuant to the Domestic Violence Prevention Act (then Civil Code, later Family Code § 6218 et seq.) is a criminal misdemeanor, distinct from general § 166(a)(4) disobedience
- Mandatory minimum jail term imposed — court may not suspend minimum on first violation
- Willful-and-knowing element: prosecution must prove defendant (a) knew of the order and (b) willfully violated a specific term
- Notice-in-court satisfies knowledge element

**UNCHANGED from 1872:** The general § 166(a) categories (in-court contempt, process resistance) were retained unchanged.

---

## Net effect

The 1872 general disobedience provision (§ 166(a)(4)) continued to apply to non-DV court orders. The new § 166(c)(1) created an enhanced, mandatory-minimum track exclusively for DVPA protective order violations. Two parallel tracks emerged: general contempt (§ 166(a)) and DV protective order enforcement (§ 166(c)).
