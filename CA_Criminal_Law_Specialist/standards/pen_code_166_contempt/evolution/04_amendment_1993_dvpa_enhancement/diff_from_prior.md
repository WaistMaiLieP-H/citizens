# Diff from Prior Stage — § 166 1993 DVPA Recidivist Enhancement

## Prior stage: 03_amendment_1989_dvpa

**Post-1989 state:** § 166(c)(1) established criminal misdemeanor for DVPA protective order violations with mandatory minimum jail for first violation. No recidivist escalation track. No coverage of § 136.2 criminal proceeding orders. Coverage limited to DVPA civil protective orders.

---

## What changed in 1993

**ADDED: § 166(c)(3) — Felony wobbler for recidivist DV violators:**
- Where defendant has two or more prior DV convictions (enumerated list including § 273.5, § 243(e), prior § 166(c)(1)) AND
- The current violation involves actual infliction of physical injury OR credible threat of injury
- The violation is chargeable as a felony (state prison exposure) or enhanced misdemeanor (wobbler at prosecution's election)

**ADDED: § 166(c)(4) — § 136.2 criminal proceeding protective orders:**
- Violation of a protective order issued under Penal Code § 136.2 (issued by court during or after criminal proceedings to protect victims/witnesses) is now expressly a criminal offense under § 166
- Same willful-and-knowing standard as § 166(c)(1)
- Same mandatory minimum structure

**MODIFIED: § 166(c)(1) — Enhanced minimum for second DVPA violation:**
- Second violation within 7 years: enhanced mandatory minimum jail term added; court may not suspend beyond baseline

---

## Net effect

The 1989 single-violation track (§ 166(c)(1)) remained. The 1993 amendments:
1. Created an escalation track for repeat DVPA offenders (§ 166(c)(3) wobbler)
2. Extended coverage to § 136.2 criminal proceeding orders — the most frequently used protective orders in active criminal cases
3. Increased the minimum for second violations

Post-1993, § 166's DV enforcement structure covers three tiers:
- First DVPA violation: § 166(c)(1) — mandatory minimum misdemeanor
- Second+ DVPA violation with violence/prior: § 166(c)(3) — wobbler to felony
- § 136.2 criminal order violation: § 166(c)(4) — mandatory minimum misdemeanor
