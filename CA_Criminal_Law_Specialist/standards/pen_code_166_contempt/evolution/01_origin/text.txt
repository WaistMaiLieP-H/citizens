Cal. Penal Code § 166 — Origin (1872 Codification)

§ 166 was part of the original 1872 California Penal Code codification. The 1872 version established criminal contempt of court as a misdemeanor:

Original § 166 (approximate):
"Every person guilty of any contempt of court, of either of the following kinds, is guilty of a misdemeanor: ...disorderly, contemptuous, or insolent behavior toward the judge while holding the court... [and other grounds]"

The original statute was primarily targeted at in-court conduct. It did not originally include violations of court orders (restraining orders, protective orders) — those came with later amendments.

Key evolution: The original § 166 covered disrespect to the court. Modern § 166(c) — the criminally significant provision for the steward's cases — covers willful violation of specified court orders including DVPA protective orders (§ 136.2), restraining orders, and custody orders. This expansion occurred through 20th century amendments, most significantly post-DVPA (1993).

Source: California Penal Code (1872).
