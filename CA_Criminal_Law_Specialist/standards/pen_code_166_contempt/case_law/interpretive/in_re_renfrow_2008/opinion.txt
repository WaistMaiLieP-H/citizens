IN RE RENFROW
164 Cal.App.4th 1251 (2008)
California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District
Decided: 2008

---

FACTS:

Contempt proceedings were initiated against a party who violated a family law court order. The central question was whether the "willful" element of § 166(a)(4) was established — specifically, whether the person had actual knowledge of the order and intentionally failed to comply.

---

HOLDING:

§ 166 WILLFUL-AND-KNOWING ELEMENT:

1. **"Willful" means intentional.** The violation must be a deliberate act, not an accident or inadvertent conduct. Pen. Code § 7(1) supplies the general definition: acting with purpose or willingness to commit the act.

2. **"Knowing" means actual knowledge of the specific terms of the order.** The defendant must know what the order prohibits — not merely that an order exists. A defendant served with an order typically has constructive knowledge of its terms; if the terms are ambiguous or service was defective, the knowing element may be contestable.

3. **Ignorance of specific terms is a rare defense.** Courts have generally rejected the defense that the defendant knew of the order but did not remember or understand its specific terms. A person served with a protective order has a duty to know and comply with its terms.

4. **Substantial compliance is not a defense when any violation is alleged.** A defendant cannot argue other provisions were followed — violation of any single provision is sufficient for a § 166(c) charge.

5. **The mental state applies at the time of the act.** Subsequent realization that the conduct violated the order does not cure the violation if the willful and knowing elements were present at the time.

---

PRIMARY DEFENSE BATTLEGROUNDS IN § 166(c) CASES:

- "I didn't know the order existed" — defeated if service can be proved
- "I didn't know the specific terms" — rarely successful after personal service
- "It was accidental / I didn't intend to violate" — depends on facts; plausible where contact was inadvertent (unexpectedly encountering the protected person in public)
- "I thought the order had expired / been modified" — depends on whether the defendant had reason to believe modification existed

Section 166(b) stalking enhancement: adds specific enhancement for defendants previously convicted of stalking (§ 646.9) who contact the victim by any means. Each contact is a separate violation under subd. (b)(2) — one of the most aggressive enforcement provisions in § 166.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

If the steward is ever charged with § 166 for alleged violation of a court order, defense must address:
1. Whether service of the order was proper (actual vs. constructive notice)
2. Whether the specific terms were sufficiently clear to provide notice
3. Whether the alleged violation was willful (deliberate act) and knowing (with awareness of terms)
4. Whether any ambiguity in the order supports a reasonable interpretation under which the conduct was not a violation

For enforcement: if the steward is pursuing contempt charges against another party, § 166 is the criminal enforcement path — the willful-and-knowing framework is the prosecution burden the other party must overcome.
