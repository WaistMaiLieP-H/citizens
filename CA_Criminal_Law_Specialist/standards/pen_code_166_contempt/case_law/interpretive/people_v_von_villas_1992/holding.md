# Holding — People v. Von Villas (1992)

**Cite:** People v. Von Villas (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 201 [PROPOSED — verify before filing]

**Key holding:** Knowledge of the court order is a required element of § 166 criminal contempt — the prosecution must affirmatively prove the defendant knew of the order at the time of the alleged violation. Formal written service of process is not the only acceptable method of proving knowledge; in-court oral notification of order terms, reflected in the minute order, is legally sufficient notice for § 166 prosecution.

**Doctrinal rule:** The prosecution bears the burden of proving knowledge; multiple methods of notice are legally sufficient, including in-court oral advisement.

**For what it stands:** The knowledge element across all § 166 categories; the in-court-notification doctrine that supports § 166(c)(1)/(c)(4) prosecutions based on minute-order advisement records.
