# Statute version cited in People v. Von Villas (1992)

*Von Villas* applied Pen. Code § 166 as it existed in 1992. The 1989 DVPA amendments (Stats. 1989, ch. 1333) added § 166(c)(1) — the DV-specific criminal contempt track for violations of § 6218 protective orders — three years before *Von Villas* was decided. The court applied the general § 166(a)(4) knowledge element.

**Relevant statutory text (1992 ≈ 2026 for subd. (a)(4)):**
The *Von Villas* court construed the knowledge element of § 166(a)(4) — holding that the prosecution must prove the defendant had actual knowledge of the order's existence and its terms. The court also held that in-court oral notification by the judge is sufficient to establish knowledge even without a written copy being served. These holdings remain controlling under current § 166(a)(4).

**Consistency check:** *Von Villas* was decided after the 1989 DVPA amendments but construed the general § 166(a)(4) track. Its knowledge analysis applies equally to § 166(c)(1) DV-specific prosecutions and to general § 166(a)(4) proceedings. The statute text the court applied matches current § 166(a)(4) text without material drift.
