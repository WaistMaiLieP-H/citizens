# Holding — People v. Greenfield (1982)

**Cite:** People v. Greenfield (1982) 134 Cal.App.3d Supp. 1 [PROPOSED — verify before filing]

**Key holding:** The willfulness element of § 166 criminal contempt requires a conscious, deliberate choice to violate the court order. Inadvertent or accidental violations do not satisfy willfulness. Mistake of law does not negate willfulness; a genuine and reasonable mistake of fact (e.g., belief that the order had expired) may negate willfulness in narrow circumstances.

**Doctrinal rule:** § 166 willfulness = intentional disobedience of a known order; the defendant must know the order and consciously choose to violate it.

**For what it stands:** The willfulness element across all § 166 categories, including § 166(c)(1) DVPA order violations and § 166(c)(4) § 136.2 order violations.
