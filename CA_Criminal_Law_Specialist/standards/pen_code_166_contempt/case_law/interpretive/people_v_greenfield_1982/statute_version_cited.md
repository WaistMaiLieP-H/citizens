# Statute version cited in People v. Greenfield (1982)

*Greenfield* applied Pen. Code § 166 as it existed in 1982. The core willfulness element — requiring that the defendant consciously and deliberately violate a court order — has been in the statute since its 1872 enactment. The 1989 DVPA amendments adding § 166(c) (DV-specific criminal contempt track) post-date *Greenfield* but did not change the general willfulness analysis in subdivision (a)(4).

**Relevant statutory text (1982 ≈ 2026 for subd. (a)(4)):**
The *Greenfield* court construed the "willful disobedience" element of what is now § 166(a)(4). The text of that provision has remained substantively stable. The court's analysis — that willfulness requires a conscious, deliberate decision to violate the order, and that a genuine mistake of fact about the order's existence may negate willfulness but a mistake of law will not — applies identically to current § 166(a)(4) prosecutions.

**Consistency check:** *Greenfield*'s holdings control current § 166(a)(4) practice. The 1989/1993 DVPA amendments added § 166(c) as a separate, enhanced track for DV orders — they did not alter the base willfulness doctrine that *Greenfield* established for general contempt.
