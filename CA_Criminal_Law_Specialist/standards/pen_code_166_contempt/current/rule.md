## Statutory Text (current — operative provisions)

### § 166(a) — Misdemeanor Contempts

A person is guilty of a misdemeanor for any of the following:
1. Disorderly, contemptuous, or insolent behavior in court during proceedings;
2. Same behavior before a referee, jury, or other authorized proceeding;
3. Breach of peace or noise tending to interrupt court proceedings;
4. **Willful disobedience of the terms of a process or court order** (including out-of-state orders and pretrial orders);
5. Willful resistance to lawful order or process of a court;
6. Contumacious refusal to be sworn or to answer material questions as a witness;
7. Publication of false or grossly inaccurate reports of court proceedings;
8. Presenting unauthorized affidavits or representations in aggravation or mitigation at sentencing.

### § 166(c) — Enhanced Penalty: Violation of Protective Orders

Willful and knowing violation of specific protective or stay-away orders — including orders under § 136.2, § 1203.097(a)(2), elder/dependent adult abuse orders, Fam. Code §§ 6320/6389, exclusion orders, and conduct-enjoining orders — is punishable by:
- Up to **1 year county jail**; and
- Up to **$1,000 fine**.

Mandatory minimum **48 hours** in jail if physical injury results from the violation.

**Second conviction within 7 years** involving violence or credible threat: county jail up to 1 year OR state prison 16 months / 2 / 3 years.

## The Critical Provision — § 166(a)(4)

Subdivision (a)(4) — willful disobedience of a court order — is the most frequently charged contempt provision in California criminal practice. It covers:
- Violations of protective orders (DVRO, CLETS, stay-away orders);
- Violations of pretrial release conditions (no-contact orders);
- Violations of probation conditions that are also court orders;
- Violations of out-of-state court orders registered in California.

The willfulness element requires that the defendant knew of the order and deliberately violated it. Accidental or unknowing violation is not criminal contempt under § 166.

---

## Domestic Violence Context — § 166(c)

In domestic violence protective order violations, § 166(c) imposes enhanced consequences and mandatory minimums. DV protective order violations are among the most frequently prosecuted contempts. The enhanced penalty applies regardless of whether actual harm occurred — the violation itself triggers the enhanced penalty, with additional enhancement if physical injury results.

---

## Distinction from Civil Contempt

California courts may exercise civil contempt (remedial, coercive — designed to compel compliance) or criminal contempt (punitive — designed to punish past violation). Section 166 is criminal contempt. Civil contempt is governed by CCP § 1209 et seq. and the family court has concurrent contempt power over its own orders.

---

## Plain English

Criminal contempt under § 166 covers a wide range of conduct that disrespects or defies court authority. The most common charge in practice is violating a protective order — any knowing violation of a domestic violence, elder abuse, or civil harassment order. Even a single text message to a protected party can constitute contempt if a court order prohibits contact. Enhanced penalties apply to protective order violations, and a second conviction involving violence or threats can result in state prison.
