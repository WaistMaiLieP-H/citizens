# Cal. Penal Code § 1054.5 — Criminal Discovery Enforcement

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_1054_5_DISCOVERY_ENFORCEMENT
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current — key provisions)

**(a)** No order requiring discovery shall be made in criminal cases except as provided in this chapter. This chapter shall be the only means by which the defendant may compel the disclosure or production of information from prosecuting attorneys, law enforcement agencies, or any other persons or agencies which the prosecuting attorney or investigating agency may have employed to assist them.

**(b)** Before a party may seek court enforcement of any disclosure required by this chapter, the party shall **make an informal request of opposing counsel** for the desired materials. If within **15 days** opposing counsel fails to provide the materials, the party may seek a court order. Upon a showing that a party has not complied with § 1054.1 or § 1054.3 **and** that the moving party complied with the informal procedure, a court may make any order necessary to enforce this chapter, including:
- Immediate disclosure;
- Contempt proceedings;
- Delaying or prohibiting the testimony of a witness;
- Exclusion of real evidence;
- Continuance of the matter;
- Advising the jury of any failure or refusal to disclose or of untimely disclosure.

**(c)** The court may prohibit the testimony of a witness pursuant to subdivision (b) **only if all other sanctions have been exhausted**. The court **shall not dismiss** a charge pursuant to subdivision (b) unless required by the United States Constitution.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 1054.5)*

---

## The Informal Request Prerequisite

A motion for discovery sanctions requires first making an informal request. The informal request must precede the court motion, and 15 days must pass without compliance. This is a mandatory prerequisite — courts have discretion to deny motions that do not follow this procedure.

---

## Sanctions Hierarchy

Courts apply sanctions in escalating order:
1. Continuance (to allow production);
2. Jury instruction regarding the failure to disclose;
3. Witness exclusion (requires exhaustion of lesser sanctions first);
4. Contempt proceedings;
5. Charge dismissal only if constitutionally required (e.g., Brady violation so severe that proceeding would deny due process).

---

## This Chapter Is Exclusive

Section 1054.5(a) establishes that this chapter — Cal. Pen. Code §§ 1054–1054.10 — is the exclusive mechanism for compelling criminal discovery in California courts. Defendants and prosecutors cannot use civil discovery mechanisms or other statutory schemes to obtain criminal case discovery.

---

## Plain English

If the prosecution fails to comply with its § 1054.1 disclosure obligations, the defendant must first send an informal letter requesting the materials. If the prosecution doesn't respond within 15 days, the defendant can go to court. The court can order immediate disclosure, exclude witnesses or evidence, continue the trial, or instruct the jury about the non-disclosure. Outright dismissal of charges is only available if the Constitution requires it — typically when the withholding amounts to a Brady violation so egregious that a fair trial is impossible.
