# Context — Penal Code § 1054.5 (Discovery Enforcement)

## What This Standard Does

§ 1054.5 is the enforcement engine for § 1054.1. The disclosure obligations in § 1054.1 are meaningless without a remedy. § 1054.5 provides the remedy ladder — from informal request through contempt and witness exclusion to jury advisory — and defines the court's authority to sanction non-compliance. For case #7 (04-23-01959), this is the mechanism that converts the 23-item Brady demand into court-ordered disclosure if the prosecution resists.

## The Mandatory Pre-Filing Step: Informal Request

§ 1054.5(b) imposes a two-step sequencing requirement before court enforcement:

**Step 1 — Informal request:** Before filing any motion for discovery enforcement, the party must make an informal request of opposing counsel. The Brady demand letter (see drafts/brady_demand_04_23_01959_draft.md) serves this function. It must be specific enough to put the prosecution on notice of what is being requested.

**Step 2 — 15-day wait:** If the prosecution fails to provide the requested materials within 15 days, the moving party may then seek a court order. Clock runs from the date of the informal request.

**Tactical note:** Send the Brady demand by certified mail or email with read receipt. Document the date of the informal request precisely — the 15-day period is jurisdictional. A motion filed before the 15 days expire will be denied as premature.

## The Sanction Ladder

Upon a showing of (1) non-compliance with § 1054.1 or § 1054.3 AND (2) completion of the informal procedure, the court may impose any of the following in escalating order:

| Sanction Level | Sanction | Tactical Use |
|---------------|---------|--------------|
| 1 | **Immediate disclosure** | Ordered production of the specific withheld items |
| 2 | **Contempt proceedings** | Against the attorney personally for willful non-compliance |
| 3 | **Continuance** | Delay trial to allow time to obtain and review materials |
| 4 | **Witness delay/prohibition** | Bar prosecution witnesses whose testimony relies on withheld evidence |
| 5 | **Real evidence prohibition** | Exclude physical evidence that was not timely disclosed |
| 6 | **Jury advisory** | Court tells the jury about prosecution's disclosure failures |

The sanction ladder is not strictly sequential — the court has discretion to impose any combination. However, witness exclusion (level 4) is available only after all other sanctions are exhausted (§ 1054.5(c)). Dismissal is not available under § 1054.5(b) unless constitutionally required.

## The Constitutional Floor: Brady-Based Dismissal

§ 1054.5(c) bars dismissal under the statute — but the Constitution is a different matter. Where the prosecution's Brady violation is so egregious that no lesser remedy can cure the prejudice, dismissal may be available under the due process clause of the 14th Amendment. This argument goes beyond the statute and into the constitutional realm. Courts grant it rarely but it is preserved as a live claim where:

- The suppressed evidence was material to the entire case (not just one issue)
- The prosecution acted in bad faith
- The defendant was unable to cure the prejudice through continuance or other means

For case #7, if the APD incident report, call logs, and child forensic interview materials were withheld for strategic reasons, the bad faith argument is available.

## Jury Advisory as Strategic Leverage

§ 1054.5(b) last sentence: "the court may advise the jury of any failure or refusal to disclose and of any untimely disclosure." This sanction is underused. A jury advisory — telling the jury that the prosecution refused to disclose evidence — creates an adverse inference without requiring the jury to see the evidence itself. The jury may reasonably infer that what was withheld would have helped the defense.

**Strategic note:** File the enforcement motion on a schedule that ensures the jury advisory issue is live before trial. If the prosecution discloses late (after the 15-day period), the untimely disclosure triggers the advisory even without an order. Document every disclosure date.

1. **Serve Brady demand** (drafted at drafts/brady_demand_04_23_01959_draft.md) via certified mail on the DA's office handling 04-23-01959.
2. **Calendar 15-day deadline** — date certain after service.
3. **If no response within 15 days:** File motion to compel under § 1054.5(b) with:
   - Proof of informal request (copy of Brady demand + proof of service)
   - Proof of 15-day non-response (absence of any response, or deficient response)
   - Specific showing of non-compliance with § 1054.1 categories
   - Request for specific sanctions (immediate disclosure + jury advisory at minimum)
4. **At hearing:** The court must find (1) non-compliance with § 1054.1 or § 1054.3, and (2) completion of the informal procedure. Both are required before any sanction issues.
5. **If prosecution complies late:** Document the date. Request jury advisory for the untimely disclosure at trial.

## Interaction with Brady/Giglio Constitutional Obligations

§ 1054.5 is the statutory mechanism; Brady v. Maryland (1963) and Giglio v. United States (1972) are the constitutional floor. The two operate in parallel:

- **Statutory violation (§ 1054.1 + § 1054.5):** Available remedies are on the sanction ladder — disclosure, contempt, continuance, witness exclusion, jury advisory.
- **Constitutional violation (Brady/Giglio):** Available remedies include exclusion of evidence, dismissal in egregious cases, and reversal on appeal (where the suppressed evidence was material under the Strickler materiality test).

A defendant can pursue both tracks simultaneously. A Brady motion filed before trial (to compel disclosure) is different from a Brady motion filed after conviction (to establish prejudice for reversal). Both use the same underlying obligation but different procedural vehicles.

## Bilateral Note

§ 1054.5 is a defense mechanism — it enforces the prosecution's § 1054.1 obligations against the defendant. The steward uses it as a defendant to compel production of favorable evidence. There is no bilateral use; this standard is one-directional.
