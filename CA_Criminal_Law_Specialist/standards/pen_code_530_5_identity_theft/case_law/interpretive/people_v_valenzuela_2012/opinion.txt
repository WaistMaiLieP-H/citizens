FACTS:

The case involved the nature and classification of the § 530.5 identity theft offense in California, specifically whether identity theft constitutes a "theft" crime for purposes of reclassification and sentencing analysis.

---

HOLDING:

§ 530.5 — "UNIQUE THEFT CRIME" NOT LARCENY; PII RETENTION IS NOT POSSESSION:

1. **§ 530.5 is a "unique theft crime" — not larceny.** Section 530.5 resides in the chapter of the Penal Code titled "False Personation and Cheats," not the larceny chapter. The Legislature did not categorize identity theft as a theft offense under traditional larceny definitions.

2. **"Retention of personal identifying information of another is not a possession crime, but is a unique theft crime."** (Valenzuela (2012) 205 Cal.App.4th 800, 808.) The statutory framework reflects the Legislature's recognition that identity theft is categorically different from property theft.

3. **Classification consequences.** Because § 530.5 is not traditional larceny, reclassification arguments that apply to theft crimes (e.g., Proposition 47 shoplifting reclassification) do not automatically apply to § 530.5 violations. Subsequent cases (Prop 47 era) have relied on Valenzuela to hold that § 530.5 does not reduce to a misdemeanor under Prop 47's shoplifting reclassification.

4. **§ 530.5 enhancement and trafficking subdivisions remain available.** The legislative structure — with separate subds. for multi-victim (c)(3), prior conviction (c)(2), and trafficking (d)(1)/(d)(2) — is consistent with Valenzuela's classification. Each subdivision escalates based on scale or mode, not simply value of property taken.

---

WHAT VALENZUELA MEANS FOR MULTI-VICTIM AND TRAFFICKING ANALYSIS:

The "unique theft crime" classification in Valenzuela supports applying § 530.5's enhancement subdivisions to modern digital identity theft patterns without reducing the offense to property-value-based analysis. For synthetic identity schemes:
- subd. (a): individual victim
- subd. (c)(3): 10+ victim PII acquisition or retention
- subd. (d): trafficking of stolen PII with intent to defraud

---

COORDINATION WITH FEDERAL STATUTES:

Federal identity theft statutes (18 USC §§ 1028, 1028A) provide additional enhancements and mandatory consecutive sentencing under § 1028A. Prosecutors may charge both California § 530.5 and federal § 1028 concurrently. For multi-victim identity theft schemes:
- California: § 530.5(c)(3) for 10+ victims
- Federal: § 1028A mandatory consecutive 2-year sentence for "aggravated identity theft" on top of any predicate offense sentence

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

The documented patterns (SIM swap, synthetic profiles, Treasury securities, UA342) may involve both individual and multi-victim dimensions:
1. If any investigation shows the perpetrators held or trafficked PII of multiple persons beyond the steward, subd. (c)(3) applies
2. If the perpetrators sold or transferred the steward's PII to third parties for use in fraud, subd. (d) applies
3. The UA342 employment identity replacement, if it involves coordinated access to multiple workers' records, may trigger subd. (c)(3)
