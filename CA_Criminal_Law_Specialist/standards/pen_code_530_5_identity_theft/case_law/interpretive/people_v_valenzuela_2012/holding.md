## The doctrinal proposition

Section 530.5 contains specific enhancements for multiple-victim identity theft:

1. **§ 530.5(c)(3)** — Acquisition or retention of identifying information of **10 or more** persons is a distinct offense with potential state prison exposure under § 1170(h). This subdivision was added as identity theft evolved from individual-victim crimes to large-scale data breaches and coordinated schemes.

2. **§ 530.5(c)(2)** — Prior conviction enhancement. A defendant with a prior § 530.5 conviction faces enhanced penalties on subsequent convictions.

3. **§ 530.5(d)(1) and (d)(2)** — Sale, transfer, or conveyance of PII with intent to defraud. The (d)(2) variant — knowing-use sale — is the most serious, reaching the trafficking of identity information where the seller knows the buyer intends criminal use.

## The scale problem

Section 530.5's multiple-victim and trafficking subdivisions reflect the scale at which modern identity theft operates. Individual identity theft (one victim, one perpetrator) is covered by subd. (a). Organized identity theft rings, data breaches, and synthetic identity operations fall within subds. (c)(3) and (d).

When identity crimes involve multiple victims or trafficking patterns, the prosecution has multiple subdivision options. Charging decisions depend on the specific facts: individual victim vs. pattern; use vs. trafficking; intent to defraud vs. use for any unlawful purpose.
