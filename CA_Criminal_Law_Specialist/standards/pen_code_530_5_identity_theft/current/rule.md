# Cal. Penal Code § 530.5 — Identity Theft

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_530_5_IDENTITY_THEFT
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current — core provisions)

**(a)** Every person who **willfully obtains personal identifying information** of another person, and **uses that information for any unlawful purpose**, including to obtain, or attempt to obtain, credit, goods, services, real property, or medical information without the consent of that person, is guilty of a public offense, and upon conviction shall be punished by:
- Fine;
- Imprisonment in a county jail not to exceed **one year**;
- Both; or
- Imprisonment pursuant to subdivision (h) of § 1170.

**(b)** Where identity information is used to commit an additional crime, the court records shall reflect that the person whose identity was falsely used did not commit that crime.

**(c)(1)** Intent-to-defraud acquisition or retention — public offense/fine/jail ≤1 year.

**(c)(2)** Second offense — enhanced penalties including potential state prison.

**(c)(3)** Acquisition/retention of information of **10 or more persons** — public offense/fine/jail or state prison.

**(d)(1)** Sale, transfer, or conveyance of personal identifying information with intent to defraud — public offense/fine/jail or state prison.

**(d)(2)** Knowing-use sale/transfer — public offense/fine/jail or state prison.

**(e)** Mail theft (as defined in 18 U.S.C. § 1708) — public offense/fine/jail ≤1 year; does not preclude other prosecutions.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 530.5)*

---

## "Personal Identifying Information" — § 530.55

Personal identifying information includes: name, address, telephone number, SSN, driver's license number, account number, PIN or password, employee ID number, mother's maiden name, demand deposit account number, savings account number, checking account number, credit card number, ATM card number, digital signature, debit card number, taxpayer identification number, passport number, and any other identifying information that could be used alone or in combination to commit fraud.

---

## Elements of the Core Offense (§ 530.5(a))

1. The defendant **willfully obtained** personal identifying information of another person;
2. The defendant **used** that information;
3. **For any unlawful purpose** — including, but not limited to, obtaining credit, goods, services, real property, or medical information;
4. **Without the consent** of the person whose information was used.

---

## Civil Remedies — § 530.8

The identity theft victim may petition the court for an order requiring the impersonating defendant to pay:
- Actual damages (including costs of clearing the victim's name);
- Attorney's fees;
- And may enjoin future violations.

---

## Victim's Right to Investigate — § 530.8 PRA Access

Section 530.8 gives identity theft victims the right to obtain copies of applications and transaction records that fraudulently used their identity, from businesses and government agencies.

---

## Interaction with § 529 (False Personation) and § 182 (Conspiracy)

Identity theft and false personation are frequently charged together when:
- The defendant obtained identity information (§ 530.5) and then
- Acted as that person in legal or financial transactions (§ 529).

When multiple defendants work together to steal and use identities, conspiracy (§ 182) is added.

---

## Plain English

Identity theft under § 530.5 is obtaining someone's personal information — Social Security number, account numbers, passwords — and using it for any unauthorized purpose. You don't need to successfully commit fraud — attempting to obtain goods, credit, or services using someone else's information is enough. Repeat offenders and those who steal the information of 10 or more people face state prison exposure. Victims have the right to see the fraudulent applications and transactions made in their name and may pursue civil remedies.
