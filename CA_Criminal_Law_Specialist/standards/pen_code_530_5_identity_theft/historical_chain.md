# Historical Chain — Pen. Code § 530.5 (Identity Theft)

| Year | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-1997 | Identity theft prosecuted under general fraud/false personation statutes | Inadequate framework for modern identity theft |
| 1997 | Pen. Code § 530.5 enacted | California's first dedicated identity theft statute |
| 1998 | Federal Identity Theft Assumption Deterrence Act; 18 USC § 1028 expanded | Federal parallel |
| 2000s | §§ 530.55 (definitions), 530.6 (civil remedy), 530.7 (expungement), 530.8 (disclosure) added | Comprehensive framework |
| 2004 | Aggravated identity theft federal statute enacted (18 USC § 1028A) | Enhanced federal penalties |
| 2010s | Cyber-era expansions: IP addresses, biometric data, electronic signatures added to § 530.55 definition | Modern scope |
| 2020s | Interactive computer service immunity clarifications | Tech industry accommodations |
| 2026 | Current form | In force |

## The wound

Before dedicated identity theft statutes, prosecutors had to shoehorn identity crimes into general fraud (§ 484), false personation (§ 529), forgery (§§ 470-477), or theft statutes. None of these cleanly reached the core harm of identity theft: the unauthorized acquisition and use of personal information, separate from any specific economic injury.

The wound was **the legal system's inability to address identity theft as a distinct harm**: victims had to prove they had suffered a specific economic loss to have their claims taken seriously, and prosecutors had to prove their cases under statutes not designed for the modern context.

## The promise

Section 530.5 promises: **unauthorized use of personal information is itself a crime, independent of whether the victim suffered specific economic loss**. The 1997 enactment recognized identity theft as a distinct category of harm requiring dedicated prosecution tools. Subsequent amendments have kept pace with technological evolution — adding biometric data, IP addresses, and electronic signatures as the scope of identifying information expanded.
