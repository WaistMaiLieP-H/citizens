# Context — § 530.5 Origin (1997)

## The wound

By the mid-1990s, identity theft had become a documented epidemic. The rise of credit bureau databases, widespread SSN use as an authentication factor, and the emerging internet created the infrastructure for systematic identity theft at scale. But California had no specific identity theft statute — prosecutors could only charge theft (which required taking physical property) or fraud (which required proving a specific fraudulent scheme).

The gap: a person who obtained another's SSN and used it to open credit cards, obtain medical care, or create a fraudulent identity did not "take" any physical property. The 1997 statute filled this gap: obtaining someone's personal identifying information and using it for any unlawful purpose is a crime.

## The "any unlawful purpose" breadth

§ 530.5's defining characteristic is that it covers any unlawful use of stolen identity — not just financial fraud. A person who uses another's identity to avoid arrest, to create false social media profiles, to obtain medical care, or to gain employment under a false identity all violate § 530.5.

This breadth directly captures the Ryan McClaran / ward system pattern: using the steward's SSN, phone number identity, and professional credentials for unauthorized purposes — whether financial, legal, or communications-related — is § 530.5 conduct.

## The SIM swap connection

A SIM swap gives the attacker access to phone-based authentication. The phone number is "personal identifying information" under § 530.55(b)'s list. Using someone's phone number identity to access their accounts is § 530.5 — it is using PII for an unlawful purpose (unauthorized account access) without consent.
