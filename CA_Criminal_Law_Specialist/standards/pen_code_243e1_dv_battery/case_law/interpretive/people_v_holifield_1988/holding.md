# People v. Holifield (1988) 205 Cal.App.3d 993

**Citation:** People v. Holifield (1988) 205 Cal.App.3d 993, 252 Cal.Rptr. 729
**Decided:** November 1988
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District
**Disposition:** Affirmed conviction; articulated the multi-factor test for "cohabiting" under § 243(e)(1).
**Statute construed:** Pen. Code § 243(e)(1), "cohabiting" relationship element

## Facts

The defendant was convicted of domestic violence battery under § 243(e)(1). The relationship between the defendant and the victim was at issue — the two had lived together for a relatively short period in a motel arrangement, shared some expenses, and had an intimate relationship, but had not been married, not been registered as domestic partners, and did not share an established residence in the traditional sense. The defendant argued on appeal that "cohabiting" required a more substantial or permanent living arrangement than the facts established.

## Holding

The Court of Appeal affirmed and articulated what has become the standard multi-factor test for cohabitation under § 243(e)(1):

**The "Holifield factors" — whether two persons are "cohabiting" under § 243(e)(1) depends on:**

1. **Sexual relations between the parties while sharing the same residence**
2. **Sharing of income or expenses**
3. **Joint use or ownership of property**
4. **The parties holding themselves out as husband and wife** (the 1988 formulation; modern courts apply this more broadly)
5. **The continuity of the relationship**
6. **The length of the relationship**

No single factor is dispositive. The totality of the circumstances determines whether the relationship rises to "cohabiting" within the meaning of § 243(e)(1). A court may find cohabitation on facts that would not support a common-law marriage determination.

The court expressly rejected a narrow definition that would have required a traditional or permanent living arrangement. The statute's protective purpose — reaching violence in intimate partner contexts that share the dynamics of DV — required a flexible definition that could capture non-traditional living arrangements.

## Why this is load-bearing

*Holifield* is the standard authority cited in every § 243(e)(1) case where the cohabitation element is contested. Its multi-factor test has been followed by every subsequent Court of Appeal decision in California and has been applied to:

- Short-term live-in relationships
- Motel or temporary accommodations
- Arrangements where the parties retained separate primary residences
- Dating relationships with partial cohabitation
- LGBTQ+ relationships (the "husband and wife" language is applied broadly as "domestic partners")

The flexibility of the *Holifield* factors means that prosecutors can charge § 243(e)(1) in a wide range of intimate partner contexts, and defendants must usually attack multiple factors to defeat the cohabitation element.

## Defense strategy under Holifield

To defeat the cohabitation element:

1. **Dispute the sexual relations factor** — if there was no sexual relationship, the first factor fails (though it is not dispositive)
2. **Dispute shared residence** — if the parties had separate primary residences, the "sharing the same residence" factor fails
3. **Dispute expense sharing** — if finances were kept entirely separate, factor 2 fails
4. **Dispute property overlap** — no joint property, no shared household goods
5. **Dispute the duration factor** — a very brief relationship (e.g., single overnight) is unlikely to satisfy continuity or length
6. **Dispute the holding-out factor** — the parties never presented themselves as domestic partners

Even failing on individual factors, the defense can argue that the totality does not rise to "cohabiting" — particularly when the relationship is casual, transient, or primarily transactional.

## Relevance to the Hartmann matter

For the Hartmann case, the cohabitation element is relevant to any past § 243(e)(1) allegations and to the general framework for evaluating DV battery claims in the sixteen-year family law history. The *Holifield* factors provide a structured analysis:

1. Was there a shared residence at the relevant time?
2. Were expenses shared?
3. Was there joint property?
4. Was the relationship continuous?
5. How long had it existed?
6. Did the parties present themselves publicly as domestic partners?

Applied to the specific facts of any given alleged DV event, this framework identifies which elements the prosecution can prove and which the defense can challenge.

## Authority status

Published California Court of Appeal decision. Consistently followed by subsequent cases. Still the leading authority on cohabitation under § 243(e)(1). Cited in CALCRIM 840 and 841 (DV battery instructions).
