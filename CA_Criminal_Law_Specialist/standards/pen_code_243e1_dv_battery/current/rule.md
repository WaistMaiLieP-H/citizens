# Cal. Penal Code § 243(e)(1) — Domestic Violence Battery

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_243E1_DV_BATTERY
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(e)(1)** When a battery is committed against a **spouse, a person with whom the defendant is cohabiting, a person who is the parent of the defendant's child, former spouse, fiancé, or fiancée, or a person with whom the defendant currently has, or has previously had, a dating or engagement relationship**, the battery is punishable by a fine not exceeding **$2,000**, or by imprisonment in a county jail for a period of not more than **one year**, or by both that fine and imprisonment.

If probation is granted or the execution or imposition of the sentence is suspended, it shall be a condition thereof that the defendant participate in, for no less than **one year**, and successfully complete, a **batterer's treatment program** as described in § 1203.097, or if none is available, another appropriate counseling program designated by the court.

**(e)(2)** If probation is granted, the court may additionally require payment to a DV shelter-based program up to $5,000, or reimbursement of victim counseling costs.

**(e)(3)** If the defendant has a prior conviction under § 243(e) or § 273.5, there is a mandatory minimum **48 hours** in jail unless the court finds good cause to waive this requirement.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 243(e)(1))*

---

## Elements of DV Battery

Battery is the willful and unlawful use of force or violence upon the person of another (§ 242). For § 243(e)(1), the additional element is:

- The victim must be in one of the qualifying domestic relationships: **spouse, cohabitant, parent of defendant's child, former spouse, fiancé(e), or current or former dating/engagement partner**.

The force required is any harmful or offensive touching — it does not require visible injury. Unlike § 273.5 (corporal injury to domestic partner), § 243(e)(1) does not require a traumatic condition or visible injury.

---

## Punishment Structure

- **Base:** Misdemeanor — fine up to $2,000, county jail up to 1 year
- **Mandatory probation condition:** 1-year batterer's treatment program
- **Prior conviction (§ 243(e) or § 273.5):** Mandatory minimum 48 hours jail
- **Victim's work-related cost reimbursement:** Optional probation condition

---

## Distinction from § 273.5 (Corporal Injury)

| Feature | § 243(e)(1) | § 273.5 |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Injury required | No — any offensive touching | Yes — traumatic condition |
| Punishment | Misdemeanor only | Felony or misdemeanor |
| Common charge | First-time DV battery without injury | DV battery with visible injury or infliction |

In most DV cases, § 243(e)(1) is charged when there is no visible injury; § 273.5 is charged when there is. Both require the same qualifying domestic relationship.

---

## CLETS Reporting and Protective Orders

Upon arrest for § 243(e)(1), law enforcement must report to CLETS (California Law Enforcement Telecommunications System). Courts routinely issue emergency protective orders at arraignment and full protective orders at sentencing — including stay-away and no-contact orders enforceable as § 166 criminal contempt.

---

## Plain English

Domestic violence battery under § 243(e)(1) is a misdemeanor that covers any unwanted offensive touching — even without visible injury — against a domestic partner, spouse, cohabitant, co-parent, fiancé, or former dating partner. A shove, a grab, or pushing someone against a wall qualifies. Conviction requires at least one year in a batterer's treatment program as a probation condition. Prior DV convictions trigger mandatory jail time. This is the lower-tier DV charge; § 273.5 (which requires actual injury) is the more serious charge.
