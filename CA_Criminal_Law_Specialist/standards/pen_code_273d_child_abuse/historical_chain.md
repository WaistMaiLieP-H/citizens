# Historical Chain — Pen. Code § 273d

## Origin

Section 273d was enacted in **1965** as part of a broader legislative effort to address child abuse as a distinct criminal category separate from general assault and battery statutes. The 1965 enactment created the felony corporal punishment/traumatic condition framework that remains the statutory core today.

**Context:** The 1960s saw growing national awareness of "battered child syndrome" (Kempe et al., 1962 JAMA article). California was among the first states to enact specific criminal statutes targeting child abuse as distinguished from ordinary battery.

## Key amendments

**1987 — Probation conditions added:** The mandatory probation conditions in subdivision (c) (36-month minimum, child abuser's treatment program, protective order) were added following studies showing high recidivism in child abuse cases without treatment intervention.

**1992 — Child abuser's treatment program formalized:** Section 273.1 was enacted to specify the criteria that child abuser's treatment counseling programs must meet; § 273d(c)(3) cross-references § 273.1 to ensure only qualifying programs satisfy the mandatory condition.

**1994 — "Realignment" predecessor sentencing provisions:** The specific 2/4/6 year triad predates realignment. Post-2011 realignment (AB 109) moved the imprisonment to subdivision (h) of § 1170 (county jail for many non-serious, non-violent, non-sex-offense felonies), but § 273d as a child abuse offense has historically been treated as appropriate for state prison.

**2014–present:** No substantive amendments to the elements. Sentencing framework has remained stable. The 4-year prior-conviction enhancement in subdivision (b) has remained unchanged since the 1990s.

## Relationship to § 273a

Section 273a (child endangerment/cruelty) was enacted earlier and focuses on risk of harm. Section 273d was enacted as a parallel track specifically requiring actual infliction of injury. Over time the two statutes have been charged together when facts support both theories.

## CALCRIM instruction

CALCRIM No. 821 is the standard jury instruction for § 273d. It tracks the statutory elements precisely: willful infliction, cruel/inhuman punishment or injury, resulting in traumatic condition, on a child.
