HOLDING:

§ 273d FALSE ACCUSATION DEFENSE FRAMEWORK:

1. **False reports are a recognized problem in custody disputes.** Courts and practitioners recognize that child abuse allegations sometimes arise in the context of custody disputes motivated by tactical advantage in family court proceedings. The criminal defense may introduce evidence of the family law context to show motive for fabrication.

2. **Willfulness is a required element — but in false accusation cases, the existence of the act is the contested fact.** Section 273d requires the defendant "willfully" inflict the punishment or injury. Where the entire premise of the charge is a false accusation, willfulness is not the contested element; the existence of the alleged act is the contested fact.

3. **The source of the accusation is always relevant.** Defense may introduce evidence showing who made the initial report, the reporter's relationship to the defendant and the child, the reporter's motive, and whether the report was coordinated with a family law strategy. Mandatory reporter violations (reporting without reasonable suspicion) can also be raised.

4. **Child witness suggestibility doctrine.** In cases where the primary evidence is a child's statement, the defense may challenge the statement on suggestibility grounds. California courts recognize that children can be led to make inaccurate accusations through suggestive interviewing techniques, coaching, or contamination by adults with a stake in the outcome. (See In re Amber B. (1987) 191 Cal.App.3d 682 — recognizing suggestibility in dependency context; cross-applicable to criminal proceedings.)

5. **Expert testimony on false accusation dynamics is admissible.** California courts permit expert testimony on the dynamics of false accusation in child abuse cases, the reliability of child statements, and the effects of suggestive interviewing — subject to Evid. Code § 801 and § 352 balancing.
