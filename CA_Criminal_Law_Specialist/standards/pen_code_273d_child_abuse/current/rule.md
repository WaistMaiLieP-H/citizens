# Cal. Penal Code § 273d — Child Abuse (Corporal Punishment)

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_273D_CHILD_ABUSE
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** Any person who **willfully inflicts upon a child any cruel or inhuman corporal punishment or an injury resulting in a traumatic condition** is guilty of a **felony** and shall be punished by imprisonment pursuant to subdivision (h) of Section 1170 for **2, 4, or 6 years**, or in a county jail for not more than one year, by a fine of up to **$6,000**, or by both that imprisonment and fine.

**(b)** Any person found guilty of violating subdivision (a) shall receive a **4-year enhancement for a prior conviction** of that offense (subject to the 10-year decay rule for prior-conviction enhancements).

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 273d)*

---

## Elements

1. **Willful infliction** of corporal punishment or injury on a child;
2. The punishment or injury was **cruel or inhuman**;
3. The punishment or injury resulted in a **traumatic condition** (defined below).

---

## "Traumatic Condition" Definition

A traumatic condition is a condition of the body, such as a wound or external or internal injury, whether of a minor or serious nature, caused by a physical force. Visible marks — bruising, cuts, welts — satisfy the element. Internal injuries without external marks may also qualify.

---

## Reasonable Parental Discipline Defense

California law recognizes a parent's right to administer reasonable physical discipline to a child. Whether discipline is "reasonable" is evaluated on the facts: the degree of force, the child's age, the manner of punishment, and whether the force was used in good faith to correct behavior. Punishment that causes traumatic conditions generally exceeds "reasonable" — courts have held that leaving marks, welts, or bruises from physical punishment crosses the line into § 273d territory.

---

## § 273d(c) — Mandatory Probation Conditions

If probation granted:
- Minimum **36 months** probation;
- Criminal court protective order protecting victim;
- Successful completion of **at least one year** of child abuser's treatment counseling (must begin immediately upon grant of probation);
- Documentation of enrollment to court within 30 days; quarterly progress reports;
- Abstinence from drugs/alcohol if offense was drug-related;
- Court may waive conditions only on record findings in best interests of justice.

---

## Distinction from § 273a (Child Endangerment)

| Feature | § 273a | § 273d |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Conduct | Endangerment, permitting suffering, failure to protect | Willful corporal punishment resulting in traumatic condition |
| Injury required | No (endangerment) | Yes — traumatic condition |
| Base charge | Wobbler (§ 273a(a)) or misdemeanor (§ 273a(b)) | Felony |

---

## Mandatory Child Abuse Reporting Intersection

Any health care provider, school employee, or other mandated reporter who observes signs of § 273d abuse on a child must report to law enforcement or child protective services. Failure to report is a separate misdemeanor (§ 11166(c)).

---

## Plain English

Section 273d is the felony child abuse statute for physical corporal punishment that leaves a mark or causes injury. Hitting a child hard enough to bruise, cut, or leave welts is § 273d — not "reasonable discipline." It is a felony with state prison exposure and mandatory treatment program conditions if probation is granted. A prior conviction adds four additional years to the sentence. Mandated reporters who observe the signs of § 273d abuse are required to report it.
