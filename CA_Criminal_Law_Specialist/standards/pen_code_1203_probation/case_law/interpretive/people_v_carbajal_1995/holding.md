# People v. Carbajal (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1114

**Citation:** People v. Carbajal (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1114, 43 Cal.Rptr.2d 681
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** 1995
**Statutes construed:** Penal Code § 1203 — probation conditions; nexus requirement; reasonableness standard for probation conditions; relationship to criminal conduct

## Facts

The defendant was convicted and placed on probation with conditions that the court imposed to promote rehabilitation and protect the community. The defendant challenged specific conditions as unreasonable, arguing they were unrelated to the crime of conviction or the defendant's personal history.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court established the framework for evaluating probation conditions:

1. **Nexus test — three-part inquiry.** A probation condition is valid if it:
   - (a) Has a relationship to the crime of which the defendant was convicted, OR
   - (b) Relates to conduct that is not criminal but that the court reasonably believes the defendant is likely to re-engage in if not prohibited, OR
   - (c) Relates to future criminality generally and is reasonably designed to prevent the type of conduct that led to the conviction

2. **Broad trial court discretion.** Trial courts have broad discretion in imposing probation conditions. An appellate court will not disturb a probation condition unless the trial court abused its discretion — i.e., unless the condition is arbitrary or bears no reasonable relationship to rehabilitation.

3. **Conditions cannot be patently unreasonable.** Even with broad discretion, conditions that are overbroad, vague, or that impose unconstitutional burdens on fundamental rights are subject to challenge. Constitutional challenges (First Amendment, Fourth Amendment) are evaluated by the sentencing court.

4. **Rehabilitative purpose governs.** The primary purpose of probation is rehabilitation, not additional punishment. Conditions must be anchored to that purpose, though the nexus can be indirect.

## The nexus test applied

**Relationship to the crime** — A condition directly related to the offense (e.g., no contact with the victim in a battery case; no alcohol for a DUI offense) satisfies the nexus test on its face.

**Likely future conduct** — Even if not connected to the specific crime, a condition can be valid if the record shows the defendant has a pattern of conduct the condition is designed to interrupt (e.g., drug testing for a person with a substance abuse history even if the conviction was not drug-related).

**General crime prevention** — The broadest prong; conditions designed to reduce the risk of recidivism generally are permissible if not arbitrary.

## Why this matters for probation strategy

For a defendant facing probation (as opposed to incarceration) on a § 273d or § 243(e)(1) charge, Carbajal defines both:

**What to expect:** The prosecution may seek broad conditions — no contact, mandatory counseling, substance abuse testing, GPS monitoring. Each condition must satisfy at least one Carbajal prong.

**What to challenge:** Any condition that (a) has no nexus to the offense, the defendant's history, or future criminality risk, (b) is facially overbroad (e.g., no contact with minors when the defendant is a parent), or (c) imposes constitutional burdens beyond what the offense justifies.

**Parenting-specific:** For a parent convicted of § 273d with sole-custody or shared-custody of children, a condition preventing contact with the children must be evaluated under Carbajal AND the Family Code framework. A probation condition cannot strip a parent of constitutionally protected family rights beyond what the offense and child safety require.

## Relevance to case #7

For case 04-23-01959 (§ 273d allegation), if probation is granted:

1. **Mandatory counseling** (domestic violence or parent-child counseling) — nexus to § 273d clear; appropriate
2. **No contact with child victim** — must be carefully tailored; overbroad no-contact condition that prevents all contact with children when defendant is custodial parent should be challenged under Carbajal
3. **Firearms prohibition** — separately mandated by statute; Carbajal does not govern
4. **Substance abuse testing** — requires nexus; if no substance abuse in the facts, condition should be challenged
5. **GPS monitoring** — privacy interest; must satisfy Carbajal + reasonableness; overbroad if not tied to a specific risk in the facts

## Authority status

California Supreme Court (1995). Controlling authority on the nexus test for probation conditions. Applied in every California probation sentencing.
