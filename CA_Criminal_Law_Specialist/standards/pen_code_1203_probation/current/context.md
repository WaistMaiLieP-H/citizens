# Context — Penal Code § 1203 (Probation)

## What This Standard Does

§ 1203 governs whether a convicted defendant receives a suspended sentence with supervised community release (probation) rather than incarceration. It is the gateway to the most favorable outcome in a contested criminal case — staying out of custody while completing court-ordered conditions. For case #7 (04-23-01959), § 1203 is the sentencing framework if the case proceeds to conviction and diversion (§§ 1001.36, 1001.95) is unavailable.

## Core Definition

**Probation** = suspension of imposition or execution of sentence + conditional and revocable release + supervision by probation officer.

**Conditional sentence** = same suspension + conditions established by court + NO probation officer supervision. Available for misdemeanors.

**Key distinction:** Probation is revocable — a violation of conditions can result in the original sentence being imposed. This is the leverage the prosecution has during the probationary period.

## Eligibility Framework

### Felony probation (subd. (b))

When a defendant is convicted of a felony and is eligible:
1. Court must refer to probation officer before sentencing
2. Probation officer investigates and submits written report with recommendation
3. Report must be available to both attorneys at least 5 days before hearing (9 days if requested)
4. Court holds hearing, considers report, determines suitability
5. Court may grant probation if: (a) circumstances in mitigation, OR (b) ends of justice served by probation

### Misdemeanor probation / conditional sentence (subd. (d))

Misdemeanor defendants: court may refer to probation officer OR summarily pronounce conditional sentence. No mandatory report. Simpler process.

## Ineligible categories (subd. (e)) — the "unusual case" exception

Probation is presumptively barred for certain serious offenses. The list includes violent and serious crimes but notably does NOT include:
- § 273d (child abuse) — NOT listed in § 1203(e); probation is available
- § 422 (criminal threats) — NOT listed; probation is available
- § 273a (child endangerment) — NOT listed; probation is available

**Critical finding for case #7:** PC § 273d (child abuse) and the likely charges in 04-23-01959 are NOT in the § 1203(e) exclusion list. Probation is available as a sentencing outcome if the case proceeds to conviction and diversion is unavailable.

The only exclusion that might apply: if the offense qualifies as a "serious felony" under § 1192.7(c) or "violent felony" under § 667.5(c), AND the defendant was on probation for a felony at the time of the new offense (subd. (k)). Verify current supervision status.

## The Mitigation Standard

§ 1203(b)(3): Court grants probation if it finds:
- **Circumstances in mitigation** of the prescribed punishment, OR
- **Ends of justice** would be served

Neither requires proof by a specific evidentiary standard — the court has broad discretion. The probation report is the primary document the court relies on. Defense counsel (or pro se defendant) can submit a sentencing memorandum addressing the mitigation factors.

## Mitigation factors to develop for case #7

The following facts support the mitigation argument if the case reaches sentencing:

1. **No prior criminal history:** If the steward has no prior convictions, this is the most powerful single factor. First-time offenders overwhelmingly receive probation over incarceration.

2. **Steward is primary caretaker of children:** Incarceration would deprive children of their sole custodial parent. Courts regularly weigh the impact on dependents.

3. **Absence of actual injury to child:** If the § 273d charge is not supported by evidence of actual physical injury (i.e., the charge rests entirely on the false accusation), the absence of actual harm is a mitigation factor even if the charge results in conviction.

4. **Coordinated false accusation context:** Even if the court does not fully credit the setup theory, evidence of the prior false report history (OPD June 2009, multiple unsubstantiated CPS referrals) provides context that the court may weigh in mitigation.

5. **Employment/community ties:** Stable employment (UA Local 342), community ties, support network. Probation officers weight these heavily in recommendations.

6. **Mental health treatment willingness:** If § 1001.36 diversion is unavailable at the conviction stage, the steward's willingness to engage in mental health treatment remains relevant to probation suitability (§ 1203.049 treatment as condition).

## Defense strategy: probation as the floor

In litigation strategy, probation is the sentencing floor for case #7:
- **Best outcome:** Diversion under § 1001.36 or § 1001.95 → charges dismissed
- **Mid outcome:** Acquittal at trial
- **Floor outcome:** Conviction → probation (not incarceration)

Preserving probation eligibility means:
- Not triggering any § 1203(e) presumptive ineligibility
- Not being on existing felony probation (subd. (k))
- Developing a strong mitigation narrative for the probation report

## Interaction with § 1203.4 (expungement)

After successful completion of probation, the defendant may petition under § 1203.4 to withdraw the guilty/no contest plea or set aside a verdict of guilty. The conviction is then dismissed. This is not full record sealing but provides significant relief. For case #7: even if the worst outcome occurs (conviction + probation), § 1203.4 relief is available after probation completion.
