# Cal. Penal Code § 1203 — Probation

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_1203_PROBATION
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text — Core Provisions (current)

**(a)** "Probation" means the suspension of the imposition or execution of a sentence and the order of conditional and revocable release in the community under the supervision of a probation officer. Both conditional sentence and probation are authorized whenever probation is authorized for infractions or misdemeanors.

**(b)(1)** If a person is convicted of a felony and is eligible for probation, before judgment is pronounced, the court shall immediately refer the matter to a probation officer to investigate and report upon the circumstances surrounding the crime and the prior history and record of the person.

**(b)(3)** The court shall hear and determine the application and the suitability of probation. If the court determines that there are circumstances in mitigation of the punishment prescribed by law or that the ends of justice would be served by granting probation, it may place the person on probation.

**(e)** Except in unusual cases in which the interests of justice would best be served, probation **shall not be granted** to persons convicted of: armed robbery, carjacking, rape with force, murder, kidnapping, or conspiracy to commit those crimes while armed; persons who used a deadly weapon; persons who inflicted great bodily injury or torture; persons with two prior felony convictions; specified public official corruption; specified drug offenses; discharge of firearm from vehicle.

**(f)** When probation is granted in a case within subdivision (e), the court shall specify on the record and enter in the minutes the circumstances indicating that the interests of justice would best be served.

**(k)** Probation shall not be granted to any person convicted of a violent felony (§ 667.5(c)) or serious felony (§ 1192.7(c)) who was on probation for a felony offense at the time of the new felony.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 1203; extensively amended through 2024)*

---

## Probation Report Requirement

For any felony conviction where probation is a possibility, the court must refer to the probation officer for a written report before pronouncing judgment. The probation officer investigates the circumstances of the crime and the defendant's history and makes recommendations on probation suitability and conditions.

---

## Probation Conditions

Probation conditions may include:
- Supervised release under probation officer oversight;
- Drug and alcohol testing;
- Counseling or treatment program completion;
- Community service;
- Payment of restitution to victim;
- Compliance with protective orders;
- Search and seizure conditions;
- Restrictions on contact with co-defendants or victims.

---

## Probation Revocation

If a probationer violates conditions, the court may:
- Issue a bench warrant for arrest;
- Hold a probation violation hearing;
- Revoke probation and impose the suspended sentence.

The probationer is entitled to a hearing, the right to counsel, and written notice of the alleged violation — but the evidentiary standard is "preponderance of the evidence," not "beyond a reasonable doubt."

---

## AB 1950 (2021) — Maximum Probation Terms

Assembly Bill 1950 (effective Jan. 1, 2021) substantially reduced maximum probation lengths:
- **Felonies:** Maximum probation term generally reduced to 2 years (with exceptions for certain serious felonies);
- **Misdemeanors:** Maximum reduced to 1 year.

Courts may not impose probation terms in excess of these limits absent a specific statutory exception.

---

## Plain English

Probation is the alternative to incarceration for defendants who are not categorically ineligible. The court suspends the sentence and releases the defendant into the community under conditions monitored by a probation officer. Violate the conditions and the suspended sentence gets executed. Many serious violent and sex offenses are excluded from probation consideration. Since AB 1950, most probation terms are capped at 2 years for felonies and 1 year for misdemeanors.
