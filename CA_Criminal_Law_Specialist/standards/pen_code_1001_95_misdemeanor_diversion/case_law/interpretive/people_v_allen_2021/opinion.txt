HOLDING:

§ 1001.95 JUDGE-INITIATED MISDEMEANOR DIVERSION FRAMEWORK:

1. **The judge initiates and controls the diversion decision.** Unlike § 1001.36 (mental health diversion), § 1001.95 does not require the prosecution's consent. The judge may grant diversion over the prosecution's objection. This was a deliberate separation-of-powers choice: the court, not the DA, controls case outcomes for eligible misdemeanor defendants.

2. **The prosecution's objection is not a veto.** The Legislature deliberately rejected a prosecution veto in § 1001.95, departing from other diversion statutes (e.g., § 1000 deferred entry of judgment, which required DA consent).

3. **"Interests of justice" is the standard.** The court must be satisfied that diversion is in the interest of justice. This is a broad, discretionary standard courts have applied widely.

4. **Successful completion results in dismissal and sealing.** A defendant who completes diversion has the charge dismissed and may answer "no" to prior arrest/conviction questions in most contexts. The record is sealed (not destroyed, but unavailable for most purposes).

5. **Domestic violence exclusion is strictly construed.** Section 1001.95 does not apply to domestic violence offenses (§ 1109), sexual offenses requiring § 290 registration, stalking (§ 646.9), or certain driving offenses. The DV exclusion (§ 13700 definition) is the primary barrier in family-context cases.
