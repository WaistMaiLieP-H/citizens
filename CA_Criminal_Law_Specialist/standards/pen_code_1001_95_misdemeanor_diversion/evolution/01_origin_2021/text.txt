Senate Bill 282 (Chapter 257, Statutes of 2021)
Enacted: October 5, 2021 (effective January 1, 2022)

Adding Cal. Penal Code § 1001.95 — Judicial Misdemeanor Diversion:

§ 1001.95 (original text):

(a) A judge in the superior court in which a misdemeanor is being prosecuted may, at the judge's discretion, and over the objection of a prosecuting attorney, offer diversion to a defendant pursuant to these provisions.

(b) A judge may continue a diverted case for a period not to exceed 24 months and order the defendant to comply with terms, conditions, or programs that the judge deems appropriate based on the defendant's specific situation.

(c) If the defendant has complied with the imposed terms and conditions, at the end of the period of diversion, the judge shall dismiss the action against the defendant.

(d) [Non-compliance hearing provision — reinstatement of criminal proceedings]

(e) Excluded offenses:
  (1) Sex offense registrable offenses (§ 290)
  (2) Domestic violence offenses (Fam. § 6211 or Pen. § 13700(b))
  (3) Stalking (§ 646.9)

Key features of the original enactment:
1. Judicial authority to grant diversion OVER PROSECUTORIAL OBJECTION — a significant departure from standard diversion schemes.
2. No requirement that the prosecution agree or refer the case for diversion.
3. Maximum 24-month diversion period.
4. Dismissal upon completion — record is sealed for most purposes.
5. Excluded domestic violence — this is a critical limitation for cases in the DV/family law interface.

Source: SB 282 (Mitchell), Chapter 257, Statutes of 2021.
