# Cal. Penal Code § 1001.95 — Judicial Misdemeanor Diversion

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_1001_95_MISDEMEANOR_DIVERSION
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** A judge in the superior court in which a misdemeanor is being prosecuted may, at the judge's discretion, and **over the objection of a prosecuting attorney**, offer diversion to a defendant pursuant to these provisions.

**(b)** A judge may continue a diverted case for a period not to exceed **24 months** and order the defendant to comply with terms, conditions, or programs that the judge deems appropriate based on the defendant's specific situation.

**(c)** If the defendant has complied with the imposed terms and conditions, at the end of the period of diversion, the **judge shall dismiss the action** against the defendant.

**(d)** If it appears to the court that the defendant is not complying with the terms and conditions of diversion, after notice to the defendant, the court shall hold a hearing to determine whether criminal proceedings should be reinstituted.

**(e)** A defendant may **not** be offered diversion pursuant to this section for any of the following:
1. Any offense for which a conviction would require § 290 sex offender registration;
2. Any offense involving domestic violence (Fam. Code § 6211 or Pen. Code § 13700(b));
3. A violation of § 646.9 (stalking).

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 1001.95; enacted Jan. 1, 2021 — AB 3234)*

---

## Key Features

**Judge may override prosecutor objection:** Unlike most diversion programs where the prosecution must agree, § 1001.95 explicitly allows the court to offer diversion "over the objection of a prosecuting attorney." This is a significant judicial discretion expansion.

**No diagnostic requirement:** Unlike § 1001.36 (mental health diversion), § 1001.95 imposes no qualifying mental health diagnosis. Any misdemeanor not in the excluded categories may be eligible.

**Court sets conditions:** The judge determines what conditions are appropriate — treatment programs, community service, restitution, or other terms.

**24-month maximum:** The diversion period cannot exceed two years for any case.

**Dismissal upon completion:** If the defendant completes diversion conditions, dismissal is mandatory ("shall dismiss").

---

## Excluded Offenses

- Sex offenses requiring § 290 registration
- Domestic violence offenses (Family Code § 6211 or Pen. Code § 13700(b))
- Stalking (§ 646.9)

---

## Plain English

For any misdemeanor not involving domestic violence, sex offenses requiring registration, or stalking, a judge may offer the defendant diversion — even if the prosecutor objects. The defendant gets up to two years to complete court-ordered conditions (treatment, community service, etc.). Complete them successfully and the case is dismissed. Fail to comply and the court can reinstate the criminal proceedings. The statute was enacted to give judges independent discretion to resolve misdemeanor cases without requiring a criminal conviction when circumstances warrant it.
