# Pen. Code § 1001.95 — General Misdemeanor Diversion

## What this statute does

Section 1001.95 (enacted 2021 via AB 3234) gives judges broad discretion to offer diversion to any misdemeanor defendant, **even over the prosecution's objection**. This is a powerful tool — the prosecution cannot veto misdemeanor diversion under § 1001.95.

## Key features

**1. Judge's discretion is unreviewable by the prosecution:**
Unlike § 1001.36 (mental health diversion), which requires specific eligibility criteria, § 1001.95 gives the judge nearly unfettered discretion. If the judge offers diversion and the defendant accepts, the prosecution's objection cannot stop it.

**2. Misdemeanors only:**
Section 1001.95 applies only to misdemeanor charges. It does not apply to felony charges. For felony charges, use § 1001.36 (mental health) or other specific diversion tracks.

**3. Up to 24 months:**
The diversion period can last up to 24 months (vs. § 1001.36's 2 years for felonies, 1 year for misdemeanors).

**4. No specific eligibility criteria:**
Unlike § 1001.36, § 1001.95 does not require a mental health diagnosis or any other specific showing. The judge may order compliance with any terms and conditions "the judge deems appropriate based on the defendant's specific situation."

**5. Dismissal on completion:**
If the defendant complies with all terms, the court **shall** dismiss the action. This is mandatory — the court has no discretion to deny dismissal if the defendant has complied.

## Excluded offenses

Section 1001.95 diversion is NOT available for:
- Sex registration offenses (§ 290)
- Domestic violence offenses (Fam. Code § 6211; Pen. Code § 13700(b))
- Stalking (§ 646.9)

If the M25-00758 charge does not fall in these categories AND is a misdemeanor, § 1001.95 is available as a backup or alternative to § 1001.36.

## Relationship to § 1001.36

| Feature | § 1001.36 | § 1001.95 |
|---|---|---|
| Charge type | Misdemeanor or felony | Misdemeanor only |
| Eligibility criteria | Mental health diagnosis + significant factor | None specified |
| Prosecution veto | No — prosecution can oppose but court may grant | No — judge has discretion over objection |
| Maximum period | 2 years (felony), 1 year (misdemeanor) | 24 months |
| Record sealing | Yes (§ 1001.9) | Dismissal; sealing under § 1001.9 or § 851.90 |

## Strategy for M25-00758

If M25-00758 involves a **misdemeanor** charge that is not in the § 1001.95 excluded list:
1. **Primary track:** File § 1001.36 mental health diversion petition (requires mental health expert and qualifying diagnosis, but provides the strongest protections)
2. **Backup track:** Request § 1001.95 general diversion from the judge — this can be done simultaneously or as an alternative if § 1001.36 is denied
3. **Combined approach:** Even if § 1001.36 is denied for insufficient mental health evidence, the judge can offer § 1001.95 diversion without any showing — purely at the judge's discretion
