# Reasoning — Cal. Penal Code § 1001.95 (Judicial Misdemeanor Diversion)

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_1001_95_MISDEMEANOR_DIVERSION
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why This Standard Exists

Section 1001.95 was enacted as part of AB 3234 (effective January 1, 2021) as a broad judicial tool to divert misdemeanor cases without prosecutorial agreement. Before § 1001.95, California had numerous category-specific diversion programs (§ 1001.36 for mental health, § 1001.80 for military, § 1000 for drug offenses) but no general misdemeanor diversion authority that judges could exercise over prosecutorial objection.

The Legislature's conclusion: the binary outcome of misdemeanor conviction (criminal record, collateral consequences, disrupted employment and housing) versus full acquittal is often disproportionate to the offense and counterproductive for low-level offenders. Diversion allows courts to resolve these cases in a way that addresses the underlying issue without creating the lasting collateral consequences of a conviction.

---

## Why Judges May Override Prosecutors

The most politically significant feature of § 1001.95 is that it explicitly allows judges to offer diversion "over the objection of a prosecuting attorney." This is a direct departure from the typical collaborative model of diversion and was contested during the legislative debate.

The rationale: prosecutors' charging and diversion decisions are influenced by factors — policy mandates, office politics, relationship with law enforcement agencies, victim pressure — that may not align with individualized justice in a particular case. Judges, with life-tenured independence and exposure to the full facts of the case, are better positioned to determine when diversion serves the interests of justice for a specific defendant.

---

## Why Domestic Violence, Sex Offenses, and Stalking Are Excluded

The exclusions for domestic violence, sex registration offenses, and stalking reflect a legislative determination that these offense categories involve specific harm dynamics that make general diversion inappropriate:

- **Domestic violence:** Diversion without prosecution has historically been used to minimize DV, exposing victims to continued risk. The Legislature determined that DV offenses require the accountability structure of the criminal justice process and victim-protective conditions that only full prosecution provides.
- **Sex registration offenses:** These carry registration requirements that reflect persistent public safety concerns for specific offense patterns.
- **Stalking (§ 646.9):** Stalking involves ongoing, pattern-based behavior targeting specific victims. Diversion without conviction may deprive victims of the protective orders and registration that prosecution provides.

---

## Application in Practice

Judicial misdemeanor diversion under § 1001.95 is most often used for:
- First-time offenders charged with low-level theft, trespass, or disorderly conduct;
- Cases where the underlying driver of the offense is an addressable condition (homelessness, unemployment, mental health not rising to § 1001.36 level);
- Cases where the prosecutor's objection reflects policy rather than case-specific facts;
- Cases where the defendant has a clear rehabilitation trajectory that diversion would support.

Defense counsel should request diversion at or before arraignment. Courts have discretion to offer diversion at any point before judgment.
