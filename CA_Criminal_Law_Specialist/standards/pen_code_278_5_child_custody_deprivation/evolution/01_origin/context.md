# Context — § 278.5 Origin (1977)

## The wound

In the 1970s, parental child abduction — one parent taking a child in violation of a custody order and crossing state or international lines — became a recognized crisis. California's family courts could issue custody orders, but when a parent violated the order and fled, the only remedy was civil contempt. There was no crime. Law enforcement would not act. The other parent had no criminal mechanism to demand assistance.

The 1977 enactment directly addressed this: parental child abduction in violation of a court order became a criminal offense.

## People v. Campos and the "good faith" limitation

The 1982 California Court of Appeal decision in People v. Campos established the rule that has never been changed: a defendant's subjective belief that they had a right to custody, or that the custody order was unfair, is NOT a defense. The crime is the knowing violation of the court order, not the subjective state of mind about the underlying parental rights.

This rule is important for Case #7 and the custody context: § 278.5 creates criminal exposure for anyone who takes or conceals children in violation of a valid custody order, regardless of their personal beliefs about the fairness of that order. The order governs.

## Steward relevance

§ 278.5 is relevant as a counter-claim vehicle for custody violations in the June 16, 2023 incident and the broader pattern of unauthorized custody deprivation. If children were taken or concealed in violation of an order — regardless of the taker's subjective justification — § 278.5 exposure exists.
