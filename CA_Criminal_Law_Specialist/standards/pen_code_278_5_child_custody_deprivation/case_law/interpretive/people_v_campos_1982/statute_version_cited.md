# Statute version cited in People v. Campos (1982)

*Campos* was decided under the custody interference statutes as they existed in 1982, before the 1988 enactment of § 278.5 in its current form. The predecessor statute framework was similar but less structured. The core propositions articulated in *Campos* — that subjective good-faith belief is not a defense and that malice means intent to deprive — carried forward into § 278.5 and remain controlling under the current statute.

**Modern application:** When citing *Campos* for a § 278.5 case today, note that the holding applies with full force despite the intervening statutory restructuring. The 1988 codification preserved *Campos*'s rule and added § 278.5(c)'s specific no-later-order provision, which is consistent with *Campos*'s refusal to recognize self-help remedies.

**Consistency check:** *Campos*'s substantive doctrine is fully consistent with current § 278.5. The only change is that the 1988 codification added structure and specific elements; the underlying principles are unchanged.
