# Cal. Penal Code § 278.5 — Deprivation of Child Custody/Visitation

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_278_5_CHILD_CUSTODY_DEPRIVATION
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** Every person who **takes, entices away, keeps, withholds, or conceals** a child and **maliciously deprives a lawful custodian of a right to custody, or a person of a right to visitation**, shall be punished by:
- Imprisonment in a county jail not exceeding **one year**; a fine not exceeding **$1,000**; or both; **OR**
- Imprisonment pursuant to subdivision (h) of Section 1170 for **16 months, or 2 or 3 years**; a fine not exceeding **$10,000**; or both.

**(b)** Nothing contained in this section limits the court's contempt power.

**(c)** A custody order obtained **after** the taking, enticing away, keeping, withholding, or concealing of a child does not constitute a defense to a crime charged under this section.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 278.5)*

---

## Elements

1. The defendant **took, enticed away, kept, withheld, or concealed** a child;
2. **Maliciously** — the defendant acted with intent to harm or endanger the health or safety of the child, or with specific intent to interfere with the lawful custodian's exercise of custody or visitation rights;
3. The act **deprived a lawful custodian** of their custody rights **or** deprived a person of their visitation rights.

---

## "Maliciously" — The Critical Element

The malice element distinguishes § 278.5 from ordinary custody disagreements. The defendant must have acted with wrongful intent — either to harm the child or specifically to deprive the other parent of custody/visitation. A parent who withholds the child based on a genuine, good-faith belief the child is in danger may have a defense; a parent who withholds the child out of spite or to harm the other parent does not.

---

## Wobbler — Misdemeanor or Felony

Section 278.5 is a wobbler — it may be charged as a misdemeanor or felony depending on:
- Duration and distance of the deprivation;
- Whether the child was transported out of state;
- The defendant's criminal history;
- Whether the deprivation caused harm to the child.

---

## The "Post-Taking Order" Defense Closes a Loophole (§ 278.5(c))

Subdivision (c) prevents a defendant from defeating the charge by obtaining a new custody order after unlawfully taking the child. Courts that issue orders after a wrongful taking do not retroactively legitimize the taking.

---

## Distinction from § 278 (Child Abduction — Custody Order Required)

Section 278 covers taking a child from a lawful custodian when there is an existing court order. Section 278.5 applies more broadly — it covers deprivation of custody or visitation rights even without a formal court order, as long as there is a lawful custodian or a person with visitation rights.

---

## Interaction with Family Court — Concurrent Jurisdiction

Family courts retain contempt power over their own orders — § 278.5(b) explicitly preserves this. A parent who violates a custody order may face both criminal prosecution under § 278.5 and contempt proceedings in the family court simultaneously. These are parallel proceedings, not duplicative.

---

## Plain English

Taking a child and keeping them from the other parent — whether by concealment, flight out of state, or simply refusing to return the child after visitation — is a crime under § 278.5 when done maliciously. It can be a misdemeanor or a felony depending on severity. The parent who wrongfully takes the child cannot escape by later getting a new custody order — the crime is complete at the moment of the wrongful withholding. Family court contempt proceedings and criminal prosecution can happen simultaneously.
