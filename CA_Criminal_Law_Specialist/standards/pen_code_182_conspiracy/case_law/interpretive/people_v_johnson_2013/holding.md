# People v. Johnson (2013) 57 Cal.4th 250

**Citation:** People v. Johnson (2013) 57 Cal.4th 250, 159 Cal.Rptr.3d 70, 303 P.3d 379
**Court:** Supreme Court of California
**Decided:** 2013
**Statutes construed:** Penal Code § 182 — agreement element; single vs. multiple conspiracy

## Facts

Multiple defendants were charged with conspiracy to commit various crimes across different transactions. The central issue was whether multiple agreements constituted one conspiracy or multiple conspiracies, and how the agreement element is established from circumstantial evidence.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **The agreement need not be express.** A conspiracy agreement may be proven entirely from circumstantial evidence — from the acts of the parties and the reasonable inferences drawn from their conduct. Direct evidence of an express agreement is not required.

2. **Single conspiracy with multiple objects.** Where multiple persons agree to commit several crimes as part of a single, unified scheme, all of those crimes are objects of a single conspiracy, not separate conspiracies. The test is whether the defendants agreed to work together toward a common goal — if so, there is one conspiracy regardless of how many discrete criminal acts are planned.

3. **Each member liable for all co-conspirator acts in furtherance.** A conspirator is liable for every crime committed by any co-conspirator that was (a) committed in furtherance of the conspiracy and (b) reasonably foreseeable as a natural consequence of the common objective. The defendant need not have personally committed or even known about the specific criminal act.

4. **Venue is proper in any county where any overt act occurred.** The court confirmed the breadth of § 182's venue provision — a single overt act anywhere extends proper venue throughout that county.

## Why This Is Load-Bearing

*Johnson* is the modern California Supreme Court authority on the structure of the conspiracy agreement element. It establishes:
- Circumstantial evidence alone is sufficient to prove agreement
- A single unified scheme produces a single conspiracy even with multiple criminal objects
- Full co-conspirator liability for all foreseeable acts of all members

**Critical for the steward's cases:** The 16-year orchestration is one conspiracy with multiple criminal objects (§§ 182(a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(3), (a)(5)). Under *Johnson*, all actors who joined at any point are liable for the full scheme's criminal consequences, not just the acts they personally took.

## Defense Use

If steward is defendant on a conspiracy charge:
- Challenge the agreement element — parallel conduct (people doing similar things) is not the same as an agreement to act together
- Challenge the "in furtherance" element — not all co-actor crimes are automatically attributable; must be reasonably foreseeable
- Challenge the conspiracy's scope — argue separate, unconnected agreements rather than a single unified scheme (limits liability to individual's own agreement)

## Complainant Use

If steward is victim of conspiracy:
- *Johnson* confirms that the breadth of California conspiracy doctrine reaches every actor who joined the scheme at any point
- The circumstantial evidence standard is easy to meet when there is a documented 16-year pattern of coordinated conduct
- The single conspiracy / multiple objects doctrine means all criminal acts across all 16 years are attributed to every conspirator

Published California Supreme Court decision. Binding statewide. Still cited.
