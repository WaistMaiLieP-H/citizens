# People v. Beeman (1984) 35 Cal.3d 547

**Citation:** People v. Beeman (1984) 35 Cal.3d 547, 199 Cal.Rptr. 60, 674 P.2d 1318
**Court:** Supreme Court of California
**Decided:** 1984
**Statutes construed:** Penal Code § 182 — specific intent element; aiding and abetting vs. conspiracy

## Facts

Defendant Beeman was convicted as an aider and abettor in a robbery. The conviction rested on whether Beeman had the requisite specific intent. The Supreme Court took the case to resolve confusion in the lower courts about the intent standard for both aiding and abetting and conspiracy.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **Conspiracy requires dual specific intent.** To be guilty of conspiracy, a person must have: (a) the intent to agree or conspire, AND (b) the intent to commit the offense that is the object of the conspiracy. Mere knowledge that others are committing a crime, without intent to further it, is insufficient.

2. **Presence at the scene and knowledge of the crime are insufficient.** A person who knows about a conspiracy and is even present when overt acts are committed is not automatically a conspirator. The prosecution must prove the defendant intended to further the criminal objective.

3. **Intent can be inferred from conduct but must be proven.** The dual intent requirement can be satisfied by circumstantial evidence — but the inference of intent must be reasonable, not speculative. The jury instruction must correctly state both layers of intent.

4. **Distinguished from strict liability and knowledge offenses.** Conspiracy is not a strict liability offense. The prosecution bears the burden of proving both the intent to agree and the intent that the crime be committed. This protects peripheral actors who may be associated with conspirators but did not join the criminal agreement.

## Why This Is Load-Bearing

*Beeman* is the foundation case for the specific intent requirement in California conspiracy. It establishes:
- Two layers of intent are required, not one
- Knowledge alone is not intent
- This is the standard by which each conspirator's culpability is individually evaluated

**For the steward as complainant:** Every actor in the scheme who had both (a) knowledge of the coordinated plan and (b) took steps to advance it satisfies *Beeman*'s dual intent requirement. The overt acts each took — filing a false report, making a coordinated phone call, submitting a false court document — are evidence of the second intent layer.

**For the steward as defendant:** If charged with conspiracy, *Beeman* requires the prosecution to prove both layers. Challenge the evidence of the second intent layer — did the steward's communications show intent to further a criminal objective, or only knowledge of another's conduct?

## CALCRIM Connection

CALCRIM No. 415 codifies the *Beeman* dual intent requirement. The jury instruction states: "A member of a conspiracy is criminally responsible for the acts or statements of any other member of the conspiracy if that member commits the act or makes the statement in furtherance of the conspiracy." The predicate to that liability is that the defendant joined the conspiracy with dual intent — CALCRIM 415 requires both intents be found.

## Authority Status

Published California Supreme Court decision. Binding statewide. The dual intent standard has been stable since 1984 and is cited in every conspiracy prosecution. Still controlling.
