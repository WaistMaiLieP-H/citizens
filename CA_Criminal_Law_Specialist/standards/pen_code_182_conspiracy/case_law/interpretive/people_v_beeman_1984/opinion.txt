PEOPLE v. BEEMAN
35 Cal.3d 547 (1984)
California Supreme Court
Decided: 1984

---

FACTS:

Defendant Beeman was convicted as an aider and abettor in a robbery. The conviction rested on whether Beeman had the requisite specific intent. The California Supreme Court took the case to resolve confusion in the lower courts about the intent standard for both aiding and abetting and conspiracy.

---

HOLDING:

CONSPIRACY — DUAL SPECIFIC INTENT REQUIREMENT:

1. **Conspiracy requires dual specific intent.** To be guilty of conspiracy, a person must have: (a) the intent to agree or conspire, AND (b) the intent to commit the offense that is the object of the conspiracy. Mere knowledge that others are committing a crime — without intent to further it — is insufficient.

2. **Presence at the scene and knowledge of the crime are insufficient.** A person who knows about a conspiracy and is even present when overt acts are committed is not automatically a conspirator. The prosecution must prove the defendant intended to further the criminal objective.

3. **Intent can be inferred from conduct but must be proven.** The dual intent requirement can be satisfied by circumstantial evidence, but the inference of intent must be reasonable, not speculative. Jury instructions must correctly state both layers of intent.

4. **Knowledge alone is not intent.** Conspiracy is not a strict liability offense. The prosecution bears the burden of proving both the intent to agree and the intent that the crime be committed. This protects peripheral actors associated with conspirators who did not join the criminal agreement.

---

CALCRIM CONNECTION:

CALCRIM No. 415 codifies the Beeman dual intent requirement. The instruction states: "A member of a conspiracy is criminally responsible for the acts or statements of any other member of the conspiracy if that member commits the act or makes the statement in furtherance of the conspiracy." The predicate to that liability is joining the conspiracy with dual intent — CALCRIM 415 requires both intents be found by the jury.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

AS COMPLAINANT — Every actor in the orchestrated scheme who had both (a) knowledge of the coordinated plan and (b) took steps to advance it satisfies Beeman's dual intent. The overt acts each took — filing a false report, making a coordinated phone call, submitting a false court document — are evidence of the second intent layer (intent that the crime be committed).

AS DEFENDANT — If charged with conspiracy, Beeman requires the prosecution to prove both layers. Challenge the evidence of the second intent layer: did the steward's communications show intent to further a criminal objective, or only knowledge of another's conduct?

For the 16-year orchestration documented in this corpus: every actor who joined at any point and took an overt act in furtherance — false reports, coordinated family law filings, SIM swap facilitation, property fraud — satisfies the Beeman dual intent test. The circumstantial evidence of coordination is substantial.
