# Context — Penal Code § 182 (Conspiracy)

## What This Standard Does

§ 182 conspiracy is the instrument that converts individual bad acts into a collective criminal enterprise. In the steward's cases, no single actor carried out the full scheme alone. The pattern — coordinated court filings, synchronized police contacts, manufactured evidence, controlled communications — is the product of an agreement among multiple persons. § 182 is the hook that reaches every participant regardless of which specific act each one performed.

## Elements of Criminal Conspiracy

California courts require three elements to establish conspiracy under § 182:

**1. Agreement between two or more persons**
The agreement need not be express, formal, or written. An implied agreement — proven through the conduct of the parties — is sufficient. Courts instruct that a "wink and a nod" is enough if circumstances show the parties were acting in concert toward a shared objective. The agreement is the conspiracy; everything else is evidence of it.

**2. Specific intent to agree AND specific intent that the target offense be committed**
Conspiracy is a specific intent crime requiring two layers of intent: (a) intent to enter the agreement, and (b) intent that the crime agreed upon be committed. This dual intent requirement means that a person who unknowingly assists the scheme is not a conspirator — but a person who knows the objective and joins the effort is liable even if they play only a minor role.

**3. An overt act by any conspirator in furtherance of the agreement**
At least one overt act must be committed by at least one conspirator after the agreement is formed. The overt act need not be criminal itself — it can be innocent in isolation. All that is required is that the act be taken "in furtherance" of the conspiracy. Each conspirator is liable for every overt act committed by any conspirator in furtherance of the agreement.

## Key Doctrinal Rules

### Each member liable for all acts in furtherance
A conspirator is criminally liable for all crimes committed by co-conspirators that are (1) committed in furtherance of the conspiracy and (2) reasonably foreseeable as a natural consequence of the conspiracy. This is the Pinkerton doctrine as applied in California. It reaches far beyond each individual's personal acts.

### Venue: any county where an overt act occurred
§ 182(a) final paragraph: the conspiracy can be prosecuted in any county where any overt act was committed. This is significant where the scheme crossed multiple counties (Contra Costa, Alameda, Marin, Sonoma) — the steward can choose the most favorable venue.

### § 182(a)(2) — Procuring false arrest is itself a conspiracy object
Subdivision (a)(2) specifically makes it a conspiracy to "falsely and maliciously indict another for any crime, or to procure another to be charged or arrested for any crime." This is not a general obstruction charge — it is a precise enumeration of the exact conduct visible in the 6/16/2023 setup: pre-positioned complainant, directed law enforcement contact, manufactured predicate.

### § 182(a)(5) — Obstruct justice and pervert administration of laws
Subdivision (a)(5) captures agreements to "pervert or obstruct justice, or the due administration of the laws." The coordinated family court filings, unsigned court documents, and manipulation of court processes over 16 years constitute a sustained conspiracy to obstruct the due administration of laws across multiple tribunals.

### § 182(a)(3) — Falsely moving or maintaining suit
Subdivision (a)(3) reaches those who conspire to "falsely move or maintain any suit, action, or proceeding." The coordinated filing of frivolous family court matters and the maintenance of a false narrative across those proceedings fits this category.

## Punishment

- **Conspiracy to commit felony:** Same punishment as the target felony. Conspiracy to commit identity theft (§ 530.5) carries up to $25,000 fine.
- **Conspiracy to commit a misdemeanor or specified other acts:** Up to 1 year county jail and/or $10,000 fine.
- The punishment tracks the most serious underlying felony where multiple objects are alleged.

## Statute of Limitations

- **Conspiracy to commit felony:** SOL = SOL of the target felony, running from the last overt act.
- **Conspiracy to commit misdemeanor:** 1 year from last overt act.
- **Critical doctrine:** The conspiracy SOL continues to run until the last overt act in furtherance — not from the initial agreement. A 16-year continuing conspiracy has SOL measured from the most recent overt act, not 2009.

- No agreement: communications that appear coordinated may have innocent explanations; coordination requires proof of agreement, not mere parallel conduct

## CALCRIM Reference

CALCRIM No. 415 — Conspiracy. The instruction requires the jury to find: (1) defendant and at least one other person agreed to commit the crime, (2) defendant intended to agree, (3) at least one conspirator committed an overt act in furtherance, (4) at least one conspirator committed the overt act. Defense counsel examines each element.
