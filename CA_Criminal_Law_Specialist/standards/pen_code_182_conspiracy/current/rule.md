# Cal. Penal Code § 182 — Criminal Conspiracy

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_182_CONSPIRACY
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current — § 182(a))

If **two or more persons conspire**:

1. To commit any crime;
2. Falsely and maliciously to indict another for any crime, or to procure another to be charged or arrested for any crime;
3. Falsely to move or maintain any suit, action, or proceeding;
4. To cheat and defraud any person of any property, by any means which are in themselves criminal, or to obtain money or property by false pretenses or by false promises with fraudulent intent not to perform;
5. To commit any act injurious to the public health, to public morals, or to pervert or obstruct justice, or the due administration of the laws;
6. To commit any crime against the person of the President or Vice President, the Governor, any federal justice or judge, or a cabinet secretary.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 182; R.S. 1872 with subsequent amendments)*

---

## Elements of Conspiracy

1. **An agreement** between two or more persons;
2. **With intent** to agree and with intent to commit the target offense;
3. **An overt act** in furtherance of the agreement (required for all conspiracies other than conspiracy to commit murder or specific crimes against constitutional officers).

The agreement need not be formal or express — an implied understanding established by circumstantial evidence is sufficient.

---

## Punishment

| Target Offense | Punishment |
|----------------|-----------|
| Felony (not § 182(a)(6)) | Same manner and extent as the underlying felony |
| Murder conspiracy | First degree murder punishment |
| § 182(a)(4) (fraud) | County jail up to 1 year or state prison under § 1170(h), fine up to $10,000 |
| Other § 182(a) violations | County jail up to 1 year or state prison, fine up to $10,000 |
| Crime against constitutional officer (§ 182(a)(6)) | State prison: 5, 7, or 9 years |
| Conspiracy to commit identity theft | Enhanced fine up to $25,000 |

---

## The Overt Act Requirement

For most California conspiracies, at least one conspirator must commit an overt act in furtherance of the conspiracy. The overt act need not be criminal in itself — even a preparatory act (making a phone call, driving to a location) satisfies the requirement. Once one conspirator commits the overt act, all co-conspirators are liable.

**Exception:** Conspiracy to commit murder does not require an overt act — the agreement itself is the crime.

---

## Each Conspirator Liable for Acts of Co-Conspirators

Once a person joins a conspiracy, they become liable for all criminal acts committed by co-conspirators in furtherance of the conspiracy — including acts committed before they joined if they were aware of and adopted the prior acts. Withdrawal from the conspiracy before the overt act may be a defense.

---

## Interaction with Underlying Offense

A defendant can be convicted of both the conspiracy and the completed underlying crime. Conspiracy is a separate offense from the substantive crime it targets — the two punishments run separately. This allows prosecutors to charge conspiracy as a standalone count even when the underlying offense is difficult to prove.

---

## Plain English

Conspiracy is the crime of agreeing with one or more other people to commit a crime, combined with at least one step toward carrying it out. You can be convicted of conspiracy even if the target crime never actually happens. And if the conspiracy succeeds, you can be convicted of both the conspiracy and the completed crime. Every co-conspirator is liable for what any co-conspirator does in furtherance of the scheme. In California, conspiring to commit a felony is punished as severely as the felony itself.
