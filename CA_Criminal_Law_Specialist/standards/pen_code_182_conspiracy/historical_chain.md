# Historical Chain — Penal Code § 182 (Conspiracy)

## Origin

**1872 — § 182 codified in original California Penal Code**
California conspiracy law derives from the common law of England, where criminal conspiracy was established as an offense by the 14th century Star Chamber courts. When California codified its Penal Code in 1872, § 182 was among the original provisions, carrying forward the common law framework: agreement + overt act + criminal objective.

**Historical loss driving the law:** Criminal combination — where multiple actors pooled resources to accomplish a crime that any individual would lack the capacity to complete — was recognized as more dangerous than individual crime. The conspiracy statute reached beyond the individual's own acts to dismantle networks.

## Material Amendments

**1872 original structure:** The 1872 statute covered the core conspiracy to commit crime (now subd. (a)(1)) and specific enumerated objectives (false indictment, false suit, fraud).

**People v. Manson (1977) 71 Cal.App.3d 1** — Though a homicide case, Manson definitively applied California's conspiracy doctrine to establish that all co-conspirators are liable for reasonably foreseeable crimes of each other. This is the California Pinkerton doctrine. It remains controlling.

**20th century — False arrest conspiracy (subd. (a)(2)) clarified**
Courts clarified that procuring a false arrest is a complete conspiracy offense even if the arrest does not result in conviction. The overt act of procuring the arrest — filing a false report, directing law enforcement — completes the conspiracy.

**2000s — Identity theft amendment**
The Legislature added the specific reference to § 530.5 (identity theft) in the punishment section, elevating the fine ceiling to $25,000 for conspiracy to commit identity theft. This reflects the Legislature's recognition that identity theft is typically a multi-actor, coordinated crime.

**Venue provision — no substantive amendment**
The venue provision ("any county in which any overt act...shall be done") has remained in the statute since 1872 without substantive change. Its breadth reflects the Legislature's intent to allow prosecution wherever the conspiracy reached.

## Relationship to RICO / Federal Conspiracy

California § 182 has no RICO equivalent for continuing criminal enterprise — there is no state RICO statute in California. However:
- For multi-actor schemes involving interstate commerce, 18 U.S.C. § 1962 (RICO) may be available in federal court
- For wire fraud + mail fraud components of the scheme, 18 U.S.C. § 371 (federal conspiracy) applies
- The § 1983 federal civil rights action covers the same actors under a civil conspiracy theory without requiring criminal charges

## Current Statute Version

§ 182 as codified in 2025 reflects incremental amendments but the core structure — agreement, overt act, enumerated objects — is unchanged from 1872. The six subdivision categories in (a) have been stable for decades.

**Consistency check:** All case law construing § 182 elements applies to the current statute without amendment-based concerns.
