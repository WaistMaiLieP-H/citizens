# Cal. Penal Code § 859 — Preliminary Hearing (Felony Initial Appearance)

**Standard ID:** PEN_CODE_859_PRELIMINARY_HEARING
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

When the defendant is charged with the commission of a felony by a written complaint subscribed under oath and on file in a court within the county in which the felony is triable, he or she shall, **without unnecessary delay**, be taken before a magistrate of the court in which the complaint is on file. The magistrate shall:

1. **Immediately deliver to the defendant a copy of the complaint**;
2. **Inform the defendant** that he or she has the right to have the assistance of counsel;
3. **Ask** the defendant if he or she desires the assistance of counsel;
4. **Allow** the defendant reasonable time to send for counsel.

If the defendant desires and is unable to employ counsel, the court shall **assign counsel** to defend him or her.

In a **capital case**, the court shall inform the defendant that representation by counsel is **mandatory** at all stages of the preliminary and trial proceedings, whether at the defendant's expense or at public expense.

If it appears the defendant may be a **minor**, the magistrate shall ascertain whether that is the case and, if probable, shall immediately notify the parent or guardian, or appoint counsel.

*(Cal. Pen. Code § 859)*

---

## Function in the Felony Process

Section 859 governs the initial appearance in a felony case — the defendant's first court appearance after being charged. It is distinct from the preliminary hearing (§ 860 et seq.) where the magistrate determines probable cause to hold the defendant for trial.

The § 859 appearance serves:
1. **Notice** — defendant receives a copy of the complaint and learns the charges;
2. **Right to counsel** — defendant is informed of and given the opportunity to assert the right to counsel;
3. **Appointment of counsel** — if the defendant cannot afford counsel, public defender is appointed;
4. **Minor protection** — parents or guardians are notified if the defendant may be a minor.

---

## The Subsequent Preliminary Hearing — §§ 860–883

After the initial § 859 appearance, the actual preliminary hearing is held pursuant to §§ 860–883. At the preliminary hearing, the prosecution must show probable cause that:
1. A felony has been committed; and
2. The defendant committed it.

If probable cause is established, the defendant is "held to answer" and an information is filed in the superior court. If not, the case is dismissed — subject to the prosecution's right to re-file or proceed by grand jury indictment.

---

## § 995 Motion to Dismiss — Post-Bindover

After being held to answer, the defendant may challenge the sufficiency of the evidence at the preliminary hearing by filing a § 995 motion to dismiss the information. The standard is whether there was probable cause at the preliminary hearing stage.

---

## Right to Counsel at Preliminary Hearing

A defendant has the right to counsel at the preliminary hearing. The preliminary hearing is a critical stage of the criminal proceeding for Sixth Amendment purposes — denial of counsel at this stage violates the constitutional right to representation and may be reversible error.

---

## Plain English

When someone is charged with a felony in California, they must be brought before a judge promptly. At that first appearance, the judge must give the defendant a copy of the complaint, tell them they have the right to a lawyer, and if they can't afford one, appoint a public defender. In capital cases, representation by counsel is non-negotiable. This is the starting gate of the felony process — after this comes the actual preliminary hearing where the judge decides whether there's enough evidence to proceed to trial.
