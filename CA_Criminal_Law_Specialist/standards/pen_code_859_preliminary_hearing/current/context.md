# Context — Penal Code §§ 859, 859b, 872 (Preliminary Hearing Framework)

## What This Standard Does

The preliminary hearing is the first substantive proceeding in a felony case. It is the prosecution's opportunity to establish probable cause that a crime was committed and that this defendant committed it. It is the defense's first opportunity to cross-examine prosecution witnesses, lock in testimony, identify weaknesses, and create the evidentiary record that supports a § 995 motion to set aside the information. For case #7 (04-23-01959), the preliminary hearing transcript is the single most important document in the defense file.

## The Three-Section Framework

### § 859 — Arraignment and right to counsel
- Defendant must appear before magistrate without unnecessary delay
- Magistrate delivers copy of complaint, informs of right to counsel, allows time to obtain counsel
- This is the formal entry point — rights must be asserted here

### § 859b — Timing and the 10-court-day rule
- Preliminary hearing must be held within **10 court days** of arraignment or plea
- If defendant is in custody and 10 days pass without prelim hearing: complaint is DISMISSED unless defendant personally waives or prosecution establishes good cause
- Absolute outer limit: **60 court days** from arraignment — complaint dismissed if exceeded (no exceptions except defendant's personal waiver)
- Subpoena power: both prosecution and defense may subpoena witnesses; the magistrate must issue defense subpoenas

### § 872 — The probable cause finding
- Standard: "sufficient cause to believe that the defendant is guilty" = probable cause
- Hearsay is admissible at preliminary hearing (Prop 115, 1990): law enforcement officer may testify to hearsay statements of out-of-court declarants
- Hearsay witness requirements: 5 years law enforcement experience OR completion of a POST-certified training course that includes preliminary hearing testimony training
- If probable cause is found → hold-to-answer order is endorsed on the complaint → information is filed in superior court → § 995 motion becomes available

## The Preliminary Hearing as Defense Intelligence

### Cross-examination opportunities

The preliminary hearing is a sworn proceeding. Every witness who testifies is locked into their testimony under oath. For case #7, critical witnesses to cross-examine at the preliminary hearing:

| Witness | What to Establish |
|---------|------------------|
| APD officer (arresting) | Exact time of call-in, what dispatcher reported, what witness told officer, officer's independent observations vs. dispatch narrative |
| APD officer (report author) | Basis for statements in incident report, whether officer interviewed child independently |
| Christina (if called) | Whether she was at the location before the incident, when she arrived, what she said to police, prior false report history |
| CPS worker (if called) | Basis for referral, what source reported, whether independent investigation occurred |
| Child forensic interviewer (if any) | Protocol followed, who observed, whether the interview was properly conducted |

### Building the § 995 record

A § 995 motion to set aside the information succeeds where there is a "complete evidentiary void" on an element (*People v. Jennings*). The preliminary hearing is the only opportunity to create that record. Cross-examination that exposes:
- No testimony establishing "traumatic condition" (§ 273d element) 
- No testimony establishing who inflicted the alleged injury
- No testimony establishing the injury was caused by the defendant rather than another actor

…creates the § 995 argument.

### Locking in prosecution witnesses

Preliminary hearing testimony is prior sworn testimony. Any material inconsistency between the prelim testimony and trial testimony is impeachable. If a prosecution witness changes their story between the preliminary hearing and trial, that impeachment is devastating — especially for a coordinated false accusation defense.

## Defense Subpoena Strategy for Case #7

Under § 859b, the defense has subpoena power at the preliminary hearing. Serve subpoenas on:

1. **APD Communications Division** — dispatch records for 6/16/2023 (time, caller ID, call content)
2. **Christina Cerretani** — if she is the complaining witness, she must appear and be subject to cross-examination
3. **Mandatory reporter (if identified)** — who filed the CPS referral and on what basis
4. **Child forensic interviewer** — the recording and transcript of any forensic interview

The prosecution cannot call a law enforcement officer to summarize hearsay in lieu of producing the actual declarant if the defense subpoenas the actual declarant.

## The Hearsay Problem at Preliminary Hearings (Prop 115)

Under § 872(b), the prosecution can present a preliminary hearing entirely through a single law enforcement officer summarizing hearsay statements from all other witnesses. This is the "one-witness prelim" — widely used to streamline the proceeding and minimize witness exposure.

**Defense counter-strategy:** If the prosecution uses a one-witness prelim:
- Cross-examine the officer aggressively on the sources of each hearsay statement
- Establish that the officer did not independently witness any of the events
- Establish the chain of information: who told whom what and when
- Flag all hearsay-within-hearsay chains where reliability is lowest
- Move for production of the underlying documents (incident report, forensic interview, CPS referral) before the hearing

## Timing Obligations for Case #7

- **10-court-day rule:** Confirm whether the steward was in custody at arraignment. If so, and the preliminary hearing was not held within 10 court days, there is a potential dismissal ground.
