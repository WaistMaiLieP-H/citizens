# Whitman v. Superior Court (1991) 54 Cal.3d 1063

**Citation:** Whitman v. Superior Court (1991) 54 Cal.3d 1063, 2 Cal.Rptr.2d 160
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** 1991
**Statutes construed:** Penal Code §§ 859, 859b, 872 — Proposition 115 preliminary hearing procedures; hearsay at preliminary hearing; one-witness prelim

## Facts

Following enactment of Proposition 115 (1990), defense challenges arose immediately to the new rules permitting hearsay at preliminary hearings through law enforcement testimony and allowing the preliminary hearing to proceed on single-witness testimony without calling the complaining witness. Defendants sought extraordinary writ relief arguing the Prop 115 procedures violated due process, the Confrontation Clause, and the California Constitution.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court upheld the Proposition 115 preliminary hearing procedures as constitutional:

1. **Hearsay is permissible at preliminary hearings.** The Confrontation Clause applies at trial, not at preliminary hearings. A preliminary hearing is a pre-trial probable cause determination, not a trial on the merits. The defendant's confrontation rights are fully preserved at trial.

2. **A single qualified law enforcement witness can satisfy the probable cause standard.** Under § 872(b) as amended by Prop 115, a qualified law enforcement officer may relate hearsay statements of witnesses. This does not violate due process.

3. **The probable cause standard governs, not proof beyond a reasonable doubt.** The preliminary hearing examines only whether a reasonable person could conclude the defendant committed the offense. The full panoply of trial rights does not apply at this stage.

4. **Magistrate retains gate-keeping function.** Even under the Prop 115 structure, the magistrate must evaluate whether the hearsay is reliable (the officer must have personal knowledge of the facts they learned from others) and whether the totality of evidence establishes probable cause.

## The doctrinal proposition

Post-Whitman, the preliminary hearing framework is settled:

1. **One law enforcement witness CAN hold a defendant to answer** if that witness has personal knowledge of the investigation and properly relates the hearsay statements of other witnesses.

2. **Complaining witness need not appear** at preliminary hearing. This is the Prop 115 design — protecting complaining witnesses from pre-trial cross-examination by withholding them until trial.

3. **Defense CANNOT compel the complaining witness to appear at the preliminary hearing** through a subpoena enforced in advance of Prop 115's protective design. However, if the complaining witness is listed as a prosecution witness, defense can subpoena for the preliminary hearing under the general subpoena power (§ 1326).

4. **Cross-examination of the testifying officer is the primary defense tool.** All information the officer claims to have received from others can be challenged by exposing internal inconsistencies, the officer's basis for claiming personal knowledge of hearsay declarants' statements, and gaps in the investigative record.

## Why this matters to the preliminary hearing strategy

The Whitman framework explains both the limitation and the opportunity at the preliminary hearing in case 04-23-01959:

**Limitation:** The prosecution can present its entire case through one APD officer. The complaining witness (child) need not testify. This is by design.

**Opportunity:** 
- Every statement the officer attributes to the child is double-filtered hearsay. The defense can attack each layer: (a) did the child actually say that? (b) did the officer accurately recall and relay it? (c) did the CPS interviewer accurately record it?
- The officer's personal knowledge of the facts underlying each hearsay statement can be tested. If the officer merely read a CPS report, the defense can expose that the "investigation" is entirely secondary.
- The preliminary hearing transcript becomes the baseline record for impeachment at trial. Every claim made at the prelim that differs from trial testimony is an inconsistency.
- A § 995 motion can attack the quality of the probable cause finding if the magistrate relied on unreliable hearsay or if the one-witness presentation had gaps.

## Relevance to case 04-23-01959

Under Whitman, the APD officer who testifies at the preliminary hearing will relay the child's statements through the hearsay exception created by Prop 115 and Whitman's validation. The defense must:

1. Obtain the preliminary hearing transcript immediately after the hearing
2. Identify every hearsay statement relayed by the officer
3. Compare against the CPS interview recording and any written reports (§ 1054.5 discovery demand)
4. Build the inconsistency map for trial cross-examination
5. Evaluate whether the probable cause finding itself can be challenged under § 995

## Authority status

California Supreme Court decision (1991) validating Proposition 115 preliminary hearing procedures. Foundational authority for the current one-witness hearsay preliminary hearing framework. Directly applicable to all California preliminary hearings.
