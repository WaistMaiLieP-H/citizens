## III. LEGAL STANDARD

### A. § 1203 probation is presumptively available

Penal Code § 1203(a) authorizes the court to suspend imposition or execution of sentence and place a defendant on probation where the court finds probation is in the interest of justice and consistent with the defendant's needs and public safety. No statute presumptively bars probation for a § 273d conviction. *See* Pen. Code § 1203(e) (listing specific offenses that trigger a rebuttable presumption against probation — § 273d is not among them).

### B. The court must consider the full range of sentencing options

California Rules of Court, Rule 4.410 sets forth sentencing objectives: protecting society, punishing the defendant, encouraging reintegration, deterring future conduct, and securing restitution. The court must evaluate each objective and determine which sentencing option best serves all of them.

### C. Probation conditions must satisfy the Carbajal nexus test

Any conditions imposed must be reasonably related to (1) the crime of conviction, (2) conduct the court believes the defendant is likely to re-engage in, or (3) future criminality generally. *People v. Carbajal* (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1114. Conditions lacking a nexus to any of these three criteria should not be imposed.
