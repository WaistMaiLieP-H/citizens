### III. LEGAL STANDARD

#### A. The § 995 Standard

Penal Code § 995(a)(2)(B) provides that the information must be set aside if the defendant "was committed without reasonable or probable cause." The superior court reviews the preliminary hearing record as if it were a reviewing court — applying a substantial evidence standard to the magistrate's factual determinations, but conducting de novo review of legal questions including whether the evidence legally establishes each element of the charged offense. (*People v. Uhlemann* (1973) 9 Cal.3d 662, 667.)

The superior court does not re-weigh evidence or make credibility determinations. Credibility findings made by the magistrate are binding. (*People v. Jennings* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 963, 978.) However, where there is a complete evidentiary void on an element — no competent evidence of any kind — the § 995 motion must be granted. (*Id.* at p. 979.)

#### B. The Probable Cause Standard

Probable cause means "a state of facts as would lead a man of ordinary care and prudence to believe and conscientiously entertain an honest and strong suspicion that the person is guilty of a crime." (*People v. Uhlemann*, supra, 9 Cal.3d at p. 667.) This is a lower threshold than proof beyond a reasonable doubt but requires more than mere suspicion — it requires specific articulable evidence on each element of the offense.

#### C. Hearsay Limitations at Preliminary Hearing

Under Proposition 115 (Penal Code § 872(b)), the prosecution may use law enforcement officer hearsay testimony at the preliminary hearing. However, the reliability of hearsay within hearsay chains is a proper subject of inquiry at the § 995 stage. Where the only evidence on an element is multi-layered hearsay from a single interested source, the Court may consider whether such evidence constitutes substantial evidence sufficient to establish probable cause on that element.
