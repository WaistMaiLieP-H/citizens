## II. LEGAL BASIS — EVIDENCE CODE §§ 1043-1047

California Evidence Code §§ 1043-1047 (the Pitchess procedure) allows a criminal defendant to discover otherwise confidential peace officer personnel records if the defendant makes a threshold showing of good cause — that the records are likely to contain relevant information material to the defense.

**The two-step process:**
1. **Ex parte filing to obtain in camera review:** Defense files a noticed motion supported by a declaration showing good cause. The court (not the prosecution) receives the motion; the specific records categories are not disclosed to the prosecution in the good-cause showing.
2. **In camera review:** The court reviews the personnel records in camera. If records contain relevant information, the court may order disclosure to the defense.

**The good cause standard is low:** A "plausible factual foundation" is sufficient. The defendant need not prove the officer actually engaged in misconduct — only that it is plausible, given the facts alleged, that the officer's personnel records would contain relevant information.

## DRAFT NOTES

### When to file:
- File after obtaining the APD incident report (which identifies the officers)
- File early in the case — before the preliminary hearing if possible, or at minimum before trial
- The Pitchess motion must be served on: (1) the custodian of records (APD), (2) the district attorney's office

### What to expect:
- **If good cause shown:** The court orders in camera review
- **In camera:** Court reviews records with no one else present; if records contain relevant information, court orders disclosure
- **If no useful records:** Court informs defense that no records were found or that records were not relevant; defense can still cross-examine on the topic at trial

### Coordination with Brady demand:
The Brady demand (brady_demand_04_23_01959_draft.md) already includes a demand for APD officer misconduct records under § 1054.1(e). The Pitchess motion provides a separate, more powerful mechanism specifically designed for this type of disclosure. File both.

### The APD forensic audit:

However, they are systemic findings, not specific findings about the officers in this case. The Pitchess motion must be grounded in specific facts about the specific officers identified in the incident report.
