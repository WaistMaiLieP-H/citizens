## I. WITNESS INFORMATION — § 1054.1(a)

1. The full legal names, addresses, and contact information of all persons the prosecution intends to call as witnesses at trial, including law enforcement officers, civilian witnesses, expert witnesses, and child witnesses.

2. The full legal name, address, and employment information of the person(s) who made the initial report to law enforcement or Child Protective Services (CPS/DCFS) in connection with the June 16, 2023 incident, if different from the persons identified as witnesses.

3. Any updated or supplemental witness list as new witnesses are identified.

---

## II. DEFENDANT'S STATEMENTS — § 1054.1(b)

4. All oral, written, or recorded statements attributed to the defendant, including:
   - Any statements made to Antioch Police Department officers at the scene on June 16, 2023;
   - Any statements made during any subsequent police interview;
   - Any statements made in any other context that the prosecution has knowledge of or possession of.

5. The full audio or video recording and transcript of any custodial interview of the defendant.

---

## III. REAL EVIDENCE — § 1054.1(c)

6. All photographs taken at the scene on June 16, 2023, including photographs of any alleged victim and any physical evidence.

7. All medical records, medical reports, or examination results relating to any alleged victim in this case, obtained by any law enforcement agency or CPS/DCFS worker in connection with this investigation.

8. All physical evidence seized or obtained during the investigation of the charged offense.

9. All APD incident reports, supplemental reports, call logs, and dispatch records relating to June 16, 2023, including:

   - The time officers were dispatched;
   - The time officers arrived at the scene.

## IV. MATERIAL WITNESS PRIOR CONVICTIONS — § 1054.1(d)

10. The existence of any felony conviction of any material witness whose credibility is likely to be critical to the outcome of the trial, including any witnesses to be called by the prosecution who have prior felony convictions.

## V. EXCULPATORY EVIDENCE (Brady) — § 1054.1(e)

The defendant specifically demands production of all evidence favorable to the accused, whether exculpatory of guilt or mitigation of punishment, including but not limited to:

11. **Prior inconsistent statements:** All prior statements made by any child witness in this case, in any context, whether to police, CPS/DCFS workers, therapists, family members, or other persons, that are inconsistent with or different from the statements provided to police or the prosecution in connection with this case.

12. **Prior unsubstantiated reports:** All CPS/DCFS records, law enforcement records, or other agency records showing any prior report of child abuse or neglect involving the same child and the defendant, including the disposition of any such prior report (whether the report was substantiated, unsubstantiated, inconclusive, or unfounded).

13. **Fabrication motive evidence:** Any evidence that the person(s) who made the initial report in this case had a motive, personal interest, or other reason to fabricate or exaggerate the allegations, including:
    - Any evidence of a concurrent family law proceeding (dissolution, custody, visitation) involving the parties;
    - Any evidence of communications between the reporting party and attorneys, social workers, or other persons in connection with pending family law proceedings;
    - Any prior communications between the reporting party and APD or CPS/DCFS about the defendant or the child prior to June 16, 2023.

14. **APD misconduct records:** Any records of complaints or findings of dishonesty, false reporting, evidence planting, or other misconduct against any APD officer who investigated, reported on, or testified about the June 16, 2023 incident. [Note: This demand is supplemented by a separately filed Pitchess motion.]

15. **Coordinator communications:** Any communications between APD, the Contra Costa County District Attorney's office, CPS/DCFS, or any other agency, and the mother, grandmother, or other family member of the child alleged victim, before, during, or after the June 16, 2023 incident.

16. **Medical exculpatory evidence:** Any medical evidence, examination result, or expert opinion that is inconsistent with the charge that the defendant inflicted corporal punishment causing a traumatic condition.

17. **Child witness reliability evidence:** Any information known to the prosecution regarding the suggestibility of the child witness, the circumstances under which the child's statements were obtained, any coaching or prompting of the child, or any expert assessment of the child's competence or reliability as a witness.

18. **Any other exculpatory evidence:** All evidence that is favorable to the defendant, whether in the possession of the Contra Costa County District Attorney's Office, APD, CPS/DCFS, or any other investigating or governmental agency involved in this case.

## DRAFT NOTES

- **Send early:** Brady/§ 1054.1 demand should be filed as early as possible — ideally at or shortly after arraignment. The earlier the demand is on record, the clearer any subsequent noncompliance.
- **File with the court:** Consider filing the demand with the court as well as serving on the prosecution. This creates a court record of the demand.
- **Follow up:** If no response within 30 days (or within a shorter time set by the court's discovery schedule), file a motion to compel under § 1054.5.
- **Items 13 and 15:** The family law coordination angle is the most important exculpatory evidence. Push hard on APD call logs (who called, when, from what number), CPS initial referral (who called CPS and when), and family law filing dates.
