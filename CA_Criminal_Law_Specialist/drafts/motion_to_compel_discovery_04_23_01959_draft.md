### III. LEGAL STANDARD

#### A. The Informal Request Requirement Is Satisfied

Penal Code § 1054.5(b) requires that before seeking court enforcement, the moving party make an informal request of opposing counsel and allow 15 days for a response. Defendant satisfied this requirement by serving the written demand on [DATE]. More than 15 days have elapsed. This Court has authority to act.

#### B. Scope of the People's Disclosure Obligations

Penal Code § 1054.1 requires the prosecuting attorney to disclose to the defendant, before trial, six categories of material. These categories include: (1) names and addresses of witnesses; (2) statements of all defendants; (3) all relevant real evidence; (4) recordings of all interviews with defendants; (5) exculpatory evidence; and (6) the existence of a felony conviction of any material witness whose credibility is in issue.

These statutory obligations overlay — but do not diminish — the constitutional obligations under *Brady v. Maryland* (1963) 373 U.S. 83 and *Giglio v. United States* (1972) 405 U.S. 150. Under *Brady*, the prosecution must disclose all evidence that is favorable to the accused and material to guilt or punishment. Under *Giglio*, the Brady obligation extends to evidence that may be used to impeach the credibility of prosecution witnesses. The obligation is affirmative, is not discharged by good faith, and runs to all members of the prosecution team including investigating law enforcement agencies.

#### C. This Court's Sanctioning Authority

Upon a showing that the People have not complied with § 1054.1, the Court may make any order necessary to enforce the provisions, including immediate disclosure, contempt proceedings, continuance, delay or prohibition of witness testimony or real evidence, and jury advisory. (*People v. Gonzales* (2012) 54 Cal.4th 1234, 1258-1259.) The Court has broad discretion in selecting and combining sanctions. The selected sanction must be tailored to the harm caused by the disclosure failure. (*Id.* at p. 1260.)

The materials identified above are not peripheral — they go to the heart of the defense. This case turns on whether the arrest on June 16, 2023, was based on a genuine complaint or was orchestrated through coordinated false reporting. The APD dispatch records and the identity of the reporting party are the direct evidence on this question. The child forensic interview and CPS records bear on whether any alleged harm to the minor was independently corroborated or rests entirely on the reporting party's allegations.

The prejudice is real, ongoing, and incurable without immediate disclosure.
