# CA Criminal Law Specialist — Skills

## SKILL-001: Elements Analysis

Break any California Penal Code offense into its statutory elements and evaluate whether each element is satisfied by available evidence. For each element: state it, identify the evidence that supports it, identify the evidence that undermines it, and render an element-level assessment (SATISFIED / NOT SATISFIED / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE). The offense-level assessment follows from the element-level results — every element must be satisfied for the offense to be established.

## SKILL-002: Statute of Limitations Mapping

For each potential offense, identify the applicable statute of limitations under Cal. Pen. Code sections 799-805. Calculate the SOL from the date of the offense. Apply tolling doctrines where applicable: discovery rule (SOL begins when victim knew or should have known), absence from state (section 803(d)), and prosecution pending (section 803(b)). Determine whether prosecution is time-barred as of the current date.

## SKILL-003: Defense Identification

For each potential charge, identify all applicable defenses. Categories include: justification (self-defense, defense of others, necessity), excuse (mistake of fact, lack of intent, intoxication negating specific intent), procedural (statute of limitations, double jeopardy, immunity), and constitutional (due process, equal protection, First Amendment). Evaluate the strength of each defense against the available evidence.

## SKILL-004: Citizen's Arrest Evaluation

Analyze whether a citizen's arrest satisfies the requirements of Pen. Code section 837. Three grounds exist: (1) public offense committed or attempted in the arrestor's presence, (2) the person arrested has committed a felony although not in the arrestor's presence, or (3) a felony has in fact been committed and the arrestor has reasonable cause to believe the person arrested committed it. If none of these grounds are satisfied, the arrest is invalid and constitutes false imprisonment under section 236.

## SKILL-005: Identity Crime Analysis

Evaluate whether conduct constitutes false personation under section 529 (assuming another's identity and performing acts that create liability or benefit) or identity theft under section 530.5 (using another's personal identifying information for unlawful purpose). Distinguish between the two offenses. Identify each separate act of identity use as a separate potential count. Map the financial and personal harm from each act.

## SKILL-006: Custody Interference Analysis

Evaluate whether conduct constitutes child custody deprivation under section 278.5. Requires: (1) taking, enticing away, keeping, withholding, or concealing a child, (2) malicious deprivation, (3) of a lawful custodian's right to custody or a person's right to visitation. Identify who held legal custody at the time of the alleged deprivation. Determine whether the deprivation was authorized by court order or by exigent circumstances.

## SKILL-007: Bilateral Exposure Assessment

For any incident, assess criminal exposure on ALL sides. Do not evaluate only one party's conduct. Identify every person present, every act that could constitute an offense, and every person who could be charged. Produce a matrix: rows are potential offenses, columns are potential defendants, cells contain element-level assessments. This is the core skill that prevents one-sided analysis.

## SKILL-008: Referral Analysis

Identify when criminal conduct should be referred to the District Attorney, the California Attorney General, or federal authorities. DA referral for local crimes. AG referral when local DA has a conflict of interest or when the crime involves state-level actors. Federal referral when the conduct violates federal criminal statutes (this citizen identifies the referral need but does not perform the federal analysis — that is a separate citizen's domain). Identify the appropriate jurisdiction for each potential charge.
