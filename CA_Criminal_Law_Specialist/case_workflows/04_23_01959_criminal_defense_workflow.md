## Phase 2: Motion practice

### 2.1 Motion to Set Aside Information — Pen. Code § 995
**Grounds:** The defendant was committed without reasonable or probable cause.

**Standard:** The § 995 motion tests whether the evidence at the preliminary hearing established probable cause — not guilt beyond a reasonable doubt, but more than mere suspicion. The magistrate must have had a rational basis to believe a crime was committed and that the defendant committed it.

**Argument focus:**
- Was any physical evidence of a traumatic condition presented?
- Was the child's testimony credible given the suggestibility and coaching issues?
- Was the identification of the defendant as the actor supported by competent evidence?
- Were there inconsistencies in the report that undermine probable cause?

**Filing deadline:** Before or at time of arraignment in superior court. Motion must be brought before trial.

### 2.2 Pitchess motion — Evidence Code §§ 1043-1047
If APD officers responded to the scene and wrote reports, file a Pitchess motion to discover any prior complaints of dishonesty, excessive force, or false reporting against the responding officers.

- Prior complaints of: dishonesty, making false reports, planting evidence, or improper conduct
- APD forensic audit (10 violations documented in memory — project_conservatorship_breakthrough.md) supports the motion

### 2.3 Motion to suppress child statements — Evidence Code § 402
Challenge the admissibility of the child's forensic interview statements:
- Was the interview conducted by a trained forensic interviewer?
- Were leading questions used?
- Was the child coached or contaminated before the interview?
- Does the recording show suggestive techniques?

### 2.4 Brady motion — Pen. Code § 1054.1
Demand all exculpatory evidence. Focus specifically on:
- Any notes from CPS/DCFS showing inconsistency or recantation
- Any prior false reports involving the same parties
- The relationship between the reporting party and the family court proceedings
- APD internal communications about the case

### 3.2 Alternative defense theory — Parental discipline

**Backup theory (if any physical contact is conceded):** The conduct was lawful parental discipline that did not rise to "cruel or inhuman" corporal punishment and did not cause a traumatic condition. This is the "punishment track" defense under § 273d.

**Elements to contest:**
- Was there a traumatic condition? (attack medical evidence; show alternative explanations for any marks)
- Was the punishment cruel or inhuman? (context of behavior, proportionality)
- Was the punishment willful in the relevant sense?

### 3.3 CALCRIM No. 821 — Use the instruction

CALCRIM No. 821 requires the jury to find:
1. The defendant willfully inflicted
2. Cruel or inhuman corporal punishment OR injury resulting in a traumatic condition
3. On a child under 18

Use the instruction to focus the jury on the specific elements and to show what the prosecution must prove.

## Phase 4: Sentencing strategy (if conviction)

### 4.1 Probation — prefer over custody
For a § 273d first offense without great bodily injury, probation is available. Mandatory conditions under subd. (c):
- 36 months minimum
- Protective order for victim
- One year child abuser's treatment program (§ 273.1-compliant)
- Substance abstinence if applicable

### 4.2 Mental health diversion — Pen. Code § 1001.36
**Consider before trial:** If the facts support a § 1001.36 mental health diversion petition (based on documented PTSD, depression, or other qualifying disorder), file the petition before trial. Child abuse is not in the § 1001.36(d) excluded offense list — § 273d charges ARE eligible for diversion.

See standard: pen_code_1001_36_mental_health_diversion

### 4.3 Prop 36 / drug treatment — if substance issues alleged
If the charge has a substance-related component (subd. (c)(4) of § 273d), explore drug treatment diversion before trial.
