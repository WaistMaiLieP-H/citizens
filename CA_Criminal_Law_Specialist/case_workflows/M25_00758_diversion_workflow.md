## Phase 2: Build the § 1001.36 petition

### 2.1 Obtain mental health evaluation
**This is the threshold requirement.** The defense must present evidence of a qualifying mental health diagnosis within the last 5 years by a qualified mental health expert.

**Steps:**
- [ ] Contact a forensic psychologist or psychiatrist who can perform a § 1001.36 evaluation
- [ ] Gather all existing medical records that document mental health diagnosis or treatment:
  - Spine surgery 2021/2022 records (pain → depression/anxiety connection)
  - SIRVA records (COVID vaccine injury → documented physical and psychological impact)
  - Any existing mental health treatment records
  - Documentation of the 16-year orchestration impact (PTSD)
- [ ] The evaluator should prepare a written declaration or report addressing:
  - DSM-5 diagnosis (qualifying disorder — PTSD, depression, anxiety, bipolar, etc.)
  - When first diagnosed / treated (must be within last 5 years)
  - How the disorder was a significant factor in the alleged offense

**Note on the "significant factor" presumption:** Once a qualifying diagnosis is established, § 1001.36(b)(2) creates a presumption that the disorder was a significant factor. The prosecution must rebut by clear and convincing evidence. Defense does not need to prove the connection — only establish the diagnosis.

### 2.2 Identify a treatment program
The court must approve a specific treatment program before granting diversion.

- [ ] Identify outpatient mental health treatment providers in Solano County (or adjoining counties if Solano resources are limited)
- [ ] Obtain a letter of acceptance or willingness to accept from the program
- [ ] Confirm the program meets § 1001.36(f)(1)(A)(i)-(ii) criteria
- [ ] Prepare a treatment plan to present to the court

### 2.3 Draft the § 1001.36 petition
**Contents:**
1. Statement of the charge and that it is not an excluded offense under subd. (d)
2. Eligibility declaration: qualifying DSM diagnosis + "significant factor" argument
3. Mental health expert declaration or report
4. Suitability factors (subd. (c)):
   - Expert opinion that symptoms would respond to treatment
   - Defendant's consent and waiver of speedy trial
   - Agreement to comply with treatment
   - No unreasonable risk to public safety (§ 1170.18 standard — high threshold)
5. Proposed treatment program
6. Request for diversion period (up to 2 years for felony, up to 1 year for misdemeanor)

### 2.4 File and serve the petition
- [ ] File petition with Solano Superior Court
- [ ] Serve on Solano County District Attorney
- [ ] Request hearing date
- [ ] Prepare for potential prima facie hearing (informal — offers of proof and reliable hearsay)

## Phase 3: Hearing and grant of diversion

### 3.1 Prepare for the § 1001.36 hearing

**Arguments to make:**
1. **Eligibility (subd. (b)):** Present the expert's diagnosis. Invoke the presumption under subd. (b)(2) — prosecution must rebut by clear and convincing evidence.
2. **Suitability (subd. (c)):** Expert confirms treatability; defendant consents; treatment plan is in place; no unreasonable danger to public safety.
3. **Court's discretion (subd. (a)):** The court has heard the prosecution's position; it is not bound by prosecution objection. The Legislature's express policy preference is treatment over punishment for eligible defendants.

**Anticipate prosecution arguments:**
- "Significant factor" not established — rebut with the presumption
- "Danger to public safety" — § 1170.18 sets a high bar (super strike offenses); a non-violent charge will rarely meet it
- Treatment program is inadequate — have backup programs identified
- Defendant is not suitable — be prepared with specific suitability evidence

### 3.2 If diversion is granted

- [ ] Confirm the diversion period (start date, end date)
- [ ] Enroll in treatment program immediately; document enrollment
- [ ] Ensure provider is set up to make regular progress reports to the court
- [ ] Monitor restitution order (if any — subd. (f)(1)(D))
- [ ] Avoid any new violations of law during the diversion period
- [ ] 60 days before the diversion period ends, confirm "substantial compliance" status with the program and with the court

### 3.3 If diversion is denied

- [ ] Immediately assess grounds for appeal (was there an eligibility hearing? Did the court engage with the statutory criteria on the record?)
- [ ] Preserve all objections on the record
- [ ] File appeal if the denial was legally erroneous
- [ ] If appeal succeeds → *People v. Frahs* conditional reversal → remand for eligibility hearing

2. Dismiss all charges

## Notes on the Frahs retroactivity argument

If diversion was not raised at trial (by court-appointed counsel) despite documented mental health grounds, and a conviction results, *People v. Frahs* (2020) 9 Cal.5th 618 supports reversal on appeal and remand for a § 1001.36 eligibility hearing. This is also potentially an IAC (ineffective assistance of counsel) claim if trial counsel failed to investigate or raise the issue.
