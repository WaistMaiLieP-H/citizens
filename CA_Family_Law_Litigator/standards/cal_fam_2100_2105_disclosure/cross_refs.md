# Family Code §§ 2100-2105 — Cross-References

## Within Family Code Chapter 9 (the immediate disclosure cluster)

- **§ 2106** — No judgment until parties have complied with Chapter 9 (subject to § 2107/§ 2110 exceptions).
- **§ 2107** — Remedies for noncompliance: motion to compel, monetary sanctions, attorney's fees, and preclusive orders.
- **§ 2110** — Default judgments: the respondent in true default is not required to serve a PDD/FDD; the petitioner's disclosure duty remains.
- **§ 2111** — Disclosures in a dissolution are confidential and generally not subject to court-record public access.
- **§ 2112** — Judicial Council form-development authority.
- **§ 2113** — Chapter 9 is not a limit on existing discovery rights.

## Set-aside and omitted-asset machinery

- **§§ 2120-2129 (Chapter 10)** — Set-aside of judgments. § 2122 grounds include (a) actual fraud, (b) perjury, (c) duress, (d) mental incapacity, (e) mistake, and **(f) failure to comply with disclosure requirements of Chapter 9**. § 2124-2125 address relief from judgment for stipulated judgments. § 2128 expressly does not limit remedies otherwise available.
- **§ 2556** — Continuing jurisdiction to award community property or debts not previously adjudicated (omitted-asset jurisdiction). § 2102(a)(2) routes post-separation investment-opportunity nondisclosure to § 2556.
- **§ 1101** — Action for breach of spousal fiduciary duty. § 1101(g) remedies include 50% of the undisclosed asset plus attorney's fees; **§ 1101(h) mandates 100% (or greater) where breach is willful** or involves oppression/fraud/malice. Chapter 9 compliance record is the evidentiary runway for § 1101.

## Fiduciary-duty backbone

- **§ 721** — Transactions between spouses subject to rules controlling confidential relationships (partnership-type fiduciary standards). § 2102(a) explicitly imports § 721 for Chapter 9 purposes. § 721(b)(1)-(3) enumerate disclosure, accounting, and accounting-on-demand duties between spouses.
- **§ 1100** — Management and control of community personal property; subject to § 721 fiduciary duties.
- **§§ 1102, 1103** — Community real property management; § 721 fiduciary duties apply.

## Support and income disclosure

- **§ 4058** — Income definition for child support. § 2101(c) cross-references § 4058.
- **§§ 4050-4076** — Statewide uniform guideline for child support. I&E declarations under § 2104(e) and § 2105(a) feed these calculations.
- **§§ 4300-4360** — Spousal support. I&E supports these determinations.

## Sanctions and fee-shifting

- **§ 271** — Sanctions for conduct that frustrates settlement. Disclosure stonewalling commonly qualifies. § 271 sanctions can stack on top of § 2107 and § 1101 remedies.
- **§ 2030** — Need-based attorney's fees. Fee orders can track disclosure-based litigation.
- **§ 2032** — Fee orders grounded in need and ability.

## Intersections with parallel Family Code regimes

- **§ 3011** — Best-interest factors in custody. § 3011(d) gives the court weight on history of false statements about child-abuse allegations; by extension, a parent's pattern of disclosure noncompliance in § 2100-2105 context bears on credibility when § 3011(e) "other factors" weighs.
- **§ 3044** — DV presumption. Economic-control and financial-coercion evidence developed through § 1101 fiduciary-breach litigation can feed § 6320 "disturbing the peace" analysis and, derivatively, the § 3044 rebuttable presumption.
- **§ 6203, 6320** — DVPA abuse definitions. Economic control is abuse.
- **§ 3111** — Court-appointed custody evaluations. Where the evaluator is required to assess household finances or economic stability, § 2100-2105 compliance (or lack thereof) is material data the evaluator should incorporate.
- **§ 3150-3153** — Minor's counsel. § 3151(c)(5) access to records includes financial records material to the child's best interest.

## Code of Civil Procedure — discovery integration

- **CCP §§ 2016.010-2036.050** — General discovery rules. § 2113 confirms Chapter 9 is not a limit on these. Parties can combine disclosure mandates with discovery demands.
- **CCP § 2025** — Depositions. Disclosures developed in depositions can cross-reference Chapter 9 declarations to establish contradictions.
- **CCP §§ 473, 473.5** — Relief from default and judgment. Complements § 2122 set-aside but operates on different substantive grounds.

## Evidence Code

- **Evid. § 1220** — Admissions of a party. Statements in a PDD or FDD are admissions and admissible without further foundation.
- **Evid. § 622** — Rebuttable presumption: the truth of facts recited in a written contract or disclosure is conclusive between the parties. Applies where a party attempts to disavow a prior disclosure.
- **Evid. §§ 1400-1402** — Authentication. § 2104(a)/§ 2105(a) penalty-of-perjury verification simplifies authentication.

## Penal Code

- **PC § 118** — Perjury. Directly applicable to false statements in a PDD or FDD. Though rarely prosecuted in family-law context, the exposure is an element of the structural deterrent.
- **PC § 532** — Theft by false pretense. Where disclosure fraud also operates as asset theft, PC § 532 can supply a parallel criminal theory.

## Federal cross-references

- **IRC § 6013 and § 7206** — Joint-return signatures and tax perjury. Tax returns attached under § 2104(a) are themselves sworn documents. Contradictions between tax returns and Chapter 9 declarations expose the declarant to IRS scrutiny and PC § 118 perjury concerns.
- **11 U.S.C. § 523** — Bankruptcy non-dischargeability. Support and property-division debts are generally non-dischargeable (11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(5), (a)(15)); set-aside relief under § 2122(f) survives bankruptcy of the noncomplying spouse.
- **42 U.S.C. § 1983** — Where state-actor misconduct (including fraudulent disclosure practices by a state-employed spouse or where a court systematically fails to enforce Chapter 9 in violation of due process) contributes to the deprivation, federal civil-rights exposure can attach.
