# Historical Loss — Cal. Fam. Code § 6321

## How Parties Lose on This Standard

### Failure Mode 1: Failing to Contest the Ex Parte Order Before It Hardens
Ex parte exclusion orders become quasi-permanent when the excluded party fails to request an OSC hearing promptly. Many excluded parties miss the window to contest the factual basis or fail to appear at the OSC, and the order is extended or made permanent on default. The excluded party loses the right to contest the underlying facts if they do not act within the response window. File a response and appear at every hearing.

### Failure Mode 2: Accepting the Harm Allegation as True
The most common tactical error is accepting the moving party's characterization of the harm — fear, emotional distress, physical danger — without requiring proof. Courts will issue the order if the moving party's declaration is not specifically contradicted. At the OSC, the excluded party must put on affirmative evidence showing the specific allegations are false, not just generally deny them.

### Failure Mode 3: Ignoring § 6321(b) When Property Rights Are at Stake
Where the excluded party is an owner of the home being excluded from, failure to explicitly invoke § 6321(b) allows the other party to conflate the exclusion with a property adjudication. The excluded party then loses practical control of the property during the pendency of the proceedings. § 6321(b) must be cited explicitly to preserve property rights arguments for subsequent proceedings.

### Failure Mode 4: Confusing Credible Threat with Fear
Courts occasionally grant § 6321 orders based on the moving party's expression of subjective fear, without specific conduct constituting an objective credible threat. These orders are reversible error, but only if the excluded party raises the standard and shows the absence of specific threatening conduct. Failure to distinguish subjective fear from objectively credible threat — by citing the statutory language — results in the order standing.

### Failure Mode 5: Not Connecting the Exclusion Order to § 3046(c) Consequences
An exclusion order under § 6321 triggers the § 3046(c) exception, which removes the absent parent's protection against having their departure used against them in custody proceedings. Parties who do not see this connection fail to challenge the § 6321 order aggressively enough. A fabricated § 6321 exclusion order is a mechanism to strip § 3046 protection — challenge it on that basis as well.
