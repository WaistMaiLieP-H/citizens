NAKAMURA v. PARKER
156 Cal.App.4th 327
California Court of Appeal, 2007

FACTS:
Hiromi Nakamura sought a domestic violence restraining order against Anthony Parker,
seeking exclusion from their shared dwelling. The trial court issued the exclusion
order. Parker appealed, arguing that the evidence was insufficient to meet the
§ 6321 standard and that his due process rights were violated by the ex parte
exclusion without adequate notice.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 6321:
The Court of Appeal addressed the sufficiency of evidence standard for § 6321
exclusion orders. The court confirmed that an exclusion order under § 6321 requires
specific evidence satisfying both prongs of the conjunctive test: (1) physical or
emotional harm would otherwise result, and (2) the party to be excluded has assaulted
or made credible threats. Conclusory allegations of fear, without specific conduct
meeting the statutory definition, are insufficient. The court further confirmed that
§ 6321(b)'s protection of property rights means the exclusion order cannot be used
as a vehicle to determine ownership or possessory rights.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Nakamura is the primary California appellate authority establishing that § 6321
exclusion orders are subject to rigorous evidentiary review. The court's requirement
of specific conduct evidence — not just generalized fear — provides the framework
for challenging fabricated exclusion orders. In the steward's case, any § 6321
order obtained on the basis of Christina's counter-filing pattern (unsupported
allegations, fabricated police reports) should be challenged under the Nakamura
specificity standard. The court's articulation of § 6321(b)'s property protection
is also relevant to the 2958 Honeysuckle equity claim.
