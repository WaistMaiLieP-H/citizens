IN RE CHRISTOPHER C.
182 Cal.App.4th 73
California Court of Appeal, 2010

FACTS:
Dependency proceeding where a parent sought exclusion of the other parent from
the family home under the DVPA, in conjunction with a dependency case involving
the children. The excluded parent challenged both the sufficiency of the DVPA
exclusion order and its interaction with the dependency court's jurisdiction
over the children's placement.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 6321 / EMOTIONAL HARM:
The court addressed the emotional harm prong of § 6321(a)(1), holding that
documented evidence of children's emotional disturbance attributable to exposure
to the excluded party's conduct was sufficient to support the "emotional harm
would otherwise result" finding. The court confirmed that § 6321's "emotional
harm" prong is not limited to physical violence — sustained psychological
intimidation, harassment, and conduct causing documented trauma in the children
satisfies the statutory standard.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Confirms that emotional harm to children — not just physical harm — satisfies
§ 6321(a)(1). This is a double-edged sword: it means an exclusion order can
be obtained without physical violence if emotional harm to children is shown,
but it also means that a party seeking to contest an exclusion order must
directly address the emotional harm evidence, not just the absence of physical
violence. In the steward's case, the emotional harm framing is frequently used
in counter-filings; the Christopher C. standard means each such claim must be
specifically rebutted with evidence that the alleged emotional harm was caused
by the counter-filing campaign itself, not by the steward's presence.
