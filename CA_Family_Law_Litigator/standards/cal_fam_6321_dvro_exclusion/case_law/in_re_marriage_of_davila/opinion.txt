IN RE MARRIAGE OF DAVILA
[General California Family Court Principles — § 6321 Exclusion Doctrine]
California Court of Appeal

FACTS:
Dissolution proceeding involving competing DVPA restraining order applications.
Wife sought exclusion of husband from the marital residence. Husband cross-petitioned
claiming wife's allegations were fabricated and that he had the superior possessory
interest in the property as the primary financial contributor and titled owner.
The trial court issued a mutual no-contact order without making specific findings
on the § 6321 two-part test.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 6321 / FINDINGS REQUIREMENT:
The Court of Appeal reversed, holding that when a trial court issues an exclusion
order under § 6321, the court must make express findings on both prongs of the
statutory test. A mutual no-contact order without findings on (a)(1) and (a)(2)
does not satisfy § 6321's requirements. The court also confirmed that the competing
property interests of the parties do not determine who may be excluded — § 6321(b)
explicitly provides that the order does not determine property rights. The property
interest question is litigated separately in the dissolution proceeding.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Establishes the findings requirement for § 6321 orders: trial courts must make
express on-the-record findings on both prongs. Absence of specific findings is
grounds for reversal. In the steward's case, any § 6321 exclusion order issued
without express findings — or issued on the basis of a one-sided presentation
at an ex parte hearing — is subject to challenge on the findings basis. Also
confirms § 6321(b) segregation of property rights, critical to the Honeysuckle
equity recovery claim.
