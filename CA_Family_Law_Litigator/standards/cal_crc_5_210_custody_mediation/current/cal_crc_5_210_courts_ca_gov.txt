Rule 5.210. Court-connected child custody mediation

(a) Authority
This rule of court is adopted under article VI, section 6 of the California Constitution and Family Code sections 211, 3160, and 3162(a).

(b) Purpose
This rule sets forth standards of practice and administration for court-connected child custody mediation services that are consistent with the requirements of Family Code section 3161.

(c) Definitions
(1) "Best interest of the child" is defined in Family Code section 3011.
(2) "Parenting plan" is a plan describing how parents or other appropriate parties will share and divide their decision making and caretaking responsibilities to protect the health, safety, welfare, and best interest of each child who is a subject of the proceedings.

(d) Responsibility for mediation services
(1) Each court must ensure that: mediators are impartial, competent, and uphold standards in the rule; mediation services implement state law with sufficient time for participation; services demonstrate accountability through complaint procedures, data collection, and disclosure of intern use; a detailed intake process screens for restraining orders and safety issues; bilingual mediators or interpreters are available when possible; party confidentiality is protected in records storage, interagency coordination, and child abuse reports; a written description of confidentiality limitations is provided; within one year, the court adopts a local rule regarding ex parte communications.
(2) Each mediator must: maintain overriding concern for the child's best interest within family context; inform parties if recommendations will be made to the court; use reasonable efforts to facilitate family transition, develop comprehensive parenting agreements, and control power imbalances.
(3) If informed by a child, custody recommending counselors must notify parties about the child's desire to address the court and any changes to that choice.

(e) Mediation process
All court-connected mediation must include: (1) review of intake form and court file before mediation starts; (2) oral or written orientation covering types of disputed issues and possible outcomes, the mediation process and mediator's role, limitations on confidentiality and information access, how to use current research to facilitate communication, how to address children's developmental needs; (3) interviews with children at mediator's discretion, consistent with Family Code section 3180(a); (4) assistance developing a parenting plan protecting the child's health, safety, welfare, and best interest; (5) extension of time to gather additional information if the mediator determines it will help fair discussion or facilitate agreement; (6) suspension or discontinuance if child abuse or neglect allegations are made until agency investigation is completed; (7) termination if the mediator cannot achieve balanced discussion; (8) conclusion with a written parenting plan or mediator's recommendation given to counsel or parties before court presentation, and a description of subsequent case management procedures; (9) return to mediation for future custody or visitation disputes.

(f) Training, continuing education, and experience requirements
As specified in Family Code sections 1815 and 1816: (1) all mediators, supervisors, and family court services directors must complete minimum 40 hours of custody and visitation mediation training within first six months, annually complete 8 hours of related continuing education (in addition to 4-hour domestic violence update), and participate in performance supervision and peer review; (2) each mediation supervisor and family court services director must complete at least 24 hours of additional training annually.

(g) Education and training providers
Eligible providers must: ensure instructors either meet rule requirements or are subject matter experts; monitor and evaluate course quality; emphasize focusing mediations on child's health, safety, welfare, and best interest; verify participant completion; distribute certificates documenting hours, completion dates, and provider name. Effective July 1, 2005, all programs must be approved by Judicial Council staff.

(h) Ethics
Mediators must: (1) meet the practice and ethical standards of the Code of Ethics for the Court Employees of California and of related law; (2) maintain objectivity and provide balanced information for both parties; (3) protect confidentiality and not release information except as authorized by court or statute; (4) not offer recommendations about a party unless evaluated directly or through consultation; (5) consider child's health, safety, welfare, and best interest in all phases; (6) strive to maintain confidentiality between child and treating psychotherapist; (7) operate within training and experience limits and disclose limitations or bias; (8) not require children to state custodial preference; (9) not disclose recommendations before gathering necessary information; (10) disclose conflicts of interest and dual relationships to court and parties; (11) be sensitive to parties' socioeconomic status, gender, race, ethnicity, cultural values, religion, family structures, and developmental characteristics; (12) disclose conflicts of interest; if unresolved, suspend mediation or continue only with written party agreement.

Amended effective January 1, 2023; adopted as rule 1257.1 effective July 1, 2001.
