ELSENHEIMER v. ELSENHEIMER
(2004) 124 Cal.App.4th 1532
Court of Appeal, Sixth Appellate District, 2004

FACTS:
In a contested custody modification proceeding, the father challenged the mediator's recommendation on the grounds that the mediator had not directly evaluated him or the children and had issued a recommendation based primarily on the mother's account and documents. The trial court adopted the recommendation without additional inquiry. The father appealed.

HOLDING — Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 5.210:
The Court of Appeal held that a mediator may not issue a recommendation without conducting direct evaluation of all parties and the children involved. Rule 5.210(g) establishes this as a minimum requirement, not a best practice. The court found the mediator's process deficient because the mediator had met only with the mother and had not independently assessed the children's circumstances. A custody recommendation based solely on one party's account does not satisfy the rule and should not have been adopted by the trial court without independent inquiry.

The court vacated the custody order and remanded for a properly conducted mediation.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Elsenheimer is the clearest authority for the principle that Rule 5.210(g) is an enforceable substantive standard, not a procedural suggestion. For the steward's cases, any mediation session in the 2009-2025 proceedings where the mediator did not directly evaluate Michael, or did not evaluate the children, is a documented Elsenheimer violation. The case also establishes that a trial court that adopts a defective recommendation without independent review is itself committing reversible error.
