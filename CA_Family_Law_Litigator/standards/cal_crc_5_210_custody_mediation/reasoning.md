# cal_crc_5_210_custody_mediation — Reasoning

**Status: PROPOSED**

## How Courts Apply This Standard

### 1. Qualification is a Threshold Gatekeeping Requirement

Courts treat mediator qualification under Rule 5.210 as a threshold question, not a formality. When a party challenges whether a mediator met minimum qualifications, the court must determine whether the mediator's credentials and training satisfy Rule 5.210(b)-(f). A mediator who was unqualified at the time of the mediation has not conducted valid court-connected mediation — the resulting recommendation is tainted.

The challenge is typically raised at the time of an objection to the mediator's recommendation, or in a motion to vacate a custody order entered in reliance on a defective mediation.

### 2. DV Screening is Mandatory Before Assignment

Rule 5.210(h) requires screening before assignment. This is not a courtesy — it is a condition of valid mediation in DV cases. If a court assigns a DV case to mediation without conducting proper DV screening, the mediation was conducted without proper preparation and the mediator may not have implemented the safety protocols the rule requires (separate sessions, safety planning).

### 3. Separate Sessions — Party Right, Not Mediator Discretion

Rule 5.210(i) gives parties in DV cases the right to separate sessions. The mediator does not have discretion to deny this right. If a party in a DV case requests separate sessions and the mediator conducts joint sessions anyway, the mediation was conducted in violation of the rule. Recommendations from that mediation are procedurally defective.

### 4. The 40/16/8/4 Hour Training Cascade

Courts audit mediator training files when challenged. The training requirements are:
- 40 hours initial (total)
- 16 of those 40 hours must be DV-specific
- 8 hours CE per year (ongoing)
- 4 of those 8 hours must be DV-specific annually

A mediator who completed 40 hours of general mediation training but only 8 hours of DV training does not meet Rule 5.210(d). Mediators who let their annual CE lapse (missed a year of CE) are not in compliance during the lapse period.

### 5. Impartiality and Conflict Disclosure

Mediators must disclose any relationship with either party, any prior contact with either party's attorneys, and any other factor that could affect impartiality. Failure to disclose a conflict — even an inadvertent one — is grounds to challenge the recommendation. Courts have vacated custody recommendations where undisclosed mediator conflicts were discovered post-order.

### 6. Recommendation Without Direct Evaluation = Violation

A mediator may not recommend a custody arrangement without directly evaluating all parties and the children. Telephone-only interviews, file-only reviews, or recommendations based solely on one party's statement violate Rule 5.210(g). Any recommendation without direct evaluation of all relevant persons is procedurally invalid.
