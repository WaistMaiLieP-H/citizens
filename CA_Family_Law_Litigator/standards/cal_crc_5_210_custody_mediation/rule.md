# California Rules of Court, Rule 5.210 — Qualifications and Training of Court-Connected Child Custody Mediators

**Status: PROPOSED**
**Steward: Michael Hartmann**
**Citizen: CA_Family_Law_Litigator**

---

## Rule Text (Key Provisions)

*Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 5.210:*

**(a) Purpose:**
This rule establishes minimum qualifications and training requirements for mediators and evaluators providing family court services consistent with Family Code section 3161 et seq.

**(b) Minimum Qualifications:**
A mediator or evaluator must hold a master's or doctoral degree in psychology, social work, marriage and family therapy, or a related field from an accredited institution, OR have equivalent experience as defined by the court.

**(c) Initial Training — 40 Hours Minimum:**
Before beginning mediation duties, a mediator must complete a minimum of 40 hours of training in mediation skills and process, with specific components including:
- Mediation theory and practice
- Family dynamics in separation and divorce
- Child development and the impact of family disruption
- Cultural and socioeconomic factors
- Legal aspects of family law (including evidence handling and protective orders)

**(d) Domestic Violence — Mandatory Training:**
Initial training must include a minimum of 16 hours of domestic violence training covering:
- The nature and dynamics of domestic violence
- Safety planning with victims
- Impact of domestic violence on children
- How to conduct separate sessions safely
- Legal requirements including mandatory reporting

**(e) Continuing Education — 8 Hours Annual / 4 Hours DV:**
After initial qualification, each mediator must complete a minimum of 8 hours of continuing education per calendar year, including at least 4 hours in domestic violence updating each year.

**(f) Cross-Reference to Statute:**
Mediators must meet the training requirements of Family Code §§ 1815 and 1816. A mediator who does not meet § 1815 requirements violates both the statute and this rule.

**(g) Conduct Standards:**
- Mediators must be impartial
- Mediators must maintain the child's best interests as the overriding concern
- Mediators must not require children to state a custodial preference
- Mediators must disclose all potential conflicts of interest
- Mediators must not offer recommendations without direct evaluation of all parties and children

**(h) Screening for Domestic Violence:**
Courts must screen all custody cases for domestic violence before assigning to mediation. Cases involving DV must be offered separate sessions.

**(i) Separate Sessions:**
Parties have the right to separate sessions. Upon request, the mediator must conduct the sessions separately and without either party having contact with the other.
