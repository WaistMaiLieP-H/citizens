## Junctions (Cross-Citizen / Cross-Domain)

- **PEN § 1368 → FAM § 3044** — competency proceedings in the criminal track produce records relevant to § 3044's "perpetrator" finding. A fraudulent competency evaluation can contaminate both the § 1368 track and the § 3044 predicate.
- **PEN § 273.6 → FAM § 3044** — violation of a protective order is itself a DV act satisfying the § 3044(b)(2)(E) (restraining-order compliance) and § 3044(b)(2)(F) (further acts of DV) factors; a § 273.6 conviction within five years triggers § 3044 directly.
- **EVID § 730 → FAM § 3044(e)** — court-appointed expert evaluations under Evidence Code § 730 interact with § 3044(e)'s prohibition on findings based solely on evaluator conclusions. A § 730 evaluator's report cannot be the sole basis for a § 3044 finding.
- **US Federal 42 USC § 1983 → FAM § 3044** — when state actors (judges, mediators, FCS staff) apply § 3044 in a manner that deprives a parent of due process — e.g., by inverting the burden without a valid § 6203 predicate — the § 1983 claim lies in the federal track.
- **WIC § 300(a) → FAM § 3044** — juvenile dependency findings of physical abuse can feed § 3044 in the family track; the two tracks must be coordinated because § 3044(d)(2) accepts "a finding of domestic violence pursuant to this code or… a finding of abuse… pursuant to the Domestic Violence Prevention Act" from "another court."

## Critical Distinction — § 3044 vs. § 3011 Best-Interest Weighing

§ 3011 is a multi-factor best-interest balancing test. § 3044 is a burden-shifting presumption that operates *inside* § 3011. Once § 3044 attaches, the burden inverts and the § 3020(b) / § 3040(a)(1) contact preference is removed from the rebuttal. A court that treats § 3044 as just another § 3011 factor — weighed against frequent contact, stability, and so on — is misapplying the statute. § 3044 is not a factor; it is a gate.

## Critical Distinction — § 3044 vs. Criminal Track

A § 3044 finding is a civil family-court finding. It does not require criminal conviction. § 3044(d)(1) lists criminal convictions as one satisfying mechanism, but § 3044(d)(2) accepts DV findings made "pursuant to this code or… pursuant to the Domestic Violence Prevention Act." A DVRO finding alone can attach § 3044 without any criminal process. Conversely, a dismissed criminal case does not defeat § 3044 if a civil § 6203 finding was made.
