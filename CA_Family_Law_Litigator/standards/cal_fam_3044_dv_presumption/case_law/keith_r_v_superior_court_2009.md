# Keith R. v. Superior Court

**Citation:** (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1047
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, Division Seven (2009)

## FACTS

The father, who had a DV finding against him, sought joint custody on the theory that the § 3020(b) policy of "frequent and continuing contact with both parents" controlled and that the trial court should balance the DV finding against the contact preference. The trial court adopted that balancing approach and awarded joint custody. The mother sought writ review.

## HOLDING — Family Code § 3044

The Court of Appeal granted the writ. The § 3020(b) "frequent and continuing contact" preference cannot be used as a basis to rebut the § 3044 presumption. § 3044(b)(1) expressly strips the § 3020(b) and § 3040(a)(1) contact preferences from the rebuttal analysis. A trial court that balances the DV finding against the contact preference has misapplied the statute. The perpetrator must satisfy § 3044(b)(1)'s best-interest showing on independent grounds and produce § 3044(b)(2) factor evidence; the contact preference is not a rebuttal ground.

## SIGNIFICANCE

*Keith R.* is the preference-override case. It makes clear that § 3044 overrides the § 3020 contact preference — the most common intuitive rebuttal argument is statutorily foreclosed. Trial courts that frame the custody decision as "the child needs both parents, so the DV finding is outweighed" are misapplying § 3044(b)(1).

Relevant to the steward's case file: the 2010-09-02 modification record, to the extent it can be reconstructed, appears to rely on a continuity-of-contact rationale for removing supervised visitation. Under *Keith R.*, that rationale cannot support rebuttal of the § 3044 presumption against Christina. Independent § 3044(b)(1) best-interest findings plus § 3044(b)(2) factor evidence would have been required; the record does not contain them.
