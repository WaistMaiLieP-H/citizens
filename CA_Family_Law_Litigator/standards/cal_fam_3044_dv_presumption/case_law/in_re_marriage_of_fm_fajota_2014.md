# In re Marriage of F.M. (Fajota)

**Citation:** (2014) 230 Cal.App.4th 1487
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District, Division One (2014)

## FACTS

Post-dissolution custody modification proceeding. The trial court had issued a DVRO against the father following findings of § 6203 abuse against the mother. In a subsequent modification proceeding, the trial court awarded joint custody without applying the § 3044 presumption, treating the DV findings as historical rather than operative. The mother appealed, contending that the § 3044 presumption attached automatically upon the DV finding and that the trial court was required to apply the burden-shifting analysis.

## HOLDING — Family Code § 3044

The Court of Appeal reversed. The § 3044 presumption attaches automatically upon a court finding of DV perpetration within the five-year lookback window. § 3044(d)(2) expressly provides that a DV finding under the Family Code or under the DVPA — including a DVRO finding — satisfies the predicate. The trial court cannot avoid applying § 3044 on the ground that the DV finding was in a prior proceeding; it cannot convert the statutory presumption into an optional factor. Failure to apply § 3044 where its predicate is established is reversible error.

## SIGNIFICANCE

*Fajota* establishes that § 3044 is self-executing. A DVRO finding in one proceeding carries forward as a § 3044 predicate in a subsequent custody proceeding within the five-year window. The inverted burden attaches regardless of whether the court that issued the DVRO and the court deciding custody are the same. Directly applicable to any case with a prior DVRO: the DVRO finding is the § 3044 predicate, full stop.

Relevant to the steward's case file: any § 6203 finding ever made against Michael in the 16-year family law history — including any finding that could be read out of the 2010-09-02 Ajaniku/Delucchi modification — would, under *Fajota*, attach the § 3044 presumption against him in subsequent custody proceedings. The defensive work is at the § 6203 predicate layer (OPD records pickup; authentication challenges), because once a § 6203 finding exists, *Fajota* makes the § 3044 consequence automatic.
