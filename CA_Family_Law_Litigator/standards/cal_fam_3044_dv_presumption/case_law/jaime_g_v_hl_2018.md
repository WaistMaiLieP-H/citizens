# Jaime G. v. H.L.

**Citation:** (2018) 25 Cal.App.5th 794
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District, Division One (2018)

## FACTS

A custody dispute in which the trial court found the § 3044 rebuttable presumption applied (DV perpetration established) but then determined the presumption was rebutted and awarded joint custody to the perpetrator. The trial court's rebuttal order recited that the § 3044(b) factors had been considered but did not make specific findings on each factor individually. The victim parent appealed, contending that a conclusory rebuttal order fails to comply with § 3044(b)'s structured framework.

## HOLDING — Family Code § 3044

The Court of Appeal reversed. Section 3044(b) requires the trial court, in determining whether the presumption has been overcome, to make specific findings on each of the § 3044(b)(1) and (b)(2) factors. A conclusory order that the presumption "has been rebutted" without factor-by-factor analysis is reversible error. The court must state on the record or in writing: (1) why the perpetrator has demonstrated custody serves the child's best interest under §§ 3011 and 3020; and (2) why the § 3044(b)(2) factors, on balance, support the § 3020 legislative findings.

## SIGNIFICANCE

*Jaime G.* is the specificity case. It made clear that the § 3044(b) framework is not a box to check — it is a structured analysis the trial court must perform on the record. SB 899 (Stats. 2024, Ch. 544, § 7) codified *Jaime G.* directly in new § 3044(f), which now operates as of January 1, 2026. Any custody order that finds the § 3044 presumption rebutted without *Jaime G.*-compliant specific findings is facially defective and reversible on appeal.

Directly applicable to any review of the 2010-09-02 Ajaniku/Delucchi modification in the steward's case file: the modification removed protective supervised visitation (the pre-existing § 3044 protective posture against Christina), which is functionally a rebuttal determination. The record contains no § 3044(b)(1) finding that Christina had demonstrated custody served the child's best interest and no § 3044(b)(2) factor findings. Under *Jaime G.*, such an order would be reversible on appeal if timely challenged.
