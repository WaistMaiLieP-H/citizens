# Family Code § 3044 — Judicial Reasoning and Doctrinal Application

## Overview

Section 3044 is the most consequential custody statute in California when domestic violence is alleged. It creates a rebuttable presumption that awarding sole or joint custody to a person who has "perpetrated domestic violence" within the previous five years is detrimental to the child's best interest. The presumption operates on the custody determination independently of, and in addition to, the best-interest factors under § 3011. Once it attaches, the burden of proof inverts: the perpetrator — not the victim — must affirmatively show by a preponderance that custody to the perpetrator serves the child's best interest, with the "frequent and continuing contact" preference of § 3020(b) and § 3040(a)(1) expressly stripped from the rebuttal analysis.

## Key Doctrinal Points

### 1. The Presumption Attaches Mechanically on a § 3044(c) Finding

The § 3044(a) presumption is not discretionary once its predicate is met. If the court finds, by any of the § 3044(d) satisfying mechanisms, that the custody-seeking party has "perpetrated domestic violence" within the previous five years, the presumption attaches. § 3044(c) defines "perpetrated domestic violence" by direct reference to Family Code § 6203 and § 6320 — intentional or reckless bodily injury, sexual assault, reasonable apprehension of imminent serious bodily injury, or § 6320-enjoinable conduct. A prior § 6203 finding in a DVRO proceeding, a criminal conviction, or the present-case finding all satisfy the § 3044(d) predicate.

### 2. The Symmetry Problem — § 3044 Is Weaponizable

The statute is symmetrical. It operates against whichever party is found to have perpetrated domestic violence. In cases where the abusive party files first with fabricated or unsigned allegations, a facially-granted DVRO against the true victim can trigger § 3044 against that victim in the subsequent custody proceeding. The procedural collapse between the DVRO and custody dockets — where a DVRO finding is taken as a § 3044 predicate without a separate custody-stage evidentiary hearing — is the failure mode. § 3044(e) is the statutory correction: findings may not rest "solely on conclusions reached by a child custody evaluator or on the recommendation of the Family Court Services staff."

### 3. § 3044(b)(1) Strips the "Frequent and Continuing Contact" Preference From the Rebuttal

The Legislature foreclosed the most common rebuttal argument: that denying joint custody disserves the child by reducing parental contact. § 3044(b)(1) expressly states the § 3020(b) and § 3040(a)(1) frequent-contact preferences "may not be used to rebut the presumption, in whole or in part." A perpetrator cannot rebut § 3044 by arguing the child needs both parents. The perpetrator must instead satisfy the § 3044(b)(2) factors (batterer's treatment completion, substance counseling, parenting classes, probation/parole compliance, restraining-order compliance, absence of further DV, firearm compliance).

### 4. § 3044(e) — The Anti-Evaluator-Capture Clause

§ 3044(e) is the most under-invoked safeguard in the statute. When courts base § 3044 findings "solely on conclusions reached by a child custody evaluator or on the recommendation of the Family Court Services staff," the finding is procedurally defective. The court must consider "any relevant, admissible evidence submitted by the parties." This is the doctrinal hook for challenging custody orders that adopt a mediator's or evaluator's conclusions without independent judicial factfinding — including where the underlying mediator lacks § 1815/§ 3164 qualifications, or where the evaluator's report violates § 3111 confidentiality or Judicial Council standards.

### 5. § 3044(f) — The Jaime G. Specificity Requirement

The 2024 recodification (SB 899, operative January 1, 2026) statutorily codified the rule of *Jaime G. v. H.L.* (2018) 25 Cal.App.5th 794: the court must make specific findings on each § 3044(b) factor when determining the presumption has been overcome. A conclusory order that the presumption "has been rebutted" without specific factor-by-factor findings is reversible error. The court must state its reasons in writing or on the record as to (1) why § 3044(b)(1) is satisfied and (2) why the § 3044(b)(2) factors on balance support the § 3020 legislative findings.

### 6. § 3044(g) — Sequencing Rule

The court must determine § 3044 applicability before issuing a custody order. Temporary custody orders pending that determination are permitted only where a continuance is necessary and the temporary order complies with § 3011 and § 3020. Courts that issue permanent custody orders without first resolving § 3044 applicability — which is common where DV is mentioned in filings but not squarely litigated — produce structurally defective orders.

### 7. § 3044(h) — Mandatory Notice Before Mediation

When a party has alleged the other's perpetration of domestic violence "in accordance with the terms of this section," the court must inform both parties of § 3044's existence and provide a copy before any custody mediation. This is a procedural prerequisite. A mediation conducted without the § 3044(h) notice, in a case where DV has been alleged, produces a mediator recommendation that the court should not adopt. Pair with § 1816 (mediator DV training) and § 3164 (mediator qualifications) for the full mediator-integrity stack.

### 8. The 2024 Recodification — Continuity, Not Break

SB 899 (Stats. 2024, Ch. 544, § 7) repealed and re-added § 3044 effective January 1, 2025, operative January 1, 2026. The re-enacted § 3044 carries forward the core structure and adds § 3044(f) to statutorily codify *Jaime G.* specificity. Cases arising before January 1, 2026 were decided under the prior § 3044, but the core elements — rebuttable presumption, five-year lookback, § 6203/§ 6320 predicate, preponderance rebuttal, stripped frequent-contact preference — have been stable since 1999 (Stats. 1999, Ch. 980 — the original enactment as part of AB 840).

## How § 3044 Maps to the Steward's Case File

Every custody order against Michael Hartmann from 2010 forward sits downstream of a § 3044 analytical failure. The 2009-06-29 sole-custody order and the 2009-07-02 Paredes-recommended supervised-visitation order were structurally correct applications of § 3044 predicates against Christina Cerretani (given the TRO and sole-custody finding in Michael's favor). The rupture occurred at the 2010-09-02 Ajaniku/Delucchi modification, where the supervised-visitation protection was removed without any § 3044-compliant rebuttal: no § 3044(b)(1) best-interest showing by the perpetrator, no § 3044(b)(2) factor findings, no § 3044(e) compliance (the modification appears to have rested on the Ajaniku recommendation alone), no § 3044(f) specific findings on the record. Every subsequent counter-filing by Christina that presupposed "unsupervised" as the baseline inherited that 2010 defect.

The 2023-06-16 arrest and 2025-10 Marin hearings are § 3044 collision points: any § 6203 finding in those proceedings would attach the § 3044 presumption against Michael in future custody proceedings. § 3044 is also the reason the OPD records pickup finding (the 6/2/2009 report not existing in OPD records) is dispositive — if the underlying § 6203 predicate evaporates, the § 3044 presumption has no foundation.

## Common Citations

- *Jaime G. v. H.L.* (2018) 25 Cal.App.5th 794 — specific findings required on each § 3044(b) factor; codified in § 3044(f) by SB 899 (2024)
- *In re Marriage of F.M.* (formerly In re Marriage of Fajota) (2014) 230 Cal.App.4th 1487 — § 3044 presumption attaches from a DVRO finding; reversal required where trial court failed to apply presumption
- *Keith R. v. Superior Court* (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1047 — § 3044 overrides § 3020(b) frequent-contact preference in the rebuttal analysis
- *Celia S. v. Hugo H.* (2016) 3 Cal.App.5th 655 — § 3044(e) prohibits basing findings solely on evaluator or Family Court Services recommendation
- *S.M. v. E.P.* (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1249 — § 3044 applies to both initial and modification custody proceedings
