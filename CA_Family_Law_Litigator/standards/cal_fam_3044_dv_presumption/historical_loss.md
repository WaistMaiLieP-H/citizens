# Family Code § 3044 — How Parties Lose

## Why the Legislature Enacted § 3044

Section 3044 was enacted by AB 840 (Stats. 1999, Ch. 980) in response to a documented pattern in California family courts: abusers routinely obtained joint or sole custody of children despite findings or admissions of domestic violence against the other parent, because judges applied the § 3020 "frequent and continuing contact" policy as a default override. Empirical studies commissioned by the Judicial Council in the mid-1990s showed that DV findings were being treated as one factor among many, with the contact preference functionally controlling. AB 840's answer was to invert the presumption: once DV is established, the abuser must prove custody serves the child, not the other way around, and the frequent-contact preference is stripped from that rebuttal.

Subsequent amendments — the 2003 addition of § 3044(b)(2) factors, the 2012 revision of the rebuttal framework, the 2018 *Jaime G.* specificity doctrine, and the 2024 SB 899 recodification that codified *Jaime G.* in § 3044(f) — all respond to the same failure mode: trial courts treating § 3044 as optional or discretionary, instead of as the mandatory burden-shifting rule it was designed to be.

## Failure Modes — Perpetrators (the statute's intended target)

### 1. Relying on the Frequent-Contact Preference as the Rebuttal

The most common § 3044 rebuttal failure: the perpetrator argues that denying joint custody will reduce the child's contact with a parent, so the presumption should be rebutted. § 3044(b)(1) expressly forecloses this argument — the § 3020(b) and § 3040(a)(1) contact preferences "may not be used to rebut the presumption, in whole or in part." A perpetrator who leads with "the child needs both parents" has already lost the rebuttal.

### 2. Failing to Complete § 3044(b)(2) Factor Work Before the Rebuttal Hearing

The § 3044(b)(2) factors are concrete and checkable: batterer's treatment completion under § 1203.097(c) PC, substance counseling, parenting class, probation/parole compliance, restraining-order compliance, absence of further DV, firearm compliance. A perpetrator who arrives at the rebuttal hearing without documentation of these completions has nothing to offer the court; the § 3044(b)(2) balancing is unsatisfiable.

### 3. Producing a Conclusory Record that Violates § 3044(f) / Jaime G.

Even where the trial court finds the presumption rebutted, if it does so without specific written or on-the-record findings on each § 3044(b) factor, the order is reversible on appeal. *Jaime G. v. H.L.* (2018) and its 2024 codification in § 3044(f) make this explicit.

## Failure Modes — Victims (and the structural perversion of § 3044)

### 4. The Symmetry Weapon — § 3044 Turned Against the True Victim

§ 3044 is symmetrical. It operates against whoever is found to have "perpetrated domestic violence." In cases where the abusive party files a counter-DVRO with fabricated allegations and obtains a facially-valid order — or where a DVRO is granted without independent § 6203 findings — the § 3044 presumption attaches to the true victim in the custody docket. The victim then faces the inverted burden and the stripped contact preference, even though the underlying § 6203 predicate is defective. This is the central failure mode. The fix is at § 3044's front door: a defective § 6203 predicate cannot support the § 3044 presumption (§ 3044(c)).

### 5. Not Challenging § 3044(e) — Evaluator/FCS-Capture Findings

§ 3044(e) prohibits basing § 3044 findings "solely on conclusions reached by a child custody evaluator or on the recommendation of the Family Court Services staff." Where a trial court adopts a mediator's or evaluator's conclusion without independent factfinding on the § 6203 predicate, the finding is procedurally defective. The victim's counsel (or pro se victim) must object on § 3044(e) grounds contemporaneously. Failure to object waives the issue on appeal.

### 6. Not Challenging § 3044(g) Sequencing Error

§ 3044(g) requires the court to determine § 3044 applicability before issuing a custody order. Courts that issue permanent custody orders without first resolving whether the presumption applies produce structurally defective orders. The objection must be specific: "Your Honor, before the court issues a custody order, § 3044(g) requires a determination of whether this section applies. The record contains [a DVRO, a criminal conviction, a prior § 6203 finding] that triggers § 3044. I request the court make that determination on the record."

### 7. Not Invoking § 3044(h) Mandatory Notice Before Mediation

Where a party has alleged DV perpetration "in accordance with the terms of this section," the court must inform both parties of § 3044's existence and provide a copy before custody mediation. Mediations conducted without the § 3044(h) notice produce recommendations that the court should not adopt. The failure is procedural and appears nowhere in the mediation transcript — it must be raised by the victim. If mediation occurred without the notice, the resulting recommendation is challengeable.

## Specific Losses in Michael Hartmann's Case File

### Loss 1 — 2010-09-02 Ajaniku/Delucchi Modification (RF09459897 / RF09456481 file)

The 2009-06-29 sole-custody order and 2009-07-02 Paredes-recommended supervised-visitation order protecting Michael were structurally sound § 3044 applications against Christina Cerretani: Michael's TRO established the § 6203 predicate; § 3044 attached against Christina; supervised visitation reflected the protective posture.

The **2010-09-02 Ajaniku/Delucchi modification** is the § 3044 rupture point. The modification removed supervised visitation — a protective restriction — without any record of:

- § 3044(b)(1) best-interest showing by Christina as the perpetrator
- § 3044(b)(2) factor findings (batterer's treatment, substance counseling, parenting class, no further DV, firearm compliance)
- § 3044(e) compliance — the modification appears to rest on the Ajaniku recommendation alone
- § 3044(f) / *Jaime G.* specific findings on the record
- The OPD records pickup finding (October 2025) has since proved the 6/2/2009 OPD report anchoring the original § 6203 predicate against Christina does not exist in OPD records; the 6/11/2009 counter-report (09-040089) carries POST-002B overlay violations

The doctrinal consequence: every custody arrangement post-2010-09-02 inherited the 2010 defect as its baseline. Subsequent counter-filings by Christina that presupposed "unsupervised" as the status quo carried the 2010 error forward through 16 years of family court proceedings.

### Loss 2 — The Symmetry Weapon Cases (RF09459897, RF09470833, and 2025 FL cases)

Each Christina counter-filing is a § 3044 symmetry attempt — an effort to obtain a § 6203 finding against Michael in order to attach § 3044 to him. The 2023-06-16 arrest, the 2025 Marin and Solano proceedings, and the pending 25FL122591 / 25FL125059 cases all sit in this pattern. § 3044's symmetry is the reason the § 6203 predicate integrity must be defended at every entry point — a § 6203 finding on a fabricated OPD report, on an unauthenticated document, or on mediator recommendation alone attaches the § 3044 presumption against Michael in any future custody proceeding.

### Loss 3 — § 3044(h) Notice Failure in Mediation History

The case file contains no documented evidence that the § 3044(h) notice was provided before the 2009-07-02 Paredes mediation, the 2010-09-02 Ajaniku mediation, or subsequent mediations where DV had been alleged. The notice requirement is procedural and the burden is on the court, but the absence of documented notice is itself an integrity defect in the mediation record.

### Loss 4 — The Mother-as-State-Actor / Conservatorship Overlay

The conservatorship-since-age-14 central discovery (MEMORY reference: project_conservatorship_discovery.md) means that every § 3044 finding against Michael may have been made with an undisclosed structural conflict: the state actor directing the proceedings (the steward's mother) had a legal interest in the custody outcome independent of the DV record. § 3044 does not address conservatorship-induced conflict of interest. That is a § 1983 federal due process issue layered on top of the § 3044 analysis.

### Loss 5 — The Ward System Against Cole

The ward-system pattern documented in the case file (Cole as monitor; conservatorships in unknown jurisdiction; MEMORY reference: project_ward_system.md) interacts with § 3044 in an unresolved way: if Cole is a ward, decisions about his custody are not governed solely by § 3044 but by the conservatorship instrument. § 3044 has no mechanism for detecting undisclosed wardship, and the Alameda family court proceedings presume a standard parent-child custody framework that may not reflect Cole's actual legal status.

## The Doctrinal Takeaway

§ 3044 is an inversion statute: it was designed to protect victims by inverting the burden once DV is established. In the steward's case file, the statute has been used as a weapon — symmetry attempts that would attach the presumption against the true victim — and as a blind spot — modifications that removed protections without § 3044-compliant rebuttal findings. Every custody order in the file must be re-audited against § 3044(b), (e), (f), (g), and (h). The 2010-09-02 Ajaniku modification is the first and most consequential defect; every order after it is structurally downstream of that error.
