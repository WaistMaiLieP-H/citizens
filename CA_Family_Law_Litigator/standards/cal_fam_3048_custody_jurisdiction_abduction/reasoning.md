# Family Code § 3048 — Layer 2: Reasoning

§ 3048 does two distinct jobs:

## Subsection (a) — Make orders enforceable

A custody order that does not state its jurisdictional basis, give notice detail, describe the rights granted, warn of consequences, and identify habitual residence is not enforceable under the UCCJEA in sister states, not enforceable under the Hague Convention across borders, and may not be enforceable criminally under Penal Code § 278 / § 278.5 child-abduction statutes. The Legislature made these elements **mandatory face-of-order content** so that every California custody order can travel — to another state, another country, or another court — and be recognized.

## Subsection (b) — Prevent abduction before it happens

Post-abduction recovery is devastating. A child taken to a non-Hague country may never be returned. A child taken to a Hague country may still face a year+ of litigation. The Legislature's judgment was that even modest front-end measures (passport surrender, travel bond, UCCJEA registration) are far better than back-end recovery. § 3048(b) forces the court to *look for the risk* rather than wait for a party to raise it.

The (b)(2) risk factors are the empirically validated indicia developed by the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children and adopted by the Uniform Child Abduction Prevention Act, which § 3048 partially mirrors.
