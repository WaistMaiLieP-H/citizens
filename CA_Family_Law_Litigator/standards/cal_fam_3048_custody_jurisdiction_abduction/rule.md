# Family Code § 3048 — Jurisdictional Findings and Abduction Risk in Custody Orders

## Layer 1 — Rule

### Statutory text (current, as of 2026-04-15)

**§ 3048(a).** "Notwithstanding any other law, in any proceeding to determine child custody or visitation with a child, every custody or visitation order shall contain all of the following:

(1) The basis for the court's exercise of jurisdiction.
(2) The manner in which notice and opportunity to be heard were given.
(3) A clear description of the custody and visitation rights of each party.
(4) A provision stating that a violation of the order may subject the party in violation to civil or criminal penalties, or both.
(5) Identification of the country of habitual residence of the child or children."

**§ 3048(b)(1).** "In cases in which the court becomes aware of facts which may indicate that there is a risk of abduction of a child, the court shall, either on its own motion or at the request of a party, determine whether measures are needed to prevent the abduction of the child by one parent. To make that determination, the court shall consider the risk of abduction of the child, obstacles to location, recovery, and return if the child is abducted, and potential harm to the child if he or she is abducted."

**§ 3048(b)(2).** Lists the risk factors: previous threats or attempts to abduct; lack of strong ties to California; strong ties to another state or country (especially non-Hague-Convention); history of domestic violence, child abuse, or neglect; citizenship / passport status; unstable employment / financial / family situation; prior filings in other jurisdictions; violated custody orders previously.

**§ 3048(b)(3).** Lists the preventive measures the court may order: supervised visitation; travel bond; passport surrender; registration of order under UCCJEA in other jurisdictions; prohibition on removing child from state/county; notification of consulate; other reasonable measures.

### Plain English

Every California custody or visitation order **must** on its face state five things:

1. **Why this court has jurisdiction** (usually UCCJEA home-state or significant-connection basis — Fam. Code §§ 3421–3424).
2. **How notice was given** to the other party.
3. **Clear description of custody and visitation** — not ambiguous schedules.
4. **Warning** that violation can result in civil or criminal penalties.
5. **Country of habitual residence** of the child (key for Hague Abduction Convention, 22 U.S.C. § 9001 et seq. / 1980 Hague Convention).

If any of these are missing, the order is defective on its face.

§ 3048(b) is a separate mandate: if the court learns of abduction-risk facts, it **must** evaluate the risk and consider preventive measures (supervised visits, travel bonds, passport surrender, UCCJEA registration in the other state, consulate notification).
