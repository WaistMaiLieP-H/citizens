# Family Code § 3100 — Layer 2: Reasoning

§ 3100 reconciles two competing policies embedded in California family law:

1. **§ 3020(a)** — California's "frequent and continuing contact" policy favoring both parents' involvement.
2. **§ 3020(c)** — primacy of child safety when those contact interests conflict with safety from abuse.

§ 3100(a) operationalizes the contact policy (presumption of reasonable visitation). Subsections (b)–(e) operationalize the safety override (specific safeguards where DV is in the picture).

The "time, day, place, and manner" requirement in (c) and (e) is deliberate. Visitation-exchange violence is a documented pattern: the handoff itself is often where the most serious DV incidents occur, even after separation. By requiring the order to specify the logistics rather than leaving them for the parties to work out, the Legislature closes a known high-risk gap.

The § 3100(d) CPO-precedence rule resolves a recurring conflict: a family court judge unaware of a criminal court's § 136.2 stay-away order could inadvertently order contact that violates the CPO. § 3100(d) puts the burden on the family-court order to *reference* and yield to the CPO.
