# Family Code § 3100 — Layer 3: Historical Loss

## Pre-DVPA framework

Before the Domestic Violence Prevention Act (Stats. 1993, ch. 219, operative 1994) organized DV protections into Fam. Code Division 10, visitation provisions were scattered across former Civil Code provisions. Courts routinely issued "reasonable visitation" orders without addressing DV history, and DV victims were repeatedly forced into unsupervised exchanges with perpetrators.

## 1993 Family Code enactment

Stats. 1992, ch. 162 enacted the Family Code, consolidating visitation in §§ 3100–3105 and DV protection in Division 10. § 3100(b) and (c) codified the court's obligation to consider DV history when setting visitation and to specify exchange logistics where a protective order existed.

## Late-1990s / 2000s expansions

- **Stats. 1998** — added § 3200 / § 3200.5 establishing supervised visitation provider qualifications.
- **Stats. 2003** — strengthened § 3100(d) CPO-precedence language after documented family-court orders conflicting with CPOs.
- **Stats. 2004 (Hendy's Law-era reforms)** — added § 3100(e) specificity requirement for cases where DV is alleged but no restraining order is yet in place; this closed the "no-order-yet" gap where exchanges were most volatile.

## Triggering incidents

California produced multiple publicly documented family-court homicides during unsupervised visitation exchanges in the 1990s and 2000s. Legislative findings supporting § 3100 amendments repeatedly cited the exchange-point vulnerability. The § 3100(c) / (e) specificity requirements are direct policy responses to that pattern.

## Case law

- ***In re Marriage of McLoren* (1988) 202 Cal.App.3d 108** — pre-§ 3100 best-interest / detriment framework; still cited for the detriment showing required to deny visitation.
- ***In re Marriage of Fajota* (2014) 230 Cal.App.4th 1487** — court's failure to conduct § 3044 DV analysis and to apply § 3100(b) safeguards reversible error.
- ***S.Y. v. Superior Court* (2018) 29 Cal.App.5th 324** — courts must make explicit findings where restricting visitation for detriment.
