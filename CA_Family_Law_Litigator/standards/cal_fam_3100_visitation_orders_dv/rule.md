# Family Code § 3100 — Visitation Orders: Best Interest, DV Limits, and Protected-Party Safeguards

## Layer 1 — Rule

### Statutory text (current, as of 2026-04-15)

**§ 3100(a).** "In making an order pursuant to Chapter 4 (commencing with Section 3080), the court shall grant reasonable visitation rights to a parent unless it is shown that the visitation would be detrimental to the best interest of the child. In the discretion of the court, reasonable visitation rights may be granted to any other person having an interest in the welfare of the child."

**§ 3100(b).** "If a protective order, as defined in Section 6218, has been directed to a parent, the court shall consider whether the best interest of the child requires that any visitation by that parent be limited to situations in which a third person, specified by the court, is present, or whether visitation should be suspended or denied. The court shall include in its deliberations a consideration of the nature of the acts from which the parent was enjoined and the period of time that has elapsed since that order."

**§ 3100(c).** "If visitation is granted to a parent against whom a protective order has been issued, the court's order granting visitation shall specify the time, day, place, and manner of transfer of the child for visitation, in order to limit the child's exposure to potential domestic conflict or violence and to ensure the safety of all family members."

**§ 3100(d).** "If a criminal protective order has been issued pursuant to Section 136.2 of the Penal Code, the visitation order shall make reference to, and acknowledge the precedence of enforcement of, any appropriate criminal protective order."

**§ 3100(e).** "In a case in which domestic violence is alleged and there are no criminal or civil protective or restraining orders against the alleged perpetrator, the court's order for visitation with the alleged perpetrator shall specify the time, day, place, and manner of transfer of the child, so as to limit the child's exposure to potential domestic conflict or violence and to ensure the safety of all family members. If a court determines that a supervised visitation provider is necessary, the court shall consider whether the provider is qualified pursuant to Section 3200.5."

### Plain English

The default rule is that a parent gets **reasonable visitation**. The court can only restrict or deny it if visitation would be **detrimental to the best interest** of the child.

Where a restraining order exists against the parent seeking visitation, § 3100 forces extra scrutiny:

- The court **must consider** whether visits should be supervised, suspended, or denied.
- The court must look at what conduct the restraining order covered and how recent it was.
- If visitation is granted anyway, the order **must specify** time, day, place, and manner of exchange — to minimize conflict at handoff and to keep a DV victim from having to face the perpetrator unsupervised.
- A criminal protective order (Penal Code § 136.2) takes precedence over any family-court visitation order that conflicts with it.

Even if there is no restraining order yet — but DV has been alleged — the court still must specify time/day/place/manner and must consider whether a § 3200.5-qualified supervised visitation provider is needed.

Non-parents (grandparents, siblings, step-parents) may be granted visitation in the court's discretion, not by right.
