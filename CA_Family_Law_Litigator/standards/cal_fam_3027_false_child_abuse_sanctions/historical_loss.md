# Family Code § 3027 / § 3027.1 — Layer 3: Historical Loss

## Pre-1990 landscape

Before § 3027.1, California family courts had no section-specific tool to address knowingly false abuse claims. A wronged parent's only remedies were:

- Code of Civil Procedure § 128.5 sanctions for frivolous tactics (hard to prove in emotionally charged custody cases)
- A separate malicious prosecution action (unavailable — custody proceedings generally do not terminate in the same way civil actions do for MP purposes; see *Bidna v. Rosen* (1993) 19 Cal.App.4th 27)
- Civil defamation (barred by litigation privilege, Civ. Code § 47(b))

This left a gap. Custody litigants learned that an abuse accusation could be filed with near-zero downside: if it stuck, the accuser won custody; if it fell apart, no meaningful consequence attached.

## The triggering pattern (1980s)

By the late 1980s California family courts were seeing a documented surge in sexual-abuse allegations made for the first time during custody disputes. A 1988 study by the Association of Family and Conciliation Courts (reported to the Judicial Council) found abuse allegations in roughly 2% of custody cases overall but noted a subset that arose only after custody litigation commenced and did not survive investigation. Judges, mediators, and child-protective workers were being drawn into the same recurring dynamic: allegation → ex parte temporary custody change → months of investigation → unfounded finding → no remedy for the accused parent.

Assembly Bill 2700 (Speier, 1990), which became Stats. 1990, ch. 1363, was the Legislature's response. The bill's committee analyses cite the problem directly: custody-driven false accusations were imposing costs "in the tens of thousands of dollars" on accused parents with no recourse.

## Amendments

- **1992** Family Code enactment (Stats. 1992, ch. 162, operative Jan. 1, 1994) reorganized former Civil Code divorce/custody provisions into the Family Code.
- **2001** (Stats. 2000, Ch. 926, Sec. 2, effective Jan. 1, 2001): verified section-history source of current § 3027.1 via renumbering from former Family Code § 3027.
- **Post-2001 lineage** for § 3027.5 (companion no-chill provision) requires archive verification.

## Case-driven refinement

- *Marriage of Davenport* (2011) 194 Cal.App.4th 1507 — confirms knowing-falsity standard; preponderance of evidence; sanctions cap is actual defense costs not punitive.
- *Marriage of Sahafzadeh-Taeb & Taeb* (2019) 39 Cal.App.5th 124 — clarifies that the § 3027.1(b) notice-and-hearing requirement is mandatory; imposing sanctions without separate noticed hearing is reversible error.
