# Family Code § 3027 / § 3027.1 — Layer 2: Reasoning (Legislative intent)

False abuse accusations in custody proceedings are uniquely destructive. They weaponize protective statutes — Penal Code § 11165.6 mandatory reporting, CLETS flags, emergency protective orders, Welfare & Institutions Code removal authority — against an innocent parent while the child sits in the middle. Even when cleared, the accused parent has already absorbed investigator interviews, supervised visitation costs, attorney fees, employment damage, and the presumption of guilt that attaches in family court until an investigation completes.

The Legislature's intent in enacting § 3027.1 (current section added by renumbering via Stats. 2000, Ch. 926, Sec. 2, carrying forward a knowing-falsity sanctions concept whose pre-2001 lineage is flagged for archive verification) was twofold:

1. **Deterrence.** Create a financial disincentive for using fabricated abuse claims as a custody-litigation tactic.
2. **Restitution.** Make the wronged parent whole for the direct costs of defending a knowing lie.

The "knew it to be false" scienter requirement is deliberate. The Legislature did not want parents who genuinely feared for a child's safety to be chilled from reporting. The statute threads the needle: § 3027(b) authorizes **protective** temporary steps on *any* good-faith allegation; § 3027.1 imposes **sanctions** only on *knowing* falsity proven after notice and hearing.
