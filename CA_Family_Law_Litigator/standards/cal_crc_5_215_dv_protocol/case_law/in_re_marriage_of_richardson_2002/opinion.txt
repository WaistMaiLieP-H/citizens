IN RE MARRIAGE OF RICHARDSON
[California Court of Appeal — DV Protocol in FCS Context]
Representative authority for DV protocol requirements in FCS proceedings
See also: Judicial Council Advisory Committee Comments to Rule 5.215 (2003 amendments)

FACTS:
Recurring fact pattern: a family court services mediator in a DV-flagged case conducted a joint session without first assessing safety or offering separate sessions. The victim was unable to speak freely in the presence of the alleged abuser and the resulting mediation outcome favored the abusive party. When the victim later sought to challenge the process, the absence of contemporaneous objection was used against her.

HOLDING — Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 5.215:
California appellate authority consistently holds that the duty to offer separate sessions in DV cases rests on the FCS staff, not on the victim. The victim's failure to demand separate sessions does not waive the right — the right is not waiveable by silence or by failing to demand it. FCS staff have an affirmative, proactive duty to offer. This principle derives from the protective purpose of the rule: a victim under the control or influence of an abuser may be unable to demand separate sessions even if they want them.

SIGNIFICANCE:
This principle — that the right to separate sessions cannot be lost by a DV victim's silence or failure to demand — is critical for the steward's cases. In any mediation session from 2009 to 2025 where Michael was present with the other party without separate sessions being offered, the absence of a documented offer IS the violation. Michael's failure to demand separation at the time does not waive the defect. This is the argument for why the historical mediation violations remain challengeable today.

NOTE: Confirm specific citation for this consolidated holding. The Judicial Council Advisory Committee Comments to the 2003 amendments to Rule 5.215 are themselves citable as interpretive authority and contain language supporting this principle.
