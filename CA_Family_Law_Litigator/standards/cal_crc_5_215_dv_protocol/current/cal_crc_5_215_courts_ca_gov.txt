Rule 5.215. Domestic violence protocol for Family Court Services

(a) Authority
This rule of court is adopted under Family Code sections 211, 1850(a), and 3170(b).

(b) Purpose
This rule establishes procedures for handling domestic violence cases within Family Court Services, consistent with Family Code section 3170(b) requirements.

(c) Definitions
"Domestic violence" references Family Code sections 6203 and 6211. "Protective order" draws from Family Code sections 6215, 6218, and Penal Code section 136.2. "Mediation" refers to Family Code section 3161 proceedings. "Evaluation" and "investigation" are treated as synonymous. "Family Court Services" encompasses court-connected custody services under Family Code section 3160. "Family Court Services staff" includes mediators, evaluators, investigators, and counselors. "Differential domestic violence assessment" involves assessing domestic violence to protect victims and address power imbalances.

(d) Family Court Services: Description and duties
Services must follow state law and develop local protocols. Key responsibilities include: identifying and coding domestic violence cases; making reasonable efforts to ensure the safety of victims, children, and other parties; making appropriate referrals; conducting differential assessments and offering services like evaluations, mediation, and supervised visitation; prohibiting negotiation about violence or restraining orders; recommending restraining orders when appropriate under Family Code section 3183(c); providing domestic violence information to families; offering separate sessions upon request with documentation of protective orders or allegations. When separate sessions occur, arrangements must protect the confidentiality of each party's times of arrival, departure, and meeting. Staff must not treat such requests as evidence of non-cooperation. Family Court Services must refer families to appropriate services including perpetrator programs, counseling, parent education, and legal resources.

(e) Intake
Courts must ensure programs use detailed intake screening for restraining orders, dependency petitions, and safety issues. Intake forms must inform parties of their right to request separate sessions. Procedures must include reviewing intake forms and court files.

(f) Screening
Screening for domestic violence history occurs throughout the process. Methods may include questionnaires, interviews, and standardized screening devices. Procedures involve checking for protective orders, reviewing court documents, conducting interviews, and consulting collateral sources like law enforcement and child protective services. Screening questions should address separation dates, frequency and recency of incidents, future violence concerns, individuals exposed, and severity. When neither party requests separate sessions, Family Court Services staff must confer with the parties separately and privately to determine whether joint or separate sessions are appropriate.

(g) Safety issues
When violence is identified, staff must consult separately with the alleging party and discuss safety planning. This may include housing, workplace safety, family protection, financial resources, and local agency information. Offices should develop safety procedures. Staff should maintain confidentiality of addresses, contact information, and related details on all Family Court Services documents.

(h) Support persons
Staff must advise protected parties of their right to bring a support person under Family Code section 6303. Staff may exclude support persons who participate actively, advocate, or disrupt the process. Support persons remain bound by confidentiality.

(i) Accessibility of services
Programs should provide services in participants' languages, with certified interpreters when possible. Minor children cannot serve as interpreters; adult family members only when necessary. Staff should separately confirm comfort with family member interpretation. Facility design should minimize contact between parties through separate waiting areas, conference rooms, directional signage, and secure parking.

(j) Training and education
All staff must participate in continuing domestic violence instruction. Staff must complete 16 hours of advanced domestic violence training within their first 12 months of employment and 4 hours of update training annually thereafter. Content must match requirements for court-appointed child custody investigators and evaluators under Rule 5.230. Support staff should participate in domestic violence training when possible.

Adopted January 1, 2002; renumbered and amended effective January 1, 2003; amended January 1, 2007 and January 1, 2016.
