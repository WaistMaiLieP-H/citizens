# cal_pen_836_arrest_authority — Historical Loss

**Status: PROPOSED**

## How Parties Lose Under This Standard

### 1. Victims Lose: Officer Fails to Notify of Citizen's Arrest Right

The most common failure: an officer responds to a DV call, no arrest is made for 243(e)(1) or 273.5, but the officer never informs the victim of the right to make a citizen's arrest. The victim leaves without knowing they had that option. Later, when no arrest is made and no case is filed, the victim has no recourse because the procedural step was skipped. The window closes.

### 2. Victims Lose: Defective or Uninformed Citizen's Arrest Waiver

An officer presents a citizen's arrest waiver form to a victim before or instead of explaining what citizen's arrest is and what waiving it means. The victim signs without understanding. The waiver is facially valid but substantively defective because the § 836(b) "good faith effort to inform" predicate was not met. This closes the door on the citizen's arrest pathway without the victim ever having meaningfully chosen to close it.

### 3. Agencies Win: Dominant Aggressor Analysis Not Documented

Officers who make a dominant aggressor determination but do not document the four-factor analysis in their report create a gap in the record. Later challenges to the arrest (by the person arrested) succeed not because the analysis was wrong but because there is no evidence it was done. Conversely, failure to conduct the analysis at all can expose the agency to civil liability when the wrong party is arrested.

### 4. DV Victims Lose: Protective Order Violations Not Enforced

An officer arrives, sees evidence of a protective order violation (the restrained person is present, the protected person has the order), but makes a judgment call not to arrest. Under § 836(c)(2), that discretion does not exist when PC is present and notice is established. The victim is left without enforcement. This is the factual predicate for § 1983 claims based on equal protection and DV enforcement failures.

### 5. Defendants Lose: Warrantless Arrest Challenged Too Late

A defendant fails to timely challenge a warrantless DV arrest under § 836(d) — specifically, fails to raise that probable cause did not exist "as soon as" the arrest was made, or that there was meaningful delay. By the time the issue is raised (at trial rather than pretrial suppression motion), the vehicle for the challenge is gone.

### 6. APD Event #23-033556 — Live Failure Pattern for This Steward

The charge was PC 273d. The citizen's arrest waiver was signed. The question is: was Ann Hillberg first informed of her right to make a citizen's arrest in a genuine good faith effort, or was she handed a form to sign? If the officer went straight to the waiver without fulfilling the notification duty, § 836(b) was violated. The waiver obtained under those conditions does not extinguish the procedural defect — it documents it.
