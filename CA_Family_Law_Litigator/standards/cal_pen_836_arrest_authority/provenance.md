## Statute Origin

**Code:** California Penal Code
**Section:** 836
**Title:** Arrest by Peace Officer

## Current Form

**Leginfo URL:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=836.&lawCode=PEN

## Amendment History

- **1984:** Original DV-specific provisions added in response to advocacy following landmark DV enforcement litigation
- **1994:** Expanded warrantless arrest authority for DV misdemeanors (§ 836(d))
- **2000:** Citizen's arrest notification duty clarified; "good faith effort" language added
- **2001:** Dominant aggressor identification provisions added via AB 1026 in response to documented dual-arrest problems in California DV cases
- **2013:** Technical amendments to cross-references to Family Code DVPA
- **Current:** As of January 1, 2024

## Governing Authority

The Legislature declared in § 836(c)(1) that its intent is for peace officers to make a lawful arrest when probable cause exists in DV calls. This is a statement of legislative intent supporting a mandatory, not discretionary, interpretation of the arrest provisions.

## Standard Setting Body

California Legislature. No regulatory agency has rulemaking authority over § 836 itself. However, the Commission on Peace Officer Standards and Training (POST) issues DV training guidelines that implement the statute's requirements at the training level.
