# California Penal Code § 836 — Arrest by Peace Officer; Domestic Violence Calls

**Steward: Michael Hartmann**
**Citizen: CA_Family_Law_Litigator**

## Verbatim Statute Text (Relevant DV Provisions)

*Cal. Pen. Code § 836(b):*

Any time a peace officer is called out on a domestic violence call, it shall be mandatory that the officer make a good faith effort to inform the victim of their right to make a citizen's arrest, unless the peace officer makes an arrest for a violation of paragraph (1) of subdivision (e) of Section 243 or Section 273.5.

*Cal. Pen. Code § 836(c)(1):*

When responding to a domestic violence call, it is the intent of the Legislature that a peace officer make a lawful arrest of the person suspected of committing a crime involving domestic violence, if the peace officer has probable cause to believe a crime has been committed.

*Cal. Pen. Code § 836(c)(2):*

When a peace officer responds to a domestic violence call and the suspect is present, the officer shall make a lawful arrest if the officer has probable cause to believe the person violated a protective order issued pursuant to Part 4 (commencing with Section 6200) of Division 10 of the Family Code, if the person is on notice of the order as defined in Section 6291 of the Family Code, and if the person has violated that order.

*Cal. Pen. Code § 836(c)(3):*

When a peace officer is responding to a domestic violence call and both parties claim to be the victim, if a mutual protective order has been issued pursuant to Division 10 of the Family Code, the peace officer shall make a reasonable effort to identify the dominant aggressor. The dominant aggressor is the person determined to be the most significant, rather than the first, aggressor. The officer shall consider:
(A) The intent of the Legislature to protect victims of domestic violence from a continuing pattern of abuse;
(B) The threats creating fear of physical injury;
(C) The history of domestic violence between the persons involved;
(D) Whether either person acted in self-defense.

*Cal. Pen. Code § 836(d):*

A peace officer may arrest a person without a warrant if the officer has probable cause to believe that the person to be arrested has committed a felony, whether or not a felony has in fact been committed, or a violation of any of the following:
— Section 243(e)(1) [battery against current/former spouse, cohabitant, dating partner, child, or relative within second degree]
— Section 273.5 [corporal injury to spouse, cohabitant, or dating partner]

And the arrest is made as soon as probable cause arises.

## Steward Note — APD Event #23-033556

The charge in this event was **PC 273d** (corporal injury to child), NOT PC 243(e)(1) or PC 273.5.

Under § 836(b), the citizen's arrest waiver mechanism applies only when the officer does NOT arrest for 243(e)(1) or 273.5. Because the arrest charge was 273d, § 836(b)'s requirement that the officer inform the victim of her right to make a citizen's arrest was in force.

Ann Hillberg signed a "Citizen's Arrest Waiver" in that event. Whether that waiver was procedurally valid — and whether the officer properly discharged the § 836(b) mandatory notification duty — is a live issue for the forensic audit.
