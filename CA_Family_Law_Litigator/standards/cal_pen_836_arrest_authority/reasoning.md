# cal_pen_836_arrest_authority — Reasoning

**Status: PROPOSED**

---

## How Courts Apply This Standard

### 1. The Mandatory Citizen's Arrest Notification Duty (§ 836(b))

The notification duty is triggered any time an officer responds to a domestic violence call. The duty is mandatory — the statute uses "it shall be mandatory." The only exception is if the officer makes an arrest under PC 243(e)(1) or PC 273.5. For every other DV charge (including PC 273d, 273a, and 368), the notification duty survives.

Courts interpret the exception narrowly: the officer must actually make an arrest for 243(e)(1) or 273.5 to be relieved of the duty. A charge of 273d does not qualify for the exception. The notification must be a "good faith effort" — perfunctory mention or failure to obtain acknowledgment undercuts compliance.

### 2. Mandatory Arrest on Protective Order Violations (§ 836(c)(2))

When an officer has probable cause to believe a person (1) had notice of a protective order, and (2) violated it, the arrest is mandatory, not discretionary. Notice is defined by Family Code § 6291 — the person need not have a physical copy; service by any lawful method suffices.

Courts have rejected arguments that the officer's subjective doubt about the scope of the order excuses non-arrest. If PC exists, the arrest must happen.

### 3. Dominant Aggressor Analysis (§ 836(c)(3))

When both parties claim to be the victim and a mutual protective order is in place, the officer cannot simply arrest both or neither. The statute requires a dominant aggressor determination using four factors. This is not a simple "who called first" or "who has visible injuries" test.

Courts scrutinize whether officers documented the four-factor analysis. Failure to conduct or document a dominant aggressor analysis when the facts call for it is a procedural defect in the arrest.

### 4. Warrantless Arrest Authority for DV Misdemeanors (§ 836(d))

California law allows warrantless DV misdemeanor arrests when PC exists and the arrest is made "as soon as probable cause arises." Delay undercuts the warrantless arrest basis. Officers who allow a significant time gap between PC formation and arrest create suppression exposure.

### 5. Interaction with DVPA and Family Code

PC 836 arrest authority is the enforcement arm of Family Code DV protective orders. An officer who fails to arrest on a known order violation is not just exercising discretion — they are violating a statutory mandate. This creates potential civil liability and § 1983 exposure for the agency under Balistreri.

### 6. Citizen's Arrest Waiver — Procedural Requirements

When § 836(b) applies and the officer informs the victim of citizen's arrest rights, a signed waiver documents that the victim chose not to proceed. If the officer fails to properly inform the victim first, the waiver is obtained without the required predicate — it is procedurally defective. A defective waiver does not insulate the officer from the § 836(b) duty failure.
