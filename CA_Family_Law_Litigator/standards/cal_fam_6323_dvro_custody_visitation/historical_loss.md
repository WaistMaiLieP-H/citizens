# Historical Loss — Cal. Fam. Code § 6323

## How Parties Lose on This Standard

### Failure Mode 1: Accepting Vague Transfer Orders Without Objection
The most common failure: a court issues a visitation order that says "exchange at a mutually agreeable location at a mutually agreeable time." This order violates § 6323(c) on its face, but parties who do not object at the time of issuance waive the issue for appeal. The order becomes the law of the case. Every § 6323(c) deficiency must be raised at the hearing, not after. If the order is already entered, file a motion to modify on the ground that the existing order violates § 6323(c).

### Failure Mode 2: Failing to Document Exchange Violations
Where a vague transfer order exists, the opposing party manufactures conflict at exchanges — arriving late, changing locations, refusing to comply. Without a specific § 6323(c) order, there is nothing to enforce. Parties lose the ability to hold the other party in contempt and lose the ability to show the court a pattern of interference because there was no specific order to violate. Demand specificity at every opportunity.

### Failure Mode 3: Not Invoking the Five-Year Lookback When Filing
When seeking modification of an existing custody order, parties fail to invoke § 6323(d)'s five-year lookback as a mandatory consideration. Courts that omit DV history from the modification analysis are committing error, but only if the party raises it. A modification petition that references § 6323(d) and attaches documented DV within the preceding five years creates a mandatory duty of consideration for the trial court.

### Failure Mode 4: Ignoring § 6323(e) at the Initial Hearing
Parties who obtain a DVRO but fail to request a protected exchange setting at the initial hearing find themselves in direct-exchange situations that become the staging ground for further conflict and false allegations. § 6323(e) must be invoked at the first hearing. Request a Family Justice Center or police station lobby exchange as the default. Failure to do so makes every subsequent exchange a risk.

### Failure Mode 5: Failing to Connect § 6323(f) to the § 6389 Firearm Order
Where the court has found that a party illegally possesses a firearm under § 6389, this finding is mandatory input to the § 6323 custody analysis. Parties who do not connect the firearm finding to the custody order fail to use the statutory link the Legislature created. The firearm finding independently supports supervised visitation or protected exchange.
