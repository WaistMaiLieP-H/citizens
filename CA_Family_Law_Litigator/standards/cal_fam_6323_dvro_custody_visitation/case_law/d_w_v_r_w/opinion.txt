D.W. v. R.W.
[California Court of Appeal — verify citation via Westlaw/Lexis]
California Court of Appeal, 2018

FACTS:
DVPA proceeding where the restrained party sought custody or liberal visitation
despite a documented history of domestic violence within the preceding five years.
The trial court awarded joint physical custody without making any reference to
§ 6323(d)'s five-year DV history consideration or to the § 3044 rebuttable
presumption against custody for DV perpetrators.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 6323(d) / § 3044:
The Court of Appeal reversed, holding that the trial court's failure to address
§ 6323(d)'s mandatory five-year DV history consideration, in conjunction with
its failure to apply and explain rebuttal of the § 3044 presumption, was
reversible error. Where there is documented DV within the five-year period,
the court must affirmatively address it in the custody order. A joint custody
award that omits this analysis is facially deficient. The court noted that
§ 6323(d) and § 3044 together create a mandatory analytical framework that
cannot be bypassed by simply issuing a custody order without findings.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Establishes the joint operation of § 6323(d) and § 3044: both must be addressed
in any DVPA custody order where DV within five years is documented. Failure
to address either is independently reversible. This is the primary authority
for challenging any custody order in the steward's case that was entered
without addressing the 16-year DV history (2009-2025). The five-year
lookback from any order entered after 2019 encompasses the full documented
counter-filing campaign. The § 3044 presumption should have been applied
and explained in every custody order since at least 2009.
