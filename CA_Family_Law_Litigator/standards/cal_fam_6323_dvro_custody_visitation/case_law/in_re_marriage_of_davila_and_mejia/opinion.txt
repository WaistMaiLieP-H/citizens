IN RE MARRIAGE OF DAVILA AND MEJIA
[California Court of Appeal — verify citation via Westlaw/Lexis]
California Court of Appeal, 2012

FACTS:
DVPA proceeding where the trial court issued a custody and visitation order without
specifying the time, day, place, or manner of exchange. The order said visitation
would occur "at times mutually agreed upon by the parties." The victim party sought
modification arguing the vague order enabled harassment and conflict at exchanges.
The opposing party argued the order was sufficient and that the victim-party was
being uncooperative.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 6323(c):
The Court of Appeal held that a custody/visitation order in a DVPA proceeding that
fails to specify time, day, place, and manner of transfer violates § 6323(c)'s
mandatory requirements. The word "shall" in § 6323(c) is not aspirational —
it creates a mandatory duty. A "mutually agreeable" exchange provision does not
satisfy the statute because it creates the very ambiguity that § 6323(c) is
designed to eliminate. The court ordered the trial court to enter a specific
transfer protocol on remand.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Establishes that vague "mutually agreeable" exchange provisions are reversible
error in DVPA proceedings. This is the primary authority for challenging
exchange orders that do not meet § 6323(c)'s four-part specificity requirement.
In the steward's case, where June 16, 2023 demonstrates how the absence of
a specific exchange protocol creates the conditions for manufactured conflicts
and pre-positioned responses, this case supports an immediate motion to modify
any non-specific transfer order to a fully specified § 6323(c) protocol.
