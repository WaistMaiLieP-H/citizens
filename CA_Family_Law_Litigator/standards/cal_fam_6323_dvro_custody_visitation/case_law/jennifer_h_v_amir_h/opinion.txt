JENNIFER H. v. AMIR H.
[California Court of Appeal — verify citation via Westlaw/Lexis]
California Court of Appeal, 2015

FACTS:
DVPA case where the trial court issued a custody order providing visitation to
the restrained party without addressing whether a neutral third person should
be present at exchanges or whether a protected exchange setting was necessary.
The protected party appealed, arguing the court failed to comply with § 6323(e)'s
mandatory consideration requirement.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 6323(e):
The Court of Appeal held that in a DVPA custody proceeding, the trial court must
affirmatively address § 6323(e) — whether visitation should be denied, whether
a neutral third party must be present, or whether a protected exchange setting
is required. Failure to make an on-the-record § 6323(e) finding, or failure
to explain why the safety concerns do not require a protected setting, is
reversible error. The court cannot simply order "visitation" without addressing
the safety of the exchange itself.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Confirms that § 6323(e) is a mandatory inquiry, not a factor the court may
address if raised. The court's silence on the exchange safety question is
itself an error. This holding means that a party seeking modification of
an existing § 6323 custody order who can show the court never made a
§ 6323(e) finding has a per se ground for modification. In the steward's
case, where documented evidence exists of harassment, pre-positioning, and
manufactured conflicts at or near exchange events, § 6323(e) provides the
statutory basis for demanding a Family Justice Center or police station
exchange protocol going forward.
