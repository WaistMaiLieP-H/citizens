# Reasoning — Cal. Fam. Code § 6323

## How Courts Apply This Standard

### 1. "Shall" Is Mandatory — Failure to Apply Is Reversible Error
Four subsections of § 6323 use "shall." Courts commit reversible error when they fail to: (a) specify time/day/place/manner of transfer; (b) consider DV history within five years; (c) assess whether a third party or protected exchange setting is needed; (d) address firearm findings. These are not discretionary factors — they are non-waivable requirements. An order that omits any "shall" item is defective on its face.

### 2. Transfer Protocol Must Be Specific (§ 6323(c))
The transfer specification must identify: (1) time — specific clock time, not "reasonable time"; (2) day — specific day(s) of week or dates; (3) place — specific location, not "mutually agreed location"; (4) manner — who transports the child, how the handoff occurs, whether parents exchange directly or through a neutral party. Vague transfer orders violate § 6323(c) and create the conditions for manufactured conflicts at exchange.

### 3. Five-Year DV History Lookback Is Active Duty (§ 6323(d))
The court must affirmatively consider whether any party seeking custody has committed DV in the preceding five years. This is not a burden that falls on the victim-party to raise — the court must consider it independently. Where a party has a documented history of DV within five years, failure to reference that history in the custody order is error. The five-year window is calculated from the date of the custody order, not the date of the proceeding's commencement.

### 4. Protected Exchange Setting Is the Default When Safety Is at Issue (§ 6323(e))
Where the safety of any party — not just the children — requires a protected exchange, the court shall consider it. Courts apply this by looking at: prior incidents of violence or harassment at exchanges; the nature of the restraining order; whether the parties can be in each other's proximity without risk. Family Justice Centers, police station lobbies, and neutral third parties are all recognized protected settings. A court that orders direct parent-to-parent exchanges without addressing § 6323(e) when DV is alleged has committed error.

### 5. Interaction with § 3044 DV Presumption
Where § 3044's rebuttable presumption against custody for a DV perpetrator applies, § 6323 orders must be consistent with that presumption. A court cannot order regular, unsupervised custody in a DVPA proceeding without first finding that the § 3044 presumption has been rebutted.

### 6. Paternity Exclusion (§ 6323(b)) Is a Limitation on the Order's Effect
The § 6323 order does not establish paternity. Where paternity is disputed, the custody order under § 6323 is temporary and does not constitute a judicial finding on the question of who is the child's legal parent. This matters when opposing parties attempt to use a § 6323 custody order as evidence of established legal parentage.

## Practical Test
Review every custody/visitation order issued in a DVPA proceeding against the four "shall" requirements: (1) Is the transfer protocol specific as to time, day, place, and manner? (2) Does the order reflect consideration of the five-year DV history? (3) Does the order address whether a third party or protected setting is required? (4) Does the order address firearm findings? Any "no" answer is a deficiency in the existing order and a basis for modification or appeal.
