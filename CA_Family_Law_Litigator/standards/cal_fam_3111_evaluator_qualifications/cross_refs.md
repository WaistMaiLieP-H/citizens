# Family Code § 3111 — Cross-References

## Direct statutory cross-references on the face of § 3111

- **§ 3117** — Judicial Council standards adoption authority. § 3111's consideration bar incorporates the standards adopted under § 3117 (Cal. Rules of Court rule 5.220 — evaluator conduct and methodology; rule 5.225 — evaluator qualifications, training, continuing education).
- **§ 3150** — Counsel appointed for the child. § 3111(a) requires service of the report on any § 3150 minor's counsel.
- **§ 3025.5** — Limited disclosure of custody reports to parties and counsel in subsequent proceedings.
- **Welfare & Institutions Code § 204** — Information-sharing in connection with juvenile court proceedings.
- **Probate Code § 1514.5** — Disclosure in guardianship proceedings.
- **Welfare & Institutions Code § 827(a)(4) and (e)** — Confidentiality of juvenile court case files.

## Implied statutory cross-references (Chapter 6 architecture)

- **§ 3110** — Definition of child custody evaluator.
- **§ 3110.5** — Evaluator qualifications, training, continuing education (implemented through rule 5.225).
- **§ 3113** — Court-ordered child custody investigation and report (FCS; distinct from § 3111 evaluation).
- **§ 3115** — Admissibility of reports and recommendations.
- **§ 3118** — Child sexual abuse allegation investigation; overlapping but distinct from § 3111.

## Rules of Court incorporated by reference

- **Cal. Rules of Court rule 5.220** — Uniform standards of practice for court-ordered child custody evaluations. Defines methodology floor: collateral contacts, document review, interview documentation, disclosure of sources, written report content requirements.
- **Cal. Rules of Court rule 5.225** — Evaluator qualifications, training, continuing education. Credentialing prerequisites; form declarations on file.

## Doctrinal bridges within Family Code

- **§ 3020** — Best-interest policy. § 3111(a) appointment requires a best-interest finding; § 3011 best-interest factors structure the content of the evaluation.
- **§ 3011** — Best-interest factor inventory. The evaluator's report addresses these factors; § 3011 provides the analytical framework.
- **§ 3044** — DV rebuttable presumption. § 3044(e) prohibits basing a DV finding solely on evaluator conclusions. § 3044(b)(2) factors include items an evaluator typically assesses. Two independent gates: § 3111 compliance + § 3044(e) no-delegation.
- **§ 3164** — Mediator qualifications in custody mediation. Separate role from § 3111 evaluator, but the same doctrinal concern: no delegation of factfinding.
- **§ 217** — Right to live testimony in FL evidentiary hearings. § 3111 report stipulation bypass (subdivision (c)) is not a substitute for live testimony where § 217 rights are invoked; cross-examination of the evaluator is separately preserved.
- **§ 2030** — Need-based attorney's fees. Challenging a defective § 3111 report often requires retained counsel or a rebuttal expert; § 2030 fee awards are a structural remedy for the cost asymmetry.

## Evidence Code bridges

- **Evid. § 720** — Foundation for expert witness. § 3111 compliance is necessary but not sufficient; Evid. § 720 qualification for the specific subject matter is a separate gate.
- **Evid. § 801** — Subject matter of expert opinion must be sufficiently beyond common experience.
- **Evid. § 802** — Bases of expert opinion must be made known.
- **Evid. § 730** — Court-appointed experts generally (FL § 3111 is a specialized § 730-type appointment).
- **Evid. § 1016** — Patient-litigant exception to psychotherapist-patient privilege (relevant when evaluator relies on psychotherapy records or communications).

## Criminal-code bridges

- **Pen. § 1368** — Criminal competency standard; analytically distinct from civil custody evaluation but doctrinally related on the question of evaluator specialization. An evaluator qualified for custody evaluation under rule 5.225 is not automatically qualified for Pen. § 1368 competency evaluation, and vice versa.

## Business & Professions Code bridges

- **Bus. & Prof. § 2290.5** — Telehealth scope of practice; implicated if a § 3111 evaluation was conducted remotely across county or state lines.
- **Bus. & Prof. § 2900 et seq.** — Psychology licensing; primary-license state and California secondary-license status are verifiable through the Board of Psychology and through NPPES.
- **Bus. & Prof. § 4980 et seq.** — LMFT licensing.
- **Bus. & Prof. § 4996 et seq.** — LCSW licensing.

## Federal / administrative cross-references

- **NPPES (National Plan and Provider Enumeration System)** — CMS registry. Primary-license state, taxonomy code (207T00000X Neurological Surgery, 2084P0800X Psychiatry & Neurology, etc.), and listed California practice addresses are all NPPES fields. Cross-check is operationally decisive for out-of-jurisdiction appointment challenges.
- **HIPAA 45 C.F.R. § 164** — Privacy Rule; implicated where the evaluator's report contains protected health information and dissemination exceeds § 3111(b) channels.

## Citizen cross-tethers

- **CA_Family_Law_Litigator** — primary owner.
- **CA_Mental_Health_Litigator** — cross-tether for expert-witness foundation attacks and psychotherapist-patient privilege issues (Evid. § 1016, Evid. § 730).
- **CA_Healthcare_Fraud_Litigator** — cross-tether for licensure-scope and telehealth-scope attacks (Bus. & Prof. § 2290.5).
- **CA_Medical_Privacy_Officer** — cross-tether for CMIA / HIPAA disclosure issues arising from report dissemination.
- **US_Federal_Civil_Rights_Litigator** — cross-tether where a non-compliant § 3111 report supports a custody order that independently violates a federally protected interest.

## Findings bridges

- **FAMLAW-002** — Wiita § 3118/§ 730 fraud finding. § 3111 is the direct attack gate. The NPPES cross-check (FAMLAW-006 F-006-01) produced a verifiable mismatch between the evaluator's primary license jurisdiction and the California county of appointment. The § 3111 Track A (qualifications) and Track B (procedure) attacks both apply.
- **FAMLAW-004** — § 3044 never rebutted. § 3111 and § 3044 are analytically independent gates; both can fail simultaneously in a single custody order.
