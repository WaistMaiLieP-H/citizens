# Seagondollar v. Seagondollar (2006) 139 Cal.App.4th 1116

## Holding pertinent to § 3111

The Court of Appeal held that a trial court's custody order could not stand where the court relied on a child custody evaluator's report that failed to comply with the uniform standards adopted by the Judicial Council under § 3117 (implemented as Cal. Rules of Court rule 5.220). The decision reinforces § 3111(a)'s consideration bar — the report "may be considered by the court only if" the underlying evaluation complies with the incorporated standards.

## Why this case matters for the § 3111 attack framework

- Establishes that rule 5.220 methodology defects are substantive under § 3111(a) — not "nonsubstantive or inconsequential" errors that would be excused by the last clause of subdivision (a).
- Treats the consideration bar as a mandatory gate, not a weight adjustment.
- Supports a per-defect challenge approach: each rule 5.220 requirement that the evaluator failed to satisfy is an independent basis for exclusion.

## Typical rule 5.220 defects that pattern-match to Seagondollar

- Failure to conduct collateral interviews.
- Failure to review source documents the evaluator's conclusions rely on.
- Failure to disclose data sources in the written report.
- Failure to document interview content.
- Reaching conclusions on topics outside the evaluator's disclosed methodology.

## Pairing with rule 5.225 (qualifications)

Seagondollar addresses methodology; rule 5.225 addresses qualifications. The § 3111(a) consideration bar incorporates both. A report can fail under Seagondollar (methodology) or under rule 5.225 (qualifications), and either failure is independently sufficient to bar consideration.

## Operational use

Cite Seagondollar for the proposition that rule 5.220 compliance is a mandatory component of § 3111(a) consideration, and that the reviewing court will reverse where the trial court relied on a non-compliant evaluation.
