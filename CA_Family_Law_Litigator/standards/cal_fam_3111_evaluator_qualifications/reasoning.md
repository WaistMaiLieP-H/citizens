# Family Code § 3111 — Reasoning

## What § 3111 is doing in the statutory architecture

§ 3111 sits at the center of a five-section custody-evaluation framework in Family Code Division 8, Part 2, Chapter 6 [§§ 3110-3118]:

- **§ 3110** defines "child custody evaluator" and authorizes the evaluator role.
- **§ 3110.5** imposes evaluator qualification, training, and continuing-education requirements (incorporated through Cal. Rules of Court rule 5.225).
- **§ 3111** — the subject of this standard — controls **the report itself**: who may be appointed, under what standards, how the report must be filed and served, and the floor that must be met before the court may consider the report.
- **§ 3117** directs the Judicial Council to adopt uniform evaluation standards (implemented as Cal. Rules of Court rule 5.220).
- **§ 3118** governs investigations triggered by allegations of child sexual abuse.

§ 3111 is the **gate**. The statute's operative words are in subdivision (a): "A child custody evaluation, investigation, or assessment, and a resulting report, **may be considered by the court only if** it is conducted in accordance with the requirements set forth in the standards adopted by the Judicial Council pursuant to Section 3117." This is not discretionary weight-adjustment language. It is a mandatory consideration bar.

## Why the Legislature wrote the consideration bar as a bar

The 1993 recodification (Stats. 1993, Ch. 219, Sec. 116.81) consolidated custody-evaluation provisions that had been scattered across the former Civil Code. By the mid-2000s, the Judicial Council's Family and Juvenile Law Advisory Committee documented chronic problems: evaluators practicing outside their training, reports issued without source disclosure, untimely service that denied parties adequate review, and ad-hoc local practices that varied from county to county. AB 1817 (Stats. 2019, Ch. 115) was the most recent refinement; earlier amendments (AB 2475 / Stats. 2010, SB 1030 / Stats. 2011) added the 10-day service rule and the confidentiality form mandate.

The statute's architecture reflects three legislative judgments:

1. **Evaluations have disproportionate weight in family court.** A written "expert" report is the single most influential document in a contested custody case. Courts rely on it. If the underlying evaluation is defective, the resulting custody order is defective.

2. **The remedy is a consideration bar, not harmless-error review.** The Legislature refused to let trial courts admit and then "weigh" non-compliant reports. Non-compliance is not a factor — it is a disqualification. Subdivision (a)'s last clause ("however, this does not preclude the consideration of a child custody evaluation report that contains nonsubstantive or inconsequential errors") is narrow: *nonsubstantive* and *inconsequential* are the limits. A qualifications defect, a standards-compliance defect, or a service defect is substantive by definition.

3. **Confidentiality is a party-rights structure, not a convenience.** Subdivisions (b), (d), and (f) form a self-contained confidentiality regime with closed-list permitted channels and monetary sanctions for breach. The system presumes the report will be handled only as the statute directs.

## Doctrinal pairings

- **§ 3044(e) bridge.** § 3044(e) independently prohibits the court from basing a DV-perpetration finding "solely on conclusions reached by a child custody evaluator or on the recommendation of the Family Court Services staff." § 3111 compliance is a prerequisite even before § 3044(e)'s no-delegation rule attaches. If the § 3111 gate is not met, the § 3044(e) analysis is moot — the report cannot be considered at all.

- **§ 3020 best-interest policy floor.** The § 3111(a) best-interest prerequisite for the appointment itself means the court must make an on-the-record or written finding that appointing an evaluator serves the child. A rote appointment order is facially defective.

- **Evidence Code § 720 foundation.** Even when § 3111 is satisfied, the evaluator's report remains subject to expert-witness foundation challenges. An evaluator qualified under rule 5.225 (education + continuing education thresholds) is not automatically qualified as a § 720 expert on every clinical or forensic issue in the case. Foundation attack survives § 3111 compliance.

- **Business & Professions Code licensure cross-check.** An evaluator whose license is primarily in another jurisdiction, or who is practicing in a California county where the evaluator has no established practice address, raises a distinct B&P issue layered on top of § 3111. NPPES registry cross-check is operationally useful here; primary license state, taxonomy, and listed California practice addresses are all verifiable.

- **Cal. Rules of Court rule 5.220 (standards) + rule 5.225 (qualifications).** § 3111 incorporates both by reference through § 3117. A rule 5.220 methodology failure (no collateral-contact protocol; no source-document log; no written summary of interview content) or a rule 5.225 qualifications failure (no compliant education; no continuing-education declaration on file) is a § 3111 substantive defect — not a nonsubstantive error.

## The structural leverage § 3111 gives the challenging party

A party contesting an evaluator report has **two parallel attack tracks** available under § 3111:

1. **Track A — Qualifications / standards attack (consideration bar).** Challenge the report's eligibility for consideration under § 3111(a) by showing rule 5.225 (qualifications) or rule 5.220 (methodology) non-compliance. If successful, the report may not be considered at all — no weighing, no partial reliance.

2. **Track B — Procedural attack (same consideration bar).** Challenge on the 10-day service rule, the missing § 3111(e) confidentiality form, or the subdivision (b) dissemination limits. These are independent grounds to exclude consideration.

Both tracks rest on the statute's mandatory "may be considered only if" language. Neither requires a showing of prejudice as a threshold — the statute itself establishes the consideration bar.
