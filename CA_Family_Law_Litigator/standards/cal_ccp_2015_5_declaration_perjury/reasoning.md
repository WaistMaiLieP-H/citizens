# Reasoning — How Courts Apply CCP § 2015.5

## Core Function

CCP § 2015.5 converts any requirement for a "sworn statement, declaration, verification, certificate, oath, or affidavit" into an unsworn declaration under penalty of perjury. It is the California analog to 28 U.S.C. § 1746 — but with an important structural difference: CCP § 2015.5 applies to ALL California proceedings (state and quasi-judicial), while § 1746 applies in federal proceedings only.

## Form (a) vs. Form (b) — The Critical Distinction

**Form (a)** — "executed within this state" — requires BOTH date AND place of execution. Courts have strictly construed this. A Form (a) declaration with the place blank is defective and may be disregarded.

**Form (b)** — "executed at any place, within or without this state" — requires only the DATE. No place is required. Courts universally accept Form (b) declarations. The phrase "under the laws of the State of California" is what triggers California perjury law regardless of where signed.

**Practice rule:** Always use Form (b). There is no benefit to Form (a) and a meaningful risk of defect if the place field is missed or challenged.

## What "Under Penalty of Perjury" Means

A person who signs a false CCP § 2015.5 declaration commits perjury under Penal Code § 118. Perjury requires that the declarant: (1) willfully stated something they knew was false; (2) under oath or its equivalent; (3) in a proceeding where an oath is required or authorized. A CCP § 2015.5 declaration satisfies the "oath or its equivalent" prong for perjury prosecution.

## What It Does NOT Require

- A notary or other officer to administer an oath
- The declarant to appear in court
- A separate jurat or acknowledgment

## Family Court Specific Applications

Every declaration filed in family court — RFO declarations (FL-300), income declarations (FL-150), custody declarations, DV declarations (DV-100/DV-110) — must satisfy § 2015.5. The preprinted Judicial Council forms include the § 2015.5 language. Custom declarations must include it or they are not properly sworn.

A declaration that says "I swear" but does not include the statutory "under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California" language is not a CCP § 2015.5 declaration.

## Relationship to CalVCB

CalVCB is a California state agency. Declarations submitted to CalVCB must use CCP § 2015.5, Form (b). 28 U.S.C. § 1746 (federal unsworn declarations) does not apply to CalVCB proceedings. This is a critical distinction documented in HERALD's USC_28_1746 manifest.
