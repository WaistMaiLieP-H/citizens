# Historical Loss — CCP § 2015.5 Failures in California Courts

## Failure Mode 1 — Form (a) Place-of-Execution Defect

**The Loss:** A party files a declaration using Form (a) language (executed within California) but leaves the place field blank. The opposing party objects. The court treats the declaration as defective — it does not satisfy § 2015.5 because Form (a) requires BOTH date AND place. The motion is denied or the evidence is disregarded.

**Why it happens:** Attorneys and pro se filers copy a generic "I declare under penalty of perjury" template that does not include the place, or confuse the two forms.

**How to avoid:** Always use Form (b). Form (b) requires only a date — the phrase "under the laws of the State of California" substitutes for the place requirement. There is no scenario in which Form (a) is preferable.

---

## Failure Mode 2 — Omitting "Under the Laws of the State of California"

**The Loss:** A declaration filed in a California proceeding uses 28 U.S.C. § 1746 language: "under penalty of perjury under the laws of the United States." This satisfies federal proceedings but does NOT satisfy § 2015.5. A California court may refuse to treat it as a sworn declaration for purposes of a motion, RFO, or DVRO.

**Why it happens:** Federal practitioners crossing into state court, or pro se filers who found a federal-court template online.

**How to avoid:** California state proceedings — including family court, and ALL quasi-judicial state agency proceedings (CalVCB, DMV, DLSE, DCA) — require CCP § 2015.5 language. The phrase "under the laws of the State of California" is the operative trigger.

---

## Failure Mode 3 — Custom Declaration Missing Statutory Language Entirely

**The Loss:** A party files a narrative document titled "Declaration of [Name]" that says "I swear" or "I attest" but does not include the § 2015.5 Form (b) certification. The court does not treat it as a sworn declaration. In an RFO for child custody modification, this means the factual showing fails for lack of competent sworn evidence.

**Why it happens:** Pro se litigants draft their own documents without realizing the Judicial Council forms include preprinted § 2015.5 language as a structural requirement.

**How to avoid:** Every custom declaration filed in California must include verbatim (or substantially similar) Form (b) language immediately before the signature line.

---

## Failure Mode 4 — False Declaration Not Prosecuted Because "Penalty of Perjury" Not Included

**The Loss (inverse):** A party submits a declaration making false statements but the declaration lacks proper § 2015.5 language. Because the declaration was not properly sworn, a perjury prosecution under Penal Code § 118 is unavailable. The false declarant suffers no criminal accountability.

**Significance for this case:** Declarations filed by opposing parties in Family Law Case [Hartmann dissolution], the OPD 09-011438 initial report narrative, and CalVCB application filings may be evaluated against § 2015.5 standards to determine whether false statements in those documents can support a PC § 118 referral.

---

## Failure Mode 5 — Agency Proceeding Uses Wrong Standard

**The Loss:** A claimant submitting declarations to a California state agency (CalVCB, CalHR, Workers' Comp Appeals Board) uses 28 U.S.C. § 1746 because an online guide said "federal declarations are universally accepted." The agency rejects the declaration as non-compliant.

**CalVCB specific:** CalVCB is a California state agency. It operates under California Government Code and Welfare & Institutions Code. It is NOT a federal tribunal. § 1746 does not apply. CCP § 2015.5 Form (b) is required.

**Why it matters for App A25-10117946:** All declarations submitted to CalVCB in support of Application A25-10117946 must comply with § 2015.5 Form (b). Any prior submissions that used § 1746 language should be replaced with compliant declarations on any resubmission or reconsideration.
