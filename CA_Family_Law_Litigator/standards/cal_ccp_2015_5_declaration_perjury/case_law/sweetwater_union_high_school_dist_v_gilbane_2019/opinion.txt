Sweetwater Union High School Dist. v. Gilbane Building Co. (2019) 6 Cal.5th 931

CITATION: 6 Cal.5th 931, 434 P.3d 1152, 243 Cal.Rptr.3d 880
COURT: Supreme Court of California
DATE DECIDED: February 7, 2019

HOLDING:
Evidence offered in support of or opposition to a special motion to strike under CCP § 425.16 need not be presented through live testimony, but declarations must satisfy Evidence Code requirements for admissibility — personal knowledge, authentication, and hearsay rules apply. Attached documents require authentication; unsworn summaries of witness statements are inadmissible hearsay. The admissibility standard for anti-SLAPP evidence mirrors summary judgment.

KEY FACTS:
In a qui tam/bid-rigging action, the defendant construction firm moved to strike under § 425.16. Plaintiff opposed with declarations attaching transcripts from a grand jury proceeding and a criminal plea colloquy. Defendant challenged admissibility. The trial court admitted the evidence; the Court of Appeal reversed.

REASONING:
"The Evidence Code applies with full force to declarations offered on an anti-SLAPP motion." Declarations must be based on personal knowledge (Evid. § 702); attached documents must be authenticated (Evid. § 1400); hearsay is inadmissible absent exception (Evid. § 1200). A declaration's statement that "attached hereto is a true and correct copy" is sufficient to authenticate a document only if the declarant has personal knowledge of the document's authenticity. Court records and official records may qualify under § 1280 (official records exception) or § 452 (judicial notice).

APPLICATION TO § 2015.5:
Even a facially compliant § 2015.5 declaration fails if its content does not satisfy Evidence Code admissibility rules. Form + substance must both be satisfied. A declaration that recites "under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California" but contains inadmissible hearsay is still defective as to the hearsay content.

APPLICATION TO VERNEN CASES:
— Case #1 (Christina declarations): Declarations that summarize what third parties (mother, APD officers, Ryan McClaran) allegedly said are inadmissible hearsay unless a specific exception applies. § 2015.5 compliance does not cure hearsay.
— Case #2 (Wiita competency evaluation declarations): Attached psychiatric records require § 1280 official records foundation or § 1271 business records foundation; bare attachment is insufficient.
— Cross-case: Most opposing declarations in the 39+ case portfolio layer hearsay on hearsay. Motion to strike declarations + attached exhibits is a viable procedural attack.
