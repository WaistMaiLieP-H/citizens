# Family Code § 2030 — Layer 2: Reasoning

Divorce pits two parties against each other, but marital assets and earning capacity are almost never equal. Without a fee-shifting mechanism, the higher-earning spouse can outspend the other into submission — burying them in motions, running out the clock on discovery, and forcing unfavorable settlement simply because the lower-earning spouse runs out of money.

The Legislature's intent in § 2030 is structural **access to justice**:

1. **Level the playing field.** Ensure the poorer spouse can hire competent counsel before strategic decisions are made — not after damage is done.
2. **Protect against attrition tactics.** Deny the higher-earning party the ability to use fees as a weapon.
3. **Preserve community property principles.** Marital assets should fund both sides' reasonable access to counsel, not just whichever spouse controls the checkbook.
4. **Make awards mandatory where disparity exists.** The 2010 amendment (AB 939, same Elkins-era reform as § 217) replaced permissive language ("may") with directive language ("shall") when the findings support it.

§ 2032's "just and reasonable" factors — incomes, needs, assets, obligations, earning capacity, duration of marriage, age and health — prevent mechanical disparity-equals-award outcomes and let the court tailor awards to the relative circumstances.
