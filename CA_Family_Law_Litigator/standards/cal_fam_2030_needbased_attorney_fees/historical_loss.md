# Family Code § 2030 — Layer 3: Historical Loss

## Pre-Family Code

California had need-based fee-shifting in divorce since the 19th century (former Civ. Code § 137, then § 4370). The mechanism existed, but the standard was discretionary: the court *could* award fees. In practice, awards were modest, slow, and often denied altogether, leaving lower-earning spouses without meaningful access.

## 1992 Family Code enactment

When the Family Code was enacted (Stats. 1992, ch. 162, operative 1994), former § 4370 became § 2030 / § 2031 / § 2032, splitting authority (§ 2030), procedure (§ 2031), and factors (§ 2032). The substantive standard remained discretionary.

## Elkins Task Force and the 2010 reform

The Elkins Family Law Task Force Final Report (April 2010) identified need-based fees as a second critical access-to-justice failure (the first being "declarations only" practice — addressed by § 217). Findings:

- Fee motions were routinely deferred to trial, meaning lower-earning spouses litigated the entire pendency without counsel.
- When awards were made, they were often token amounts unconnected to actual costs.
- Self-represented parties were shut out: fee statutes assumed you already had a lawyer.

**AB 939 (Stats. 2010, ch. 352)** rewrote § 2030. Key changes:

- Added mandatory-findings requirement: the court **must** make findings on appropriateness, disparity, and ability to pay when a request is made.
- Added mandatory-award language: "If the findings demonstrate disparity in access and ability to pay, the court **shall** make an order awarding attorney's fees and costs."
- Added explicit authorization for pro per parties to request fees **to hire** counsel.
- Emphasized "early in the proceedings" — fees must be available at the pendente lite stage, not just at trial.

## Post-2010 case refinement

- ***In re Marriage of Morton* (2018) 27 Cal.App.5th 1025** — § 2030 findings are mandatory; failure to make them is reversible error even if some award is made.
- ***In re Marriage of Ciprari* (2019) 32 Cal.App.5th 83** — court must make actual findings, not conclusory references.
- ***In re Marriage of Sharples* (2014) 223 Cal.App.4th 160** — § 2030 findings required even where request made by higher-earning party for other reasons.
