# Family Code § 217 — Layer 2: Reasoning (Legislative intent)

§ 217 was enacted by Stats. 2010, ch. 352 (AB 939, Feuer), operative January 1, 2011, to overrule a widespread family-court practice of deciding contested motions on paper declarations alone.

The Legislature's concerns:

1. **Due process.** Family-court rulings — custody, restraining orders, support — carry consequences equal to or greater than civil judgments, yet parties were being denied the basic right to confront, cross-examine, and be heard in open court.
2. **Credibility determinations on paper.** Many family-court issues turn on credibility. A judge cannot meaningfully assess credibility from competing declarations. Live testimony is the traditional and constitutionally grounded tool for credibility findings.
3. **Appellate review.** "Declarations only" practice produced records that gave appellate courts nothing to review: no transcript of the decisive credibility call, only a minute order.
4. **Equal treatment with civil litigation.** Nothing in a general-civil motion entitles the court to refuse live testimony where the facts are disputed; family-court litigants deserved the same floor.

The "good cause" exception preserves the court's ability to manage its calendar where declarations genuinely suffice (uncontested facts, purely legal issues, repeat motions on a well-developed record).
