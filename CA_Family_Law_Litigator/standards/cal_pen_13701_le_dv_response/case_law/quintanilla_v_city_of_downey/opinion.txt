QUINTANILLA v. CITY OF DOWNEY
84 F.3d 353
9th Circuit Court of Appeals, 1997

FACTS:
Plaintiff brought a § 1983 claim alleging that the City of Downey police department had a
policy or custom of inadequate response to domestic violence calls, failing to arrest
abusers with probable cause and failing to document incidents properly. The claim arose
from repeated DV calls where officers declined to arrest despite visible evidence of assault.

HOLDING — CAL. PEN. CODE § 13701 / 42 U.S.C. § 1983:
The Ninth Circuit addressed whether a municipality's failure to implement adequate
domestic violence response policies — as required by Cal. Pen. Code § 13701 — could
establish the "policy or custom" element under Monell for § 1983 purposes. The court
confirmed that documented policy failures in the DV response context, including failure
to arrest when probable cause existed and failure to follow written policy requirements,
could support municipal liability. The court noted that § 13701's mandatory "shall"
language sets the baseline against which departmental conduct is measured.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Establishes that Cal. Pen. Code § 13701 is properly used as the evidentiary anchor
for the Monell "custom or policy" element in § 1983 DV response cases. An agency's
failure to maintain compliant written policies — or failure to enforce them — is
admissible as evidence of the requisite municipal policy for civil rights liability.
Relevant to APD Report #23-004494 (unsigned) as evidence of § 13701(c)(10) non-compliance
supporting a pattern-and-practice theory.
