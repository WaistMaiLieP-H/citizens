BALISTRERI v. PACIFICA POLICE DEPARTMENT
855 F.2d 1421
9th Circuit Court of Appeals, 1988

FACTS:
Jena Balistreri was repeatedly beaten by her estranged husband. Officers repeatedly
declined to arrest, failed to enforce existing restraining orders, and at one point
an officer told her he "didn't believe in" EPO enforcement. After her husband
firebombed her home, she sued under § 1983 alleging the police department's
deliberate indifference to her DV complaints constituted a due process violation.

HOLDING — 42 U.S.C. § 1983 / DV POLICY:
"[A] police department's policy of providing inadequate protection against abusers to
victims who had restraining orders could state an equal protection claim and a
substantive due process claim under § 1983." The Ninth Circuit reversed the district
court's dismissal, holding that where an officer's conduct showed "gross disregard"
for a victim's safety and the department had a policy of non-response, the plaintiff
had stated a cognizable § 1983 claim. The case established that deliberate indifference
to DV victims can constitute state action sufficient for § 1983.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Pre-Castle Rock Ninth Circuit precedent establishing that systematic police failure to
respond to DV calls and enforce restraining orders can state a § 1983 claim. After
Castle Rock (2005) narrowed the due process path, equal protection theory survives.
Balistreri remains significant for the proposition that discriminatory or deliberately
indifferent police non-response to DV is actionable. In the steward's case, where APD
officers responded to pre-positioned false reports and accepted a citizen's arrest
without following § 13701 protocols, Balistreri supports the argument that the
structural failure (missing dominant aggressor analysis, unsigned report) is part of
a pattern of deliberate indifference.
