# Reasoning — Cal. Pen. Code § 13701

## How Courts Apply This Standard

### 1. Mandatory Policy Obligation — Not Discretionary
The statute uses "shall" throughout — agencies have no discretion to omit any of the 10 required policy categories. Courts have consistently held that failure to adopt or implement written policies is itself a statutory violation, separate from any individual officer's conduct. The policies must exist in writing and must cover each enumerated subsection.

### 2. Dominant Aggressor Determination Is an Affirmative Duty
Officers are not permitted to arrest the "first" aggressor or the person who called 911. The statute requires affirmative analysis of four factors: (1) legislative intent to protect victims from continuing abuse; (2) threats creating fear of physical injury; (3) history of domestic violence between the parties; (4) self-defense. Failure to conduct this analysis — particularly ignoring history — is a policy violation. Courts look at whether the department's written policy actually operationalizes these four factors.

### 3. Report Writing Is a Mandatory Policy Category (§ 13701(c)(10))
Every agency must have written standards for "writing of reports." An unsigned report — or a report missing required fields — is evidence that the agency's written policy either does not exist, is not complied with, or is inadequate. In § 1983 litigation, the unsigned report can anchor a "custom or policy" claim: the failure to sign is the visible tip of a systemic policy deficiency.

### 4. Citizen Arrest Use Is Regulated (§ 13701(c)(3))
Policies must specifically address the use of citizen arrests in domestic violence contexts. Where an officer facilitates, accepts, or encourages a citizen arrest without the required written policy framework, the statutory floor has not been met. The absence of policy on citizen arrests is itself actionable.

### 5. Dual Arrest Discouragement Does Not Prohibit — But Requires Dominant Aggressor Finding
Agencies must "discourage" dual arrests "when appropriate." This means the policy must contain affirmative guidance on dominant aggressor analysis before a dual arrest is made. A department that routinely makes dual arrests without documented dominant aggressor analysis is operating outside the statutory framework.

### 6. Written Notice to Victims Is Non-Negotiable (§ 13701(c)(9))
Officers must furnish written notice at the scene. Failure to provide this notice at the time of response is a direct violation of the required policy standard. Victim notice is not optional and cannot be deferred.

## Practical Test
To establish a § 13701 violation: (1) obtain the agency's written DV response policy; (2) compare the policy to each of the 10 required categories; (3) identify any category that is absent or inadequate; (4) document any officer conduct that deviates from the written policy or that would have been prevented by a compliant policy. The unsigned report (APD #23-004494) is direct evidence of a (c)(10) failure.
