# Historical Loss — Cal. Pen. Code § 13701

## How Parties Lose on This Standard

### Failure Mode 1: Confusing § 13701 with a Private Right of Action
§ 13701 does not itself create a private right of action for damages. Litigants who sue directly under § 13701 in state court for money damages are routinely dismissed. The correct vehicle is 42 U.S.C. § 1983 (federal civil rights) anchored to a substantive constitutional right (equal protection, due process), with § 13701 providing the evidentiary basis that a policy deficiency existed. Litigants lose when they plead § 13701 as a standalone damages claim.

### Failure Mode 2: Castle Rock Trap — No Affirmative Duty to Protect
Town of Castle Rock v. Gonzales (2005) 545 U.S. 748 held that a restraining order does not create a constitutionally protected property interest enforceable through § 1983. Courts that follow Castle Rock reject § 1983 claims premised solely on failure to enforce a TRO. To survive, the § 1983 claim must be anchored to equal protection (selective non-enforcement based on sex/class) or a special relationship/state-created danger theory — not simple failure to act.

### Failure Mode 3: Failure to Obtain the Agency's Written Policy
Litigants who assert § 13701 violations without first obtaining the actual written policy through a Public Records Act request are dismissed on evidentiary grounds. You cannot prove the policy is deficient if you have not obtained the policy. Obtain the policy before filing; compare each of the 10 subsections.

### Failure Mode 4: Not Connecting Policy Gap to the Incident
Courts require that the policy deficiency be causally connected to the harm. Showing that a policy is inadequate in the abstract is not enough — the specific inadequacy must be the moving force behind the constitutional violation. The unsigned report (§ 13701(c)(10)) must be shown to be the product of the missing or inadequate written report-writing policy.

### Failure Mode 5: Ignoring the Dominant Aggressor Analysis in the Incident Report
Where no dominant aggressor analysis is documented in the incident report, litigants sometimes fail to raise this as a statutory violation — they instead focus on the arrest decision. The missing analysis is itself a policy violation under § 13701(b). Failure to raise it means it is waived for purposes of pattern-and-practice claims.

### Failure Mode 6: Citizen Arrest Acceptance Without Policy Challenge
Where a citizen arrest is accepted by officers without the required written policy framework (§ 13701(c)(3)), litigants frequently fail to challenge the citizen arrest on statutory grounds. The absence of a citizen-arrest-specific written policy is an independent basis for challenging the legality of the arrest.

## Steward's Case Trap
APD Report #23-004494 is unsigned. The unsigned status is a DOC-001 FAIL. The § 13701(c)(10) argument is: APD's written report policy either (a) does not exist, (b) does not require officer signatures, or (c) is not enforced. Any of the three options is a § 13701 violation. This violation supports the § 1983 equal protection claim by demonstrating that the agency's DV response infrastructure is systematically deficient — not just a one-time error.
