# Family Code § 3011 — Judicial Reasoning and Doctrinal Application

## Overview

Section 3011 is the operative best-interest checklist courts must work through in any custody or visitation proceeding under § 3021. It does not create a closed list — courts may consider additional factors — but the five enumerated categories are mandatory. The statute operates within the policy framework established by § 3020.

## Key Doctrinal Points

### 1. Health, Safety, and Welfare Is the Threshold Factor (§ 3011(a)(1))

California courts treat health, safety, and welfare as the threshold inquiry. When a child's physical safety is at issue, all other factors yield. This is the direct statutory mandate of § 3020(c): when the § 3020(a) safety interest conflicts with the § 3020(b) contact interest, safety prevails. Under § 3011(a)(1), the court must affirmatively find that any custody arrangement protects the child's welfare — a passive showing of absence of harm is insufficient.

### 2. The Corroboration Requirement Is a Floor, Not a Ceiling (§ 3011(a)(2)(B))

The statute says the court "may" require independent corroboration before considering abuse allegations. Courts have interpreted this to mean: (a) corroboration is not required to raise the allegation, but (b) a court that ignores well-corroborated abuse evidence abuses its discretion. The corroboration provision was designed to screen out fabricated claims — not to provide a procedural shield against documented abuse. In re Marriage of Burgess (1996) confirmed that the court retains broad discretion but must actually exercise it.

**Key corroboration sources recognized by statute:** law enforcement reports, CPS reports, court records, medical facility records, nonprofit DV service organizations.

**Trap for respondents:** An unsigned, unverified police report does not constitute corroboration. A signed law enforcement report does. The quality and chain of custody of the corroborating document matters.

### 3. Contact with Both Parents Is a Policy Goal, Not an Absolute Right (§ 3011(a)(3))

The nature and amount of contact factor expressly cross-references § 3046 (contact when DV exists) and § 3100 (supervised visitation). Courts applying § 3011(a)(3) must read it together with those provisions. Frequent contact is the statutory default; reduction or supervision of contact is the remedy when § 3011(a)(1)-(2) factors are present. In re Marriage of Biallas (1998) held that the trial court must explain its reasoning when departing from frequent contact.

### 4. Written Findings Are Mandatory When Abuse Allegations Are Before the Court (§ 3011(a)(5))

When a party raises abuse or substance abuse allegations, and the court nonetheless grants sole/joint custody or unsupervised visitation to the alleged abuser, the court MUST state reasons in writing or on the record. Failure to do so is reversible error. Montenegro v. Diaz (2001) established that a stipulated custody order that omits mandatory findings is not insulated from review — the best-interest requirement is non-waivable.

### 5. The Non-Discrimination Mandate Is Absolute (§ 3011(b))

Added to address bias, § 3011(b) prohibits courts from considering sex, gender identity, gender expression, or sexual orientation of any parent, legal guardian, or relative. This applies even to relatives who are not seeking custody. No exception exists; any order that facially or implicitly relies on a prohibited characteristic is void on its face.

### 6. The 2023 SB 599 Amendment — Expanded Abuse Targets

The 2023 amendment expanded the categories of persons against whom prior abuse history is relevant. Before 2024, the statute focused on abuse of the child and the other parent. After SB 599, abuse against any person with whom the abuser had a dating or engagement relationship is also a mandatory consideration. Courts applying pre-2024 analysis to post-2024 facts are applying the wrong law.

## Practical Tests Courts Apply

- **The "all of the following" test:** The statute says "shall consider all of the following." Omitting any factor is error. Courts check the record for explicit mention of each factor.
- **The written-findings trigger:** If any party has placed abuse allegations in the record, the § 3011(a)(5) written-findings requirement automatically activates.
- **The corroboration-source hierarchy:** Law enforcement and medical records rank highest. Victim testimony alone, without corroboration, may be given less weight — but corroboration plus victim testimony is entitled to full weight.
