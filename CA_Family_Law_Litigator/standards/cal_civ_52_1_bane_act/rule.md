# California Civil Code § 52.1 — The Bane Act (Tom Bane Civil Rights Act)

**Steward: Michael Hartmann**
**Citizen: CA_Family_Law_Litigator**

## Verbatim Statute Text (Key Provisions)

*Cal. Civ. Code § 52.1(b):*

The Attorney General, or any district attorney or city attorney may bring a civil action for injunctive and other appropriate equitable relief in the name of the people of the State of California, as parens patriae, to protect the exercise or enjoyment of the right or rights secured by the Constitution or laws of the United States, or of the rights secured by the Constitution or laws of this state from any person who interferes, or attempts to interfere, whether by threat, intimidation, or coercion, with the exercise or enjoyment of those rights.

*Cal. Civ. Code § 52.1(c):*

Any individual whose exercise or enjoyment of rights secured by the Constitution or laws of the United States, or of rights secured by the Constitution or laws of this state, has been interfered with, or attempted to be interfered with, as described in subdivision (b), may institute and prosecute in their own name and on their own behalf a civil action for damages, including, but not limited to, damages under Section 52, injunctive relief, and other appropriate equitable relief to protect the peaceable exercise or enjoyment of the right or rights secured.

*Cal. Civ. Code § 52.1(i):*

In any action under subdivision (b) or (c), the court may award attorney's fees.

*Cal. Civ. Code § 52.1(k):*

Speech alone is not sufficient to support an action brought pursuant to subdivision (b) or (c), except upon a showing that the speech itself threatens violence against a specific person or group of persons; and the person or group of persons against whom the threat is directed reasonably fears that, because of the speech, violence will be committed against them or their property; and the person threatening violence had the apparent ability to carry out the threat.

*Cal. Civ. Code § 52.1(n):*

The rights protected by this section include, but are not limited to, the following rights protected by the California Constitution: The right to vote. The right to hold public office. The right to equal protection under the law. The right to be free from discrimination based on sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, sexual orientation, citizenship, primary language, or immigration status. The right to privacy. The right to due process.

## Damages Reference — Cal. Civ. Code § 52(b)

Violations entitle the plaintiff to:
- Actual damages (no cap)
- Up to three times actual damages
- A civil penalty of $25,000
- Attorney's fees
- Injunctive relief

---

## Government Code Immunity Does NOT Apply

*Cal. Civ. Code § 52.1(n) (cross-reference to exclusion):*

The immunities provided by Sections 821.6, 844.6, and 845.6 of the Government Code do NOT apply to peace officers sued under § 52.1.
