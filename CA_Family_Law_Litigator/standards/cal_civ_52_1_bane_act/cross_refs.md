## Federal Parallel

**42 U.S.C. § 1983 (federal):** For claims against state actors. Color of law required. § 1988 fee-shifting available but less certain than Bane Act's mandatory fee provision. Often pled in parallel with § 52.1.

## Steward Case Connections

**Federal § 1983 Complaint (NDCA Oakland):**
The Bane Act claim is the state law parallel to the federal § 1983 claim. Both should be pled. State court Bane Act claims can also proceed independently in Alameda or Contra Costa Superior Court. The $25,000 per violation civil penalty + triple damages make the state claim potentially higher value than the federal claim.

**Christina Cerretani (private actor):**
Because the Bane Act does not require color of law, Christina's 16-year counter-filing pattern — each counter-filing being a coercive act designed to interfere with Michael's custody rights and legal rights — can be characterized as a series of Bane Act violations by a private actor. The coercion element is satisfied by the threat-backed nature of each filing (threat of custody loss, threat of arrest via false reports, threat of financial destruction via litigation).

**SIM Swap / Communication Denial:**
The Bane Act is a vehicle for the communication fraud claim because blocking phone access to medical services, disability applications, and legal assistance interferes with constitutional-level rights (privacy, medical decision-making, access to courts) through coercive technical means.

**Ryan McClaran (IT operator):**
If Ryan McClaran conducted digital surveillance and SIM swap operations as an agent of or in coordination with other defendants, he is a private actor who may be liable under § 52.1 without any need to show state action. His technical interference with communications is coercive interference with rights.
