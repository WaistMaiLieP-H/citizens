Durning v. Balent/Flood
22 Cal.App.4th 1491 (1994)
California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, 1994

FACTS:
Custody modification case in which the trial court was asked to address the impact of domestic violence in the home on custody and visitation decisions. The court was required to apply California's policy that DV in the household where children reside is detrimental to child welfare.

HOLDING — Family Code § 3020 (predecessor Civil Code § 4600):
The Court of Appeal confirmed that under California's statutory policy, a finding that domestic violence has occurred in the household where a child resides is sufficient to establish that the child's health, safety, and welfare have been jeopardized. Courts need not conduct a separate inquiry into whether each individual incident of DV had a measurable negative effect on the child — the Legislature has made the policy finding that DV in the home is detrimental. Custody orders must be structured to protect the child from that environment.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Applies the § 3020(a) legislative finding that DV in the household is detrimental. The significance for this record: documented DV in any proceeding involving children triggers the § 3020 primary-concern analysis. The court cannot minimize or compartmentalize DV as "between adults" without addressing its statutory impact on custody and visitation.
