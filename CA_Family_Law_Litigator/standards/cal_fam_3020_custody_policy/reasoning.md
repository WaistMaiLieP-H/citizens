# Family Code § 3020 — Judicial Reasoning and Doctrinal Application

## Overview

Section 3020 is California's foundational custody policy statute. It establishes the two core public policies — child safety (§ 3020(a)) and frequent parental contact (§ 3020(b)) — and resolves their conflict in § 3020(c): safety wins. Section 3020 is not an operative rule courts apply directly to individual facts; it is the policy framework within which § 3011's enumerated factors operate. Courts routinely cite § 3020 as the interpretive backdrop for every custody decision.

## Key Doctrinal Points

### 1. Safety Is Primary — "Primary Concern" Is Not Aspirational Language

Section 3020(a) states that health, safety, and welfare "shall be the court's primary concern." Courts have held this means safety is not one factor among equals — it is the overriding consideration. In re Marriage of Burgess confirmed that the best-interest standard operates "subject to the safety of the children." In re Marriage of Battenburg established that when there is a documented risk to child safety, the court must prioritize safety even at the cost of contact with the other parent.

"Primary" in § 3020(a) means exactly that: if a custody arrangement favors contact but risks safety, the arrangement must be restructured to ensure safety first.

### 2. DV in the Household Is Expressly Declared Detrimental (§ 3020(a))

Section 3020(a) contains a specific legislative finding: "the perpetration of child abuse or domestic violence in a household where a child resides is detrimental to the health, safety, and welfare of the child." This is not a factual determination requiring proof in each case — it is a legislative declaration that courts apply as a presumption. When domestic violence is established, the court need not separately prove the impact on the child. The detriment is declared by statute.

This legislative finding directly connects to § 3046's rebuttable presumption against awarding custody to a perpetrator of domestic violence.

### 3. Frequent Contact Is a Policy, Not an Absolute Right (§ 3020(b))

Section 3020(b) declares it public policy that children have frequent and continuing contact with both parents. Courts have used this provision to resist modifying established contact arrangements. However, § 3020(b) contains its own internal exception: "except when the contact would not be in the best interests of the child, as provided in subdivisions (a) and (c) of this section and Section 3011." The frequent-contact policy applies unless the § 3020(a) safety policy or the § 3011 factors establish otherwise.

**Critical:** Courts that treat § 3020(b) as a near-absolute rule, overriding documented safety concerns, are misapplying the statute. The internal exception is explicit.

### 4. The § 3020(c) Conflict-Resolution Rule — Safety Prevails

When the two policies conflict — and they do whenever there is documented DV or child abuse — § 3020(c) provides the resolution: the order "shall be made in a manner that ensures the health, safety, and welfare of the child and the safety of all family members." The word "shall" is mandatory. Courts cannot balance safety against contact when the two are in genuine conflict — safety must prevail.

Courts citing § 3020(b) to maintain contact arrangements despite documented safety concerns are violating § 3020(c).

### 5. The 2019 SB 495 Amendment — Non-Discrimination in Custody

Section 3020(d) (added by Stats. 2019, Ch. 551, SB 495) codified the non-discrimination rule at the policy level: sex, gender identity, gender expression, and sexual orientation of a parent, legal guardian, or relative are not to be considered. This reinforces § 3011(b) at the policy level and applies to all custody and visitation determinations.

### 6. Section 3020 as Statutory Interpretation Guide

Courts use § 3020 to resolve ambiguities in other custody statutes. When a provision of Chapter 11 (mediation) or Chapter 1 (general custody) is ambiguous, the court interprets it consistent with the § 3020(a) safety-first policy. This means, for example, that § 3164's mediator qualification requirement is interpreted to serve § 3020(a)'s safety mandate — an unqualified mediator jeopardizes the safety determination.

## Common Citations

- In re Marriage of Burgess (1996) 13 Cal.4th 25 — § 3020 framework; safety primary
- In re Marriage of Biallas (1998) 65 Cal.App.4th 755 — § 3020(b) contact policy; departure requires explanation
- In re Marriage of Battenburg (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 1338 — § 3020(a) safety prevails over contact when risk is documented
