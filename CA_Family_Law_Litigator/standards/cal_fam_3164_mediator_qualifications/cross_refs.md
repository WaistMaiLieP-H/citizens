## Steward Case Connections

**The § 3164(b)/§ 1815 Chain Applied to September 2010**
- § 3164(b) effective date: January 1, 1994 — sixteen years before the 2010 Ajaniku mediation.
- The statute was fully in force and unambiguously applicable to any court-designated mediator in Alameda County in 2010.
- Ajaniku's designation "by the court" under § 3164(a) triggered the mandatory § 3164(b) qualification requirement.
- The § 3164(b) SHALL provision is not a notice requirement, a best-efforts requirement, or a preference. It is a categorical prerequisite.
- An unqualified mediator's recommendation is a nullity for purposes of custody law. The court cannot cure the defect by accepting the recommendation.

**Collateral Attack Strategy**
- Montenegro v. Diaz (2001): no valid judicial best-interest determination was ever made because the input (Ajaniku's recommendation) was procedurally defective under § 3164(b)/§ 1815.
- Result: the changed-circumstances rule does not protect the September 2010 order.
- The 2010 order is subject to full § 3011 re-examination on a modification motion without the movant needing to show a substantial change in circumstances.
- Supporting evidence: absence of Ajaniku from BBS and Board of Psychology public databases; the court's order substantially adopting the mediator's recommendation; absence of any independent § 3011 written findings in the court record.
