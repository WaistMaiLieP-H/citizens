# Family Code §§ 3150-3153 — Cross-References

## Statutory cross-references within the Family Code

- **§ 3011** — "Best interest" factors the court considers. Minor's counsel's § 3151(a) duty to "gather evidence that bears on the best interests of the child" is keyed to these factors. Counsel's investigative reach exists in service of § 3011.
- **§ 3020** — Legislative policy on custody/visitation. Chapter 10 operationalizes § 3020 by giving the child a dedicated advocate in contests where the policy is most at risk.
- **§ 3040** — Custody-priority hierarchy. Minor's counsel's evidence feeds into the § 3040 analysis without being tied to any priority party.
- **§ 3041** — Nonparent-custody gate. When a nonparent seeks custody, minor's counsel's § 3151(a) investigation is independently relevant to the (a) "required to serve the best interest" finding and to the (d) de-facto-parent predicate findings.
- **§ 3044** — DV rebuttable presumption. In a § 3044 contest, minor's counsel's evidence about the child's exposure, preferences, and safety is an independent evidentiary channel. § 3044(e) prohibits sole reliance on evaluator conclusions; minor's counsel supplies a non-evaluator channel.
- **§ 3111** — Court-appointed child custody evaluation. § 3111(a) requires the evaluator's report to be served on minor's counsel. Counsel has standing to challenge qualifications (rule 5.225) and methodology (rule 5.220) on the child's behalf.
- **§ 3177** — Mediation confidentiality. § 3151(c)(5) permits minor's counsel to interview mediators only "subject to Sections 3177 and 3182" — mediation confidentiality survives.
- **§ 3182** — Recommending-mediator reports. Same subject-to clause applies.

## Rules of Court (California)

- **Rule 5.240** — Procedure for appointment of counsel to represent a child. Incorporated by § 3150(a).
- **Rule 5.241** — Compensation of court-appointed counsel. Incorporated by § 3150(a); operationalizes § 3153.
- **Rule 5.242** — Qualifications, training, continuing education, and responsibilities of counsel appointed to represent a child. Incorporated by § 3150(a). Supplies the professional-standards scaffolding the statute itself does not provide.
- **Rule 5.220** — Court-ordered child custody evaluations: methodology and content requirements. Relevant where minor's counsel challenges evaluator output under § 3111.
- **Rule 5.225** — Qualifications and training of child custody evaluators. Relevant for Track A evaluator challenges.
- **Rule 5.250** — Children's participation and testimony in family-law proceedings. Intersects with § 3151(a)'s voice duty when the child testifies or otherwise participates directly.

## Evidence Code

- **§ 720** — Qualifications of an expert. Where minor's counsel's statements drift toward opinion territory, the § 3151(b) witness bar combined with Evid. § 720 is the cross-check.
- **§ 1010 et seq.** — Psychotherapist-patient privilege. § 3151(c)(7) places assertion/waiver of this privilege in counsel's hands for child-held privilege questions.
- **§ 994** — Physician-patient privilege. Same § 3151(c)(7) framework.

## Welfare and Institutions Code

- **§ 827** — Confidentiality of juvenile court records. Interacts with § 3152 where CPS records emanate from or are cross-filed in juvenile proceedings.
- **§ 10850** — Confidentiality of public-social-services records. CPS records fall within this confidentiality scheme; § 3152 provides the family-law access procedure.

## Federal constitutional backdrop

- **Troxel v. Granville (2000) 530 U.S. 57** — Parental-liberty-interest floor. In custody proceedings implicating the fit-parent presumption, minor's counsel's advocacy must be understood as representing the child's interests — not as a counterweight to the parental liberty interest itself. The child's interests and the parent's constitutional interests can coexist without collapse.

## 42 U.S.C. § 1983 bridge

Where minor's counsel is selected, compensated, or instructed in ways that compromise independence — particularly where a state actor (court, agency, or institutional party) structures the appointment to align counsel with the state's preferred outcome — the child's (and collaterally, the parent's) due-process rights are implicated. The federal § 1983 vehicle addresses the state-actor misuse of the appointment mechanism; § 3150/§ 3151 furnish the state-law standard against which that misuse is measured.

## Findings bridges

- **FAMLAW-005** (mother-as-dispatcher orchestration) — Where institutional parties used the custody process to shape outcomes, absent minor's counsel the child's interests were never independently advocated. The § 3150 motion is the retroactive and forward-looking remedy.
- **FAMLAW-006** (actor-verification pass — evaluator NPI / licensure / appointment-jurisdiction mismatches) — Minor's counsel under § 3151(c)(8) can seek independent examination; where an existing evaluator is compromised on Track A (qualifications) grounds, § 3150-appointed counsel is the party-equivalent standing to make that challenge on the child's behalf.
