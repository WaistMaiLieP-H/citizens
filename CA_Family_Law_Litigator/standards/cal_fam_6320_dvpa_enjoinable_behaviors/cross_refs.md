| Standard | Relationship |
|---|---|
| `cal_fam_6203_dvpa_abuse_definition` | § 6203(a)(4) defines "abuse" to include any conduct enjoinable under § 6320; these two statutes form a closed loop: § 6320 defines enjoinable conduct, § 6203 makes enjoinable conduct = abuse |
| `cal_fam_6321_dvro_exclusion` | § 6320 is the authority for behavioral prohibitions; § 6321 is the authority for move-out/exclusion orders; they are typically combined in a DVRO |

| `cal_crc_5_215_dv_protocol` | Court must apply DV protocol under CRC 5.215 when DVRO is sought under § 6320; separate mediation process applies |

## Steward Case Connections

**Marin DVRO (Christina Cerretani as petitioner)**
- The Marin DVRO against Michael Hartmann was issued under or consistent with § 6320. The critical question is whether the petitioner met the ex parte emergency standard and whether the factual basis — including unsigned police reports — was sufficient to support issuance.
- The DVRO's effect on Cole Hartmann's custody (now in Christina's custody under the Marin DVRO) flows through § 6323, which governs custody within DVRO proceedings.

**Coercive Control Pattern (§ 6320(c)) as Applied to Michael Hartmann**
- The 16-year pattern of SIM swap/clone fraud (§ 6320(c)(3): monitoring communications), coordinated third-party contacts including law enforcement (§ 6320(c) — indirect conduct through third parties), financial fraud affecting economic resources (§ 6320(c)(3): controlling economic resources), and isolation from support (§ 6320(c)(1)) constitutes coercive control against Michael Hartmann — not the other way.
- The SB 50 amendment (effective January 1, 2026) extends § 6320 to include SB 50's third-party orchestration provisions, directly applicable to coordinated conduct through family members and state actors.
