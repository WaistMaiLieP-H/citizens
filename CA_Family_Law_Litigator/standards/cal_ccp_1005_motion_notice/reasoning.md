# Reasoning — How Courts Apply CCP § 1005

## Core Function

CCP § 1005(b) sets the default notice period for California civil motions: 16 court days advance notice, counted backward from the hearing date. The statute applies to family court motions (RFOs, ex parte modifications, custody motions) unless a specific provision provides otherwise.

## Court Days vs. Calendar Days

**Court days** are days the courthouse is open — excluding weekends and court holidays. Counting backward from a hearing date, the practitioner must skip weekends and every listed court holiday. A mistake of even one day renders the motion improperly noticed.

**Example:** Hearing on Tuesday, April 22. Counting back 16 court days (no holidays): service/filing deadline is Monday, March 31 (not April 2, which would be a calendar-day count).

## The Service Extension Rules

The 16-court-day base period is extended based on the method of service:
- Mail within California: +5 calendar days (so service must go out 16 court days + 5 calendar days before hearing)
- Fax or overnight/express: +2 calendar days
- Electronic service (CCP § 1010.6): +2 court days

These extensions exist because the law recognizes that service by methods other than personal delivery creates a lag between when papers leave the serving party and when the opposing party actually receives them.

## Opposition and Reply Deadlines

The statute also sets deadlines running backward from the hearing:
- Opposition: at least 9 court days before hearing
- Reply: at least 5 court days before hearing

These are hard minimums absent court order. Filing opposition the day before a hearing — a common emergency tactic — violates § 1005 and the court may disregard late-filed papers.

## What § 1005 Does NOT Do

**§ 1013 confusion:** CCP § 1013 extends response deadlines when a triggering document (like a complaint or demand) is served by mail — giving the recipient extra time to respond. § 1005 does something different: it sets how far in advance a moving party must give notice before a hearing. § 1013's mail-extension rules do not apply to the advance-notice period under § 1005. Courts regularly hold that § 1013 cannot be used to shorten the § 1005 notice window.

**Exceptions:** § 1005(c) carves out arbitration and proceedings with specific statutory notice periods. Family court has its own notice rules for some proceedings (e.g., ex parte applications under CRC 5.165), but for standard RFOs, § 1005 applies.

## Family Court Application

An RFO (FL-300) sets a hearing date. The party filing the RFO must serve the moving papers at least 16 court days before the hearing (plus mail extension if served by mail). If the opposing party receives papers fewer than 9 court days before the hearing, the opposing party may seek a continuance or object to the motion as improperly noticed.

**Shortened notice by court order:** A court may shorten the § 1005 notice period by order. Emergency or ex parte applications may be heard on shorter notice — but only with court authorization, not by the moving party's unilateral choice.

## Pro Se Application

A pro se respondent who receives motion papers must count backward from the hearing date to determine whether they received the required notice. If papers arrive fewer than 16 court days + applicable service extension before the hearing, the respondent has grounds to object to insufficient notice — and the court has discretion to continue the hearing or deny the motion on procedural grounds.
