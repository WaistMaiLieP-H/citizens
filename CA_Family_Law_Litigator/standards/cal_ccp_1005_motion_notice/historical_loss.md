# Historical Loss — CCP § 1005 Failures

## Failure Mode 1 — Motion Denied for Short Service

**The Loss:** A party files a motion and sets the hearing 14 court days out instead of 16. The opposing party objects at the hearing that service was insufficient. The court continues the hearing to a new date — adding several weeks' delay — or denies the motion on procedural grounds. The substantive relief is delayed or lost.

**Why it happens:** Calendar-day counting instead of court-day counting; failure to account for court holidays.

---

## Failure Mode 2 — Mail Service Extension Missed

**The Loss:** A party serves moving papers by mail 16 court days before the hearing without adding the 5-calendar-day mail extension. The opposing party receives papers effectively 2-3 days before the required cutoff. The court finds improper notice and the motion is denied or continued.

**Why it happens:** Confusion between § 1005's advance-notice framework and § 1013's response-extension framework. Practitioners know § 1013 adds time; they sometimes misapply it in the wrong direction for § 1005.

---

## Failure Mode 3 — Late Opposition Disregarded

**The Loss:** An opposing party files opposition 6 court days before the hearing instead of the required 9. The moving party objects. The court disregards the opposition as untimely. The responding party's evidence is not before the court, and the motion is granted based only on the moving party's presentation.

**Significance:** In family court RFOs for custody or support modification, a respondent who misses the opposition deadline may lose a major substantive hearing without ever having their evidence considered. Pro se litigants are particularly vulnerable.

---

## Failure Mode 4 — Shortened Notice Used Without Court Order

**The Loss:** A moving party unilaterally sets a hearing on 10 court days' notice, reasoning that "emergency circumstances" justify it. Without a court order shortening time, the short notice is improper. The court at the hearing either denies the motion or reschedules, wasting both the court's and parties' time.

**Prevention:** Shortened notice requires either a court order (from an ex parte application) or a specific statutory exception. The party cannot self-authorize shortened time.

---

## Failure Mode 5 — Opposing Party Not Given Notice of Remote Hearing

**The Loss:** An RFO is filed, served by mail, and the hearing is scheduled for remote/video appearance. The opposing party receives proper § 1005 notice of the hearing date but is not notified of the video appearance instructions or link. The opposing party appears at the courthouse on the hearing date only to find the hearing proceeded without them by video. Default orders are entered.

**Note:** Post-COVID remote hearings add a practical layer to § 1005 compliance — the notice must convey not just the date/time/department but also the appearance format. Courts have found due process violations where parties received paper notice but not video access instructions.
