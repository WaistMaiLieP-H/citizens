# Family Code § 3170 — Layer 3: Historical Loss (what drove the statute)

## Pre-1981 California

Before AB 1126 (Stats. 1980, ch. 48), contested custody went straight to hearing. Typical pattern: two lawyers, two declarations, one judge with a crowded calendar, ruling on best-interest questions with minutes of argument. Agreements were rare, appeals common, and children often shuttled through multiple temporary orders before anything stabilized.

## The 1980 reform

AB 1126 was a package reform, not a single section:
- Mandatory mediation (former Civ. Code § 4607, now Fam. Code § 3170)
- Mediator qualifications (§ 4607, now § 3164 / § 1815)
- Confidentiality (§ 3177 predecessor)

California's experiment was watched nationally. Within 15 years the mandatory-mediation model had spread in some form to a majority of states.

## The DV problem (1990s)

Empirical evaluation of California's model in the late 1980s and early 1990s surfaced a consistent finding: **mandatory mediation disadvantaged DV victims.** Joint sessions replicated the power imbalance. Mediators without DV training pressured compromise. Recommending counties sometimes produced recommendations that normalized abuse history.

Legislative responses (pre-2017 chapter numbers PRE-2017-CHAIN-PENDING per provenance.md):
- **Stats. 1991, ch. 839** — codified separate-session right and DV screening (§ 3181 lineage).
- **Stats. 1993** — Family Code renumbering carried reforms into § 3170 et seq.
- **Stats. 1999, ch. 982 (AB 840)** — mandatory DV training for mediators (§ 1816).
- **Stats. 2003, ch. 243** — further refinement: § 3170(b) DVPA case-by-case determination.

## Recent

- **2012 (Stats. 2012, ch. 155)** — clarified that Child Custody Recommending Counseling (CCRC) in recommending counties is governed by the same § 3170 framework as Child Custody Mediation in non-recommending counties.
- **2017 (Stats. 2017, Ch. 330, AB 1692)** — current § 3170 derives authority from this repeal-and-reenact chapter; effective January 1, 2018; operative January 1, 2020 by its own provisions.
- **CRC Rule 5.210** (adopted 2003, amended 2012) — sets uniform statewide standards for how mediation is conducted.
- **CRC Rule 5.215** (adopted 2003, amended repeatedly) — DV protocol, screening, separate sessions, safety planning.
