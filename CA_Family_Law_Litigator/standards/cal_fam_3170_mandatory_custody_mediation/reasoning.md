# Family Code § 3170 — Layer 2: Reasoning (Legislative intent)

California adopted mandatory custody mediation in 1981 (Stats. 1980, ch. 48, operative 1981) — the first state in the nation to do so. The Legislature's stated purposes:

1. **Reduce adversarial harm to children.** Custody litigation intensifies parental conflict; parental conflict is the strongest predictor of adverse child outcomes post-separation. Mediation lowers the temperature before issues reach a contested hearing.
2. **Improve agreement durability.** Parties who shape their own parenting plan comply with it more often than parties who have an order imposed on them.
3. **Manage court capacity.** By 1980, custody caseloads were overwhelming family courts; diverting uncontested or negotiable issues to mediation freed bench time for genuinely disputed matters.
4. **Screen for DV.** Mandatory mediation created a guaranteed touchpoint at which a trained mediator could detect domestic violence, substance abuse, or child safety concerns that had not surfaced in pleadings.

The DV carve-out (added 1993, refined through 2003) reflects later understanding that forced joint mediation can replicate the coercive dynamic of the abusive relationship. § 3181's separate-session right and § 3170(b)'s case-by-case appropriateness determination for DVPA cases are the Legislature's attempt to preserve the benefits of mediation without re-victimizing survivors.
