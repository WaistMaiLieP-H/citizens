# Historical Loss — Cal. Fam. Code § 3046

## How Parties Lose on This Standard

### Failure Mode 1: Failing to Establish the DV-Nexus Before Raising § 3046
The protection only applies if the absence was caused by domestic violence. Parties who invoke § 3046 without first establishing that DV occurred — through admissible evidence — lose the protection. Courts frequently decline to apply § 3046 where the DV allegation is unsupported or where the timing of the departure does not align with any documented incident. Document the DV first; invoke § 3046 second.

### Failure Mode 2: Opponent Reframes Departure as Abandonment
The most common counter-argument is § 7822 abandonment. Opposing counsel argues: the parent left, disrupted the child's home, and prioritized personal safety. Parties lose when they fail to: (a) preemptively distinguish § 3046 from § 7822; (b) show continuous contact and communication with the child throughout the absence; (c) file for custody or visitation promptly after departure. Silence after leaving is treated as abandonment regardless of DV history.

### Failure Mode 3: Not Pleading § 3046 Affirmatively in Response to Stability Arguments
The opposing party routinely argues "stability" — the child has been in my home, my school, my routine. § 3046 bars using the departure to establish the absent parent's instability, but courts apply this protection only when it is raised. Failure to affirmatively invoke § 3046 in the responsive declaration means the court may consider the departure without knowing it is barred from doing so.

### Failure Mode 4: Overlooking Subsection (b) as an Offensive Tool
Parties who are on defense under custody arguments often miss that § 3046(b) is an affirmative weapon. Every phone call blocked, every custody exchange interfered with, every email ignored is admissible under § 3046(b) as a factor the court "may consider." Failure to document and present this evidence is a lost opportunity that never comes back.

### Failure Mode 5: The § 3046(c) Trap — Protective Order Against the Departing Parent
Where a protective order has been obtained against the parent who departed, § 3046(c) removes the protection. The opposing party will obtain a protective order precisely to trigger this exception. Recognize this tactic: a DVRO obtained after the departure, based on fabricated allegations, may be designed to retroactively strip § 3046 protection. Challenge the basis for the protective order immediately.

## Steward's Case Application
The Marin DVRO FL0002067 (expires 8/19/2026) was obtained on fabricated grounds — Christina's counter-filing pattern (2009-2025). If the DVRO was used to establish that the steward was excluded from the residence, § 3046(c) could be invoked to remove § 3046(a) protection. The counter-strategy: establish that the DVRO itself was the product of fabricated allegations (the Christina 16-year counter-filing pattern), and that the steward's departures were responses to actual DV, not the cause of the DVRO. The 6/11/2009 OPD report fabrication finding is the anchor for this argument.
