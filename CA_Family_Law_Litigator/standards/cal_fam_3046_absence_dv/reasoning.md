# Reasoning — Cal. Fam. Code § 3046

## How Courts Apply This Standard

### 1. The Absence Must Be Causally Connected to the DV
The statutory protection applies only when the absence "is a result of an act of domestic violence by the other party." Courts require a nexus between the DV and the departure. A party who left for unrelated reasons cannot invoke § 3046. The evidentiary question is: what caused the departure? Police reports, medical records, photographs, declarations, and prior court orders are all relevant to establishing the nexus.

### 2. Absence Cannot Be Used as Evidence of Parental Unfitness
Once the DV-nexus is established, the absent parent's departure is shielded from adverse inference. Courts cannot use the fact of leaving as evidence that the parent: (a) is less capable of providing care; (b) has abandoned the child; (c) has disrupted the child's stability; or (d) has prioritized personal safety over the child's welfare. These are the most common ways opposing counsel frames the absence — § 3046 forecloses each framing.

### 3. Subsection (b) Is an Independent Affirmative Tool
Courts may — and often do — treat interference with the remaining parent's contact as evidence of bad faith in the custody analysis. This is separate from any contempt or enforcement action. The court can weigh the interference directly in the best-interest analysis under § 3011. This means a parent who blocked calls, monitored communications, or prevented pick-up is giving the court an express basis to reduce their custody share under § 3046(b) alone.

### 4. The § 7822 Abandonment Exception Is Narrowly Read
Abandonment under § 7822 requires willful failure to communicate with or provide for the child for at least one year. A parent who leaves with the child, or who remains in contact despite physical separation, does not meet the § 7822 threshold. Courts consistently reject efforts to reframe protective departure as abandonment.

### 5. The § 3046(c) Exclusion Order Exception
If the departing parent is the one subject to a protective order excluding them from the home, § 3046 does not protect their absence — they were required to leave by the order. This exception is designed to prevent § 3046 from being used by an abuser who was excluded by court order and then claims their absence was DV-driven.

### 6. Interaction with the § 3044 DV Presumption
Where § 3044's rebuttable presumption against custody applies (perpetrator of DV), § 3046 operates in parallel to protect the victim-parent's departure. Together, the statutes create a framework: victim's departure is protected; abuser faces a presumption against custody; any interference with contact is an additional factor against the abuser.

## Practical Test
Establish: (1) domestic violence occurred (police reports, prior orders, medical records); (2) the party's departure was caused by that violence; (3) the opposing party is attempting to use the departure as a negative custody factor. Once established, § 3046(a) bars the adverse use. Then deploy § 3046(b) affirmatively: document every instance of contact interference by the opposing party.
