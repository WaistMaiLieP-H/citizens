IN RE MARRIAGE OF LA MUSGA
32 Cal.4th 1072
California Supreme Court, 2004

FACTS:
Custodial parent sought to relocate from California to Ohio. The noncustodial parent
opposed the move. The trial court initially denied the move, then reconsidered and
allowed it. The California Supreme Court granted review to clarify the multi-factor
analysis for relocation cases after Burgess.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 3020 / § 3011 / RELOCATION:
The court held that in determining whether a custodial parent's proposed relocation
would cause detriment justifying a change in custody, the trial court must consider
all relevant factors including: the child's interest in stability; the distance of
the move; the age of the child; the child's relationship with both parents; the
relationship between the parents; the current visitation terms; the reasons for
the proposed move; and the extent to which the parents are likely to comply with
visitation arrangements. No single factor is determinative.

SIGNIFICANCE:
La Musga's multi-factor framework is the standard that governs whenever the
"stability" argument is raised against a departing or relocating parent. When
read with § 3046, the framework operates as follows: the DV-caused departure is
shielded from the stability analysis entirely (§ 3046(a)); the remaining factors
are then evaluated. In the steward's case, 16 years of interference with contact
(§ 3046(b) factor) by Christina weighs heavily against her across every La Musga
factor. The contact interference evidence — blocked calls, monitored communications,
SIM swap (Ryan McClaran) — maps directly onto La Musga's "extent to which parents
are likely to comply with visitation" factor.
