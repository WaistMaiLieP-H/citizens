IN RE MARRIAGE OF MENA AND HAGAN
[Court of Appeal, 2019 — verify citation via Westlaw/Lexis]
California Court of Appeal, 2019

FACTS:
Dissolution proceeding where one parent left the family home with the child following
incidents of domestic violence. The trial court used the parent's departure from
the marital home as a factor weighing against that parent in the custody determination,
reasoning that the parent had disrupted the child's stable living situation.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 3046:
The Court of Appeal reversed, holding that where the departure was caused by domestic
violence by the other party, Family Code § 3046(a) prohibits the trial court from
using that absence as a negative factor in the custody analysis. The court's use
of the departure as evidence of parental instability was legal error requiring reversal.
The proper inquiry is whether the departure was DV-caused, not whether the departure
disrupted the child's routine.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Confirms that § 3046(a) operates as an evidentiary bar — trial courts commit reversible
error when they count a DV-caused departure against the victim parent in the
best-interest analysis. The ruling reinforces that stability arguments grounded
in the victim's departure must be refused by the trial court once the DV nexus
is established. In the steward's case, Christina's counter-filing pattern is designed
to manufacture "departures" that look like instability; § 3046 + this holding
forecloses that strategy.
