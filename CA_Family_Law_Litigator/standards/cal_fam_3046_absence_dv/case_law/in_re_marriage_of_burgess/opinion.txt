IN RE MARRIAGE OF BURGESS
13 Cal.4th 25
California Supreme Court, 1996

FACTS:
Relocation case. The custodial mother sought to move with the couple's children for
employment reasons. The father opposed the move. The trial court denied the relocation.
The Court of Appeal reversed. The California Supreme Court granted review to address
the standard governing a custodial parent's right to relocate.

HOLDING — CAL. FAM. CODE § 3020 / BEST INTEREST:
"[A] parent who has sole physical custody has the presumptive right to change the
residence of the child, subject to the power of the court to restrain a removal that
would prejudice the rights or welfare of the child." The court held that the noncustodial
parent bears the initial burden of showing the move would be detrimental to the child.
The custodial parent's reasons for moving are generally not subject to court approval.

SIGNIFICANCE:
While Burgess predates the DV-specific § 3046 framing, it establishes the foundational
principle that the court cannot use a custodial parent's relocation decision as
evidence against them without the noncustodial parent first showing detriment.
§ 3046 extends this logic to DV victims: the departure is presumptively protected
once the DV nexus is shown. Burgess is cited alongside § 3046 to argue that a
DV victim-parent who departed cannot be treated as having disrupted the child's
custody arrangement without the abuser first demonstrating actual detriment —
a near-impossible burden where the departure was caused by the abuser's own violence.
