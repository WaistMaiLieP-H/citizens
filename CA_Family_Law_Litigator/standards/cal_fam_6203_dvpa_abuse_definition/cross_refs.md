## Statutory Cross-References

- **Family Code § 6200** — Domestic Violence Prevention Act declaration of purpose; § 6203 provides the operative definitions for the entire Act
- **Family Code § 6211** — "domestic violence" definition; requires conduct that constitutes "abuse" under § 6203 AND is committed against a covered relationship
- **Family Code § 6300** — grounds for protective order; the court must find "reasonable proof of a past act or acts of abuse" under § 6203
- **Family Code § 6320** — enjoinable behaviors; these are forms of "abuse" within § 6203's definition
- **Penal Code § 273.5** — corporal injury on intimate partner; criminal analog to civil abuse under § 6203
- **Penal Code § 243(e)(1)** — domestic battery; criminal analog
- **Welfare & Institutions Code § 300(a)** — child abuse in dependency proceedings; parallel but distinct definition; § 6203 is the Family Court standard

## Critical Distinction — § 6203 vs. Criminal "Abuse"

§ 6203 defines "abuse" more broadly than California criminal statutes require. Under § 6203(a)(4), "abuse" includes "disturbing the peace of the other party" — conduct that may not be criminal but causes substantial emotional distress. Courts have held that § 6203(a)(4) encompasses coercive control behaviors, stalking-adjacent conduct, and systematic harassment, even without physical contact.

This means a party can obtain a DVRO under the DVPA based on § 6203(a)(4) conduct even if no criminal charges were filed or could be sustained. The civil and criminal standards are independent.
