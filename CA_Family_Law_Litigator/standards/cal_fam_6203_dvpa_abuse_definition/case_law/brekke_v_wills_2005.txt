Brekke v. Wills
125 Cal.App.4th 1400 (2005)
California Court of Appeal, Third Appellate District, 2005

FACTS:
Non-spousal domestic violence proceeding (adult children and parent household). The case presented the question of whether § 6203's definition of "abuse" required a showing of actual physical injury or could be satisfied by a pattern of threatening and harassing conduct that did not result in physical contact.

HOLDING — Family Code § 6203:
The Court of Appeal confirmed that § 6203(a)(3) — "sexual assault" and "placing a person in reasonable apprehension of imminent serious bodily injury" — and § 6203(a)(4) — "disturbing the peace" — are independent grounds for finding "abuse." No physical injury is required under either provision. A pattern of conduct designed to cause fear, mental suffering, or loss of calm and emotional security constitutes "abuse" under § 6203. Courts construing the DVPA should give effect to the Legislature's intent to protect victims from the full range of abusive conduct.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Confirms the breadth of § 6203's coverage beyond physical violence. The case supports the principle that systematic harassment — even without physical contact — is actionable as DV "abuse." Relevant to cases where the abusive conduct consists primarily of digital, financial, or institutional interference rather than physical confrontation.
