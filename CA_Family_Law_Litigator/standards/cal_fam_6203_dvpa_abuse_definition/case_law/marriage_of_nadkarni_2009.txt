In re Marriage of Nadkarni
173 Cal.App.4th 1483 (2009)
California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District, 2009

FACTS:
Post-dissolution case in which one spouse accessed the other's private email account without authorization and used communications found there in litigation. The affected spouse sought a DVRO based on this conduct under Family Code § 6203(a)(4) — "disturbing the peace of the other party." The trial court denied the DVRO, finding that accessing email did not constitute "abuse."

HOLDING — Family Code § 6203:
The Court of Appeal reversed. The court held that "disturbing the peace of the other party" under § 6203(a)(4) is not limited to physical proximity or direct confrontation — it includes conduct that destroys the mental or emotional calm of the other party. Unauthorized access to private communications, used to surveil and control a former spouse, qualifies as "abuse" under § 6203(a)(4). The Legislature intended the DVPA to reach the full range of abusive conduct, not only physical violence.

SIGNIFICANCE:
The landmark case expanding § 6203(a)(4) to cover digital surveillance, privacy violations, and coercive control conduct. Directly applicable to SIM swap / call interception scenarios — unauthorized access to a party's communications to surveil, control, or interfere with their life is "abuse" under § 6203 and can be the basis for a DVRO. Critical for any case involving phone interception, account hijacking, or communications monitoring.
