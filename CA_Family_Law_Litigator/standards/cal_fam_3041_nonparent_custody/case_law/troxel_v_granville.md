# Troxel v. Granville (2000) 530 U.S. 57

## Core holding

The Supreme Court held that a Washington state non-parental-visitation statute, as applied, violated the 14th Amendment substantive-due-process rights of a fit parent. The statute permitted "any person" to petition for visitation and gave the trial court broad discretion to order visitation based on the child's best interest. The Court held that a fit parent's decisions about child custody are entitled to "special weight" and that state courts cannot treat third-party preferences as co-equal with the parent's decisions absent a showing tied to parental harm.

## Why this case matters for § 3041

*Troxel* supplies the constitutional floor that § 3041 implements at the state level. Three propositions carry:

1. **Parental fundamental right.** The right to make decisions concerning the care, custody, and control of one's children is among "the oldest of the fundamental liberty interests recognized by this Court."
2. **Presumption of fitness.** A fit parent is presumed to act in the child's best interest.
3. **Special weight.** When a fit parent's decision is at issue, the parent's determination is entitled to "at least some special weight" — not treated as one factor among many.

## How *Troxel* maps to § 3041

- § 3041(a)'s **"required to serve" standard** is the state-level implementation of the *Troxel* floor: mere preference for the nonparent is insufficient; the state must show necessity tied to the child's interest.
- § 3041(b)'s **clear-and-convincing evidence standard** reflects the elevated evidentiary burden *Troxel* implicitly demands when a fundamental right is implicated.
- § 3041(a)'s **pleading-limitation rule** protects parents from public character attack before the constitutional finding is made, consistent with *Troxel*'s "special weight" doctrine.

## Operational use

Cite *Troxel* for:

- The constitutional premise that a fit parent's decisions are entitled to special weight.
- The requirement that state interference be based on specific findings tied to the child, not to general third-party preferences.
- The proposition that "best interest" alone is insufficient when it operates to override a fit parent's decision — the standard must incorporate parental fitness and detriment.

*Troxel* is the federal backstop that gives § 3041's detriment standard teeth. Where a state court applies § 3041 loosely or conflates preference with necessity, *Troxel* supplies the constitutional basis for reversal.

## Interaction with 42 U.S.C. § 1983

Where state actors manipulate the state custody process to deprive a parent of the *Troxel*-protected interest, the deprivation is actionable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 as a constitutional violation. § 3041 is the state-court vehicle; § 1983 is the federal-court vehicle. The two are not mutually exclusive.
