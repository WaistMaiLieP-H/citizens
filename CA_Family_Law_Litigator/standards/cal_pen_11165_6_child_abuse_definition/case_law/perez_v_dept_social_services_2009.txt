Perez v. Department of Social Services
174 Cal.App.4th 322 (2009)
California Court of Appeal, First Appellate District, 2009

FACTS:
Administrative proceeding challenging a substantiated child abuse finding under CANRA. The petitioner argued the conduct at issue did not meet the § 11165.6 definition of "child abuse or neglect." The case addressed how CANRA definitions apply in the administrative context versus the criminal context.

HOLDING — Penal Code § 11165.6:
The Court of Appeal held that § 11165.6's definition governs in all contexts where CANRA applies — criminal, administrative, and family court proceedings. The definition is not more restrictive in administrative proceedings than in criminal ones. A substantiated CANRA finding requires the agency to establish that conduct falls within the § 11165.6 definition by a preponderance of the evidence. The court confirmed that the aggregating structure of § 11165.6 means any conduct falling within §§ 11165–11165.5 constitutes reportable and actionable "child abuse or neglect."

SIGNIFICANCE:
Confirms that CANRA definitions apply uniformly across proceedings. A substantiated CPS report is based on the same § 11165.6 standard as a criminal prosecution — the threshold is the same, though the evidentiary standard differs. In family court proceedings where a party seeks to use or challenge a CPS substantiation, the § 11165.6 definition is the operative framework.
