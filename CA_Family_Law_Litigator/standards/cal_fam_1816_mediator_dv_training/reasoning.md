# Reasoning — How Courts Apply Family Code § 1816

## Core Function

Family Code § 1816 establishes mandatory domestic violence training requirements for "evaluators" as defined in subdivision (a)(2). The statute covers:

- **Supervising and associate counselors** (§ 1815) — Family Court Services mediators
- **Mediators** (§ 3164) — the same population covered by the credential floor in § 1815/§ 3164
- **Child custody evaluators** (§ 3110.5) — private and court-connected
- **Court-appointed investigators** (§ 3110, Evidence Code § 730)

This means § 1816 applies to every professional who evaluates families in a California custody dispute that involves domestic violence allegations.

## The Training Mandate

The statute creates a three-tier continuing training obligation:

**Tier 1 — Basic instruction** (subd. (c)): No hour minimum specified; the evaluator "shall participate." Seven enumerated content areas including effects of DV on children, legal rights of victims, and community resources.

**Tier 2 — Advanced training** (subd. (d)): 16 hours within a 12-month period. Of that:
- 4 hours: community resource networking (geographic — the communities where the evaluated family may live)
- 12 hours: structured curriculum approved by Administrative Office of the Courts, covering 5 mandatory content areas with detailed sub-topics

**Tier 3 — Annual update** (subd. (e)): 4 hours annually after completing advanced training. Must cover changes in law/case law and updated social science research.

## What "SHALL" Means

Subdivision (b) uses SHALL — "an evaluator SHALL participate." Subdivision (d) uses SHALL — "shall also complete 16 hours." This is mandatory. A court may not waive the training requirement for a mediator or evaluator. An evaluator who has not completed the required training is not qualified to conduct the evaluation under California law.

## The Ajaniku Challenge Framework

Family Code § 1816 is the mechanism for challenging an evaluator's or mediator's DV training credentials. When a mediator conducts a custody evaluation in a DV case without proper § 1816 training:

1. The mediator's recommendations lack the statutory predicate qualification
2. The court should not rely on recommendations from an unqualified evaluator
3. A motion challenging the mediator's qualifications may be filed under § 1816
4. The mediator must produce certificates of completion under subdivision (g)(2)

**Practice rule:** At the outset of any custody evaluation in a DV case, demand proof of § 1816 training from the assigned evaluator. Under subdivision (g)(2), the evaluator is required to attach copies of certificates. Failure to produce certificates — or production of certificates showing noncompliant training — is grounds to object to the evaluation and move for a different evaluator.

## Relationship to § 1815 and § 3164

§ 1816 depends on § 1815 and § 3164 for its definition of "evaluator." Together these sections form the credential tripod:

- § 1815: minimum academic and experience qualifications for counselors
- § 3164: minimum qualifications for mediators (mirror of § 1815)
- § 1816: mandatory DV training for both — regardless of how many years of experience

An evaluator may satisfy § 1815/§ 3164 credential floors but still fail § 1816 if the 16-hour advanced training was not completed. The two inquiries are independent.

## Certificate Production

Subdivision (g)(2) creates an affirmative obligation: absent a local court rule specifying the procedure, the evaluator SHALL attach copies of certificates to documentation submitted to the court. This gives parties a direct mechanism — if certificates are not attached, the omission is a statutory violation, not just a procedural oversight.

## 2025 Amendment (AB 1974)

The most recent amendment (Stats. 2024, Ch. 303, effective January 1, 2025) updated the training framework, including adding subdivision (d)(5)(L) on firearms risk. Any evaluator whose most recent training predates January 1, 2025 may need to verify their curriculum covered the new firearms risk element.
