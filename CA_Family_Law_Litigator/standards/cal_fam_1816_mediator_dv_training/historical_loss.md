# Historical Loss — Family Code § 1816 Failures

## Failure Mode 1 — Untrained Mediator Misreads DV Dynamics, Court Follows Recommendation

**The Loss:** A court-connected mediator without § 1816 advanced training conducts a custody evaluation in a case with documented DV history. The mediator lacks training in the "dynamics of power and control in battering relationships" (§ 1816(d)(5)(C)) and the distinction between "high conflict" and genuine DV (§ 1816(d)(5)(H)). The mediator recommends joint custody or unsupervised visitation. The court adopts the recommendation. The victim parent and children are returned to contact with the abusive parent.

**Why it matters:** § 1816 exists precisely because untrained evaluators systematically misread DV situations. The Legislature identified specific content areas — PTSD in children, intergenerational violence, power/control dynamics, collateral information from police and CPS — because evaluators without training in these areas produce recommendations that harm DV survivors.

---

## Failure Mode 2 — Credentials Assumed, Not Verified

**The Loss:** A party in a DV custody case assumes the court-appointed mediator or evaluator has completed § 1816 training. The mediator produces a report unfavorable to the DV victim. The victim's attorney (or pro se party) never requests the certificates of completion under § 1816(g)(2). The report is admitted without challenge. After the case, it emerges the mediator's last DV training was from 1998 — before the advanced training requirement was meaningfully enforced.

**How to prevent:** At the first appearance, request proof of § 1816 compliance. Under § 1816(g)(2), the evaluator must attach certificates. If certificates are not produced, object in writing and document the noncompliance.

---

## Failure Mode 3 — "High Conflict" Misdiagnosis

**The Loss:** A mediator trained only in general mediation (but not the specific § 1816(d)(5)(H) "high conflict vs. DV" distinction) characterizes a DV case as a "high-conflict" custody dispute. The victim's documented fear responses, hypervigilance, and apparent "combativeness" in mediation are interpreted as mutual conflict rather than trauma symptoms. The mediator recommends co-parenting counseling. The abusive party gains continued contact and uses court-ordered co-parenting sessions as a new vector for coercion and harassment.

**Legislative recognition:** Subdivision (d)(5)(H) — "Understanding the dynamics of high conflict relationships and relationships between an abuser and victim" — was specifically added because courts identified this misdiagnosis pattern in the legislative record.

---

## Failure Mode 4 — Collateral Evidence Ignored

**The Loss:** A mediator without § 1816 training does not obtain police incident reports, CPS records, or prior restraining order pleadings — the exact sources enumerated in § 1816(d)(5)(I). The mediator's report is based solely on the parties' in-person statements. In a case where the abusive party is a skilled manipulator and the victim presents poorly under stress, the report favors the abuser. The collateral record — which would have corroborated the victim — is never reviewed.

**Significance for this case:** The 2009 OPD report (09-011438), the 09-040089 counter-filing report, Contra Costa case 04-23-01959, and CalVCB Application A25-10117946 are all collateral sources within the meaning of § 1816(d)(5)(I). A § 1816-trained evaluator is required to seek and review these sources.

---

## Failure Mode 5 — Certificate Fraud

**The Loss (inverse):** An evaluator produces certificates of completion that are backdated or from a non-eligible provider. Because courts rarely audit certificates, the evaluator's claimed training cannot be verified. The party challenging the evaluator has no practical mechanism to authenticate the certificates without discovery.

**Mitigation:** Under § 1816(a)(1), only "eligible providers" authorized by the Administrative Office of the Courts may issue valid certificates. Demand the provider name and verify against the AOC's authorized provider list.
