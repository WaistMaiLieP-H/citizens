# Junction: Family Law custody orders ↔ Penal Code § 278.5 deprivation of custody

## Doctrinal relationship

Penal Code § 278.5 criminalizes a person who takes, entices away, keeps, withholds, or conceals a child from a lawful custodian in violation of a custody or visitation order. The Family Code establishes the order; the Penal Code enforces it criminally.

**The doctrinal loop.**
1. Family court issues § 3048 custody order.
2. A party willfully violates the order → § 278.5 criminal liability attaches.
3. Conversely, § 278.5 cannot be charged absent a lawful order — so the order's facial validity is an element of the offense.

**The Vernen 6/16/2023 setup.** The reconstructed timeline shows APD acting on a family-law pretext to effectuate a child removal. If the underlying family-law order was facially defective (absent declaration under § 2015.5, absent § 3118 evaluation, etc.), then the § 278.5 predicate for any enforcement action is missing. APD's warrantless action collapses into a § 1983 claim rather than a lawful enforcement of a valid order.

## Key doctrinal points

1. § 278.5 requires a "lawful" custody order — not merely a filed order. Defects go to legality.
2. § 278.5(b) is a wobbler (felony or misdemeanor); § 278 (without order) is a separate offense.
3. "Keeps, withholds, or conceals" is a continuing offense; statute of limitations runs from recovery.
4. A custody order made without proper § 3044 findings, without § 3011 best-interest analysis, or without § 3118 evaluation may be void rather than merely voidable.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- When a party threatens § 278.5 charges, first validate the underlying order's compliance with § 3011/§ 3044/§ 3118.
- Where APD or other LE has effectuated removal under color of an order, trace the order's legal chain.
- A void order does not support § 278.5 prosecution — it supports a counter-claim for civil rights violation.
