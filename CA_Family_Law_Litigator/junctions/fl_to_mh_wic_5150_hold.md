# Junction: Family Law best-interest analysis ↔ WIC § 5150 involuntary hold

## Doctrinal relationship

When a parent has been placed on a WIC § 5150 72-hour hold, family courts frequently treat the hold itself as evidence of unfitness. That treatment is legally incorrect. A § 5150 hold is a probable-cause detention standard — "danger to self, danger to others, or gravely disabled" — decided by a peace officer or clinician under emergency conditions. It is not an adjudication. It is not a finding of parental unfitness. Family courts must conduct independent § 3011 best-interest analysis, not import § 5150 probable cause as if it were a merits finding.

## Key doctrinal points

1. § 5150 is NOT an adjudication. It is a clinician/officer judgment under time pressure with a lower evidentiary threshold.
2. The patient is released at 72 hours unless a § 5250 certification is filed. Non-certification = no finding of illness.
3. Family Code § 3020(a) identifies "health, safety, and welfare of children" as the paramount concern, but § 3020(b) explicitly states that frequent continuing contact with both parents is presumptively in the child's best interest.
4. Conflating § 5150 with § 3044 DV presumption is doctrinally wrong — § 3044 is triggered by adjudicated DV, not by mental-health detention.

## Manufactured-hold pattern

In the Vernen record, § 5150 holds have appeared at moments convenient to opposing custody strategy (Case #1, #6, #14). A junction flag here forces family court to:
- demand the § 5150 clinician affidavit
- verify whether a § 5250 certification followed (usually not)
- exclude the detention itself from the best-interest calculus absent independent evaluation

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- When opposing party introduces a § 5150 hold as evidence: invoke this junction, demand the clinician documentation, and move to exclude the hold as insufficient under § 3011.
- Document the absence of § 5250 certification as affirmative evidence that no illness was found.
