# Junction: Family Law § 6320 et seq. ↔ Penal Code § 273.6 DVRO violation

## Doctrinal relationship — criminal enforcement of a civil order

Family Code §§ 6320–6345 establish domestic-violence restraining orders (DVROs). Penal Code § 273.6 criminalizes intentional and knowing violation of a DVRO. § 273.6 is the criminal-enforcement bridge: once a DVRO is in place and the restrained party has notice (Fam. Code § 6291), any act violating the order is simultaneously a civil contempt (CCP § 1209) and a criminal offense (§ 273.6), and in some circumstances also Pen. Code § 166(a)(4) contempt of court.

**The bidirectional issue.** § 273.6 presumes a validly-entered underlying DVRO. A DVRO procured by a defective declaration (CAL_CCP_2015_5 audit) or by unmapped allegations (CAL_FAM_6203 audit) is vulnerable — and the § 273.6 prosecution is vulnerable with it. Defense in the criminal case can collaterally attack the civil order's validity; vacatur in family court can undercut a pending § 273.6 charge.

## Key doctrinal points

1. **§ 273.6(a) elements:** (a) valid DVRO in effect; (b) knowledge of the order by the restrained party (Fam. § 6291 notice); (c) intentional and knowing violation.
2. **Penalty escalation:** misdemeanor default; felony exposure on second offense within 7 years involving violence or a threat of violence (§ 273.6(e)).
3. **Parallel civil track:** CCP § 1209 contempt of court is the civil sanction pathway; Pen. § 166(a)(4) is the generic contempt criminal track.
4. **Collateral vulnerability:** a DVRO vacated post-issuance (e.g., for § 2015.5 declaration defect under Kulshrestha) removes the § 273.6 predicate prospectively; retroactive effect is a live issue.
