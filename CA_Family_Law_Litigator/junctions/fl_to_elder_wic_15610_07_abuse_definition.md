# Junction: Family Law § 3044 / § 6203 / § 6211 ↔ Elder Abuse WIC § 15610.07 / § 15610.30 / § 15657

## Doctrinal relationship — parallel authority

Welfare & Institutions Code § 15610.07 defines "abuse of an elder or dependent adult" broadly — including physical abuse, neglect, abandonment, isolation, abduction, and *financial* abuse (defined further at § 15610.30). Family Code § 6211 defines DVPA "domestic violence" and § 6203 defines "abuse" for DVPA purposes. Both frameworks cover overlapping conduct when the victim is both (a) in a DV-qualifying relationship under § 6211 and (b) an elder (65+) or dependent adult under § 15610.23 / § 15610.27.

**Parallel-authority relationship:** a single course of conduct can violate both frameworks simultaneously, triggering:
- Fam. Code § 3044 DV presumption in any related custody/visitation proceeding (if applicable);
- Fam. Code § 6320 enjoinable conduct supporting a DVRO;
- WIC § 15657 enhanced remedies (attorney's fees, pre-death pain-and-suffering damages, punitive damages) upon clear-and-convincing showing;
- Prob. Code § 859 double-damages + attorney's fees for property-taking in bad faith.

## Key doctrinal points

1. **WIC § 15610.07 coverage:** physical, neglect, abandonment, isolation, abduction, financial, + mental suffering. Financial abuse is defined separately at § 15610.30.
2. **WIC § 15610.30 financial abuse:** (a) taking, secreting, appropriating, obtaining, or retaining real or personal property of an elder or dependent adult for wrongful use, with intent to defraud, or by undue influence; (b) or assisting therein.
3. **WIC § 15657 enhanced remedies:** require clear-and-convincing evidence of recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice; attorney's fees and pre-death pain-and-suffering damages unlock.
4. **Prob. § 859 double damages:** for bad-faith takings of elder's property; independent of § 15657.
5. **DVPA overlap:** where the elder victim is also in a DV-qualifying relationship with the abuser, § 6203 abuse findings and § 6320 DVROs run alongside — neither preempts the other.
