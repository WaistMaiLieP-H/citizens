# Junction: Family Law DVRO / § 6203 ↔ Gov. § 13956 CalVCB denial criteria

## Doctrinal relationship — evidentiary bridge

Government Code § 13956 enumerates the CalVCB Board's *denial criteria* — the grounds on which a facially-eligible application may still be denied (applicant involved in crime; failure to cooperate with LE; safety or public-interest concerns; contribution to harm). A standing DVRO (or its denial) is probative evidence on the § 13956 factors but is not itself dispositive.

The bridge operates in three modes:
1. **DVRO-supports-eligibility:** a granted DVRO in the applicant's favor is strong evidence of victim status under § 13956's "bona fide victim" analysis.
2. **DVRO-denial-cuts-both-ways:** a DVRO *denied* (petition rejected) is evidence against the petitioner's § 13956 victim claim *only if* the denial was on the merits rather than procedural. A denial for procedural defect (service, § 2015.5 declaration defect) is not a merits denial.
3. **DVRO-against-applicant:** a DVRO issued *against* the CalVCB applicant bears on the "contributed to the crime" denial factor under § 13956(b) — but § 13956(b) is narrowly construed (Gonzales v. CalVCB progeny); mere adverse DVRO without finding that the applicant's conduct was a substantial factor in producing the victimization does not trigger § 13956(b).

## Key doctrinal points

1. **§ 13956(a) — "involvement in events":** denial where applicant's own involvement in the qualifying-crime events contributed. Requires a factual record, not mere DVRO issuance.
2. **§ 13956(b) — "failure to cooperate with LE":** denial where applicant refused to cooperate in reasonable investigation / prosecution. Cooperation is distinct from filing a DVRO.
3. **§ 13956(c) — "safety / public interest":** residual category; CalVCB must articulate specific basis.
4. **Regulatory overlay:** 2 CCR § 649.56 specifies the operational rules; pro-se applicants often lose at this stage due to documentation gaps rather than substantive ineligibility.
5. **Appeal vehicle:** CCP § 1094.5 administrative mandamus, with standards of review from CA_Administrative_Law_Specialist canonical build.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- On any CalVCB denial that cites a DVRO record, audit the cited DVRO for: (a) merits vs. procedural basis; (b) whether findings go to "involvement in events" or mere adverse posture; (c) § 2015.5 / § 6203 defects in the underlying order.
- Coordinate with CA_Victim_Compensation_Litigator on the § 13956 statutory construction and with CA_Administrative_Law_Specialist on the § 1094.5 writ vehicle (anti-SLAPP and APA hooks also available).
- Where the steward is the CalVCB applicant and adverse DVRO findings are being used against him, the § 6203 / § 2015.5 / § 6320(c) third-party-orchestration audits (from this Citizen's standards) apply directly to the § 13956(a) analysis.
