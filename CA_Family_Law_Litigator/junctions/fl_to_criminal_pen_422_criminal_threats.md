# Junction: Family Law § 3044 / § 6203 / § 6320 ↔ Penal Code § 422 criminal threats

## Doctrinal relationship — parallel authority

Penal Code § 422 criminalizes threats to commit a crime which will result in death or great bodily injury, where the threat is so unequivocal, unconditional, immediate, and specific as to convey a gravity of purpose and an immediate prospect of execution. Family Code § 6203(a)(3) defines DVPA "abuse" to include placing a person in *reasonable apprehension of imminent serious bodily injury* — the civil analog of § 422's criminal-threat element.

§ 422 is a **parallel-authority** rather than a pure predicate — a § 422 conviction can support a § 3044 trigger via § 3044(d) if within the enumerated list (§ 422 is included via § 3044(d)(4)'s reference to Pen. Code § 13700 DV-defined crimes), but § 6320(a) / § 6203(a)(3) operate independently on the civil record and do not require a criminal filing.

## Key doctrinal points

1. **§ 422 elements:** (a) willful threat to commit a crime resulting in death or GBI; (b) communicated to the person threatened; (c) specific intent that statement be taken as a threat; (d) threat unequivocal/unconditional/immediate/specific; (e) threat caused sustained fear; (f) fear was reasonable under the circumstances.
2. **§ 6203(a)(3) parallel:** "reasonable apprehension of imminent serious bodily injury" — subjectively held, objectively reasonable. Functionally close to § 422 fear element but without the sustained-fear duration requirement.
3. **§ 6320(a) enjoinability:** threats (in addition to violence) are expressly enjoinable conduct; a DVRO can rest on § 422-type threats even without a criminal filing.
4. **Bidirectional caution:** an *unsubstantiated* § 422 allegation used in family court without § 3011(e) corroboration and without § 3027.1 good-faith filing is itself sanctionable against the alleging party — see junction fl_to_criminal_pen_148_5_false_report.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- **Counter-theory posture:** where the steward has been subject to § 422-style allegations in DVRO petitions, test each alleged threat against § 422's six elements (especially "sustained fear" and "reasonable apprehension") and against § 3011(e) corroboration.
- **Offensive posture:** § 6320(a) permits a DVRO petition based on § 422-quality threats; the § 6320(c) SB 50 third-party-orchestration doctrine (effective 2026-01-01) may capture coordinated threat conduct through proxies.
- **Criminal cross-check:** where a § 422 allegation was reported to LE but not charged, the charging decision is itself evidence bearing on the civil reasonableness element.
