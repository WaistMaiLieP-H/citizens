# Junction: Family Law § 3011 ↔ Penal Code § 273a child endangerment

## Doctrinal relationship

Penal Code § 273a criminalizes willful infliction, permitting, or causing of unjustifiable physical or mental suffering of a child. A § 273a conviction or even arrest is frequently weaponized in family court to establish a § 3044 DV presumption or § 3011 best-interest disqualification against a parent.

**The evidentiary gap.** § 3044 requires a finding of DV; an arrest is NOT a finding. Family courts often conflate them. § 3011(e) specifically requires corroboration for uncorroborated allegations.

**The Vernen pattern.** Allegations of child endangerment have been raised against the steward without corresponding criminal charges being filed, sustained, or proven. Under § 3011(e), uncorroborated allegations require corroborating evidence before the court may rely on them. This junction makes explicit the evidentiary rule that family court must not convert an unproven criminal allegation into a § 3044 presumption.

## Key doctrinal points

1. § 273a requires willful conduct AND unjustifiable suffering OR circumstances likely to produce great bodily harm.
2. Arrest ≠ probable cause finding ≠ conviction. Each is a distinct evidentiary step.
3. Family Code § 3011(e)(1) (as amended) imposes corroboration requirements on DV/child-abuse allegations for § 3044 application.
4. Family Code § 3044(c) defines the evidentiary threshold required to trigger the presumption.
5. A § 273a conviction triggers § 3044; an acquittal does not preclude independent family court finding.
