# Junction: Family Law § 3118 evaluation ↔ Evid. Code § 1016 psychotherapist privilege

## Doctrinal relationship

Evidence Code § 1014 establishes psychotherapist-patient privilege. § 1016 creates an exception when the patient tenders his or her mental/emotional condition as an issue. A parent contesting custody does NOT automatically tender mental condition — the § 1016 exception attaches only to specific, affirmative pleadings.

**The scope rule.** § 3118 DV custody evaluations frequently demand broad access to a parent's mental-health history. That demand must be tested against § 1016:
- If the parent has affirmatively pleaded mental condition (e.g., rebutted a § 3044 presumption with a rehabilitation argument), § 1016 exception applies to THAT issue only.
- If the parent merely contests custody without tendering mental condition, § 1016 does NOT strip privilege.

**The Wiita compound problem.** In Case #2/#6/#14, Dr. Wiita's access to mental-health records predicated on a nominal § 3118 evaluation fails on two fronts:
1. The evaluation itself is procedurally defective (no MC-350, no qualifications).
2. The privilege waiver was assumed, not established — parent did not tender mental condition as § 1016 requires.

## Key doctrinal points

1. § 1014 privilege holder is the patient, not the therapist.
2. § 1016 exception is narrow — only the condition affirmatively tendered is stripped.
3. Filing a custody petition is NOT tendering mental condition (Manela v. Superior Court (2009)).
4. A court-ordered evaluation under § 3111/§ 3118 does not constitute a patient's voluntary waiver; the court must still find an exception.
5. The Tarasoff exception (duty to warn) is separate and narrow.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- When § 3118 evaluator demands mental-health records, demand § 1016 exception analysis: which condition has been tendered, by whom, in which pleading?
- Move to quash overbroad records demands that exceed the tendered condition.
- Where records were obtained without proper § 1016 analysis, move to exclude the evaluation product as fruit of a privilege breach.
- Pair with Evid. Code § 1024 (patient dangerous to self/others) only when evidentiary predicate is shown.
