# Junction: Family Law § 3027.1 ↔ CalVCB eligibility / Gov. § 13955

## Doctrinal relationship — eligibility conditional

Family Code § 3027.1 authorizes monetary sanctions against a party or attorney who *knowingly makes a false accusation of child sexual abuse or other child abuse* in a custody proceeding. Government Code § 13955 governs CalVCB victim-compensation eligibility — which in turn presumes the applicant is a *bona fide* victim of qualifying crime.

The junction runs bidirectionally:
1. **Defensive posture:** where a family-court allegation of abuse has been adjudicated (or preponderance-found) to be knowingly false under § 3027.1, that finding is evidence against CalVCB eligibility of the alleging party under Gov. § 13955 + 2 CCR § 649.56 "not a bona fide victim" / "contributed to the qualifying crime" grounds.
2. **Offensive posture:** where a child actually has been subjected to abuse *and* the abuse was leveraged in false-counter-allegations to discredit the actual victim, the *real* victim's CalVCB eligibility is not extinguished by the false allegations; the § 3027.1 finding against the abuser supports, rather than undermines, the real victim's claim.

## Key doctrinal points

1. **§ 3027.1 elements:** (a) accusation of child abuse, child sexual abuse, or falsely procured report of abuse; (b) made in a custody proceeding; (c) made with knowledge that accusation is false or with reckless disregard; (d) reasonable monetary sanctions authorized.
2. **Gov. § 13955 eligibility:** applicant must be a "direct victim" or "derivative victim" of a qualifying crime; 2 CCR § 649.56 excludes applicants whose own conduct materially contributed to the crime.
3. **CalVCB deference to court findings:** CalVCB may rely on family-court "knowingly false" findings under § 3027.1, but the deference runs on the specifics of the finding — generalized credibility observations are not a § 3027.1 finding.
4. **Reverse bar (derivative victim child):** a child whose actual victimization was *masked* by adult counter-allegations is not barred from derivative eligibility by the adult's conduct.
