# Junction: Family Law § 3118 evaluation ↔ Penal Code § 1368 competency

## Doctrinal relationship

Family Code § 3118 and CRC 5.220 govern DV custody evaluations within a family law proceeding. Penal Code § 1368 governs competency determinations in criminal proceedings. They are parallel frameworks — NOT interchangeable. A § 1368 competency evaluation (criminal) does not satisfy § 3118 (family law), and a § 3118 custody evaluation cannot be used to determine criminal competency.

**The Wiita violation (Case #2/#6/#14).** Dr. Wiita conducted what was represented as a family-law-adjacent competency assessment without the required MC-350 order, without the § 3118 DV-evaluator qualifications (Fam. Code §§ 1815–1816), and without the § 1368 doubt-declaration procedure. The evaluation is thus defective under BOTH frameworks: neither the family law nor the criminal standard was satisfied. The result is a § 3118/§ 1368 compound defect that voids the evaluation.

## Procedural bridge

1. If a parent's mental competency is at issue in a family law case, the court may appoint a § 730 evaluator under § 3111/§ 3118. This is NOT § 1368.
2. If the parent is a criminal defendant whose competency is in doubt, § 1368 procedure applies — a doubt declaration, suspended proceedings, and two court-appointed experts.
3. A family law judge may not invoke § 1368 procedure; a criminal judge may not invoke § 3118. Jurisdictional overlap is architectural, not substantive.
4. Where the same underlying mental-health record is relevant in both proceedings, Evid. Code § 1016 psychotherapist privilege exception analysis is required separately in each forum.

## Why this junction exists

The systematic pattern in the Vernen record is invocation of mental-health frameworks without the jurisdictional anchor that authorizes them. Dr. Wiita's evaluation was issued across both family and criminal dockets to manufacture incompetency findings. Declaring this junction forces the question: "Which statute authorizes this evaluation?" If the answer is "neither," the evaluation is void.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

— In drafting a challenge to any evaluation filed under § 3118, check whether the evaluator actually satisfies § 1815/§ 1816 qualifications. If not, the evaluation is inadmissible in family court.
— If the same evaluation appears in a criminal docket, flag the § 1368 procedural defects separately (no doubt declaration, no suspension of proceedings, only one expert).
— Do NOT treat a § 1368 competency finding as preclusive in family court. Competency for criminal trial ≠ parental fitness under § 3011.
