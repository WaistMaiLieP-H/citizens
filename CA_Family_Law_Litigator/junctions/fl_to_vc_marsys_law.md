# Junction: Family Law DVPA ↔ Marsy's Law (Cal. Const. Art. I § 28)

## Doctrinal relationship

Marsy's Law guarantees victim rights in California criminal proceedings — notice, presence, heard, restitution, privacy. DVPA proceedings under Family Code § 6320 et seq. are CIVIL, but they routinely intersect with criminal cases arising from the same conduct. Where a true victim exists, Marsy's Law protections apply in the criminal parallel track; they do NOT confer civil DVPA benefits.

**The inversion problem.** If the alleged "victim" in a civil DVPA proceeding is in fact the aggressor or orchestrator (as the Vernen record documents), Marsy's Law protections are being claimed by a non-victim. The civil DVPA frame does not confer Marsy's Law status — Marsy's Law requires an actual victim of a crime.

**The true-victim inversion in Case #10.** The steward is the actual victim in the criminal track (per the 04-23-01959 record) but has been forced into the defendant role in the family-law civil track. CalVCB denial (Case #10) then layers on top: the actual victim is denied compensation while the orchestrator asserts civil protective orders.

## Key doctrinal points

1. Marsy's Law applies to crime victims; it is a constitutional right in the criminal proceeding.
2. A civil DVPA petitioner is not ipso facto a "crime victim" — that requires a criminal case and a true-victim determination.
3. Fam. Code § 6228 gives DVPA litigants access to specified law-enforcement records. § 6228 is a discovery tool, not a victim-status determination.
4. CalVCB eligibility under Gov. Code § 13955 requires victim status of a qualifying crime — the CalVCB decision is a separate determination from family court findings.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- In cases where victim status is contested (true victim vs. orchestrator), trace the criminal track separately. Marsy's Law rights live in the criminal case, not the DVPA petition.
- If CalVCB has denied the true victim (as in Case #10), the CalVCB denial is appealable separately — do not rely on family-court findings to establish victim status for CalVCB purposes.
- Family-court findings of DV are NOT preclusive for Marsy's Law purposes in the criminal track.
