# Junction: Family Law parental rights ↔ WIC § 5350 LPS conservatorship

## Doctrinal relationship

If a parent is under a § 5350 LPS conservatorship, the conservator — not the parent — holds decision-making authority over residence, treatment, and certain personal rights. But § 5350 does NOT automatically terminate parental rights under the Family Code. Termination of parental rights is a separate proceeding under Family Code § 7820 et seq. or Welfare & Institutions Code § 366.26.

**The critical distinction.** LPS conservatorship limits the conservatee's personal autonomy; it does not by itself extinguish parental status. A conservatee parent retains Family Code rights unless and until a termination order issues.

**The Vernen anchor.** The conservatorship discovery (secret conservatorship since age 14) raises the inverse question: a conservatee's ability to be a party to family law proceedings. CCP § 372 / Prob. Code § 1871 address party-representation for conservatees. If the steward is himself under conservatorship, every document he signed in family court proceedings going back to 2009 must be re-examined for § 372/§ 1871 compliance.

## Preemption logic

Where a conflict arises between LPS conservator authority and Family Code orders (e.g., conservator wants to relocate a conservatee-parent across state lines; family court has issued § 3048 custody orders), § 5350 does NOT preempt family court jurisdiction. The two operate in parallel; neither extinguishes the other. The conservator must work within family court orders; family court must respect conservator authority over personal decisions within the conservatee's limits.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- When a party is under LPS conservatorship, confirm CCP § 372 guardian ad litem appointment.
- Do NOT treat conservatorship as a bar to parental rights absent a separate termination proceeding.
- If the steward himself has been under conservatorship, every family-law document requires re-validation of his legal capacity to sign/file.
- For the mother (potential late-onset dementia per project memory) — if conservatorship is filed, family-law orders she orchestrated must be re-examined for retroactive capacity defects.
