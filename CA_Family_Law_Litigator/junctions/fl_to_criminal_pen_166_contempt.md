# Junction: Family Law § 6320 DVRO ↔ Penal Code § 166 contempt

## Doctrinal relationship

Penal Code § 166(a)(4) criminalizes willful disobedience of a lawful court order. Family Code § 6388 specifically makes violation of a DVPA restraining order a misdemeanor. The two operate concurrently: a § 6320 order violation can be prosecuted under either § 166 (generic contempt) or § 6388 (DVPA-specific misdemeanor) or both.

**The 'lawful order' element.** § 166 requires the disobeyed order to be "lawful." An order entered without due process — no notice, no hearing, no § 2015.5-compliant supporting declaration — is not "lawful" for § 166 purposes, even if it is filed and enforceable until vacated.

**The Vernen § 6320 SB 50 finding.** Project memory records a § 6320 SB 50 third-party orchestration finding: that DVRO procurement was engineered through third parties rather than by the alleged victim. If a DVRO was procured through orchestration rather than authentic complaint, the "lawful order" element of § 166 fails, because the order was obtained by fraud on the court.

## Key doctrinal points

1. § 166(a)(4) is a misdemeanor; § 166(c) provides enhanced penalties for DVRO violation.
2. § 6388 (Family Code) is also a misdemeanor for DVPA violation — parallel track.
3. Willfulness requires knowledge of the order. Serving a defective order can fail the knowledge element.
4. Collateral attack on the order is generally barred in the contempt proceeding (must be pursued separately) — BUT the order's void-ness (as opposed to voidable-ness) may be raised.
5. Fraud on the court voids the order ab initio; § 166 cannot attach to a void order.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- When § 166/§ 6388 is charged, audit the underlying DVRO for fraud-on-court indicators: third-party orchestration, missing declaration, CCP § 2015.5 defect, ex parte procedural violations.
- Distinguish void orders (no § 166 liability) from voidable orders (contempt liability until vacated).
