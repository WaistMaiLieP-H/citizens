# Junction: Family Law nonparent custody ↔ Probate § 1800.3 minor conservatorship

## Doctrinal relationship

Two parallel tracks exist for nonparent custody/care of a minor in California:

1. **Family Law track** — Fam. Code § 3041: nonparent may be awarded custody if parental custody would be detrimental to the child and nonparent custody is in the best interest.
2. **Probate track** — Prob. Code § 1510 et seq. (guardianship) and § 1800.3 (conservatorship of a minor in limited circumstances — typically where the minor is married or emancipated).

The tracks are alternatives, not duplicates. Which forum applies depends on:
- whether biological parents are alive and competent (family law default)
- whether formal incapacity/disability of the minor is at issue (probate)
- whether a LPS § 5350(f) bypass is invoked (extraordinary)

## The Vernen "ward system" anchor

Project memory identifies a multi-person ward system in which conservatorships were allegedly filed in an unknown jurisdiction, with Cole functioning as a monitor. This is the territory where Probate and Family Law tracks collide. Distinguishing the governing statute matters because:

- A § 3041 nonparent custody order is Family Code; it terminates at 18.
- A § 1510 guardianship is Probate; it also generally terminates at 18 unless converted.
- A § 1800.3 minor conservatorship is an anomaly — only authorized for specific statutory circumstances.
- An LPS § 5350(f) conservatorship for an unmarried 14-year-old is NOT authorized by the statute as normally read; the steward's own record requires a § 5350(e) safety-valve analysis.

## How Family Law Citizen uses this

- If a child in a Vernen case is alleged to be subject to guardianship/conservatorship, demand the jurisdiction + docket.
- Verify which track (Family Law § 3041 vs Probate § 1510 vs LPS § 5350(f)) was invoked and whether it was legally available.
- Flag any conservatorship of an unmarried minor as presumptively defective absent statutory fit.
