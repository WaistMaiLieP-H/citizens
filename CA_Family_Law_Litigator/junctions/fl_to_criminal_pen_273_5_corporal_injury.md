# Junction: Family Law § 3044 ↔ Penal Code § 273.5 corporal injury

## Doctrinal relationship — predicate for DV presumption

Family Code § 3044 imposes a rebuttable presumption against awarding custody to a parent who has "perpetrated domestic violence" within the preceding five years. § 3044(d)(1) expressly incorporates Penal Code § 273.5 (corporal injury to a spouse, cohabitant, or dating partner) as one of the qualifying predicate offenses.

A § 273.5 conviction is conclusive for § 3044 trigger purposes; an arrest without conviction is NOT — and Fam. Code § 3011(e) imposes corroboration requirements before the family court can credit an uncorroborated allegation. § 3044 also requires the court to make specific written findings on the seven rebuttal factors before awarding custody notwithstanding the presumption.

**The audit bite.** Where the record shows § 3044 was "applied" without a qualifying § 273.5 conviction, without § 3011(e) corroboration, or without the seven § 3044(b) written rebuttal findings, the resulting custody order is vulnerable under § 473(d) (void order) and directly appealable.

## Key doctrinal points

1. **§ 273.5 elements:** (a) willful infliction upon a spouse, former spouse, cohabitant, former cohabitant, fiancé(e), dating-relationship partner, or parent of defendant's child; (b) of corporal injury resulting in a traumatic condition.
2. **§ 3044(d) qualifying predicates:** §§ 273.5, 243(e)(1), 243.4, 261, 262, 273d (child corporal injury), and others — a specific enumerated list.
3. **§ 3044(b) rebuttal factors:** (1) best-interest override; (2) completion of batterer's treatment; (3) drug/alcohol program if ordered; (4) parenting class if ordered; (5) probation compliance; (6) no further DV; (7) compliance with protective order.
4. **§ 3011(e) corroboration:** required before court may credit an uncorroborated allegation for best-interest decisioning.
5. **Written-findings requirement:** court must find each rebuttal factor in writing to override the § 3044 presumption — absence of written findings is reversible error (In re Marriage of Fajota (2014) 230 Cal.App.4th 1487).
