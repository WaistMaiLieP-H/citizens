## Part 1 — Anomaly narrative

The steward has reason to believe, based on case-file review and on-the-record representations by courthouse personnel, that during 2025 **Michael Hartmann was simultaneously designated a *petitioner* in one or more of the above cases and a *respondent* in one or more overlapping cases**, and that **filing fees were assessed against him in both capacities**.

This dual status is materially abnormal for the following reasons:

1. Under the Family Code and Judicial Council forms, a single individual is petitioner **or** respondent in a given family-law proceeding, not both (Fam. Code § 2331; CRC Rule 5.50; Judicial Council forms FL-100, FL-105, FL-120).
2. Where two or more related proceedings involve the same parties and subject matter, CRC **Rule 5.63** (related cases) and CRC **Rule 3.300** (related civil cases) obligate the court to consolidate or coordinate, not to maintain inconsistent party designations.
3. Charging a filing fee to a party **in both capacities** in the same underlying controversy is at odds with Gov. Code § 70611 and § 70612, which tie fees to party status (petitioner/respondent) on a per-case basis.
4. A dual petitioner/respondent status in overlapping dockets can be the mechanism by which:
   - A party is charged fees they are not liable to pay, masking or defeating a pending fee-waiver application (see Investigation 08);
   - Notices of hearing are split across dockets, so the self-represented party receives partial notice of proceedings affecting his own status and his child's custody;
   - Service of process requirements are manipulated (a "petitioner" is not served under CCP § 415.10 as a "respondent" is), creating due-process gaps;
   - Jurisdiction (including UCCJEA jurisdiction over the minor child Cole Hartmann) is flipped between counties on the strength of whichever status is asserted where.
