## How to use this template

1. Use this template in **either** of two postures:
   - **Posture A — Rebuttal by an accused DV perpetrator.** Movant is the party against whom a § 3044 presumption has attached and seeks custody. Movant must overcome the presumption by a preponderance of the evidence, factor by factor, under § 3044(b)(1)-(7).
   - **Posture B — Opposing rebuttal as the protected party.** Movant is the party protected by the § 3044 presumption and the accused is attempting to overcome it. Movant opposes the rebuttal.
2. The template defaults to **Posture B (opposition to rebuttal)**. Sections are labeled; flip the caption and argument structure for Posture A.
3. § 3044 is mandatory — if the predicate DV finding is within the preceding five years, the presumption is automatic; the court has no discretion to ignore it. *S.M. v. E.P.* (2010) 184 Cal. App. 4th 1249, 1265.
4. § 3044(f) requires the court, **when ruling contrary to the presumption**, to state its reasons in writing or on the record. Failure to make § 3044(f) written findings is reversible error. *Celia S. v. Hugo H.* (2016) 3 Cal. App. 5th 655, 663; *Jason P. v. Danielle S.* (2017) 9 Cal. App. 5th 1000.
5. The seven factors under § 3044(b) are **conjunctive in effect**: the perpetrator must satisfy factor (1) preponderance on overcoming the presumption, *and* the court must consider each of factors (2)-(7). This is the key analytical lever of the statute.
6. File as part of the responsive pleading to a § 3040 custody motion or as standalone opposition to a § 3044 rebuttal motion. Serve per CCP § 1005 unless the court has set an expedited schedule.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Family Code § 3044(a) creates a **rebuttable presumption** that an award of sole or joint legal or physical custody to a person who has perpetrated domestic violence within the previous five years is detrimental to the best interest of the child. The presumption is mandatory — when the predicate is satisfied, it attaches automatically. § 3044(a); *S.M. v. E.P.* (2010) 184 Cal. App. 4th 1249, 1265.

In this case, the § 3044 presumption has attached to **[ACCUSED PARTY]** because **[state the predicate: e.g., "on [date], this Court issued a DVRO under Fam. Code § 6200 et seq. against [party] finding abuse under § 6203" — OR — "on [date], [party] was convicted of a DV offense enumerated in Pen. Code § 6211 / § 6340.5"]**.

**[ACCUSED PARTY]** now seeks to overcome the presumption. Under § 3044(b), the presumption may be overcome only upon a showing, **by a preponderance of the evidence**, that the court's factor-by-factor analysis of seven enumerated factors favors the accused. As set out below, **[ACCUSED PARTY]** has not carried that burden on factor (1) and the court's consideration of factors (2) through (7) further defeats rebuttal.

## 2. STATUTORY FRAMEWORK

### 2.1 The presumption

Family Code § 3044(a):

> Upon a finding by the court that a party seeking custody of a child has perpetrated domestic violence within the previous five years against the other party seeking custody of the child, or against the child or the child's siblings, or against a person in subdivision (f) of Section 6211, there is a rebuttable presumption that an award of sole or joint physical or legal custody of a child to a person who has perpetrated domestic violence is detrimental to the best interest of the child[.]

### 2.2 The seven § 3044(b) factors

§ 3044(b) lists seven factors the court shall consider in determining whether the presumption has been overcome:

1. **(b)(1)** — Whether the perpetrator has demonstrated that giving sole or joint physical or legal custody of the child to the perpetrator is in the best interest of the child pursuant to Sections 3011 and 3020. As used in this paragraph, the child's health, safety, and welfare shall be the court's primary concern. **The perpetrator also must demonstrate that giving custody to the perpetrator does not conflict with Section 3020.**
2. **(b)(2)** — Whether the perpetrator has successfully completed a batterer's treatment program that meets the criteria outlined in subdivision (c) of Section 1203.097 of the Penal Code.
3. **(b)(3)** — Whether the perpetrator has successfully completed a program of alcohol or drug abuse counseling, if the court determines that counseling is appropriate.
4. **(b)(4)** — Whether the perpetrator has successfully completed a parenting class, if the court determines the class to be appropriate.
5. **(b)(5)** — Whether the perpetrator is on probation or parole, and whether the perpetrator has complied with the terms and conditions of probation or parole.
6. **(b)(6)** — Whether the perpetrator is restrained by a protective order or restraining order, and whether the perpetrator has complied with its terms and conditions.
7. **(b)(7)** — Whether the perpetrator has committed further acts of domestic violence.

### 2.3 § 3044(f) written-findings requirement

§ 3044(f)(1) requires:

> In any custody or restraining order proceeding in which a party has alleged that the other party has perpetrated domestic violence in accordance with the terms of this section, the court shall inform the parties of the existence of this section and shall give them a copy of this section prior to custody mediation in the case.

§ 3044(f)(2) requires:

> If the court determines that the presumption in subdivision (a) has been overcome, the court shall state its reasons in writing or on the record as to why paragraph (1) of subdivision (b) is satisfied and why the factors in paragraphs (2) to (7), inclusive, of subdivision (b), on balance, support the legislative findings in Section 3020.

Failure to make written findings when ruling contrary to § 3044 is reversible error. *Celia S. v. Hugo H.* (2016) 3 Cal. App. 5th 655, 663-664; *Jason P. v. Danielle S.* (2017) 9 Cal. App. 5th 1000, 1007; *In re Marriage of Fajota* (2014) 230 Cal. App. 4th 1487, 1499-1500.

§ 3020(c) announces: "When the policies set forth in subdivisions (a) and (b) of this section are in conflict, a court's order regarding custody or visitation shall be made in a manner that ensures the health, safety, and welfare of the child and the safety of all family members." Health and safety of the child and all family members is the overriding policy.

§ 3011 requires the court to consider (a)(1) the health, safety, and welfare of the child; (a)(2) any history of abuse; and (a)(5)(A) — critically — to state in writing or on the record its reasons for ruling contrary to the § 3044 presumption.

## Practice notes — HERALD

- The **seven-factor analysis is the frame** — do not collapse it into a general "rehabilitation" argument. Appellate panels reverse on absence of factor-by-factor treatment. *Celia S.* and *Fajota* are the baseline reversal cases.
- **Factor (7) is often case-dispositive.** One post-predicate DV incident generally defeats rebuttal. Be precise about dates.
- **§ 3044(f) written findings are mandatory when the court rules contrary to the presumption.** The protected party's strongest post-ruling appellate argument is absence-of-written-findings. Preserve the record — request the findings in open court and on the minute order.
- For Posture B rebuttals, ATTACK the predicate rebuttal evidence directly — challenge the program's § 1203.097(c) compliance, the counseling provider's qualifications, the completeness of compliance records.
- Pair § 3044 rebuttal with § 3011 best-interest analysis — the two statutes interlock under § 3044(b)(1).
