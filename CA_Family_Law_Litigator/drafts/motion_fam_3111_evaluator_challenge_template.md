## How to use this template

1. Fill every bracketed blank. If a blank is not applicable, delete it.
2. This motion attacks a § 3111 child-custody evaluation on **two independent tracks**:
   - **Track A — Qualifications defect.** The evaluator does not meet the Fam. Code § 3110.5 and CRC rule 5.225 qualifications (education, licensure, experience, domestic violence training, child sexual abuse training as applicable).
   - **Track B — Procedural defect.** The appointment, scope, or production of the report does not comply with § 3111, § 3118 (if abuse allegations are present), CRC rule 5.220, or rule 5.230.
3. The two tracks are **conjunctive** in effect — proving either one defeats the report's evidentiary weight. Plead both in the alternative where the facts support it.
4. Strike-and-exclude is the primary remedy. Lesser remedies: (i) order for a new § 3111 evaluation by a qualified evaluator; (ii) order limiting the scope of reliance on the report; (iii) Evid. Code § 720 preclusion from testimony.
5. File as part of responsive pleading, on the court's schedule for objections to the report, or as a pretrial motion in limine. Check local rules for § 3111 objection deadlines — many counties impose a 10-day window after report service for formal objections.
6. Where the evaluator's report will be admitted in evidence, preserve the objection at every stage: written motion (this template), oral argument at hearing, and — if admitted — timely objection at trial under Evid. Code § 353.

## 2. MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES

### 2.1 Introduction

A § 3111 child-custody evaluation carries substantial evidentiary weight in California custody proceedings. It is therefore qualified — by statute and by rule of court — with preconditions on (i) the evaluator's qualifications and (ii) the procedural conduct of the evaluation. Failure on either axis defeats the report's evidentiary foundation.

The report of **[EVALUATOR NAME]** dated **[DATE]** fails both axes. [Evaluator] [does not meet the § 3110.5 / rule 5.225 qualifications as detailed below / produced a report that violates the § 3111 / § 3118 procedural requirements as detailed below / both]. The report should be stricken, the evaluator precluded from testimony, and — if a court-connected evaluation is still needed — a new evaluator appointed who meets the statutory qualifications.

### 2.2 Statutory and rule framework

**Fam. Code § 3111 — Child-custody evaluation report.** § 3111(a) provides that in any contested custody or visitation proceeding the court may appoint a child-custody evaluator to conduct an evaluation. § 3111(b) requires that the report be in writing, be filed with the court and served on the parties, and remain confidential. § 3111(d) authorizes sanctions for dissemination outside the proceeding.

**Fam. Code § 3110.5 — Qualifications and training.** § 3110.5(a) makes rule 5.225 the governing standard for evaluator qualifications and training. § 3110.5(c) specifies education, licensure, and experience requirements.

**Fam. Code § 3118 — Evaluations in cases with child-abuse allegations.** § 3118 prescribes an additional protocol when allegations of child sexual abuse are made: qualifications in § 3118(b), scope in § 3118(c), methodology in § 3118(d)-(e), and specific cost allocation under § 3118(g). A § 3118 evaluation is distinct from — and more demanding than — a generic § 3111 evaluation.

**CRC rule 5.225 — Qualifications.** Rule 5.225(c) requires the evaluator to hold a qualifying license (listed in the rule) and have specified education and experience. Rule 5.225(d) requires domestic-violence training as outlined in Fam. Code § 1816. Rule 5.225(h) requires annual continuing-education credits. The Judicial Council publishes the required credentialing form (FL-325 Declaration of Court-Connected Evaluator) — its absence or material inaccuracy defeats the evaluator's prima facie qualification.

**CRC rule 5.220 — Court-ordered child-custody evaluations.** Rule 5.220 governs scope, method, and content of the evaluation: (c) scope of work; (d) parties' access to records; (e) minimum report elements; (f) information-gathering procedures.

**CRC rule 5.230 — Domestic violence training.** Rule 5.230 specifies the DV-training requirements an evaluator must complete before conducting an evaluation in a case with DV allegations.

**Evid. Code § 720 — Expert qualifications.** Expert testimony requires a foundational showing of "special knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education" sufficient to qualify the witness as an expert. Absence of § 720 foundation is a ground for exclusion. Evid. Code § 802 requires the court to pass on the expert's qualifications if challenged.

**Appellate authority.**

- *In re Marriage of Adams* (2020) 52 Cal. App. 5th 1, 11-15: § 3111 report's admission reversed where evaluator's qualifications and procedural compliance were not established; the court must exercise its gatekeeping function.
- *Leslie O. v. Superior Court* (2014) 231 Cal. App. 4th 1191, 1207: § 3118 protocol must be followed when abuse allegations are present; a generic § 3111 evaluation does not substitute.
- *S.Y. v. Superior Court* (2018) 29 Cal. App. 5th 324, 336-338: the court must ensure the evaluator satisfies rule 5.225 qualifications before relying on the report.

### 2.5 The report should be stricken; in the alternative, a new evaluation ordered

Where the evaluator's qualifications or the evaluation's procedural conduct defeat the foundation of a § 3111 report, the court has authority and obligation to strike the report and preclude related testimony. *In re Marriage of Adams*, 52 Cal. App. 5th at 15; *S.Y.*, 29 Cal. App. 5th at 338; Evid. Code §§ 720, 802.

Where the evaluation is still needed to resolve a disputed custody question, the remedy is a new evaluation by a properly qualified evaluator under a properly scoped order. § 3111; rule 5.220; rule 5.225.

## Practice notes — HERALD

- **Plead both tracks** where facts support. Qualifications alone can be cured by a new evaluation; procedural defects alone can be cured by a supplemental order. Both together often require striking and restart.
- **Evid. Code § 720 challenge** can be brought at trial even if the motion to strike was denied — the report may be filed but the evaluator's **testimony** is a separate § 720 gatekeeping question. Preserve the objection.
- **§ 3118 is the heightened protocol** for child-sexual-abuse cases. Many family courts use § 3111 when § 3118 is required. *Leslie O.* is the governing authority. Flag § 3118 override prominently when applicable.
- **NPPES / licensing-board cross-check** is powerful — the Department of Consumer Affairs license-search result is a business-records-compliant exhibit. Pull it the same day as filing and attach it in hard copy. If the evaluator's primary license state is not CA, that is often case-dispositive on § 3110.5 compliance.
- **In the Wiita case context** (FAMLAW-002), this template is the vehicle for the § 720 + § 3118 + § 730 three-layer attack. F-006-01 (NPPES primary-SC / secondary-CA licensing posture) plugs directly into Track A.1 and A.7.
