## How to use this template

1. Fill every bracketed blank. If a blank is not applicable, delete it.
2. Fam. Code § 271 is fee-shifting based on **conduct** — specifically conduct that "frustrates the policy of the law to promote settlement of litigation and, where possible, to reduce the cost of litigation by encouraging cooperation between the parties and attorneys." It is NOT need-based (contrast Fam. Code §§ 2030, 2032, 7605). The court considers all evidence of the parties' incomes and assets but there is **no requirement that the party requesting fees demonstrate financial need**. § 271(a).
3. Section 271 requires **notice and opportunity to be heard**. § 271(b). A surprise oral request at hearing is insufficient.
4. A § 271 award may not impose an unreasonable financial burden on the sanctioned party. § 271(a) (last sentence). Be prepared for the court to consider the sanctioned party's ability to pay even though "need" is not the movant's burden.
5. Best practice: cite specific instances in a declaration. *In re Marriage of Feldman* (2007) 153 Cal. App. 4th 1470 affirms § 271 sanctions based on itemized discovery-violation conduct. *In re Marriage of Davenport* (2011) 194 Cal. App. 4th 1507 affirms sanctions for uncivil and obstructionist conduct including vexatious communications.
6. File as a FL-300 Request for Order (CRC rule 5.92) unless local rules specify otherwise. Attach the FL-319 (Request for Attorney's Fees and Costs Attachment) and FL-158 (Supporting Declaration for Attorney's Fees and Costs Attachment) — the Judicial Council forms are mandatory for fee requests under some local rules even when the fee request is § 271 (conduct-based) rather than § 2030 (need-based). Confirm locally.

## 2. MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES

### 2.1 Introduction

Family Code § 271 authorizes the court to impose attorney's fees and costs as a **sanction** — not as need-based fees — when a party or attorney has engaged in conduct that "frustrates the policy of the law to promote settlement of litigation and, where possible, to reduce the cost of litigation by encouraging cooperation between the parties and attorneys." Fam. Code § 271(a). The sanction advances the statutory policy of encouraging cooperation. *In re Marriage of Corona* (2009) 172 Cal. App. 4th 1205, 1225-1226.

Movant respectfully requests an order under § 271 against [Opposing Party] in the amount of **$[AMOUNT]** for the following course of sanctionable conduct, detailed below.

### 2.2 Statutory standard

Fam. Code § 271(a) reads in relevant part:

> Notwithstanding any other provision of this code, the court may base an award of attorney's fees and costs on the extent to which the conduct of each party or attorney furthers or frustrates the policy of the law to promote settlement of litigation and, where possible, to reduce the cost of litigation by encouraging cooperation between the parties and attorneys. An award of attorney's fees and costs pursuant to this section is in the nature of a sanction. In making an award pursuant to this section, the court shall take into consideration all evidence concerning the parties' incomes, assets, and liabilities. The court shall not impose a sanction pursuant to this section that imposes an unreasonable financial burden on the party against whom the sanction is imposed. In order to obtain an award under this section, the party requesting an award of attorney's fees and costs is not required to demonstrate any financial need for the award.

§ 271(b) requires notice and opportunity to be heard before sanctions are imposed.

Appellate authority construing § 271:

- *In re Marriage of Feldman* (2007) 153 Cal. App. 4th 1470: sanctions affirmed for repeated disclosure and discovery violations that drove up litigation costs.
- *In re Marriage of Falcone & Fyke* (2012) 203 Cal. App. 4th 964, 995: § 271 sanctions appropriate for conduct including "obstructionist" litigation tactics, unwarranted motions, and baseless allegations.
- *In re Marriage of Davenport* (2011) 194 Cal. App. 4th 1507: sanctions affirmed for "uncivil and obstructionist" conduct including vexatious communications and baseless procedural challenges.
- *In re Marriage of Corona* (2009) 172 Cal. App. 4th 1205: § 271 requires evidence the sanctioned party's conduct actually increased the cost of litigation or frustrated settlement; generalized allegations are insufficient.

## Practice notes — HERALD

- § 271 is **conduct-based**, not need-based. Do not frame it as a § 2030/§ 2032 need request. If a concurrent § 2030 need-based request is also appropriate, file both but keep the theories separate.
- § 271 sanctions can be awarded to a pro per party; the lack of a retained attorney does not defeat recovery of costs and losses attributable to the opposing party's conduct, though "attorney's fees" for pro se time is a contested area — cite *Trope v. Katz* (1995) 11 Cal. 4th 274 (general rule against pro se attorney fees) and *Musaelian v. Adams* (2009) 45 Cal. 4th 512 (§ 128.7 context) for framing, and rely on documented out-of-pocket costs rather than imputed attorney rates unless well-supported.
- The best § 271 motions are itemized: date, act, cost impact, exhibit. Generalized "she is obstructionist" framing fails under *Corona*.
- Consider pairing § 271 with § 2030 (need-based), CCP § 128.5 (bad-faith actions), or CCP § 128.7 (frivolous filings) where the conduct fits multiple authorities. Each requires different pleading; do not collapse them.
