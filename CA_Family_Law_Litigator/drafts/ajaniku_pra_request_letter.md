## I. Background and basis for request

On **September 2, 2010**, an individual identified in the case file as **"Sala Ajaniku"** issued a child custody mediation recommendation in Case No. RF09456481 that was adopted by the Hon. Paul A. - Alameda County Superior Court Family Court Services court-connected ADR mediator panels
- Any general professional directory or scholarly publication index

Under **California Family Code § 3164(b)**, every child custody mediator "shall meet the minimum qualifications required of a counselor of conciliation as provided in Section 1815." The mandatory "shall" language has been in effect since the section's enactment by Stats. 1993 Ch. 219 § 116.87 (effective January 1, 1994).

**California Family Code § 1815(a)** requires every counselor of conciliation to have, at minimum:

1. A master's degree in psychology, social work, marriage/family/child counseling, or other behavioral science substantially related to marriage and family interpersonal relationships;
2. At least two years of experience in counseling or psychotherapy;
3. Knowledge of the California court system and family law procedures;
4. Knowledge of community resources for client referral;
5. Knowledge of adult psychopathology and family psychology;
6. Knowledge of child development, child abuse, effects of divorce on children, effects of domestic violence on children, and child custody research; and
7. **Training in domestic violence issues as described in Section 1816.**

**California Family Code § 1816** further requires every "evaluator" (which expressly includes § 3164 mediators per § 1816(a)(2)) to participate in basic continuing instruction in domestic violence and to complete:

- **16 hours of advanced training** within a 12-month period from an Administrative-Office-of-the-Courts-eligible provider, structured around 12 specific subject areas (§ 1816(d));
- **4 hours of annual updated training** thereafter (§ 1816(e));
- Compliance verified by certificates of completion documenting hours, dates, and provider (§ 1816(f)(5));
- **Notification to the court** of completed training, either by compliance with a local court rule or by attaching the completion certificates to the evaluator's filings (§ 1816(g)).

The above is not a procedural technicality. It is the substantive licensing floor that the Legislature has required of every California custody mediator since 1994. Sala Ajaniku's September 2, 2010 mediator role was therefore legally subject to all of the foregoing requirements as a matter of California statutory law.

Because no public-source record establishes that Sala Ajaniku possessed any of the required credentials at the time of her appointment, I am requesting the records below in order to determine whether the documentary support for her statutory qualifications exists in the institutional records of the appointing court.
