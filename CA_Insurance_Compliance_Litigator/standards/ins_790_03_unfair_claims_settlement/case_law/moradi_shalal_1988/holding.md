# Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Insurance Cos. (1988)
## § 790.03 Case Law — Controlling California Authority

**Citation:** Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos. (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287
**Court:** California Supreme Court

## Holding

The California Supreme Court eliminated the direct private right of action under INS §790.03 for third-party claimants (injured persons claiming against tortfeasor's insurer). The Court held that the UIPA was an administrative enforcement statute — it creates CDI authority, not private rights of action for individual claimants.

The Court overruled Royal Globe Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1979), which had recognized a third-party private right of action under §790.03.

---

## Key Doctrine

1. **No private right of action for third parties:** A person injured by an insured cannot sue the insurer directly under §790.03 for unfair claims settlement practices. The claim must be brought through CDI administrative enforcement or through common law bad faith (which requires a direct insurance relationship).

2. **First-party bad faith survives:** An insured (the policyholder) can still sue their own insurer for breach of the covenant of good faith and fair dealing in tort. §790.03 violations are admissible as evidence of bad faith in these first-party claims.

3. **CDI enforcement preserved:** California Department of Insurance retains full authority to investigate and enforce §790.03 violations through administrative proceedings, cease-and-desist orders, and penalties.
