# INS § 790.03 — Unfair Claims Settlement Practices: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Insurance_Compliance_Litigator / ins_790_03_unfair_claims_settlement
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Two-Track Threshold — Individual vs. General Business Practice

Section 790.03(h) requires that the insurer **knowingly commit** the practice **OR perform it with such frequency as to indicate a general business practice.** This two-track threshold matters:

**Track 1 — Knowing individual violation:** A single knowing violation of any §790.03(h)(1)-(16) practice triggers CDI enforcement authority and is evidence of bad faith in civil litigation.

**Track 2 — General business practice:** When a pattern of violations across multiple claims or claimants is documented, the "general business practice" element is satisfied — which opens the door to broader CDI enforcement, pattern-based civil claims, and class action theories.

For the Blue Shield prior authorization pattern: documenting denials across multiple claimants for similar procedures establishes the general business practice element. A single denial is evidence; ten denials using the same algorithm or the same non-physician reviewer is the pattern.

## The 16 Unfair Claims Settlement Practices — Applied to This Citizen's Cases

### Most Relevant to Blue Shield (Prior Authorization)

**§790.03(h)(1) — Misrepresenting policy provisions:**
If Blue Shield misrepresented what the policy required for prior authorization approval — for example, claiming a procedure was not covered when coverage existed — §790.03(h)(1) applies.

**§790.03(h)(4) — Failing to affirm or deny within reasonable time:**
UR decisions must be made within 5 business days (standard) or 72 hours (urgent) under §10123.135. Failure to meet these timelines is a §790.03(h)(4) violation — failing to affirm or deny within a reasonable time.

**§790.03(h)(5) — No good faith settlement when liability clear:**
Once the treating physician's recommendation is in and the procedure is indicated, liability under the policy is "reasonably clear." Refusing to authorize is a §790.03(h)(5) failure to effectuate prompt, fair settlement.

**§790.03(h)(8) — Settling on altered application:**
Blue Shield duplicate MRI pages in the prior auth file. If the record presented to the UR reviewer was altered or substituted without notice to the insured or treating physician, §790.03(h)(8) applies directly.

**§790.03(h)(13) — No explanation of denial basis:**
The denial must include a clear and concise explanation of the basis in the policy, the clinical reasons, and the criteria used. A denial letter without this information is a §790.03(h)(13) violation.

### Most Relevant to RedJag / Vehicle Insurance

**§790.03(h)(1):** Misrepresenting what the policy covered regarding the vehicle (GAP insurance, total loss coverage, theft/stripped condition).

**§790.03(h)(13):** If any vehicle insurance claim was denied without a clear explanation of the policy basis, §790.03(h)(13) applies.

### Most Relevant to Any Insurer-Initiated Adverse Action

**§790.03(h)(14) — Advising claimant not to get an attorney:**
Any insurer representative who told Michael Hartmann or any related party not to consult an attorney committed a §790.03(h)(14) violation. This is independently actionable as bad faith regardless of the merits of the underlying claim.

**§790.03(h)(15) — Misleading about SOL:**
If any insurer misrepresented the time within which a claim had to be filed or a suit commenced, §790.03(h)(15) applies. This is a direct civil bad faith trigger.

## The Moradi-Shalal Limitation and Its Current Scope

**Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos. (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287:** Eliminated the private right of action directly under §790.03 for third-party claimants. This case overruled Royal Globe Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1979) 23 Cal.3d 880.

**What remains after Moradi-Shalal:**
- CDI administrative enforcement under §790.03 (full authority preserved)
- First-party bad faith tort (insured vs. their own insurer): §790.03 violations are admissible as evidence of bad faith
- No direct private cause of action for third parties (injured claimants against tortfeasor's insurer)

**For this Citizen's cases:** Michael Hartmann is the insured under the Blue Shield policy — making this a first-party claim where Moradi-Shalal's limitation does not bar recovery. §790.03 violations are directly relevant evidence of bad faith in the first-party civil action.

**Steward must confirm:** The ERISA preemption question — if Blue Shield was providing coverage under an employer ERISA plan, state bad faith claims are preempted and must proceed under ERISA §502(a).
