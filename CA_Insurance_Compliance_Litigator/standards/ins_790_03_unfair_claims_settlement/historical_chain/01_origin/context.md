# INS § 790.03 — Historical Chain: Origin

**Stage:** 01 — Origin
**Year:** 1947 — California UIPA; §790.03(h) unfair claims settlement practices specifically expanded and refined through subsequent amendments (1972 NAIC model act, ongoing)

---

## The Wound

The 16 practices enumerated in §790.03(h) are not theoretical. Each represents a documented abuse pattern that insurers had developed and refined over decades:

**The delay pattern:** §790.03(h)(2), (3), (11) — Insurers discovered that delay is profitable. Claimants who needed immediate funds would settle for less. Claimants with terminal illnesses might die before collecting. The playbook was systematic delay: require preliminary reports, then require identical formal proof, while the clock ran.

**The misrepresentation pattern:** §790.03(h)(1), (7), (8) — Misrepresenting what the policy covered, what the claimant was entitled to, and settling on the basis of altered documents were documented insurer practices. The altered application practice — §790.03(h)(8) — was particularly egregious: the insurer would present the UR reviewer or adjuster with a version of the application that differed from what the insured signed.

**The litigation compulsion pattern:** §790.03(h)(6) — Offering substantially less than the policy required, then settling for policy limits after the insured spent years in litigation, was an explicit strategy: most claimants cannot sustain litigation; the ones who can will settle for less. The practice was enumerated and prohibited when its systematic use became documented.

**The attorney suppression pattern:** §790.03(h)(14) — Telling claimants not to consult attorneys was a direct interference with the insured's ability to understand their rights. It required express prohibition because it was a specific documented practice.

---

## The 1972 NAIC Model Act

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners developed a model Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act in 1972, which many states adopted. California's §790.03(h) tracks this model closely — the enumeration of specific practices was a national insurance regulatory project, not just a California response.

The AI prohibition in §10123.135(j)(2) (added 2024) represents the most recent addition to this tradition: each wave of insurance technology creates new abuse patterns; the regulatory framework responds by enumerating the specific prohibited applications.

---

## The Promise

California promised that insurance claims would be processed honestly — that the insurer who collected premiums on the promise of protection would honor that promise with prompt, good-faith handling of claims. The 16 enumerated practices are the list of ways that promise was systematically broken before the UIPA stopped it. Each practice that remains in the statute means the Legislature has determined the pattern of abuse is sufficiently persistent to require ongoing express prohibition.
