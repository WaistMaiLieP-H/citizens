# Rule — Ins. Code § 790.03 Unfair Claims Settlement Practices

**Standard ID:** ins_790_03_unfair_claims_settlement
**Authority:** Cal. Ins. Code § 790.03

## Core Rule

Section 790.03 defines specific conduct as unfair methods of competition and unfair/deceptive acts in the insurance business. Subdivision (h) enumerates 16 specific unfair claims settlement practices. Violation requires:
1. The insurer (or person in the insurance business) committed one or more of the enumerated acts; AND
2. The conduct was performed either knowingly OR with such frequency as to indicate a general business practice.

## Critical Procedural Constraint — Moradi-Shalal Wall

**Per Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287:** Section 790.03 does NOT create a private right of action. Only the California Department of Insurance (CDI) may enforce § 790.03 through administrative proceedings. A private plaintiff alleging insurer misconduct must proceed on a separate common-law bad faith tort theory (see `ins_bad_faith_brandt_gruenberg`).

The Moradi-Shalal wall does NOT mean insurer conduct is unreachable by private plaintiffs — it means the VEHICLE is different:
- CDI route: §790.03 → CDI complaint → administrative fine/license action
- Private route: bad faith tort (Gruenberg standard) → civil action → policy benefits + Brandt fees + §3294 punitive damages
- Both routes run in parallel per §790.09

## The 16 Enumerated Practices (§790.03(h))

| Act | Prohibited Conduct |
|---|---|
| (h)(1) | Misrepresenting pertinent facts or policy provisions to claimant |
| (h)(2) | Failing to acknowledge and act reasonably promptly on communications |
| (h)(3) | Failing to implement reasonable investigation standards |
| (h)(4) | Failing to affirm or deny coverage within reasonable time after proof of loss |
| (h)(5) | Not attempting in good faith to effectuate prompt, fair settlement where liability is reasonably clear |
| (h)(6) | Compelling insureds to litigate by offering substantially less than amounts ultimately recovered |
| (h)(7) | Settling for less than reasonable person would believe entitled, based on advertising |
| (h)(8) | Settling on basis of altered application without insured's knowledge |
| (h)(9) | Failing to inform insured of coverage basis after payment |
| (h)(10) | Appealing arbitration awards to compel settlement below award |
| (h)(11) | Delaying via duplicate preliminary/formal proof of loss submissions |
| (h)(12) | Failing to settle one coverage portion to influence another |
| (h)(13) | Failing to provide reasonable explanation of denial basis |
| **(h)(14)** | **Directly advising claimant not to obtain an attorney** |
| **(h)(15)** | **Misleading claimant as to applicable statute of limitations** |
| (h)(16) | Delaying AIDS/ARC benefits beyond 60 days to investigate preexistence |

## Steward Application Notes

**Communications fraud pattern:** §790.03(h)(14) (advising not to get attorney) and (h)(15) (SOL misrepresentation) are particularly relevant. If Christina or others posing as insurance contacts advised Michael not to retain counsel or gave false limitation dates, each such act is a specific §790.03(h) violation — reportable to CDI.

**Blue Shield prior auth:** §790.03(h)(2) (failure to promptly acknowledge), (h)(3) (no reasonable investigation standards), (h)(4) (failure to affirm/deny within reasonable time), (h)(13) (failure to explain denial basis) — all potentially applicable.

**"General business practice" prong:** Pattern evidence (multiple denials, repeated delay) is as sufficient as knowing intent. Prior CDI complaint history of the insurer is discoverable and relevant.
