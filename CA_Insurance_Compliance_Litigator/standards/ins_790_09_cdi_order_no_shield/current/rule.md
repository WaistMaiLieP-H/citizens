# Rule — Ins. Code § 790.09 CDI Order Provides No Shield

**Standard ID:** ins_790_09_cdi_order_no_shield
**Authority:** Cal. Ins. Code § 790.09

## Core Rule

A CDI cease-and-desist order issued under the Unfair Practices Act (§§790–790.10) does NOT relieve or absolve the insurer from:
1. **Administrative license action** — CDI can separately proceed against the insurer's license or certificate
2. **Civil liability** — the civil bad faith tort action (Gruenberg/Tomaselli track) is independent and unaffected
3. **Criminal penalty** — criminal prosecution for underlying fraudulent conduct is independent and unaffected

All three tracks run in parallel. A CDI enforcement action is additive to, not a substitute for, civil and criminal liability.

## What §790.09 Is NOT

§790.09 does NOT create a private right of action under §790.03. Moradi-Shalal (1988) confirmed that §790.03 enforcement is CDI-only. §790.09 speaks to what happens after CDI acts — it ensures the CDI order does not operate as a settlement or release of other claims.

## The Multi-Track System

| Track | Authority | Who Initiates | What it Achieves |
|---|---|---|---|
| CDI Administrative | §790.03 | CDI (complaint-triggered) | Fine, license suspension/revocation, cease-and-desist |
| Civil bad faith tort | Gruenberg/Tomaselli | Private plaintiff | Policy benefits + Brandt attorney fees + consequential damages + §3294 punitive damages |
| Criminal | Pen. §550 / 18 USC §1347 | DA / US Attorney | Fine, imprisonment |
| License Revocation (separate) | §790.09 expressly named | CDI | Revocation/suspension independent of cease-and-desist |

## Practical Application

If CDI issues a cease-and-desist order against Blue Shield for §790.03(h) violations in the prior authorization denials:
- That order runs against Blue Shield's license
- It does NOT settle, release, or affect Michael's bad faith tort claim for policy benefits + Brandt fees
- It does NOT prevent criminal referral for fraud if the denial was knowing
- It does NOT prevent a parallel CDI license revocation proceeding

The insurer cannot point to CDI settlement or consent order as a defense to civil liability.
