# Historical Chain — § 790.09 Origin (1947)

§790.09 was enacted as part of the original 1947 California Unfair Practices Act. It reflects a deliberate legislative choice: administrative enforcement of insurance market conduct is additive to, not a replacement for, individual civil and criminal liability.

The provision mirrors the NAIC model act structure, which was designed to satisfy the McCarran-Ferguson "state regulation" requirement while explicitly preserving civil and criminal remedies that existed under state law before the model act's adoption.

The key concern in 1947: if CDI issued an enforcement order, would insurers argue the order settled all related claims? §790.09 answers: no. The order is directed at market conduct going forward. Retrospective harm to claimants is addressed through civil and criminal channels.

Post-Moradi-Shalal (1988), this provision became structurally critical because it is now the only explicit statutory text confirming that the Moradi-Shalal limitation (CDI-only §790.03 enforcement) does not convert CDI administrative resolution into a release of civil/criminal claims.
