# Historical Chain — § 1861.02 Origin (Proposition 103, 1988)

## Pre-103 Insurance Landscape (1970s–1988)

California auto insurance was regulated under an "open competition" model — insurers filed rates and they became effective automatically. CDI reviewed rates only reactively. No mandatory rating factor hierarchy existed. Insurers used dozens of underwriting factors, with geographic location (often a proxy for race and income) being the dominant driver of premium differences.

Consumer advocates in the 1980s documented that drivers in South Los Angeles and other majority-minority urban areas paid two to three times more for the same auto liability coverage as drivers in white suburban neighborhoods with comparable driving records. The system was structurally discriminatory, though individual insurers' practices were difficult to challenge.

## Proposition 103 (November 8, 1988)

Consumer activist Harvey Rosenfield drafted Proposition 103 as a direct voter initiative. Key provisions:
- Prior approval requirement for rate changes
- Mandatory rating factors in decreasing order of importance (§1861.02(a))
- Good Driver Discount mandate (§1861.02(b))
- Prohibition on prior insurance absence alone as criterion (§1861.02(c))
- Rate rollback to 1987 levels
- CDI Commissioner made subject to election (instead of appointment)

The insurance industry spent an estimated $60 million opposing Proposition 103 in 1988. Voters approved it 51-49%. Immediately after passage, insurers filed 22 lawsuits challenging its constitutionality.

## Calfarm Insurance Co. v. Deukmejian (1989) 48 Cal.3d 805

The California Supreme Court upheld the core provisions of Proposition 103, including the mandatory rating factor hierarchy and the Good Driver Discount requirement. The Court struck only the rate rollback provision as requiring a guaranteed reasonable profit (constitutional takings concern), which the Legislature subsequently addressed through a CDI administrative process.

## Operative Date

§1861.02(e): The section became operative November 8, 1989 — one year after voter approval, to allow CDI to adopt implementing regulations and insurers to file conforming rate schedules.

## Current State

§1861.02 is the permanent statutory implementation of Proposition 103's core rate regulation provisions. As a voter initiative, it can be amended only by a 2/3 legislative supermajority or a subsequent voter initiative. CDI's rate approval authority and the mandatory rating factor hierarchy have been continuously operative since 1989.
