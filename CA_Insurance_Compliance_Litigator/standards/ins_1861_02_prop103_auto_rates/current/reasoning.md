# Reasoning — Why § 1861.02 Exists (Proposition 103)

## The Problem Before 1988

Before Proposition 103, California auto insurance rates were not subject to meaningful prior approval or mandatory rating factor requirements. Insurers used a wide variety of factors to set rates, some of which correlated poorly with actual driving risk:
- Geographic location (ZIP code) — which correlated with race and income
- Prior insurance history — which trapped the uninsured in a permanent penalty
- Credit scores — not directly related to driving safety
- Vehicle type and age — fine for property damage coverage, but not always for liability

The result: drivers in urban areas, lower-income drivers, and drivers without prior insurance history paid dramatically higher rates for comparable risk profiles. The insurance system was pricing demographics, not driving.

## Proposition 103 — Voter Initiative (November 8, 1988)

Proposition 103 was a voter initiative that fundamentally restructured California auto insurance regulation:
- Required CDI prior approval of all rate changes (any rate increase requires Commissioner review and approval)
- Established the mandatory rating factor hierarchy (§1861.02(a)) — driving record is primary; everything else is secondary
- Created the Good Driver Discount mandate (§1861.02(b)) — insurers cannot refuse to serve qualified good drivers
- Prohibited use of prior insurance absence alone as a disqualifier (§1861.02(c))
- Rolled back rates to 1987 levels as a starting point

The initiative passed despite fierce industry opposition and litigation challenging its constitutionality. The California Supreme Court upheld Proposition 103 in Calfarm Insurance Co. v. Deukmejian (1989) 48 Cal.3d 805.

## The Connection to Yo-Yo Financing and Forced Insurance

The forced insurance schemes associated with yo-yo financing (financing conditionally extended, then revoked to force re-signing at worse terms, often with forced add-on products) exploit the information asymmetry Proposition 103 was designed to correct. When a buyer has "no prior insurance history" (because they are a first-time buyer or between vehicles), a dealer-arranged insurer can argue they fall outside the Good Driver Discount — but §1861.02(c) says that absence of prior coverage alone cannot be used. If that distinction was used to justify inflated rates on the forced GAP product, it is a direct §1861.02 violation.

## Current State

§1861.02 remains in effect as enacted by Proposition 103 (a voter initiative, which cannot be amended by the Legislature without a 2/3 supermajority or separate voter approval). CDI maintains rate approval authority and has issued extensive regulations under §1861.02(a)(4). The Good Driver Discount program is actively enforced.
