# Holding — Harlick v. Blue Shield of California

**Case:** Harlick v. Blue Shield of California, 671 F.3d 1108 (9th Cir. 2012)
**Verification Status:** UNVERIFIED — from training knowledge; citation format consistent with Ninth Circuit opinions but not confirmed against official reporter

## The Rule This Case Stands For

An insurer that applies more restrictive UR/UM criteria to one category of covered benefits than to analogous categories violates the statutory parity requirement. The insurer cannot use "medical necessity" determinations as a mechanism to discriminate between benefit categories.

## Why It Matters for Blue Shield Cases

Harlick establishes that Blue Shield has a documented, court-recognized pattern of applying inconsistent and more restrictive UR criteria to specific benefit categories. This is directly relevant to any Blue Shield prior authorization claim:

- If Blue Shield denied or delayed a benefit using criteria that would not have been applied to an analogous medical/surgical benefit, the denial may violate parity law
- If the UR criteria Blue Shield applied were not disclosed, not clinically grounded, or not developed with physician input, each element is a §10123.135(f) violation
- Pattern evidence: a prior judicial finding of discriminatory UR criteria strengthens a bad faith claim based on systematic denial practices

## Limitation

This is a federal ERISA/parity case, not a state §10123.135 case. The holding does not directly establish §10123.135 liability. It is relevant as pattern evidence and as corroboration of Blue Shield's UR practices in the period relevant to steward cases.
