# Amendment Context — §10123.135(j) AI Prohibition

**Enacted:** SB 1120 (2024), effective January 1, 2025
**The Wound:** AI systems issuing mass medical necessity denials without physician review — same harm as non-physician reviewers in 1990s, now at algorithmic scale

---

## The 2024 Pattern That Built §10123.135(j)

As AI and algorithmic systems became commercially available for health insurance UR/UM functions, insurers adopted them rapidly. The automation economics were compelling: a single algorithm could review thousands of prior authorization requests per day, applying denial criteria at scale, with no physician labor cost.

The structural harm was identical to the 1990s non-physician reviewer problem — but now operating at machine speed:

- AI systems were denying claims based on statistical patterns across group datasets, not individual clinical circumstances
- Physicians were rubber-stamping algorithm outputs without substantive independent review
- Denial rates increased at multiple major insurers after AI adoption
- Specific denial pattern: denials issued for services previously approved, using algorithm-detected "criteria deviation" without physician clinical judgment

A 2023 ProPublica investigation documented large-scale algorithmic denials at major health insurers, with reviewing physicians spending an average of 1.2 seconds per denial — physically impossible to constitute meaningful review.

## What the Legislature Did in 2024

SB 1120 added subdivision (j) to §10123.135. The Legislature made two choices:

1. **Regulation:** AI tools may be used for UR/UM support functions — but must comply with 11 specific requirements (§10123.135(j)(1)(A)–(K))
2. **Absolute prohibition:** AI tools may not make the medical necessity determination — ever (§10123.135(j)(2))

The absolute prohibition in (j)(2) is worded to close the rubber-stamp loophole: the determination of medical necessity must be made by a licensed physician reviewing the specific requesting provider's recommendation, the specific insured's history, and the specific individual clinical circumstances.

## The Promise Made in 2025

§10123.135(j)(2) makes the same promise §10123.135(e) made in 2000, at the scale of the 2024 AI context:

> No machine will decide whether you receive medical care. A qualified physician who has actually reviewed your specific case will make that decision.

The legislative history of SB 1120 confirms the Legislature specifically intended to reach practices where a physician technically "reviews" the AI output but does so perfunctorily without independent clinical analysis.
