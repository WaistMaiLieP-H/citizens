# INS § 790 — Unfair Trade Practices Purpose: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Insurance_Compliance_Litigator / ins_790_unfair_practices_purpose
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## What § 790 Does

Section 790 is the purpose clause for the California Unfair Insurance Practices Act (UIPA), INS §§790-790.10. It does three things:

1. **Anchors California regulation to federal authority:** The federal McCarran-Ferguson Act (Public Law 15, 1945) returned insurance regulation to the states. California §790 expressly implements this — California's UIPA is the state's exercise of the regulatory authority Congress reserved for states.

2. **Defines the scope:** The article regulates "trade practices in the business of insurance" — broad enough to cover every practice by an insurer in conducting its business, from marketing to claims handling.

3. **Authorizes two regulatory tracks:** The article covers both (a) "unfair methods of competition" (insurer-to-insurer competitive practices) and (b) "unfair or deceptive acts or practices" (insurer-to-policyholder or claimant practices). The second category — unfair and deceptive acts — is what §790.03 enumerates and what this Citizen's cases implicate.

## The §790 Framework

§790 → §790.03 (enumerated prohibited acts) → §790.09 (CDI enforcement doesn't preclude civil/criminal liability) → Insurance bad faith tort (first-party) or CDI administrative action.

**The Moradi-Shalal rule (UNVERIFIED — steward must confirm):** The California Supreme Court in Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos. (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287 held that there is NO private right of action directly under §790.03 for third-party claimants. This overruled Royal Globe Ins. Co. v. Superior Court (1979). The result: §790.03 violations do not directly create a civil cause of action for non-insured claimants. However:
- First-party insureds can sue for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, and §790.03 violations are evidence of bad faith
- CDI can still bring administrative enforcement for §790.03 violations
- Criminal penalties remain available

**Current scope of Moradi-Shalal:** Courts have carved back some of its breadth. Steward must verify current status of third-party bad faith claims in California before relying on Moradi-Shalal analysis.
