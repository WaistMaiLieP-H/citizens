# United States v. South-Eastern Underwriters Ass'n (1944) + McCarran-Ferguson Act (1945)

**Primary citation:** United States v. South-Eastern Underwriters Ass'n, 322 U.S. 533 (1944) — VERIFIED SCOTUS
**Legislation:** McCarran-Ferguson Act, Pub. L. 79-15, 59 Stat. 33 (1945) — VERIFIED

---

## Holding / Effect

South-Eastern Underwriters: Insurance is interstate commerce subject to federal regulation under the Commerce Clause. This threatened to federalize all insurance regulation, displacing state regulators.

McCarran-Ferguson response: Congress returned regulatory authority to states. States that regulate insurance are exempt from federal antitrust law as applied to insurance; states that fail to regulate lose the exemption.

California §790's reference to this Act is the express statutory acknowledgment: California regulates insurance because the federal-state bargain of 1945 requires it, and California accepted that obligation.

**Relevance to California cases:** An insurer operating in California cannot escape California UIPA regulation by claiming federal preemption — McCarran-Ferguson explicitly preserves state authority. Exception: ERISA §514 preempts state insurance regulation as applied to ERISA benefit plans (the deemer clause analysis).
