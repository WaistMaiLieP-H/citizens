# INS § 790 — Historical Chain: Origin

**Stage:** 01 — Origin
**Year:** 1947 — California UIPA enacted following federal McCarran-Ferguson Act (1945)

---

## The Wound — Federal Abdication Creates State Obligation

The history of §790 begins at the federal level. Before 1944, insurance was regulated exclusively by states. In 1944, the U.S. Supreme Court decided United States v. South-Eastern Underwriters Ass'n, 322 U.S. 533 (1944), holding that insurance was interstate commerce subject to federal regulation, which threatened to federalize insurance oversight entirely.

Congress responded with the McCarran-Ferguson Act of 1945 (Public Law 15): it returned regulation of insurance to the states, with the condition that states must regulate. If states failed to regulate, federal antitrust law would apply. The Act created a federal-state bargain: states get to regulate insurance; in exchange, they must actually regulate it.

California enacted its UIPA in 1947 — directly in response to this federal mandate. Section 790's reference to "the Act of Congress of March 9, 1945 (Public Law 15)" is the statutory acknowledgment of this bargain: California regulates because Congress told it to, and California is the regulatory sovereign for insurance practices within its borders.

---

## Why the UIPA Was Needed Beyond the Federal Mandate

The practices that §790.03 prohibits — misrepresenting policy terms, delaying claims, refusing to settle, misleading claimants about their SOL — were documented patterns before 1947. The insurance industry, operating across state lines, had developed systematic practices that worked against policyholders. No single state had jurisdiction over the full scope of an insurer's operations.

The UIPA gave California the regulatory tool to define and prohibit these practices statewide, backed by CDI administrative enforcement and the implicit threat of losing the McCarran-Ferguson exemption from federal antitrust law.

---

## The Promise

California promised policyholders that the business of insurance within this state would be conducted fairly. The insurer who misrepresents terms, delays claims, and misleads claimants breaks this promise — and faces the regulatory machinery that the state-federal bargain of 1945-1947 created to enforce it.
