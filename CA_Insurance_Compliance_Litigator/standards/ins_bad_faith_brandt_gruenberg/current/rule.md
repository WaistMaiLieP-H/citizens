# Rule — Insurance Bad Faith Tort (Gruenberg/Brandt)

**Standard ID:** ins_bad_faith_brandt_gruenberg
**Authority:** Common law implied covenant — Gruenberg v. Aetna (1973), Brandt v. Superior Court (1985)

## Core Rule — Elements of Bad Faith Tort

1. **The policy:** Plaintiff was insured under a valid insurance policy
2. **The benefit:** The insurer was obligated to pay or provide a benefit under the policy
3. **The denial/delay:** The insurer denied or delayed payment of the benefit
4. **Unreasonableness:** The denial or delay was unreasonable — no reasonable basis existed for the denial at the time it was made, AND the insurer knew or recklessly disregarded that there was no reasonable basis for denial (objective + subjective components)
5. **Harm:** The insured suffered damage as a result of the unreasonable denial

## Recoverable Damages

| Damage Type | Standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Policy benefits | Breach of contract | Basic recovery — what was owed |
| Consequential damages | Tort (Gruenberg) | Foreseeable damages flowing from bad faith denial |
| **Brandt attorney fees** | Compensatory (Brandt) | Fees to recover policy benefits — separate jury determination |
| Emotional distress | Tort (Gruenberg) | Available in bad faith tort; not available in contract |
| **Punitive damages** | §3294 — malice/oppression/fraud | Requires C&C showing; separate from Brandt fees |

## The Reasonableness Standard

"Unreasonable" means: no legitimate reasonable dispute existed as to coverage or liability. The court evaluates reasonableness at the time of the denial — not retroactively. Evidence that the insurer had no reasonable basis is distinct from evidence that the insured's position was correct.

A denial can be: (a) wrong (insurer loses contract claim) but not unreasonable (no bad faith); or (b) wrong AND unreasonable (bad faith + contract claim); or (c) unreasonable even if insurer's position was ultimately vindicated (bad faith if no reasonable basis at time of denial).

## Brandt Fee Calculation

Brandt fees require the plaintiff's attorney to:
1. Track time spent on the contract/insurance claim (bad faith + policy benefits) separately from other claims
2. Submit a declaration with time records at trial or as part of damages phase
3. Jury determines Brandt fee amount as an element of compensatory damages

Brandt fees are NOT recoverable under a §1021.5 or other fee-shifting statute — they are a damages element specific to insurance bad faith. If the case settles, Brandt fees must be specifically addressed in the settlement.

## Application to Cases

**Blue Shield prior auth:** Was the denial unreasonable at the time? Medical necessity determination + plan's own criteria + treating physician recommendation = evidence of no reasonable basis. If plan had no legitimate coverage dispute, the tort applies.

**Communications fraud / SIM swap:** If insurer received intercepted or misdirected communications and denied on that basis, was the investigation method reasonable? An investigation that relied on fraudulent inputs is not ipso facto unreasonable — but failure to follow up direct insured communications may be.
