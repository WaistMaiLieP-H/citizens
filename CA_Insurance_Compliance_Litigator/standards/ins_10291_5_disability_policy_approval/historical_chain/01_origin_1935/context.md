# Historical Chain — § 10291.5 Origin (mid-20th century)

## Background: Insurance Form Regulation

Disability insurance — covering loss of income or medical expenses due to illness or injury — became widespread in California in the early-to-mid 20th century. Early disability policies were notorious for restrictive definitions of disability, exclusions that effectively nullified coverage, and benefit structures that paid out far less than premiums suggested.

California's regulation of disability policy forms developed through successive statutory amendments to the Insurance Code's disability provisions. Section 10291.5 reflects the accumulated legislative judgment that CDI must have specific enumerated grounds to disapprove forms, not merely a general "unfair" standard.

The HIV/AIDS testing notice requirement in §10291.5(c) reflects a later amendment (1980s–1990s) when insurers were using HIV testing in the application process in ways that discriminated against LGBTQ+ individuals. The Legislature added specific procedural requirements around HIV testing to prevent discrimination while permitting legitimate underwriting.

## Current State

§10291.5 is a policy-form approval statute. It is administered by CDI's Rate Regulation Branch (disability and life forms). The contra proferentem interpretive rule it supports is well-established California common law applied in coverage disputes. No recent material amendments have altered the core disapproval grounds in §10291.5(b).
