# Rule — Ins. Code § 10291.5 Disability Policy Form Approval Standards

**Standard ID:** ins_10291_5_disability_policy_approval
**Authority:** Cal. Ins. Code § 10291.5

## Core Rule

Section 10291.5 governs CDI review and approval of disability insurance policy forms. The Commissioner SHALL NOT approve a disability policy form if it contains:

| Disapproval Ground | Subdivision | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Unintelligible, uncertain, ambiguous, or misleading provisions | §10291.5(b)(1) | Ambiguous coverage terms → construed against insurer in coverage disputes |
| Unreasonable benefit/premium ratio | §10291.5(b)(1) | Actuarial review by CDI |
| Benefit disparity (>3:1 ratio for same loss) | §10291.5(b)(2) | Discriminatory benefit structure |
| Benefits not of real economic value | §10291.5(b)(7) | Policy must provide meaningful coverage |
| Non-conformance with California law | §10291.5(b)(13) | Any code violation = disapproval |
| Discriminatory application questions | §10291.5(c) | No race/color/religion/national origin discrimination in application |

## Key Interpretive Rule — Ambiguity Against Insurer

§10291.5(b)(1) provides the regulatory foundation for a well-established interpretive rule in California insurance law: **ambiguous policy terms are construed against the insurer** (contra proferentem). The rationale flows from §10291.5 — if a term is ambiguous, CDI should not have approved the form containing it; the insurer bears responsibility for the ambiguity it drafted.

In coverage litigation:
- If Blue Shield's disability policy form contains ambiguous terms regarding "medically necessary" or the scope of prior authorization requirements → those terms are construed in the insured's favor
- The §10291.5(b)(1) standard confirms the Legislature intended policy terms to be clear — ambiguity is the insurer's drafting failure

## What §10291.5 Is NOT

§10291.5 is a CDI policy form approval standard. It does NOT:
- Create a private right of action (Moradi-Shalal limitation applies)
- Authorize private punitive damages claims
- Create a separate bad faith tort cause of action

The ambiguity/contra proferentem rule exists as a contract interpretive principle, grounded in the general principle that ambiguities in adhesion contracts are construed against the drafter. §10291.5 provides regulatory confirmation of why clarity is required — it does not create a new litigation vehicle.

## Application — Blue Shield Prior Auth

If Blue Shield's policy form (as approved by CDI under §10291.5) contains ambiguous terms defining what constitutes "medically necessary" or what qualifies for prior authorization exemptions:
1. The ambiguity in the approved form is construed against Blue Shield
2. Any coverage denied based on an ambiguous policy interpretation is presumptively wrong
3. Combined with §790.03(h)(13) (failure to explain denial basis) and the bad faith tort (Gruenberg unreasonableness): three separate analytical tracks all pointing to liability
