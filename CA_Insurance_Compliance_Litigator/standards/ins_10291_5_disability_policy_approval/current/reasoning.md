# Reasoning — Why § 10291.5 Exists

## The Policy Form Approval Problem

Before CDI policy form approval requirements, insurance companies could sell policies containing provisions that were internally contradictory, so vague as to be meaningless, or structured to minimize actual payout. The insured paid premiums for years, then discovered at claim time that coverage was far narrower than the policy's plain language suggested.

California's disability insurance form approval requirements (§10291.5 and its predecessors) were designed to prevent this structural information asymmetry. The Commissioner reviews policy forms BEFORE they are sold. If a term is ambiguous at the approval stage, it should not be approved.

## The Residual Ambiguity Problem

CDI review is not perfect. Ambiguous terms sometimes reach consumers in approved policy forms. When they do, the ambiguity was not the consumer's creation — the insurer drafted the form, CDI reviewed it, and if ambiguity survived, the insurer is the party best positioned to have prevented it.

Contra proferentem — construing ambiguity against the drafter — is the common law response to this structural problem. §10291.5(b)(1) provides the regulatory foundation: if the Commissioner cannot approve a form with unintelligible or ambiguous terms, those terms, if found in an approved form, must be read in the way that gives the insured coverage.

## Connection to the Prior Authorization Problem

Prior authorization criteria — what counts as "medically necessary," what physician attestations are required, what timeframes apply — are frequently ambiguous in the policy form itself. When an insurer denies prior authorization based on a narrow interpretation of these criteria, and the criteria were ambiguously written, the insurer's interpretation cannot stand.

§10291.5(b)(1) confirms why: CDI would not have approved a form with ambiguous prior auth criteria if the Commissioner had identified the ambiguity. The approved form creates a commitment to clarity that the insurer cannot undermine through self-serving narrow interpretation.
