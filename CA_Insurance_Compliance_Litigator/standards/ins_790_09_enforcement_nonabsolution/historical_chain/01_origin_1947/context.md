# Origin Context — INS §790.09

**Enacted:** 1947 (Part of original UIPA, Stats. 1947, Ch. 1120)
**The Wound:** Regulators settling enforcement actions while private victims remained uncompensated

---

## The Problem §790.09 Was Answering

The 1947 California UIPA framers had watched a structural pattern play out in earlier state insurance markets: when a regulator stepped in and issued a compliance order, the insurer's lawyers would argue the enforcement action "settled" the matter. Courts in some jurisdictions accepted versions of this argument, effectively giving insurers a one-stop resolution: negotiate with the regulator, pay an administrative fine, and walk away from civil liability to every individual policyholder harmed.

§790.09 was designed to close this loophole at the moment the UIPA was enacted. The California Legislature made the judgment that:

1. Regulatory enforcement protects the market as a whole
2. Civil liability compensates the individual who was actually harmed
3. Criminal penalties deter intentional misconduct
4. These three purposes are distinct and none can substitute for the others

## The Structural Promise

The promise §790.09 makes to policyholders is specific:

> Even if CDI comes in and issues a cease-and-desist order against your insurer, your civil lawsuit is not gone. The state pursued its interest. You still have the right to pursue yours.

This is the same structural logic that runs through most California consumer protection law: the public enforcement track and the private enforcement track are cumulative, not alternative.

## Why This Still Matters in 2026

The argument that CDI administrative resolution bars civil claims is still raised. When an insurer settles a market conduct examination, resolves a CDI order, or enters a consent decree with the Department, defense counsel will sometimes argue that the regulatory resolution should weigh against civil liability or estop certain claims. §790.09 is the direct answer to each version of that argument.

**The statute's text is categorical:** No order "shall in any way relieve or absolve" the person. "In any way" forecloses partial arguments as well as total preclusion arguments.
