# CIV §§ 1102 / 1102.3 — Reasoning

**Layer:** current/reasoning
**Status:** PROPOSED

## Why the TDS Exists

### The Pre-1985 Wound

Before 1985, California had no mandatory seller disclosure statute for residential real estate. A seller could know about a termite infestation, a leaky roof, a failed foundation, a boundary dispute, a toxic spill — and sell the property without disclosing any of it. The buyer's only protection was caveat emptor: buyer beware.

The doctrine of caveat emptor was modified by case law (sellers couldn't actively conceal known defects), but the modification was hard to enforce. By the time a buyer discovered the concealed defect, the seller was gone and memories had faded. Proving the seller knew and concealed required expensive litigation with uncertain outcomes.

### The 1985 Transfer Disclosure Statement

California enacted CIV §§1102-1102.19 in 1985. The TDS replaced caveat emptor with mandatory affirmative disclosure. The seller must complete a standardized form (§1102.6) — three sections:

- **Section I:** Seller's personal knowledge disclosure — condition of the property, systems, known defects
- **Section II:** Seller's agent disclosure — inspection findings, anything observed
- **Section III:** Buyer's agent disclosure (if applicable)

The TDS must be delivered before transfer of title. If it's late, the buyer has 3-5 days to cancel.

### Why §1102(c) Makes Waiver Void

The waiver-void provision was essential to the TDS's purpose. Without it, real estate professionals and institutional sellers (builders, banks, estates) would have simply included TDS waiver clauses in standard-form contracts. The bargaining power advantage always rests with the seller. A buyer who wants the house signs whatever the seller puts in front of them.

§1102(c) removes the option from the table: there is no such thing as a valid TDS waiver. Any clause purporting to waive TDS requirements is unenforceable regardless of the buyer's signature.

### The Conservatorship Problem

The TDS's cancellation right window (3-5 days after late delivery) assumes a buyer with meaningful capacity to read the TDS, evaluate it, and make a decision to cancel. A buyer under conservatorship control who has their housing decisions made by a conservator — especially a conservator who has a financial interest in the sale proceeding — cannot exercise that cancellation right independently.

The statutory protection (3-5 day window) becomes hollow when the person entitled to exercise it lacks the autonomy to do so. This is where the TDS failure and the conservatorship control mechanism intersect. The 19 unsigned documents in the Honeysuckle sale are not just a disclosure failure — they are the physical evidence that the transaction proceeded without the seller's (or buyer's) meaningful participation in the documentation process that §1102 requires.

### Interaction with §12955

CIV §§1102/1102.3 are the disclosure mechanism. GOV §12955(i)(1) is the anti-discrimination mechanism for real estate transaction terms. When the transaction is manipulated — unsigned documents, structured disbursements, conservatorship control — both statutes may be violated simultaneously:

- §1102 violation: TDS not delivered, or delivered unsigned, or cancelled by a party without capacity to cancel
- §12955(i)(1) violation: the transaction terms were structured to discriminate against the party based on perceived disability/incapacity

The two claims run together. The forensic document evidence (19 unsigned docs) supports both.
