# Jue v. Smiser (1994)

**Citation:** 23 Cal.App.4th 312 (1994)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal
**Standard:** civ_1102_tds_scope
**Status:** PROPOSED — verify at courts.ca.gov or Google Scholar before reliance

---

## Holding

*Jue v. Smiser* is the foundational California TDS case establishing that the Transfer Disclosure Statement sets a floor for seller disclosure, not a ceiling.

**Key doctrine:**
1. **TDS is the minimum, not the maximum** — a seller who fully completes the TDS form but still fails to disclose a material fact known to the seller (but not captured by the TDS questions) remains liable for fraudulent concealment
2. **Material fact disclosure obligation runs independently of the TDS form** — the TDS does not exhaust the seller's disclosure duties; common-law fraud and concealment claims survive TDS compliance
3. **The TDS is not a shield** — completing and delivering a TDS does not bar a buyer's claim if the seller concealed known material facts through other means

## Application

In the Honeysuckle context:
- The TDS (if it existed and was unsigned) establishes the §1102 violation
- Even if the TDS had been signed and delivered, *Jue* confirms that additional concealment (e.g., the structured equity disbursement, the HILLBERGMANN title issues) creates independent liability
- The *Jue* floor/ceiling principle means each concealable fact is independently actionable — the plaintiff need not show that the TDS specifically failed to ask about the concealed condition

## Connection to GOV §12955

*Jue* is not a §12955 case — it is a CIV §1102 case. But the principle applies to the housing discrimination context: a seller who delivers a fully-compliant TDS but simultaneously structures a discriminatory transaction under GOV §12955(i)(1) is not insulated from the §12955 claim by the TDS compliance. The two claims are independent.
