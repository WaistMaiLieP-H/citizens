## The 1985 Enactment

California's Transfer Disclosure Statement was created by the Legislature in 1985 after a period of growing consumer protection advocacy in the real estate industry. The California Association of Realtors had lobbied for a standardized disclosure form that would protect sellers from failure-to-disclose liability by creating a written record of what was disclosed.

The legislature's motivations included both consumer protection (protecting buyers from concealed defects) and industry standardization (creating a uniform form that replaced the patchwork of common-law disclosure duties). The tension between these two interests is built into the TDS structure: it is simultaneously a buyer protection mechanism and a seller liability management tool.

The key provisions established in 1985:
- §1102: Scope — single-family residential, all transfer types covered
- §1102(c): Waiver-void provision — non-negotiable from the start
- §1102.3: Delivery timing and cancellation rights
- §1102.6: The standardized three-section form
- §1102.13: Rescission remedy for non-compliance

The waiver-void provision was included at enactment because the legislature recognized that without it, the TDS would be immediately defeated by standard-form contract clauses.
