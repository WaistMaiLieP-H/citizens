# Evolution Layer 2 — Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988
## Critical Expansion: Disability + Familial Status + Enforcement Teeth

**Era:** 1988
**Enacted:** Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988, Pub. L. 100-430
**Signed:** September 13, 1988
**Effective:** March 12, 1989

---

## What FHAA 1988 Added to § 3604

### New Protected Classes

**1. Handicap (Disability)** — § 3604(f)
Added § 3604(f)(1)–(f)(3), which prohibit:
- Refusal to sell or rent to a person because of their disability or the disability of someone associated with them
- Discriminatory terms and conditions based on disability
- Refusal to permit **reasonable modifications** at the tenant's expense
- Refusal to make **reasonable accommodations** in rules, policies, practices, or services

**"Handicap" defined** at § 3602(h): A physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more major life activities; a record of such impairment; or being regarded as having such impairment.

**2. Familial Status** — § 3602(k) + § 3604(a)–(e)
Extended all of § 3604(a)–(e) to cover familial status — families with children under 18, pregnant persons, persons in process of obtaining legal custody of a minor.

---

## What FHAA 1988 Did NOT Change

- The original four classes (race, color, religion, national origin) and sex (added 1974) remained as-is in § 3604(a)–(e)
- The § 3603(b) exemptions (Mrs. Murphy, single-family owner) remained
- § 3604(c) advertising prohibition remained unchanged in scope (now applied to all classes including disability and familial status)

---

## Enforcement Overhaul

FHAA 1988 fundamentally restructured enforcement:

**Before 1988:**
- HUD had conciliation power only — no adjudicatory authority
- Private damages capped at $1,000
- Weak deterrence; widespread non-compliance

**After 1988:**
- HUD empowered to conduct **administrative adjudication** before ALJs (§ 3612)
- ALJs can award actual damages, civil penalties ($10,000 first offense; $25,000 second within 5 years; $50,000 third within 7 years), injunctive relief
- **Private suits under § 3613:** No damages cap; actual damages + punitive damages + attorney's fees available
- DOJ pattern-or-practice authority strengthened (§ 3614)
- Statute of limitations extended to **2 years** for private civil actions (§ 3613(a)(1)(A))

---

## Reasonable Modification vs. Reasonable Accommodation

Two distinct obligations created by § 3604(f)(3):

| Concept | Trigger | Who Pays | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reasonable Modification** (§ 3604(f)(3)(A)) | Tenant requests physical alteration of premises | Tenant (unless federally assisted housing) | Physical structure |
| **Reasonable Accommodation** (§ 3604(f)(3)(B)) | Tenant requests change in rules/policies/services | Landlord | Rules and operations |

**Examples:**
- Modification: Installing grab bars in bathroom — tenant pays
- Accommodation: Allowing an emotional support animal despite no-pets policy — landlord's cost to administer

**SIRVA + Bilateral Ankles:**
- Post-FHAA, Michael's orthopedic disability qualifies as a "handicap" under § 3602(h)
- Any refusal to rent, discriminatory terms, or denial of reasonable modification/accommodation in housing transactions after March 12, 1989 is actionable under § 3604(f)
