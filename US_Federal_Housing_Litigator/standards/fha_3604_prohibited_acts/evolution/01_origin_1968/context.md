# Evolution Layer 1 — Origin: Civil Rights Act of 1968
## 42 U.S.C. § 3604 — Founding Scope at Enactment

**Era:** 1968
**Enacted:** Civil Rights Act of 1968, Pub. L. 90-284, Title VIII ("Fair Housing Act"), § 804
**Signed:** April 11, 1968 — one week after assassination of Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.
**Codified at:** 42 U.S.C. § 3604

---

## What § 3604 Prohibited at Enactment

At origin, § 3604 prohibited discriminatory practices in the sale or rental of housing on the basis of four protected classes:

1. **Race**
2. **Color**
3. **Religion**
4. **National origin**

**Sex** was added later as a protected class by amendment in 1974 (Housing and Community Development Act of 1974, Pub. L. 93-383, § 808).

The original § 3604 covered:
- Refusal to sell or rent (§ 3604(a))
- Discriminatory terms and conditions (§ 3604(b))
- Discriminatory advertising (§ 3604(c))
- Misrepresentation of availability (§ 3604(d))
- Blockbusting (§ 3604(e))

**Disability** and **familial status** were not yet covered — those came 20 years later with FHAA 1988.

---

## Political and Social Context

- Enacted in the immediate aftermath of the assassination of Dr. King (April 4, 1968)
- Part of the broader Civil Rights legislative wave (Civil Rights Act 1964, Voting Rights Act 1965)
- Followed urban riots in Newark, Detroit, and other cities (1967) and the Kerner Commission Report finding that America was "moving toward two societies, one Black, one white — separate and unequal"
- Senate had filibustered earlier fair housing legislation; Dr. King's death broke the political deadlock
- President Johnson signed Pub. L. 90-284 on April 11, 1968

---

## Enforcement Gap at Enactment

The original 1968 act had weak enforcement:
- HUD could only engage in conciliation — no adjudicatory power
- Private plaintiffs could sue in federal court under § 3612 but damages were capped at $1,000
- DOJ pattern-or-practice suits were available under § 3614 but rarely used
- No administrative adjudication mechanism

These weaknesses persisted until FHAA 1988, which created the administrative law judge mechanism and removed damages caps for private suits.

---

## Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co. (1968)

The same year the FHA was enacted, the Supreme Court decided **Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co., 392 U.S. 409 (1968)**, holding that 42 U.S.C. § 1982 (the Civil Rights Act of 1866) prohibits **all** racial discrimination in the sale or rental of property — both private and public. This created a parallel federal cause of action that has no exemptions and no statute of limitations (governed by the most analogous state limitations period).

**Significance for § 3604:** Jones v. Mayer fills the exemption gaps in § 3604 for racial discrimination. Where § 3603(b) exempts owner-occupied dwellings from FHA coverage, § 1982 still applies to racial discrimination.

---

## What Did NOT Change at Origin

- The "Mrs. Murphy" exemption (§ 3603(b)(2)) — owner-occupied buildings with ≤4 units — was present from 1968
- Single-family home exemption (§ 3603(b)(1)) — present from 1968
- Religious organization and private club exemptions (§ 3607) — present from 1968
