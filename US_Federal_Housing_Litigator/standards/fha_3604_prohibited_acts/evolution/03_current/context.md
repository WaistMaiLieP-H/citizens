# Evolution Layer 3 — Current State of § 3604
## As of 2026: Full Protected Class Coverage + Disparate Impact Confirmed

**Era:** 1989–present (post-FHAA 1988 effective date)
**Last major statutory change:** Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988 (effective March 12, 1989)
**Key doctrinal development:** Texas DHCA v. Inclusive Communities Project, Inc., 576 U.S. 519 (2015) — disparate impact confirmed

**Note:** Sexual orientation and gender identity are NOT explicitly listed in 42 U.S.C. § 3604 as of 2026. HUD has adopted guidance treating sex discrimination to include gender identity and sexual orientation under Bostock-related reasoning, but this is regulatory policy, not statutory text. Verify current HUD guidance before filing.

## Current Enforcement Framework

**Three tracks — private, administrative, DOJ:**

**Track 1 — Private civil action (§ 3613):**
- File in any appropriate federal or state court
- 2-year statute of limitations from occurrence or termination of discriminatory practice
- Remedies: actual damages (including emotional distress), punitive damages, injunctive relief, attorney's fees
- No damages cap

**Track 2 — HUD administrative complaint (§ 3610):**
- File within 1 year of discriminatory act
- HUD investigates; if reasonable cause found, refers to DOJ or ALJ proceeding
- ALJ can award actual damages + civil penalties ($16,000/$40,000/$95,000 — inflation-adjusted from 1988 baseline)
- Election to proceed in federal court instead of before ALJ is available

**Track 3 — DOJ pattern or practice (§ 3614):**
- Used for systemic discrimination across multiple victims
- Available for matters of general public importance
- Potentially parallel to private suits

## Disparate Impact Doctrine — Texas DHCA (2015)

**Texas Dep't of Hous. & Cmty. Affairs v. Inclusive Communities Project, Inc., 576 U.S. 519 (2015):**
- Held: FHA permits disparate impact claims, not just intentional discrimination claims
- Burden-shifting framework: plaintiff must demonstrate statistical disparity → burden shifts to defendant to show legitimate, nondiscriminatory reason → plaintiff may rebut by showing less-discriminatory alternative
- Grounded in the "otherwise make unavailable" language of § 3604(a) and § 3605
- Limits: defendants can rebut with "substantial, legitimate, nondiscriminatory interest"; courts should give deference to profit-motivated decisions that serve legitimate business objectives

**Significance for Case #19:** If the Honeysuckle sale terms or the surrounding market practices had a disparate impact on persons with disability or familial status (children in household), a disparate impact claim is available alongside an intentional discrimination claim.

## Current Regulatory Framework

**24 C.F.R. Part 100** — HUD's primary implementing regulation for FHA.
Key subparts:
- Subpart B (§§ 100.50–100.115): Discriminatory housing practices
- Subpart C (§§ 100.120–100.135): Discriminatory representations
- Subpart D (§§ 100.200–100.205): Prohibition against discrimination because of handicap
- Subpart E (§§ 100.300–100.310): Housing for older persons (55+ exemption from familial status)

**24 C.F.R. § 100.500** — HUD disparate impact rule (codifying Inclusive Communities burden-shifting).

## VAWA Housing Overlay (2013 + 2022 Reauthorization)

Violence Against Women Act reauthorization (Pub. L. 113-4, 2013; Pub. L. 117-103, 2022) added 42 U.S.C. § 12491, which prohibits denial of housing or eviction of a domestic violence victim solely based on DV victim status. This runs parallel to § 3604 where a protected class member (sex, disability) is also a DV victim.

**Case application:** Where Christina's false DVRO contributed to displacement or denial of housing rights in the Honeysuckle transaction, § 12491 + § 3604(b)/(f) may be jointly pleaded.
