# Case Law — Havens Realty Corp. v. Coleman
## 455 U.S. 363 (1982) — Tester Standing + Organizational Standing Under FHA

**Citation:** Havens Realty Corp. v. Coleman, 455 U.S. 363 (1982)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** February 24, 1982
**Holding:** FHA testers have Article III standing to sue even when they had no intention of renting or purchasing. Organizations whose mission is diverted by discriminatory housing practices also have standing.

---

## Facts

Housing Opportunities Made Equal (HOME), a fair housing organization, deployed "testers" to investigate whether Havens Realty engaged in racial steering. Testers — one Black, one white — inquired about apartment availability. Havens told the Black tester no apartments were available; the white tester was told apartments were available.

The Black tester sued even though she had no actual intent to rent. HOME sued on the theory that Havens' conduct forced HOME to divert resources from other activities to investigate and counteract the discrimination.

---

## Holding

**1. Tester standing — § 3604(d):**
The Court held that the Black tester had Article III standing. § 3604(d) prohibits representations that a dwelling is "not available" when it is in fact available. The statute confers a legal right to truthful information about housing availability on any "person." Even a tester with no intent to rent has a concrete interest in receiving accurate information, and the misrepresentation itself constitutes a cognizable injury.

**Key quote:** "[A] tester who has been the object of a misrepresentation made unlawful under § 3604(d) has suffered injury in precisely the form the statute was intended to guard against..."

The white tester (who received accurate information) had no injury and therefore no standing.

**2. Organizational standing — HOME:**
HOME had standing based on diversion of resources. When a discriminatory practice forces an organization to devote resources to identifying and counteracting the discrimination, that diversion of resources constitutes concrete, particularized injury sufficient for Article III standing.

---

## Rule Extracted

**For litigators:**
1. A tester plaintiff who was the object of a § 3604(d) misrepresentation about availability has standing, even without intent to rent or buy.
2. A fair housing organization whose resources are diverted to investigate discrimination has organizational standing.
3. The standing inquiry under the FHA is co-extensive with Article III — ask whether the plaintiff has suffered injury in the form the statute was designed to prevent.

---

## Application to Michael's Cases

**Case #19 — Honeysuckle:**
- If any party made a misrepresentation about the property's availability or terms to Michael on a protected class basis, Havens Realty establishes that even a "tester" scenario (third party confirming the misrepresentation) could support standing for that individual.
- More directly: Havens Realty's organizational standing doctrine means that if Vernen Legal or a similar advocacy entity diverted resources to document the housing fraud in Case #19, that entity could potentially have organizational standing in a § 3604 action.

**Procedural note:** Havens Realty predates FHAA 1988. The standing doctrine it established applies with equal force under the current § 3604 and is routinely cited in post-1988 FHA cases.

---

## Subsequent Treatment

- Widely cited as foundational FHA standing authority
- Applied in virtually every FHA testing case since 1982
- Organizational standing doctrine extended beyond housing context in other civil rights cases
- Still good law as of 2026
