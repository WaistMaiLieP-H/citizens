# Case Law — Fair Housing Council of San Fernando Valley v. Roommates.Com
## 521 F.3d 1157 (9th Cir. 2008) (en banc) — § 3604(c) + Online Platforms

**Citation:** Fair Housing Council of San Fernando Valley v. Roommates.Com, LLC, 521 F.3d 1157 (9th Cir. 2008) (en banc)
**Court:** United States Court of Appeals, Ninth Circuit (en banc)
**Decided:** April 3, 2008
**Holding:** An online roommate-matching website that required users to state their sex, sexual orientation, and familial status and used those characteristics to filter roommate search results is not immune from FHA § 3604(c) liability under the Communications Decency Act § 230. The website was a content creator (not just a passive host) for the discriminatory preference questionnaire.

---

## Facts

Roommates.com required users to answer mandatory profile questions including their sex, sexual orientation, and preferences regarding the sex, sexual orientation, and familial status of roommates. The site then used those preferences to filter available listings. The Fair Housing Council sued, arguing the site violated § 3604(c) (discriminatory advertising and statements regarding preferences).

Roommates.com defended on two grounds:
1. The Communications Decency Act (CDA), 47 U.S.C. § 230, immunizes websites from liability for user-generated content
2. The roommate selection context falls outside the FHA because § 3604(c) doesn't apply to shared living arrangements

---

## Holding

**1. CDA § 230 does not immunize Roommates.com:**
CDA § 230 protects websites from liability as "publishers" of third-party content. But Roommates.com was not a passive host — it created the mandatory discriminatory preference questionnaire and used responses to filter results. A website that "materially contributes to the illegality" of content is a content creator, not just a passive conduit, and loses § 230 immunity.

**2. § 3604(c) applies to discriminatory advertising in roommate context:**
The en banc court held that making, printing, or publishing a notice or statement indicating preference based on sex or familial status in connection with the rental of a dwelling violates § 3604(c). The court split on the additional user-generated "additional comments" section (finding that section was protected by § 230 because Roommates.com did not create those statements).

---

## Rule Extracted

**For litigators:**
1. § 3604(c) reaches online platforms that affirmatively create or solicit discriminatory housing preference statements
2. CDA § 230 immunity does not apply where the platform is a co-creator of the discriminatory content — design of a discriminatory intake form qualifies as co-creation
3. The voluntary roommate-matching context does not categorically exempt a platform from § 3604(c) liability for discriminatory advertising

---

## Application to Michael's Cases

**Case #19 / Housing transactions:**
- If any platform used in connection with the Honeysuckle sale, rental search, or replacement housing search implemented discriminatory filters based on disability or familial status, Roommates.com establishes that the platform operator can be liable under § 3604(c) even for algorithmically filtered results
- This is a less direct application — more relevant if a property management company's tenant screening software filtered on disability or familial status

**Broader platform accountability doctrine:**
The Roommates.com holding established that platform design choices creating discriminatory filtering are actionable. This doctrine may apply if any technology intermediary in Michael's housing chain used protected class data to filter, price, or restrict housing options.

---

## Doctrinal Significance

- One of the first major decisions analyzing FHA liability in the online/platform context
- Established the "material contribution to illegality" exception to CDA § 230 in the housing discrimination context
- Cited extensively in subsequent platform liability cases across multiple civil rights statutes
- HUD has relied on Roommates.com reasoning in guidance on algorithmic advertising and tenant screening tools

---

## Limitations

- Applies specifically to § 3604(c) — the advertising and statement prohibition
- The court left open whether other § 3604 provisions apply with equal force to online platforms
- HUD guidance (2023) on algorithmic advertising tools (Facebook, Zillow, etc.) has extended the Roommates.com reasoning to ad-targeting systems
