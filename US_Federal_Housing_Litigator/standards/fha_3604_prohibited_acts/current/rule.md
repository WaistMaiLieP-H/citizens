# Rule — 42 U.S.C. § 3604
## Fair Housing Act — Prohibited Practices in Sale or Rental of Housing

## The Rule

It is unlawful to:

**(a)** Refuse to sell or rent, or refuse to negotiate for the sale or rental of, or otherwise make unavailable or deny, a dwelling to any person because of **race, color, religion, sex, familial status, or national origin**.

**(b)** Discriminate against any person in the terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental of a dwelling, or in the provision of services or facilities in connection therewith, because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status, or national origin.

**(c)** Make, print, or publish — or cause to be made, printed, or published — any notice, statement, or advertisement, with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling, that indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination based on a protected class, or an intention to make any such preference, limitation, or discrimination.

**(d)** Represent to any person, because of a protected class, that any dwelling is not available for inspection, sale, or rental when such dwelling is in fact so available.

**(e)** For profit, induce or attempt to induce any person to sell or rent any dwelling by representations regarding the entry or prospective entry into the neighborhood of a person of a particular race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin (blockbusting).

**(f)(1)** Discriminate in the sale or rental, or otherwise make unavailable or deny, a dwelling to any buyer or renter because of a **handicap** (disability) of:
- That buyer or renter;
- A person residing in or intending to reside in that dwelling after it is sold, rented, or made available; or
- Any person associated with that buyer or renter.

**(f)(2)** Discriminate against any person in the terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental of a dwelling, or in the provision of services or facilities in connection with such dwelling, because of a handicap of the buyer, renter, or associated person.

**(f)(3)** Refusal to permit, at the expense of the person with a handicap, reasonable modifications of existing premises; or refusal to make reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services.

## Key Definitional Anchors

**"Handicap" (disability):** A physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more major life activities; a record of such impairment; or being regarded as having such impairment. (42 U.S.C. § 3602(h))

**"Familial status":** Having one or more children under 18 residing with a parent, legal guardian, or person who has written permission of a parent or legal guardian; pregnant persons; persons in the process of securing legal custody of a child. (42 U.S.C. § 3602(k)) — added by FHAA 1988.

**"Dwelling":** Any building, structure, or portion thereof which is occupied or designated to be occupied as a residence by one or more families, and any vacant land which is offered for sale or lease for the construction of a building. (42 U.S.C. § 3602(b))

## Scope and Coverage

§ 3604 applies to virtually all housing in the United States. Exemptions are narrow:
- Single-family homes sold or rented by owner without use of a broker (with conditions) — § 3603(b)(1)
- Rooms or units in dwellings with four or fewer units where the owner resides — § 3603(b)(2) ("Mrs. Murphy" exemption)
- Religious organizations and private clubs for non-commercial dwellings — § 3607

## Case #19 / Honeysuckle Application

**2958 Honeysuckle Lane** — $605,000 sale; $465,000 equity converted under suspicious circumstances; 19 unsigned documents:
- If the $465K equity diversion reflects discriminatory terms or conditions in the sale transaction tied to Michael's disability status (bilateral ankles, SIRVA), § 3604(b) applies.
- If steering, blockbusting, or misrepresentation about property availability occurred in conjunction with the sale, § 3604(d)/(e) apply.
- If the 19 unsigned documents reflect the seller's inability to complete the transaction without coercion, investigate whether § 3604(f) disability-based coercion in sale terms applies.

**Cross-case VAWA connection:** § 12491 (VAWA housing protections) is a companion standard in this Citizen. Where domestic violence circumstances contributed to coerced sale or inequitable distribution of proceeds, both § 3604 and § 12491 may apply concurrently.

**HILLBERGMANN deed fraud:** Ann Hillberg / compound identity on State Farm policy — if property deeds were manipulated in a transaction involving protected class members, § 3604(a)/(b) discriminatory denial or terms apply to any housing transaction within the fraud chain.

## Enforcement Mechanism

**Private right of action:** 42 U.S.C. § 3613 — aggrieved person may file civil action in federal district court within **2 years** of occurrence or termination of discriminatory housing practice (§ 3613(a)(1)(A)), or within **2 years** of a breach of a conciliation agreement.

**HUD administrative complaint:** § 3610 — file within **1 year** of the discriminatory act.

**DOJ enforcement:** § 3614 — for pattern or practice or matters of general public importance.

**Remedies:** Injunctive relief; actual damages (including humiliation, embarrassment); punitive damages; attorney's fees. No damages cap for private suits under § 3613.

**Testers have standing:** Havens Realty Corp. v. Coleman, 455 U.S. 363 (1982) — persons who inquire about housing availability have Article III standing even if they did not intend to rent or buy.

## Disparate Impact: Texas DHCA Ruling

**Texas Dep't of Hous. & Cmty. Affairs v. Inclusive Communities Project, Inc., 576 U.S. 519 (2015)** — FHA reaches **disparate impact** claims, not just intentional discrimination. A facially neutral policy that produces a significant statistical disparity on a protected class is actionable, subject to a burden-shifting framework.
