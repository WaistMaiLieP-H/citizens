# GOV §§ 12989.1 / 12989.2 — Operative Rule

## The Rule

GOV §§12989.1 and 12989.2 together form the complete civil enforcement mechanism for FEHA housing discrimination. §12989.1 establishes the right to file suit and the 2-year SOL. §12989.2 establishes the full range of remedies available when a discriminatory housing practice is proven. Neither requires prior CRD exhaustion.

---

## Who Can Sue and When

**§12989.1(a) — Standing and SOL:**
Any "aggrieved person" — which the FEHA defines broadly — may sue within 2 years of:
- The OCCURRENCE of the discriminatory housing practice; OR
- The TERMINATION of the practice (whichever is LAST)

The "termination" prong is critical for ongoing discrimination: if a discriminatory practice continues (e.g., ongoing management of equity proceeds, continued use of discriminatory lending terms), the SOL does not begin until the practice ends.

**Tolling:** The 2-year period does NOT include time during which a CRD administrative proceeding was pending. Filing with CRD tolls the SOL.

**§12989.1(b) — No Exhaustion:**
An aggrieved person may file a civil action WHETHER OR NOT a complaint was filed with CRD, and WITHOUT REGARD to the status of any complaint. This means:
- No need to wait for CRD investigation
- No need for a right-to-sue notice
- No need for any administrative prerequisite

## Complete Remedies Table (§12989.2)

| Remedy | Available | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Actual damages | YES — mandatory if violation found | Full compensatory damages: equity loss, market value differential, relocation costs, emotional distress |
| Punitive damages | YES — discretionary | No statutory cap under §12989.2; depends on defendant's wealth, malice, oppression |
| Temporary injunction/TRO | YES | Available even before final determination |
| Permanent injunction | YES | Court may enjoin any defendant from continuing unlawful practice |
| "Any other appropriate order" | YES | Broad catch-all; court can fashion equitable relief |
| Attorney's fees | YES — discretionary | Prevailing party (other than state); expert witness fees explicitly included |
| State-agency debt offset | YES | If defendant owes money to state agency, court may offset to satisfy judgment |

## The Punitive Damages Framework

§12989.2 allows punitive damages in a FEHA housing civil action. California punitive damages law (CIV §3294 — owned by CA_Real_Estate_Attorney) requires:
- Defendant acted with malice, oppression, or fraud
- Clear and convincing evidence standard
- Officer/director/managing agent ratification for corporate defendants

For housing discrimination cases, "malice" is shown by intentional discriminatory conduct; "oppression" is shown by subjecting a person to cruel and unjust hardship with conscious disregard of their rights; "fraud" is shown by deceit or concealment.

**Application — Honeysuckle:** A $465K equity loss following a sale involving 19 unsigned documents, conservatorship control of the seller, and a structured equity dispersal designed to obscure the destination of proceeds presents a strong punitive damages case if the discrimination was intentional.

## Attorney Fees — Practical Mechanics

- Discretionary — court awards to "prevailing party" at its discretion
- Expert witness fees explicitly included — no separate motion required
- "Against any party OTHER THAN THE STATE" — state defendants (CRD, state agencies) are exempt from fee award
- Standard: prevailing plaintiff in civil rights cases typically awarded fees under California fee-shifting statutes; defendant typically must show the action was frivolous to avoid a fee award against them

## Integration with §12955

§12989.2 remedies attach to §12955 violations. The connection is:
```
GOV §12955 (prohibited act) → GOV §12989.1 (right to sue, 2-year SOL) → GOV §12989.2 (remedies)
```
All three standards in this Citizen operate as a unit for any FEHA housing discrimination claim.
