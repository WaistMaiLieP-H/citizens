# GOV §§ 12989.1/12989.2 — Evolution Stage 02: No-Exhaustion Amendment

**Stage:** 02_no_exhaustion_amendment
**Authority:** Stats. (various amendments); Gov. Code §12989.1(b) as current
**Status:** PROPOSED

---

## The No-Exhaustion Amendment

At some point after 1980, California amended GOV §12989.1 to add subsection (b): an aggrieved person may commence a civil action "whether or not a complaint has been filed" with CRD and "without regard to the status of any complaint." This eliminated the exhaustion trap.

## Why This Matters

The elimination of CRD exhaustion for FEHA housing civil actions meant:
1. **No waiting** — a person whose equity was stolen by discrimination could go directly to Superior Court without spending years in administrative proceedings
2. **Parallel tracks** — the civil action and the CRD administrative proceeding can run simultaneously; the civil SOL is tolled during the administrative proceeding
3. **No conciliation agreement bind** — if the aggrieved person did NOT consent to a CRD conciliation agreement, they are not bound by it and can still pursue civil action (the exception only applies when CRD obtained the agreement WITH the aggrieved person's consent)

## Historical Loss — Before No-Exhaustion

Before this amendment, a discriminated-against homeowner who needed immediate legal action could not get it. The administrative process — complaint, investigation, conciliation attempt, DFEH determination — could take 18-36 months. During that time, the discriminating party kept the equity, kept operating, and often disappeared the proceeds. By the time the administrative process completed, the damages were irretrievable.
