# GOV §§ 12989.1/12989.2 — Evolution Stage 01: Origin (1980 FEHA)

**Stage:** 01_origin_1980
**Authority:** Stats. 1980 (FEHA enactment); Gov. Code §§12980 et seq.
**Status:** PROPOSED

---

## The 1980 Origin

When the California Fair Employment and Housing Act was enacted in 1980, it created the first comprehensive administrative and civil enforcement mechanism for California housing discrimination law. The Rumford Fair Housing Act (1963) had established the substantive prohibition but lacked a robust enforcement mechanism.

The 1980 FEHA created:
- The Department of Fair Employment and Housing (DFEH, now CRD) as the administrative enforcement body
- An administrative complaint process with investigation and conciliation
- A civil action right as an alternative or supplement to administrative proceedings
- The foundational remedies framework: actual damages, injunctive relief, attorney fees

## What Was Missing in 1980

The original 1980 framework required administrative exhaustion before civil suit in some circumstances. The CRD process could take years. A person whose housing equity had been stripped by discrimination was forced to wait for the bureaucracy to complete its investigation before pursuing civil remedies. The SOL ran during this waiting period, creating a trap.

## What This Stage Documents

The 1980 FEHA established the principle that housing discrimination is a civil rights violation with civil remedies — not merely an administrative infraction with administrative fines. That principle, once established, led directly to the expansion of remedies and the elimination of exhaustion requirements in later amendments.
