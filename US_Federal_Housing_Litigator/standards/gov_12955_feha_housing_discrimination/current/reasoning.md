# GOV § 12955 — Reasoning

**Standard:** gov_12955_feha_housing_discrimination
**Layer:** current/reasoning
**Status:** PROPOSED

---

## Why the Statute Works the Way It Does

### California Went Further Than the FHA

The federal Fair Housing Act of 1968 (42 USC §§3601-3631) prohibits discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. California's §12955 adds: source of income (including Section 8 vouchers), gender identity, gender expression, genetic information, and veteran/military status. California also extended coverage to appraisers (§12955(i)(2)) — federal law does not explicitly cover appraisers.

The expansion reflects California's legislative judgment that discriminatory exclusion takes forms the original federal statute didn't anticipate: refusing to rent to voucher holders is functionally identical to race discrimination when Section 8 is concentrated in communities of color.

### The §12955(e) Financial Institution Provision

Banks and mortgage companies discriminating in loan terms — higher interest rates, lower appraisals, higher fees — can produce economic discrimination without explicit exclusion. A Black borrower who qualifies for a mortgage but receives worse terms than a white borrower with identical financials experiences discrimination that doesn't look like a denial. §12955(e) captures this: the discrimination occurs "in the terms, conditions, or privileges" of financial assistance, not just in the decision to extend it.

This is the provision most directly applicable to an equity gap case. If the Honeysuckle sale terms were structured to extract equity — through discriminatory financial institution participation — §12955(e) is the hook.

### The Restrictive Covenant Rule (§12955(l))

California made the existence of a discriminatory restrictive covenant itself a §12955(l) violation, even if accompanied by a statement that the covenant is repealed or void. This is a prophylactic rule: the legislature recognized that even technically-void covenants signal exclusion and maintain discriminatory effects. A neighborhood where every deed has a voided "whites only" clause is not the same as a neighborhood that never had such clauses.

For a case involving fraudulent deed recording (HILLBERGMANN compound identity), the question is whether the fraudulently recorded documents contained any restrictive covenants — discriminatory or otherwise — that had land-use effects.

### The Perception Rule (§12955(m))

Protection for perceived characteristics was added because discriminatory actors often don't confirm a person's actual protected status before discriminating. A landlord who refuses to rent to someone because they think the person is disabled — even if the person is not actually disabled — is still engaged in discriminatory housing practice. The harm is identical; the actor's perception is what drives the conduct.

For a case involving conservatorship and controlled existence, the perception rule matters: if the control mechanism itself was driven by a perception of disability or incapacity that was exaggerated or manufactured, the entire housing-control apparatus falls under §12955(m).

### The Anti-Retaliation Provision (§12955(f))

§12955(f) is broader than it appears. "Opposing practices unlawful under this section" includes informal opposition — telling a landlord their conduct is wrong, refusing to participate in a discriminatory transaction, or consulting with a housing rights organization. The person doesn't need to have filed a complaint. The "dominant purpose" standard means that even if a landlord has other reasons for an eviction or a denial, if retaliation is the **dominant** purpose, §12955(f) is violated.

## Interaction with Other Standards

- **CIV §51 (Unruh Act):** GOV §12955(d) — persons subject to CIV §51 cannot discriminate on §12955 bases. This creates a per se Unruh Act violation when a §12955 violation occurs by a covered entity, triggering §52's $4,000/violation statutory damages.
- **GOV §12989.2 (remedies):** Actual + punitive damages + attorney's fees + injunction. The combined package — especially punitive damages and attorney's fees — is what makes a §12955 claim worth pursuing.
- **42 USC §3604 (FHA):** The federal and state claims can run simultaneously in a California federal court. The §12955 claim provides broader protected class coverage and a direct path to $4,000 Unruh damages.
