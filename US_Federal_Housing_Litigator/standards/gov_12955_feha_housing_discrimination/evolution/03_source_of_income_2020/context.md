# GOV § 12955 — Evolution Stage 03: Source of Income Expansion (2020)

**Stage:** 03_source_of_income_2020
**Authority:** Stats. 2019, Ch. 597 (AB 329); Stats. 2020, Ch. 159 (SB 329) effective 2020-01-01
**Status:** PROPOSED

---

## The Source of Income Amendments

The most significant recent expansion of §12955 was the SB 329 (2019, effective 2020) amendment that closed the Section 8 voucher loophole.

### The Pre-2020 Gap

Before 2020, California law prohibited discrimination based on "source of income" but expressly excluded federal housing assistance vouchers (Section 8) from the definition of "source of income." This created an absurd carve-out: a landlord could refuse to rent to a Section 8 voucher holder without violating §12955, even though Section 8 holders are disproportionately people of color, disabled persons, veterans, and families with children.

In practice, landlords in many California cities had blanket "no Section 8" policies. These policies were facially neutral — they didn't mention race or disability — but their discriminatory effect was well-documented.

### SB 329 (2019, effective 2020-01-01)

SB 329 amended §12955(p) to remove the federal housing voucher exclusion. As of January 1, 2020, housing assistance vouchers issued under Section 8 of the United States Housing Act of 1937 (42 U.S.C. §1437f) are expressly included within the definition of "source of income." HUD-VASH (Veterans Affairs Supportive Housing) vouchers are specifically named.

A landlord who refuses to rent to a Section 8 or HUD-VASH voucher holder now violates §12955(a) (if they're an owner of a housing accommodation) and §12955(e) (if financial institution terms reject voucher holders).

### Impact on Case Coverage

**UA342 / disability intersection:** If Michael received or was eligible for disability-based housing assistance or HUD-VASH vouchers, any housing discrimination based on that source of income is now explicitly covered. The §12955(o) provisions (credit history alternative evidence, rent subsidy income standard) also protect persons whose primary income is government benefits.

### The §12955(o) Addition

§12955(o) was added to address the credit history discrimination problem for voucher holders. A landlord with a government rent subsidy tenant cannot use a full-income standard (the voucher pays most of the rent) and must offer alternative evidence options if using credit history as a screening criterion. This provision protects the exact population — low-income, disabled, voucher-dependent — most at risk of being screened out by credit history checks that don't reflect actual ability to pay rent.
