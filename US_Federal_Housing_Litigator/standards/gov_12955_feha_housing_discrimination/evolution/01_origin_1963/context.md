# GOV § 12955 — Evolution Stage 01: Origin (1963)

**Stage:** 01_origin_1963
**Authority:** Stats. 1963 (Rumford Fair Housing Act); Cal. Health & Safety Code §§35700 et seq. (pre-recodification)
**Status:** PROPOSED

---

## The 1963 Origin

California's fair housing statute predates the federal Fair Housing Act by five years. The Rumford Fair Housing Act (1963), authored by Assemblyman W. Byron Rumford, was the first California statute to prohibit racial discrimination in housing. It applied to most housing units, including those sold or rented by private parties.

### What the 1963 Act Did

The Rumford Act prohibited discrimination in the sale or rental of housing based on race, color, religion, national origin, or ancestry. It applied to:
- Housing financed by institutional loans
- Housing that had received public financing
- Property offered for sale through licensed real estate agents
- Most rental housing

### The Proposition 14 Wound

**The Rumford Act was immediately attacked.** In 1964, one year after the Rumford Act was passed, California voters approved Proposition 14 — a constitutional amendment that repealed the Rumford Act and prohibited the state legislature from enacting any fair housing law without a statewide vote.

Proposition 14 was not a neutral measure. Its text read: "Neither the State nor any subdivision or agency thereof shall deny, limit or abridge, directly or indirectly, the right of any person, who is willing or desires to sell, lease or rent any part or all of his real property, to decline to sell, lease or rent such property to such person or persons as he, in his absolute discretion, chooses."

In plain language: a homeowner or landlord had the constitutional right to refuse to sell or rent to Black people, or to anyone else they chose to exclude.

**The California Supreme Court struck down Proposition 14** in *Reitman v. Mulkey* (1967) 64 Cal.2d 529, holding it violated the federal Equal Protection Clause by making the state complicit in private discrimination. The U.S. Supreme Court affirmed in *Reitman v. Mulkey* (1967) 387 U.S. 369.

The Rumford Act was restored. The cycle — legislate protection, attempt to constitutionally eliminate it, courts restore it — established the adversarial history of California fair housing law.

### The FEHA Recodification

The Rumford Act was eventually incorporated into the Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), recodified in Gov. Code §12955 and the surrounding provisions. The FEHA created the California Civil Rights Department (formerly DFEH) as the administrative enforcement mechanism.

---

## What Was Lost Without the 1963 Rumford Act

Between 1964 (Proposition 14 passage) and 1967 (Reitman court reinstatement), California had no fair housing law. During those three years, racially explicit housing discrimination was not merely tolerated — it was constitutionally protected. The damage done in those three years — neighborhoods locked, equity denied, communities segregated — was not reversed when the courts struck down Proposition 14. The houses that weren't sold, the equity that wasn't built, the communities that were maintained as racially homogeneous through legal means during that window persisted for decades.
