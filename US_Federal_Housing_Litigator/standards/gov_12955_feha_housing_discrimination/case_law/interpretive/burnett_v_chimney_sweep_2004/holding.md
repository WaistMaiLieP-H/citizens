# Burnett v. Chimney Sweep (2004)

**Citation:** 123 Cal.App.4th 1057 (2004)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal
**Standard:** gov_12955_feha_housing_discrimination
**Status:** PROPOSED — verify at courts.ca.gov or Google Scholar before reliance

---

## Holding

*Burnett v. Chimney Sweep* involved marital status discrimination in the rental housing context under GOV §12955.

**Key doctrine established:**
1. **§12955 applies to marital status in rental housing** — a landlord who refuses to rent to an unmarried couple on the basis of their marital status violates §12955(a)
2. **Damages available under §12989.2** — the full GOV §12989.2 remedy package (actual damages, punitive damages, attorney's fees) applies to §12955 housing discrimination claims
3. **The protected class analysis does not require proof of animus** — it is sufficient to show that the protected characteristic was a motivating factor in the adverse housing decision

## Relevance to Corpus

*Burnett* establishes the foundational damage framework for §12955 claims: actual + punitive + attorney's fees. In any §12955 case involving the Honeysuckle sale or financial institution discrimination, the *Burnett* framework confirms that punitive exposure exists for housing discrimination even in non-race contexts (marital status discrimination was found actionable with punitives).

The case also confirms that §12955 is a broadly construed remedial statute — consistent with FEHA's liberal construction mandate. Arguments that financial institution terms or appraisal decisions were facially neutral will not insulate from §12955 liability if a protected characteristic was a motivating factor.
