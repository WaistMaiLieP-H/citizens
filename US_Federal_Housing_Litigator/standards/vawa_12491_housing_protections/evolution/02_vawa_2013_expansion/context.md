# Evolution Layer 2 — VAWA 2013 Housing Expansion
## Major Overhaul: All HUD Programs + Emergency Transfer + Lease Bifurcation

**Era:** 2013
**Enacted:** Violence Against Women Reauthorization Act of 2013, Pub. L. 113-4, Title VI
**Signed:** March 7, 2013
**Effective:** HUD published implementing rule in November 2016 (24 C.F.R. Part 5, Subpart L)

---

## The Five Major Additions in VAWA 2013

### 1. Expanded Coverage — All HUD-Assisted Housing Programs

VAWA 2013 extended § 12491 protections far beyond the original public housing / Section 8 scope. Coverage now includes all programs administered by HUD, including:
- HOME Investment Partnerships Program
- HUD-VASH (Veterans Affairs Supportive Housing)
- McKinney-Vento Act transitional housing programs
- Supportive Housing Program
- Rural housing programs under the Violence Against Women Act
- Low-income housing programs receiving HUD funding

**Adding sexual assault:** VAWA 2013 added sexual assault as a covered crime alongside domestic violence, dating violence, and stalking.

### 2. Emergency Transfer Right

A DV, dating violence, sexual assault, or stalking survivor may request an emergency transfer to a different unit or building when:
- The survivor reasonably believes they are in imminent danger of harm from the abuser in the current unit, OR
- The perpetrator of the violence has already been evicted or fled and there is still danger

Housing providers must adopt emergency transfer plans and implement transfers to the extent possible. VAWA 2022 strengthened this requirement to make transfer plans mandatory with specific timelines.

### 3. Lease Bifurcation

VAWA 2013 authorized housing authorities to bifurcate leases — removing the abuser's name from a lease while preserving the victim's tenancy. This eliminated the prior binary: evict everyone, or evict no one.

Conditions: The abuser being removed must have an opportunity to establish their own DV victim status (they could be a victim of a different abuser). If the abuser is the primary tenant and the victim is the second person on the lease, lease bifurcation may result in the victim needing to qualify independently.

### 4. Documentation and Self-Certification

VAWA 2013 clarified how a survivor establishes DV victim status. **A police report is NOT required.** A survivor may establish status through:
- A self-signed certification on HUD Form 5382
- A statement from a DV service provider, legal advocate, or counselor
- A court order
- Medical or other documentation

The self-certification rule protects survivors who fear retaliation for involving police, who have reported to police before but received no response, or who are undocumented and fear deportation.

**This is the aperture for fraud.** Because self-certification requires no police involvement and no third-party corroboration, a person who fabricated a DV claim through a false restraining order could certify DV victim status on HUD Form 5382 without independent investigation.

### 5. Confidentiality Protections

Housing providers may not disclose victim information to any person without informed, written, reasonably time-limited consent from the victim — except as required by law.

## Application to Homeward Bound Marin

Homeward Bound Marin operates transitional housing and emergency shelter programs. If Homeward Bound Marin receives McKinney-Vento / HUD Continuum of Care funding, it is a covered housing program under § 12491 as expanded by VAWA 2013. Christina's placement there as a "DV survivor" would have been made under the self-certification pathway added by VAWA 2013.
