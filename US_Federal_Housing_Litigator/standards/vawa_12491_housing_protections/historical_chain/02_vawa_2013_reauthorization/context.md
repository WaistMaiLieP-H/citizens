# Historical Chain — Layer 2: VAWA 2013 — § 12491 Created
## Expansion to All Covered Housing Programs; Emergency Transfer Plans; Self-Certification

**Era:** 2005–2013 (partial protection period) → 2013 (§ 12491 created)
**Law:** VAWA Reauthorization Act of 2013, Pub. L. 113-4, Title VI

---

## What VAWA 2005 Did (First Housing Provisions)

### VAWA 2005 — Limited Scope

The Violence Against Women and Department of Justice Reauthorization Act of 2005 (Pub. L. 109-162) added housing protections for the first time — but only for:
- **Public housing** under 42 U.S.C. § 1437d (administered by local public housing authorities)
- **Section 8 / Housing Choice Voucher Program** under 42 U.S.C. § 1437f

The protections were similar to the current § 12491 framework:
- Anti-eviction based solely on DV status
- Bifurcation authority (removing perpetrator while retaining victim)
- Documentation framework (police report OR certification from victim service provider OR medical professional)

### What VAWA 2005 Left Out

Between 2005 and 2013, survivors in the following HUD-assisted programs had **no statutory VAWA housing protections**:
- HOME Investment Partnerships (Title II, 42 U.S.C. § 12721 et seq.)
- Section 202 Supportive Housing for the Elderly
- Section 811 Supportive Housing for Persons with Disabilities
- McKinney-Vento Homeless Assistance Act programs (including Continuum of Care — the program potentially covering Homeward Bound Marin)
- Rural Development housing (USDA programs)
- Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) developments

In all of these programs, a survivor could be evicted for DV-related incidents without federal statutory protection. The housing providers in these programs also had no federal authority to justify victim-protective lease modifications.

## The Road to VAWA 2013

### Congressional Hearings (2011–2012)

Congress documented the coverage gap:
- Advocates testified that tens of thousands of VAWA-eligible survivors lived in programs not covered by the 2005 protections
- Housing providers in uncovered programs reported being unable to protect victims without legal authority to modify leases or remove perpetrators
- HUD's own assessment confirmed the patchwork coverage was causing survivors to fall through the gaps

### Political Obstacles

The 2013 reauthorization passed after a contentious congressional debate. The primary obstacles were:
- Expansion of protections to LGBTQ+ survivors (VAWA 2013 was the first VAWA to expressly include sexual orientation and gender identity protections in some provisions)
- Expansion to Native American tribes (tribal jurisdiction over non-Native perpetrators)
- The housing expansion was less contested than these provisions but was part of the package

The bill was passed by the Senate 78-22 and by the House 286-138.

### VAWA 2013 — What § 12491 Created

**Pub. L. 113-4, Title VI** created the current § 12491 framework:

1. **All covered housing programs** — not just public housing and Section 8. The definition of "covered housing program" was expanded to include HOME, Section 202, Section 811, McKinney-Vento (Continuum of Care), USDA rural housing, and other HUD-assisted programs.

2. **Emergency transfer plan — mandatory:** Every covered housing provider must adopt and implement an emergency transfer plan. The emergency transfer plan must be made available to all tenants. Providers must make reasonable efforts to facilitate transfers. This was a new affirmative obligation — it did not exist in VAWA 2005.

3. **Self-certification codified:** The 2013 Act made explicit that a survivor's own written statement (HUD Form 5382 or equivalent) is sufficient documentation — a police report is not required. The victim's word, in writing, is enough. This was a significant access improvement over the 2005 standard.

4. **Confidentiality strengthened:** The 2013 Act added express prohibition on entering victim status information into shared databases and strengthened the written-consent requirement for any disclosure.

5. **Notice requirement:** Covered housing providers must notify all tenants of VAWA housing rights (currently required annually under HUD regulations).

---

## VAWA 2022 — Most Recent Amendments (Pub. L. 117-103)

The Violence Against Women Act Reauthorization Act of 2022 (part of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2022, Pub. L. 117-103, Division W) made further amendments:
- Strengthened HUD enforcement authority and oversight
- Extended protections to transitional housing programs
- Improved survivor access to their own housing records
- Clarified application to LGBTQ+ survivors
- Added requirements for covered housing providers to train staff on VAWA rights

## Why This History Matters for Homeward Bound Marin

Homeward Bound Marin operates programs for homeless individuals and families in Marin County, California. Whether § 12491 applies to Christina's placement depends entirely on whether Homeward Bound Marin received federal funding under a "covered housing program" as defined in § 12491(a)(3) as amended by VAWA 2013.

**Most likely federal funding basis:**
- **Continuum of Care (CoC) Program** (42 U.S.C. § 11381 et seq.) — HUD's primary McKinney-Vento program for homeless assistance; Marin County has an active CoC; Homeward Bound Marin is a likely CoC recipient
- **Emergency Solutions Grants (ESG)** — another McKinney-Vento stream
- **HOME Investment Partnerships** — possible for supportive housing units

If any of these apply, VAWA 2013 / § 12491 applies to the program, and Christina's admission under fabricated DV survivor status is a fraud on a § 12491-covered program — opening the False Claims Act predicate.
