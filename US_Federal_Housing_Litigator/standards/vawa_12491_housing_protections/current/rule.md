## The Rule

### Core Protection — § 12491(b)

An **individual** who is a **victim of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, or stalking** may not be:
1. **Denied admission** to a covered housing program on the basis of that status;
2. **Denied housing assistance** or denied continued assistance on the basis of that status or on the basis of an incident of such violence;
3. **Evicted** from housing on the basis of that status or on the basis of an incident of such violence;
4. **Penalized** by imposition of fees or other consequences for the incident of violence.

The protection applies to the victim regardless of whether the perpetrator is also a program participant.

### Who Is a "Covered Housing Program"

§ 12491 applies to housing providers that participate in federally funded housing programs, including:
- Public housing (42 U.S.C. § 1437 et seq.)
- HUD Section 8 / Housing Choice Voucher Program (42 U.S.C. § 1437f)
- HOME Investment Partnerships Program (42 U.S.C. § 12721)
- Section 202 (elderly housing)
- Section 811 (persons with disabilities)
- Rural Development housing programs
- McKinney-Vento homeless assistance programs

> **Note:** § 12491 is not a general private housing statute. It applies specifically to federally assisted housing programs. For non-subsidized private housing transactions (including the Honeysuckle sale), the companion track is under the FHA (§ 3604) or state law (GOV § 12955) if disability or other protected class status is the basis for discrimination.

### Emergency Transfer Plan — § 12491(e)

Covered housing providers **must** adopt an emergency transfer plan allowing a victim of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, or stalking to transfer to another available unit. The HUD-promulgated emergency transfer plan requirements (24 C.F.R. Part 5, Subpart L) specify:
- The plan must contain procedures for an emergency transfer when the victim reasonably believes they are in danger if they remain
- The housing provider must make reasonable efforts to facilitate the transfer to an available unit in the same building or complex or to a unit in another building managed by the same entity
- The victim may be required to provide documentation (HUD Form 5382, self-certification, or third-party documentation)

### Confidentiality — § 12491(c)

Information provided by a victim regarding the status as a victim of domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, or stalking is:
- **Confidential** — cannot be entered into shared databases, disclosed to conflicting parties, or used against the victim in housing proceedings
- May not be disclosed outside of the relevant housing program without the victim's **written consent** — EXCEPT in limited circumstances (protect health/safety of victim or others; court order requiring disclosure)

**This confidentiality protection is independently actionable.** A housing provider who discloses victim status to a perpetrator, family member, or third party without written consent violates § 12491(c) even if no eviction or denial of housing occurs.

### Documentation Standard — § 12491(c)(3)

A housing provider may require documentation of VAWA-covered violence but:
- The provider CANNOT require a police report as the sole form of documentation
- Acceptable documentation includes: HUD Form 5382 (self-certification form); statement from victim service provider, medical professional, or legal professional; court record; any other documentation reasonably acceptable to the housing provider
- **Self-certification alone is sufficient** — the survivor does not need police involvement, a criminal conviction, or third-party corroboration to invoke § 12491 protections

### Bifurcation — § 12491(b)(3)

Where the perpetrator is also a program participant in the same unit, the housing provider may:
- Bifurcate the lease — evicting or removing the **perpetrator** from the lease while keeping the **victim**
- The victim retains housing while the perpetrator loses it

This is a mandatory protective option, not merely a discretionary one.

### Relationship to Other Laws

**Not a limit on other protections:** Nothing in § 12491 limits rights provided under the FHA (42 U.S.C. §§ 3601-3631), the ADA, the Rehabilitation Act, or any state or local fair housing law. § 12491 protections are additive.

**Non-waiver:** A victim cannot be required to waive § 12491 rights as a condition of participation in a housing program.

## Application to Case Docket

### Fabricated DVRO — Strategic Weapon Against Victim

The core pattern in the case docket is the **inversion of VAWA protections**: a fabricated DVRO was used as a weapon to evict the actual victim (Michael) from housing while placing the perpetrator (Christina Cerretani) in a federally subsidized housing program (Homeward Bound Marin) under a false DV survivor status.

If Homeward Bound Marin is a covered housing program under § 12491 (HUD McKinney-Vento or similar federal funding):
- Christina's placement under a fabricated DV status is a fraudulent invocation of § 12491 protections
- The housing provider who admitted Christina based on false DV documentation may have violated § 12491(c) if it failed to verify (or was deceived into accepting false verification)
- Michael's exclusion from housing attributable to the fabricated DVRO implicates § 12491(b) if he is the actual victim whose housing was denied or disrupted because of an incident of violence falsely attributed to him

### Honeysuckle Sale — VAWA Companion Track

The Honeysuckle sale ($605K; $465K equity extracted) occurred in the context of ongoing domestic violence proceedings. If any element of the sale was coerced by the DV apparatus (fabricated DVRO forcing departure from the property before sale; inability to negotiate sale terms while subject to a falsely obtained court order restricting housing access), § 12491 and FHA § 3604 operate as companion tracks.

### Butsaya Housing Displacement

If Butsaya (Thai wife; Butsaya-Divorce case) was denied housing, evicted from housing, or her housing access was disrupted on the basis of DV-related circumstances in a covered housing program — § 12491 protections apply independently.

## SOL and Enforcement

**Private right of action:** § 12491 does not contain an express private right of action in the statute itself. HUD regulations and case law developments after the 2013 reauthorization have created pathways:
- Direct claims under § 1983 against government housing authorities that participate in federal programs
- HUD complaint mechanism (HUD Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity)
- FHA § 3613 companion claim if the DV-based denial or eviction also constitutes FHA-prohibited discrimination (disability, familial status, sex)

> **Steward note:** The private right of action question under § 12491 is contested and has evolved with circuit splits. Verify the current Ninth Circuit posture before filing a standalone § 12491 claim without an FHA companion. The VAWA 2022 Reauthorization (Pub. L. 117-103) strengthened HUD's enforcement authority.

**HUD FHEO complaint:** File with HUD Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity within **1 year** of the alleged violation. Concurrent with § 12491 if the housing is federally assisted.
