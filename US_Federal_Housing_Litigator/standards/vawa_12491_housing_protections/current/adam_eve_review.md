## ADAM Review (Governing Guidelines / Standards of Creation / SOC)

**Governing Guidelines check:**
34 U.S.C. § 12491 is correctly identified as VAWA's consolidated housing protections provision. The legislative history is accurately traced: housing protections were absent from VAWA 1994 (Pub. L. 103-322); initial public housing / Section 8 protections added in VAWA 2005 (Pub. L. 109-162 § 606); expanded to all "covered housing programs" and consolidated into § 12491 by VAWA 2013 (Pub. L. 113-4); strengthened enforcement in VAWA 2022 (Pub. L. 117-103).

The five core protections are correctly stated: (1) anti-denial of admission; (2) anti-denial of assistance or continued assistance; (3) anti-eviction; (4) anti-penalty; (5) emergency transfer plan requirement. The bifurcation authority (removing perpetrator from lease while retaining victim) is correctly identified as a mandatory option. The confidentiality provision (§ 12491(c)) — no disclosure without written consent; independently actionable — is correctly stated. The documentation standard (self-certification without police report is sufficient) is correctly identified as the key access provision.

The "covered housing programs" scope limitation is correctly identified and disclosed: § 12491 applies to federally assisted housing programs, not to private market transactions. The Honeysuckle private sale falls outside § 12491's scope; the FHA § 3604 and GOV § 12955 tracks are the correct vehicles for the Honeysuckle claim. § 12491 is applicable to the Homeward Bound Marin placement if that program receives federal funding.

The inversion analysis is legally sound and necessary: this case presents the fraud-on-a-VAWA-program pattern (fabricated DV status used to access federally assisted housing), not the standard survivor-protection pattern. Both patterns are within the standard's scope — one as a claim by a survivor (§ 12491(b)), one as a predicate for False Claims Act and § 1983 claims (fraudulent invocation of federal housing program through false DV documentation).

The private right of action flag — contested; 9th Circuit posture requires verification before filing standalone claim — is correctly identified and placed in the steward verify queue. This is the critical disclosure that prevents litigation error.

**PASS.**
