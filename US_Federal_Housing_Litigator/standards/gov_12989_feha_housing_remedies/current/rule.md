# GOV §§ 12989.1 / 12989.2 — Operative Rule

**Standard:** gov_12989_feha_housing_remedies
**Layer:** current/rule
**Status:** VERIFIED (live MCP fetch 2026-04-13)

---

## The Rule

GOV §§12989.1 and 12989.2 are the civil enforcement and remedies provisions for FEHA housing discrimination claims under GOV §12955.

---

## §12989.1 — Civil Action

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who may sue | "Aggrieved person" — any person who suffered discrimination under §12955 |
| SOL | **2 years** from occurrence or termination of discriminatory practice, whichever is last |
| Tolling | Period during CRD administrative proceeding is excluded from the 2-year computation |
| Exhaustion | **NO** — civil action may be filed whether or not CRD complaint filed; no exhaustion required |
| Status of admin complaint | Irrelevant to civil action filing right (unless CRD obtained conciliation agreement with aggrieved person's consent) |
| Intervention | Aggrieved persons may intervene in a pending civil action |

**Critical — SOL computation:**
- SOL is from "occurrence OR termination of discriminatory practice, whichever is last"
- For an ongoing discriminatory pattern (structured equity extraction, document fraud across a multi-step transaction), each act may reset the SOL
- For the Honeysuckle sale: SOL likely runs from close of escrow (transfer of title) OR from date of equity disbursement (whichever is last), potentially from date fraudulent recording was discovered

**No exhaustion is the strategic key:** The CRD complaint process can be slow. A plaintiff does not need to wait for CRD to investigate. Civil action can be filed immediately with no prerequisite.

---

## §12989.2 — Remedies

| Remedy | Standard |
|---|---|
| Actual damages | Available upon finding of discriminatory housing practice |
| Punitive damages | Available upon same finding — no separate "malice, fraud, oppression" showing required (FEHA housing = statutory tort) |
| Temporary injunction/TRO | To prevent defendant from continuing or threatening to engage in unlawful practice |
| Permanent injunction | Same |
| Attorney's fees | Discretionary; prevailing party standard (both plaintiff and defendant may recover); against any party other than the state |
| Expert witness fees | Included in attorney's fees award |
| State-agency offset | If defendant owes money to state agency, that amount may be offset to satisfy the §12989.2 judgment |

**Punitive damages note:** Unlike CIV §3294 (punitive damages), §12989.2 does not require a separate showing of malice, fraud, or oppression. A finding that a discriminatory housing practice occurred is sufficient to authorize punitive damages. The court has discretion over the amount.

**Attorney's fees note:** "Prevailing party" is discretionary — the court may award fees to a prevailing plaintiff or a prevailing defendant. For plaintiffs, this is a standard attorneys' fees provision. For defendants, the standard is typically higher (plaintiff's claim was frivolous or brought in bad faith).

---

## Interaction with CIV §51 / §52 (Unruh Act)

When a §12955 violation is committed by a person or entity subject to CIV §51 (Unruh Civil Rights Act), the §12955 violation is also a per se §51 violation. The §52 remedy — $4,000 per offense, plus actual damages, plus attorney's fees — runs concurrently with the §12989.2 remedy. The plaintiff is entitled to the higher remedy or both if they don't overlap.

---

## SOL Calculation — Honeysuckle

The Honeysuckle sale closed at some point prior to 2026. The 2-year SOL under §12989.1 runs from:
- Transfer of title (close of escrow), OR
- Last act of the discriminatory practice (e.g., last disbursement of equity proceeds), OR
- Discovery of the discriminatory character of the practice if concealed

If the practice was fraudulently concealed, CCP §338(d) discovery rule and equitable tolling arguments apply. Steward must establish the close of escrow date to calculate the civil action filing window.
