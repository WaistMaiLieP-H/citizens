# GOV §§ 12989.1 / 12989.2 — Reasoning

**Standard:** gov_12989_feha_housing_remedies
**Layer:** current/reasoning
**Status:** PROPOSED

---

## Why No Exhaustion

The decision not to require administrative exhaustion before civil action was deliberate. The DFEH (now CRD) complaint process can take years. A victim of housing discrimination facing imminent eviction, displacement, or continued exclusion cannot wait for an administrative process to conclude. The no-exhaustion rule was designed to keep the courthouse door open as a parallel track.

The trade-off: if the CRD obtains a conciliation agreement with the aggrieved person's consent, that agreement closes the civil action track for the same practice. The consent is required — the aggrieved person cannot be bound to a conciliation agreement they didn't agree to.

## Why Punitive Damages Without Malice Showing

The difference between §12989.2's punitive damages and CIV §3294's punitive damages is structural. Under §3294, punitive damages require a showing of malice, fraud, or oppression — the defendant's subjective state of mind is a required element. Under §12989.2, the finding of a discriminatory housing practice is sufficient.

This reflects the legislature's judgment that housing discrimination is inherently culpable conduct that warrants punitive deterrence regardless of the individual defendant's articulated subjective motive. A landlord who has a "no Section 8" policy without consciously thinking "I want to discriminate against Black people" is still engaged in discriminatory conduct that warrants punitive exposure.

## Why the State-Agency Offset

The state-agency offset provision addresses a practical collection problem: a defendant found liable for housing discrimination may also be owed money by a state agency (a licensing refund, a contractor payment, a tax refund). Without the offset provision, the plaintiff would need to enforce the judgment separately while the state independently processes the payment to the defendant. The offset allows the court to order the state to redirect that payment toward satisfying the judgment.

## Strategic Value for Housing Fraud Cases

For a case involving a $356K equity gap, §12989.2 provides:
- Actual damages = the equity extracted
- Punitive damages = multiplier on the equity extracted (in egregious cases, up to 3x or more)
- Attorney's fees and expert witness fees = the entire cost of pursuing the claim
- Injunction = prevent continued discriminatory transaction practices

The full §12989.2 package, combined with §52's $4,000/offense Unruh damages if §51 applies, makes a housing discrimination claim with an equity gap potentially very large. The deterrent effect is the point.
