# Historical Wound — GOV §§ 12989.1 / 12989.2

**Standard:** gov_12989_feha_housing_remedies
**Layer:** historical_chain
**Status:** PROPOSED

## The Wound: A Right Without a Remedy Is Not a Right

### The Pre-FEHA Administrative-Only Track

Before the FEHA created a civil action track, housing discrimination victims in California were limited to the administrative complaint process. A complaint went to a state agency; the agency investigated; the agency decided whether to pursue a hearing. The victim had no independent ability to force a hearing, no timeline guarantee, and no right to damages if the agency declined to pursue the case.

This is the structural wound: a prohibition on housing discrimination without an enforceable private remedy is not a protection — it is a policy preference. An administrative process that can be abandoned at the agency's discretion, that has no mandatory timeline, and that produces no monetary recovery for the victim is not deterrence.

### The Damages Gap

Even after the FEHA civil action track was created, the early remedies were limited. Actual damages — the financial harm the discrimination caused — were available. But in housing discrimination cases, the actual financial harm is often hard to quantify:

- A family denied rental housing because of race doesn't have a clean damages number — they found other housing eventually, but at what cost? How do you quantify the harm of being told you don't belong here?
- A buyer whose home equity was extracted through a discriminatory transaction has a number (the equity gap) but must prove the discriminatory causal link to each dollar extracted

The addition of punitive damages to §12989.2 addressed this gap: when a discriminatory housing practice is found, punitive damages signal that the law will impose consequences beyond the defendant's profit from the discrimination. Without punitives, a sophisticated discriminator could simply price in the expected actual damages liability and continue the practice.

### The Attorney's Fees Wound

Housing discrimination litigation is expensive. Expert witnesses on real estate valuation, appraisal methodology, and financial institution discrimination patterns can cost tens of thousands of dollars. Without attorney's fees, only plaintiffs with large enough damages to make private litigation economically viable could access the courthouse. A tenant denied rental housing for discriminatory reasons — actual harm of perhaps a month's rent differential — would have a $2,000-$3,000 actual damages claim. No attorney can take that case on contingency if fees are not available.

The attorney's fees provision in §12989.2 (including expert witness fees) is what makes small-damages housing discrimination cases litigable. The fee award is the economic engine of fair housing enforcement.

## The Modern Wound — Fraudulent Equity Extraction

For a structured equity extraction case (Honeysuckle), the wound §12989.2 addresses is this: a discriminatory transaction conducted by sophisticated actors — financial institutions, real estate professionals, possibly legal guardians — who know that the victim lacks the resources or capacity to pursue litigation. The actual damages (the equity gap) may run to hundreds of thousands of dollars, but the victim who is under conservatorship control, who has had their phone communications intercepted, who has no independent legal counsel, cannot pursue those damages without a financial incentive that makes the case worth taking.

The §12989.2 punitive damages + attorney's fees package makes the case worth pursuing for an attorney who understands what happened. That is the remedy's purpose.
