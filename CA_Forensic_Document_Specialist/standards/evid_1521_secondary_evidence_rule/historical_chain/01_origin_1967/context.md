# Historical Chain — Origin: Secondary Evidence Rule (1967)

**Standard:** evid_1521_secondary_evidence_rule
**Era:** 1967 — California Evidence Code, operative date

---

## The Best Evidence Rule — What §1521 Replaced

The common law best evidence rule required production of the original writing. It developed in English courts in the 17th-18th centuries (Omychund v. Barker (1744) is often cited as foundational). The premise: handwritten copies were unreliable; the original was the most trustworthy evidence of its own content.

California codified a version of the best evidence rule in pre-1965 evidence statutes. The original Evidence Code (Stats. 1965, ch. 299) included provisions governing proof of content that still reflected best evidence principles.

## The Transition to §1521

The modern §1521 Secondary Evidence Rule represents California's final move away from best evidence. It was enacted as part of a broader reform that recognized:
- Photocopies and digital copies are functionally indistinguishable from originals
- The best evidence rule was being weaponized
- Secondary evidence is often more reliable than oral testimony about document contents

§1521 made secondary evidence the default admissible and confined exclusion to two narrow circumstances.

## OXY Resources (2004) — Key Secondary Evidence Ruling

*OXY Resources California LLC v. Superior Court* (2004) 115 Cal.App.4th 874 is the leading California case applying §1521. The court held:
- When original is unavailable, admission of secondary evidence is within the court's discretion under §1521
- The balance is material dispute + fairness, not the mere preference for the original
- Party claiming unfairness must make a specific showing
