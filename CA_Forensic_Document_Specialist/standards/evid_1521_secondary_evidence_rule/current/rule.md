# EVID § 1521 Secondary Evidence Rule — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** evid_1521_secondary_evidence_rule
**Citizen:** CA_Forensic_Document_Specialist
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

The content of a writing may be proved by secondary evidence UNLESS the court determines either:
1. A genuine dispute exists concerning material terms of the writing AND justice requires exclusion, OR
2. Admission of the secondary evidence would be unfair

Secondary evidence is the DEFAULT — admissible unless exclusion is required. This is the opposite of the former best evidence rule.

---

## What "Secondary Evidence" Covers

Secondary evidence of a writing's content includes:
- Certified copies (most reliable form of secondary evidence)
- Photocopies and scanned copies
- Photographs of the writing
- Testimony about the writing's contents (subject to §1523 limitations on oral testimony)
- Written summaries or extracts (if not barred by other rules)
- Prior versions or drafts that contain the same content

---

## The Two Exclusion Grounds — § 1521(a)(1) and (a)(2)

### (a)(1) — Genuine Dispute About Material Terms + Justice Requires Exclusion

Both elements must be present:
- **Genuine dispute:** The parties actually disagree about what the writing says — not just about its legal effect, but about its content
- **Material terms:** The disputed content is material to the resolution of the case
- **Justice requires exclusion:** The court must affirmatively find that admission of secondary evidence, in light of the genuine dispute, would be unjust — not merely inconvenient

**When this applies:** A party relies on a photocopy of a contract while the other party claims the original had different terms. If this dispute is genuine (not merely argumentative), the original is essential to resolve it. A court may exclude the photocopy to force production of the original.

**Key limitation:** This exclusion ground is NOT a tool for suppressing secondary evidence simply because the opponent wants the original. The dispute must be genuine, the terms must be material, and justice must require exclusion — three separate findings.

### (a)(2) — Unfairness

Secondary evidence may be excluded if admission would be unfair. Factors:
- The secondary evidence was created or obtained through improper means
- The proponent had control of the original and chose not to produce it (when the original was accessible)
- The secondary evidence is selective — it captures only part of the writing in a misleading way

---

## The Two Non-Exceptions — § 1521(b) and (c)

**§1521(b) — Oral testimony still limited by §1523:**
§1521 does not make oral testimony about a writing's contents admissible if §1523 bars it. §1523 generally prohibits oral testimony to prove the content of a writing unless the original is lost or destroyed without the proponent's fraud, the original is not in the proponent's possession, and other conditions. §1521 is about non-oral secondary evidence; §1523 governs oral accounts of document content.

**§1521(c) — Authentication still required:**
§1521 does NOT eliminate the §1401 authentication requirement. Before secondary evidence of a writing's content may be admitted, the writing itself must be authenticated (§1401(b)). §1521 says secondary evidence may be used; it does not say secondary evidence may be used without authentication of the underlying original.

## Application to Active Cases

### OPD 6/2/2009 Missing Report

**Scenario:** The original 6/2/2009 OPD report is missing from OPD's files (confirmed by OPD records pickup October 2025). A witness, contemporaneous note, or other documentation of the report's existence exists as secondary evidence.

**§1521 analysis:**
- The content of the missing report may be proved by secondary evidence (§1521(a))
- The court will exclude only if: genuine dispute about material terms AND justice requires, OR unfairness
- If the original report is missing because OPD suppressed or destroyed it, the proponent is NOT at fault for the original's absence — §1521(a)(2) unfairness argument against exclusion is strong
- **Certified custodian records:** If OPD's index shows a report was logged for 6/2/2009 but is now missing, that index entry is secondary evidence of the report's existence. The custodian's certification of the index (§1560/§1561) authenticates the index.

**§1521(c) authentication requirement:** Authentication of the OPD index or any other secondary evidence of the report's existence and contents is required before secondary evidence is admitted.

### Honeysuckle 19 Unsigned Documents

**Scenario:** The 19 documents were supposed to be executed before the sale closed. If signed versions existed in escrow and were later replaced with unsigned versions, the escrow records (the pre-closing copies) are the originals; the unsigned documents now held are the secondary evidence — but secondary evidence of a different writing.

**§1521 analysis:**
- If the signed versions are missing/destroyed, secondary evidence (escrow records, wire instructions, title company files) of what the documents actually said when signed may be admitted
- The genuine dispute about whether these were ever signed is a §1521(a)(1) issue — but it is also the merits of the fraud case, not merely a dispute about material terms of the writing
- Key argument: the unsigned documents DO NOT prove the writing was unsigned at closing; they prove only that these particular copies are unsigned. What the signed versions said must be established by other evidence.

### Family Law Documents — 16-Year Audit

For documents from court proceedings that were altered, replaced, or have missing pages:
- Certified copies from the court clerk's file are the best secondary evidence
- Transcripts of proceedings at which documents were presented or signed are secondary evidence
- The court clerk's certificate of the file's completeness (or incompleteness) is secondary evidence of what was filed
- §1521(a)(2) unfairness argument: a party who caused the original to be suppressed cannot invoke unfairness to exclude secondary evidence of it

## Practical Strategy — When to Use §1521

| Situation | §1521 Application |
|---|---|
| Original document destroyed/lost (no fault) | Secondary evidence admissible; authentication required |
| Original withheld by opposing party | §1521(c) does not excuse §1401; may also trigger adverse inference instruction |
| Original was altered and original-as-altered is the "secondary evidence" | §1402 applies to the altered document first |
| Certified copy from official custodian | Best form of secondary evidence; minimal §1521(a) challenge |
| Photocopy of disputed document | §1521(a)(1) challenge available if genuine dispute about material terms |
