# EVID §§ 1400-1401 Authentication — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** evid_1400_authentication_definition
**Citizen:** CA_Forensic_Document_Specialist
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

Before any writing may be received in evidence — and before any secondary evidence of a writing's content may be received — the proponent must authenticate it. Authentication means producing evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that the writing IS what the proponent claims it is.

The standard is preponderance-level foundational evidence: enough to sustain a finding, not proof beyond doubt. But the finding must be available — it cannot be based on nothing.

---

## The Two-Part Definition

**Prong (a):** Introduction of evidence sufficient to sustain a finding of identity.
This is the standard method: testimony, chain of custody, distinctive characteristics, expert comparison, reply letter doctrine, etc.

**Prong (b):** Establishment by any other means provided by law.
This refers to the specific authentication methods in §§1410-1421, to judicial notice (EVID §451), to statutory presumptions of authenticity (e.g., certified public records), and to other Code provisions.

---

## The Two-Step Requirement of § 1401

**Step 1:** The original writing must be authenticated before it is received.

**Step 2:** Secondary evidence of the writing's content must also be authenticated before it is received — even if the secondary evidence itself is different from the original.

This means: if a party wants to introduce a photocopy of a document (secondary evidence), they must:
1. Authenticate that the original existed AND
2. Show that the secondary evidence accurately reflects the original

The §1521 Secondary Evidence Rule governs admissibility of secondary evidence; §1401 governs the authentication prerequisite to invoking §1521.

---

## Authentication ≠ Truth

Authentication is a threshold showing — it establishes that a document is what it purports to be. It does not establish:
- That the document's contents are accurate
- That the document was properly executed
- That the document reflects a binding agreement
- That the facts stated in the document are true

A properly authenticated document may still be contested on its contents, authenticity of signatures, accuracy, completeness, or legal effect. Authentication merely admits it to the evidentiary record.

---

## Case Application Framework — The Authentication Ladder

For each document in this case record, the authentication ladder must be climbed:

| Rung | Question | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is this a writing? | EVID §250 — broad definition includes electronic, photographic |
| 2 | Has the proponent identified what writing they claim this is? | §1400(a) — what is the claimed identity? |
| 3 | Is evidence sufficient to sustain a finding of that identity? | §1400(a) — threshold showing |
| 4 | Does the writing appear altered? | §1402 — if yes, second burden arises |
| 5 | If secondary evidence, is the original accounted for? | §1521 — secondary evidence rule |
| 6 | Does the document pass hearsay rules (if offered for the truth)? | §§1200-1300 — hearsay exceptions |

## Application to Active Cases

### Dr. Wiita CST Evaluation — MC-350 Absence

**Authentication failure:** The court-ordered competency evaluation was not produced on Judicial Council Form MC-350. What the proponent offers is an informal document.

**§1400(a) question:** Is this "the writing the proponent claims it is"? The proponent claims it is a COURT-ORDERED COMPETENCY EVALUATION under PC §1368. A court-ordered evaluation under §1368 must be conducted by a court-appointed expert and documented appropriately. The MC-350 form is the Judicial Council's prescribed format.

**Authentication gap:** Without the MC-350 form, there is no documentary chain showing this evaluation was:
- Ordered by the court (no order attached)
- Conducted by a court-appointed expert (no appointment documentation on form)
- Submitted to the court in the prescribed manner

The evaluation exists only as a private document. It cannot be authenticated as a court-ordered document because the chain of documentation for a court-ordered process does not exist.

### Honeysuckle 19 Unsigned Documents

**Authentication gap:** Documents intended to be signed legal instruments (deeds, transfer agreements, disclosures) but bearing no signatures or incomplete signatures fail §1400(a) as to the identity claimed. The proponent cannot establish a signature = an authorized act without the signature.

**Specific issue:** If the proponent claims a document is a "signed seller disclosure" but the signature line is blank, the document is not authenticated as what the proponent claims. It may be authenticated as AN UNSIGNED DRAFT — a different document entirely.

### OPD 6/2/2009 Missing Report

**Authentication required for absence:** To establish that a police report should exist and is missing from the file, the proponent must:
1. Authenticate the official records system — show OPD maintains reports in regular course under identifiable procedures
2. Establish what the records should contain for the relevant date range
3. Obtain a custodian certification of the file's completeness (or incompleteness)

The absence of a record in a properly maintained system is authenticated by the custodian certifying the file's completeness — which, if the report is missing, the custodian cannot fully do.

### Treasury Contradictory Letters

**Authentication of each letter:** Each of the four Treasury letters can be authenticated individually as an official communication from a federal agency. They are official records under EVID §1280/§1600 equivalent (28 CFR Part 16 for federal records). Each can be authenticated by its letterhead, signature, and official seal.

**The contradiction does not destroy authentication:** The letters are individually authentic. The contradiction is relevant to their TRUTH and WEIGHT, not their identity. The contradiction becomes the central evidentiary issue — which letter accurately reflects the bond ownership status — not an authentication question.
