## EVID § 1400 — Authentication: Definition

Authentication of a writing means (a) the introduction of evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that it is the writing that the proponent of the evidence claims it is or (b) the establishment of such facts by any other means provided by law.

---

## EVID § 1401 — Authentication: Required Before Admission

**(a)** Authentication of a writing is required before it may be received in evidence.

**(b)** Authentication of a writing is required before secondary evidence of its content may be received in evidence.

---

## Operative Provisions Parsed

**§1400 — What authentication IS:**
- Introduction of evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that the writing IS what the proponent claims
- OR establishment by any other means provided by law

**§1401 — When authentication is REQUIRED:**
- Before the writing itself is received in evidence
- Before secondary evidence of the writing's content is received

**What authentication is NOT:**
- Authentication is not proof of the writing's truth or accuracy — it is only proof of the writing's identity
- A document may be authenticated (it IS what it claims to be) and still be contested on its content or meaning

## Methods of Authentication (EVID §§ 1410-1421 — not separately built)

The Evidence Code provides specific methods:
- **§1410:** Subscription (signature) — evidence that the writing was signed by a particular person
- **§1411:** Handwriting — expert comparison, personal familiarity, exposure to handwriting
- **§1412:** Photographic evidence — authentication through circumstances
- **§1413:** Witness with personal knowledge of execution
- **§1416:** Handwriting — admission by party
- **§1417:** Handwriting — comparison with authenticated specimen
- **§1418:** Distinctive characteristics — content, substance, internal patterns, or other circumstances
- **§1420:** Sealed document — presumptive authentication from official seal
- **§1421:** Reply letter doctrine — writing authenticated by reference to prior writing it responds to

The California Supreme Court confirmed in *People v. Goldsmith* (2014) 59 Cal.4th 258: circumstantial evidence is sufficient for authentication; no single method is required.
