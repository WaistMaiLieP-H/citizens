## Codification

EVID §§1400-1401 were enacted as part of the 1965 Evidence Code (Stats. 1965, ch. 299, operative January 1, 1967). They replaced scattered common law authentication requirements and former Code of Civil Procedure provisions.

## Pre-1965 California Authentication

Before the Evidence Code, California courts applied common law authentication derived from English practice. The basic requirement — evidence of identity before admission — was established doctrine, but the specific methods and standards were unarticulated beyond case-by-case development.

The 1965 codification brought clarity: §1400 defined authentication; §1401 made it mandatory. Sections §§1410-1421 provided specific methods. The system became predictable and consistently applicable across all courts.

## The Goldsmith Expansion (2014)

*People v. Goldsmith* (2014) 59 Cal.4th 258 confirmed that circumstantial evidence is sufficient for authentication under §1400(a). A document need not be authenticated by a live witness with personal knowledge of its creation — surrounding circumstances (distinctive content, reply-letter logic, internal consistency, metadata) can collectively support a finding of authenticity. This expansion accommodated the authentication of digital evidence (emails, photographs, electronic records) without requiring witnesses who may be unavailable or whose testimony would be burdensome.
