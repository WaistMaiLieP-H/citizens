# Historical Chain — Origin: Business Records Exception (1965)

**Standard:** evid_1271_business_records_hearsay_exception
**Era:** 1965 — California Evidence Code Enactment

---

## Common Law Predecessor — The Shopbooks Exception

The business records exception traces to the English "shopbooks exception" — a common law rule allowing merchants to produce their own account books as evidence of transactions. The rationale: merchants kept books in the regular course of trade; those books were more reliable than potentially interested oral testimony.

California courts applied variants of this rule before 1965 under Code of Civil Procedure provisions.

## 1965 Codification

EVID §§1270-1271 were enacted as part of the 1965 Evidence Code. §1270 broadly defined "business" to include every kind of organized human activity (governmental, nonprofit, professional) — a deliberately expansive definition that ensured no organization could exclude its records from the exception by claiming it wasn't a "business."

§1271's four conditions tracked the Model Code of Evidence (1942) and the Uniform Rules of Evidence — contemporaneous with the Federal Business Records Act (1936) and anticipating the Federal Rules of Evidence (1975, FRE 803(6)).

## Federal Counterpart

California §1271 is substantially similar to FRE 803(6). This alignment means federal case law applying FRE 803(6) is persuasive authority for §1271 analysis, particularly on the trustworthiness prong.
