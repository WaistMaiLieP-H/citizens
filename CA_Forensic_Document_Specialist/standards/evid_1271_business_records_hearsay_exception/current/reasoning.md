## Why This Standard Exists

The hearsay rule — out-of-court statements are inadmissible to prove the truth of what they assert — would, applied literally, exclude almost every business record ever created. No business can produce the person who entered every data point in every record. No hospital can call every nurse who charted every medication. No bank can produce every teller who processed every transaction.

The business records exception recognizes that some out-of-court records are INHERENTLY RELIABLE — more reliable, in many cases, than live testimony from witnesses who may be mistaken, biased, or long-deceased. The conditions that make business records reliable are exactly the conditions §1271 requires: the record was made routinely (not for a special purpose), at the time of the event (when memory is fresh and stakes are neutral), by someone with knowledge, using a system designed for accuracy.

---

## The Reliability Rationale — Why the Four Conditions Work

The four conditions of §1271 track the circumstances that distinguish reliable records from fabricated ones:

**Regular course:** If a business keeps a certain type of record as a routine matter — not because litigation is anticipated — the record was created without the motive to fabricate that litigation-driven records would have. The auto dealer's normal business requires accurate VIN records; inaccurate VIN records are a liability (literally — dealer bond, VEH §11710). The motive to maintain accurate records runs with the business interest.

**At or near the time:** Memory fades and motive to falsify grows with time. A record made when an event is fresh is more accurate than one reconstructed from memory or from notes made later. The timing condition is the reliability anchor.

**Custodian testimony:** Someone who knows the system can explain how it works, where errors are caught, and what a record in the system means. This foundation testimony substitutes for the testimony of the individual who created the record.

**Trustworthiness:** The catchall. Even if a record technically satisfies (a)-(c), if the circumstances of preparation suggest it was created to serve a predetermined conclusion rather than to accurately record reality, it is unreliable.

## The Historical Development

The common law business records exception developed through English and American courts over centuries. The foundational doctrine — shopbooks exception — allowed merchants to produce their own account books as evidence of transactions, because the law recognized that requiring live witness testimony for every commercial transaction was impossible.

The modern business records exception crystallized in the American federal courts through the Federal Business Records Act (1936) and then FRE 803(6) (1975). California codified the exception in EVID §1271 as part of the 1965 Evidence Code.

---

## What Is Lost Without This Rule (and With Its Abuse)

Without the business records exception, courts could not function in commercial disputes — the paper trail that documents every transaction would be inadmissible hearsay.

But the exception has a shadow: it is also the vehicle through which fraudulent records enter evidence. A document that superficially satisfies §1271's conditions — it appears to come from a business, it has a date, it has a custodian — can be admitted if the conditions are not challenged. The fraud detection function of §1271(d) (trustworthiness) is the provision that requires courts and opposing parties to scrutinize not just the form of the record but the circumstances of its creation.

**In this case record:** The pattern of missing, backdated, and unsigned documents represents exactly the kind of §1271 failure that, if challenged at each proceeding, would have exposed the document manipulation pattern. The business records exception was either invoked without challenge or evaded through suppression of the records themselves. The audit function of this Citizen is to retroactively apply §1271's four conditions to each document in the record and identify which documents fail which conditions.
