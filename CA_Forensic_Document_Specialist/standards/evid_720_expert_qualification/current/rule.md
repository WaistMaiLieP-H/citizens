# EVID § 720 Expert Qualification — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** evid_720_expert_qualification
**Citizen:** CA_Forensic_Document_Specialist
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

A person qualifies as an expert if they have *any one* of: special knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education sufficient to qualify them on the subject at issue. The five pathways are disjunctive — the witness need not hold a formal degree or credential. Practical experience alone can qualify.

Against objection, qualification must be shown *before* the expert testifies. The proponent bears the burden of qualifying the witness. Qualification may be shown by any otherwise admissible evidence, including the witness's own testimony (§720(b)).

---

## The Qualification Standard — Not High, But Real

The California standard is permissive but not empty. The witness must have "sufficient" qualification on the specific "subject to which his testimony relates." The subject-match requirement is the operative limit:

- A general physician is not automatically qualified to opine on neurosurgical outcomes
- A document examiner with handwriting experience may not be qualified to testify on digital metadata
- A forensic accountant qualified on financial fraud is not automatically qualified on real estate valuation

The subject-specificity requirement is the mechanism for challenging expert qualification. The question is: is this witness's special knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education sufficient for *this specific subject*?

---

## Challenge Grounds — §720 in Attack Mode

A §720 qualification challenge is made by objection before or during the expert's direct examination. Challenge grounds:

### 1. No qualifying pathway established
The proponent has not shown knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education on the subject. Common when:
- Expert's CV shows adjacent field experience but not the specific subject
- Expert relies solely on reading materials prepared for the litigation
- Expert's training was in a field that has evolved and their training predates critical developments

### 2. Subject mismatch
The expert's qualifications are genuine but mismatched to the opinion offered. Example: a licensed clinical psychologist qualified to assess mental health generally attempts to opine on whether a specific evaluation was conducted in compliance with a mandatory judicial form (Judicial Council MC-350). The qualification to assess mental health does not automatically extend to judicial evaluation procedures and form compliance.

### 3. Qualification shown only by inadmissible evidence
§720(b) allows qualification by "otherwise admissible evidence" — if the foundational evidence supporting the qualification is inadmissible for other reasons, the qualification fails.

## Application to Active Cases

### Dr. Wiita Competency Evaluation — Case #26 (Family Law Audit)

**The core issue:** Dr. Wiita conducted a PC §1368 competency evaluation. The evaluation was not documented on Judicial Council Form MC-350 (which is the mandatory form for court-ordered competency evaluations). The absence of MC-350 creates compounding problems:

**§720 attack on Dr. Wiita's qualification:**
- Dr. Wiita may be licensed as a psychologist (EVID §720 knowledge/training pathway generally satisfied for psychological assessment)
- **But:** the opinion offered is not merely a psychological assessment — it is a *court-ordered evaluation under PC §1368* with specific statutory requirements
- PC §1368 court-ordered evaluations have specific procedural requirements (mandatory form, court appointment, defined scope)
- Qualification to testify on compliance with PC §1368 evaluation procedures requires familiarity with those specific requirements
- If Dr. Wiita was not formally appointed by the court under §1368, the entire predicate for the "court-ordered" evaluation collapses — and the §720 qualification for a *court-ordered* evaluation is gone

**Combined with §1401/§1402 authentication failures:**
The absent MC-350 does not merely affect authentication (§1401 failure) and burden shifting (§1402) — it also goes to whether Dr. Wiita was actually conducting a court-authorized evaluation as opposed to a private assessment. A §720 challenge on this specific subject (PC §1368 compliance and court authorization) is independent of and additional to the authentication challenges.

**The three-layer attack:**
1. §1401: No authentication path for "court-ordered" status without MC-350
2. §1402: Burden shifts to proponent to account for mandatory form's absence
3. §720: Qualification to testify on compliance with PC §1368 court-ordered evaluation procedures is not established by general psychology credentials alone

### Honeysuckle Documents — Unsigned Instruments

**Qualification for document content opinions:** If a document examiner is called to testify that the 19 unsigned documents from the Honeysuckle closing represent the final agreed versions, §720 requires that the examiner have specific knowledge of real estate closing procedures and document execution standards — not merely document examination generally.

### OPD Report Authenticity

**Law enforcement records qualification:** An expert opining on whether OPD custodial records practices conform to standard record-keeping would need demonstrated familiarity with OPD's specific record-keeping systems and law enforcement records custodian standards — not general forensic document examination alone.

## Practical Qualification-Attack Checklist

| Attack Point | What to Show | Evidence Needed |
|---|---|---|
| No qualifying pathway | Expert's CV doesn't cover the specific subject | CV + voir dire questions |
| Subject mismatch | Expert's field ≠ the specific opinion offered | Define the precise opinion; compare to credentials |
| Litigation-prepared expertise | Expert learned the subject only for this case | Deposition: when did you first study this? |
| PC §1368 evaluation procedures | Psychologist not versed in judicial evaluation compliance | MC-350 absence + voir dire on PC §1368 knowledge |
| Obsolete training | Field has changed; expert's training predates key developments | Timeline of expert's training vs. current standards |

## Burden and Timing

- **Proponent's burden:** Show qualification before opinion testimony is given (§720(a))
- **Objecting party's right:** Object and demand voir dire on qualifications before expert testifies
- **Court's role:** Gatekeeper — determines as a preliminary matter of fact (EVID §405) whether the qualification has been shown
- **Standard of review:** Abuse of discretion — trial court's ruling on expert qualification is not reversed unless clearly wrong
