## Statute Text

**(a)** A person is qualified to testify as an expert if he has special knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education sufficient to qualify him as an expert on the subject to which his testimony relates. Against the objection of a party, such special knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education must be shown before the witness may testify as an expert.

**(b)** A witness' special knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education may be shown by any otherwise admissible evidence, including his own testimony.

## Statutory Context

EVID §720 is the gateway qualification standard for all expert testimony in California. It is the threshold the proponent must clear before any opinion testimony is admitted. It works in conjunction with:

- **EVID §801:** Defines the subject matter on which an expert may opine (beyond common experience; helpful to the trier of fact)
- **EVID §802:** Expert may state the basis for their opinion; court may require disclosure of that basis
- **EVID §803:** Expert may rely on inadmissible matter if of the type reasonably relied upon by experts in the field
- **EVID §721:** Cross-examination of experts — opposing party may challenge qualifications and basis

The five qualifying pathways under §720(a) are disjunctive — any one is sufficient: **knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education.** All five need not be present.
