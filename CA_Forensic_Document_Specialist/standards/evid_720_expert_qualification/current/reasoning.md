## Why This Standard Exists — The Expert Testimony Problem

Courts resolve disputes that often involve technical questions beyond the knowledge of judges and jurors. Expert testimony is the mechanism for bringing specialized knowledge into the courtroom. The problem is that "expert" is not a protected title — anyone can claim expertise. §720 is the gatekeeping rule that separates genuine expertise from credential-laundering.

The legislature's choice of five disjunctive pathways (knowledge, skill, experience, training, education) was deliberate. It reflects California's practical orientation: a seasoned tradesperson may have greater relevant expertise than an academic who studied the trade from books. A person who spent twenty years working in a field may know more about its practice than a newly credentialed professional. §720 does not privilege credentials over experience.

At the same time, the "sufficient to qualify him as an expert on the *subject to which his testimony relates*" limitation keeps the rule honest. The qualification must match the opinion. Breadth of credential does not compensate for depth in the specific subject.

---

## The Policy Balance

**Permissive admission:** California favors broad admission of expert testimony. The bar is "sufficient" not "superior." The court does not select the best-qualified expert; it determines whether the proponent's expert clears the threshold.

**Subject specificity as the limit:** The permissive standard stops at the subject boundary. An expert who clears the threshold on subject A does not automatically have an expert ticket for subject B, even if the subjects are related.

**Jury as ultimate evaluator:** Once the threshold is cleared, the weight of the expert's testimony is for the jury. A weakly qualified expert who survives a §720 challenge may be devastated on cross-examination under §721 — but that is the jury's domain, not the court's at the qualification stage.

## The Historical Loss — What Happens Without Gatekeeping

The absence of expert qualification standards has historically allowed:

1. **Manufactured expertise:** Parties hire witnesses whose "credentials" were assembled for litigation, not earned through genuine professional practice. Without subject-specificity requirements, a general expert could testify on anything adjacent to their nominal field.

2. **Credential laundering in court-ordered contexts:** When a court orders an evaluation — a competency evaluation, a custody evaluation, a medical examination — the "court-ordered" label can be weaponized to give the evaluator's conclusions apparent official weight. If the evaluator was not properly qualified for the specific evaluation ordered, and if the mandatory form documenting the court's appointment was never completed, the "court-ordered" character of the evaluation is a fiction. Without §720 challenge, that fiction enters the record as fact.

3. **Authority-washing of private opinions:** A private clinical assessment conducted outside the court's appointment process is not a PC §1368 competency evaluation — it is a private assessment. Calling it court-ordered without the mandatory MC-350 form is an attempt to dress a private opinion in judicial authority. §720 is the tool to strip that dress.

## Application to the Case Record

The PC §1368 competency evaluation conducted by Dr. Wiita without the mandatory Judicial Council Form MC-350 is precisely this pattern:

- The evaluation was presented as a "court-ordered" PC §1368 competency evaluation
- The absence of MC-350 means there is no documentary record of the court's appointment and authorization
- Without court appointment under §1368, Dr. Wiita was conducting a private evaluation — which means the §720 qualification for a *court-authorized PC §1368 evaluation* was never established
- The "court-ordered" label gave the evaluation authority it did not earn

This is not merely an authentication problem (§1401) or a burden-shifting problem (§1402). It is an expert qualification problem: the opinion offered — that the subject was evaluated and found competent/incompetent under the court's §1368 authority — required an expert qualified to conduct exactly that type of evaluation, under court appointment, with the mandatory form in place. None of that was documented.

The three attacks compound each other:
- §1401 failure: no authentication of court-ordered status
- §1402 burden shift: proponent must account for missing mandatory form
- §720 attack: expert not shown to be qualified to conduct this specific type of evaluation (court-authorized §1368) — because the authorization itself is not documented
