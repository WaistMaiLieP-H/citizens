#### OFFENSE A-1: PC § 273.5(A) — Corporal Injury on Spouse/Cohabitant

**Charged offense in this report.**

**Elements:**
1. The defendant willfully inflicted corporal punishment or injury on the victim
2. The victim was the defendant's spouse, former spouse, cohabitant, former cohabitant, person with whom defendant has or had dating relationship, or parent of defendant's child
3. The injury resulted in a traumatic condition

**Element 1 — Willful infliction of corporal punishment/injury:**
SATISFIED. V1 describes two distinct physical acts: (a) S1 pushed her backward into a standing swing set; (b) S1 pushed her against the outside wall and forced his body weight against her until she fell to her knees. These are willful, directed physical acts. Officer Mitchell observed bruising on V1's right arm consistent with the photographs. The wall siding pattern was observed to be "consistent with the injuries V1 sustained on both her legs."

**Element 2 — Qualifying domestic relationship:**
SATISFIED. V1 and S1 are described as legal husband and wife throughout the report. S1 is identified as V1's "legal husband." The report charges PC 273.5(A) with the DOM enhancer.

**Element 3 — Traumatic condition:**
SATISFIED. V1 sustained "numerous bruises on both her right and left legs as well as both her right and left arms" per Officer Mitchell's synopsis and photographs. Bruising constitutes a traumatic condition under California law. Ten photographs were logged as evidence.

**Applicable Defenses:**
- **Self-defense/defense of others:** V1 describes grabbing S1's shirt and following him as he left with the child. S1 could claim V1 initiated physical contact by grabbing his shirt. However, the described force used by S1 — body-weight pinning against a wall until V1 fell to her knees — would be disproportionate to a shirt grab. WEAK defense, evidence does not support proportionality.
- **No traumatic condition:** S1 could contest the severity of bruising. However, ten photographs were taken and Officer Mitchell independently observed bruising. WEAK defense.
- **V1 fabrication:** S1 could claim the report is false. No S1 statement was obtained to evaluate this. INDETERMINATE without S1 statement.

**Statute of Limitations:**
PC § 273.5 is a felony. The SOL for a felony in California is generally 3 years (Pen. Code § 801) unless a longer period applies. The offense date is June 5, 2009. Standard 3-year SOL expired approximately June 5, 2012. **TIME-BARRED for prosecution** as of current date. However, tolling analysis applies:

*Discovery rule/absence from state:* If S1 was outside California for any continuous period after the offense, the SOL tolls during that absence (Pen. Code § 803(d)). **INDETERMINATE** — no evidence of S1's residence history available in this document.

*Delayed discovery — DNA:* Not applicable here.

**Assessment: ALL ELEMENTS SATISFIED. Defense weak. TIME-BARRED absent tolling.**

#### OFFENSE A-3: PC § 236 — False Imprisonment

**Not charged. Implicated by the June 5, 2009 conduct.**

The narrative describes S1 returning inside the residence and locking all doors, locking V1 outside her own home. V1 was then trapped outside the home where she legally resided.

**Elements:**
1. Intentional restraint or confinement of victim's freedom of movement
2. Without lawful privilege
3. Against the victim's will

**Element 1:** SATISFIED. Locking V1 out of her own home against her will confined her freedom. However, the restraint here is arguably the inverse of classic false imprisonment — V1 was locked *out*, not *in*. Analysis: Courts have applied § 236 to situations where a person is prevented from entering or exiting a space to which they have a right. V1 was locked out of her legal residence. **INDETERMINATE** — whether locking a person out of their legal residence (rather than in) satisfies the confinement element requires case law analysis beyond the document. Flagging for referral to appropriate counsel.

**Element 2:** S1 had no lawful privilege to exclude V1 from her legal residence. The TRO was not yet served until June 10. On June 5, V1 had full legal right to be in the home. NOT SATISFIED as to privilege.

**Element 3:** SATISFIED. V1 did not consent to being locked out.

**Assessment: INDETERMINATE on Element 1. SATISFIED on Elements 2-3. TIME-BARRED in any event** (1-year misdemeanor / 3-year felony SOL both expired).

#### OFFENSE A-4: PC § 273a — Child Endangerment

**Not charged. Implicated by conduct on June 5, 2009.**

S1 physically assaulted V1 while holding their seven-month-old child in his arms. The narrative states: "S1 was holding their child in-common and refusing to let V1 see him. Without warning S1 pushed V1 backwards... began to walk out the residence with their child. V1 immediately followed behind S1 grabbing onto the back of his shirt... S1 stopped, turned around and pushed V1 up against the outside of the residence."

This describes S1 initiating and engaging in violent physical conduct while in physical possession of a seven-month-old infant.

**Elements (§ 273a(a) — circumstances likely to produce GBH/death):**
1. Willful act causing child to suffer or be placed in dangerous situation
2. By person with care/custody of child
3. Under circumstances likely to produce great bodily harm or death

**Element 1:** SATISFIED. S1 initiated pushing and wall-pinning violence while holding an infant. An infant dropped or fallen upon during physical violence faces serious injury risk.

**Element 2:** SATISFIED. S1 was holding the child; S1 is the child's father with custodial presence.

**Element 3:** INDETERMINATE. A seven-month-old infant being held during a physical altercation involving pushing and body-weight force faces significant injury risk. Whether this meets the "likely to produce great bodily harm or death" standard is a factual question for a jury. A seven-month-old cannot protect itself, has no neck/head control, and being dropped from adult height produces serious injury risk. ANALYSIS LEANS TOWARD SATISFIED for § 273a(a) or SATISFIED for § 273a(b) at minimum.

**Assessment: ELEMENTS LIKELY SATISFIED. TIME-BARRED** (felony 3-year SOL expired June 2012; misdemeanor 1-year SOL expired June 2010).
