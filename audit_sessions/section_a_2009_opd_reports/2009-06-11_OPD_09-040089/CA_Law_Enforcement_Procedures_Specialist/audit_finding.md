#### Element 2: Mandatory Written Report
**Status: COMPLIANT**

A written incident report was completed. The call was DV-coded. A report exists. The § 13701(c) baseline obligation — that a report be written regardless of arrest outcome — is satisfied.

**Evidence:** Report exists as Incident 09-040089, authored by Mitchell, approved by Chan on 09/10/2009.

**Note on timing:** The assault occurred June 5, 2009. The report was taken June 11, 2009 — **six days after the incident.** Section 13701 requires an "immediate" written report. However, the assault was not reported to OPD until June 11 (OPD had no knowledge of the June 5 assault until Mitchell's encounter). The report was written the same day disclosure was made. The six-day gap reflects victim non-reporting, not officer delay. This element is COMPLIANT.

#### Element 3: Mandatory Report Contents — Six Required Fields

**(a) Physical symptoms of abuse or injury visible to the officer**

**(b) Demeanor of both parties at time of officer arrival**
**Status: DEFICIENT**

The report describes V1's statements and actions but contains **no description of V1's emotional state, demeanor, affect, or presentation** at contact. There is no notation of whether V1 appeared fearful, distressed, calm, or agitated. S1 was not present on scene; his demeanor is necessarily not documented. However, the victim's demeanor at contact is a required field that is entirely absent from the narrative.

This is an auditable omission. The required field was not completed.

**(c) Names and ages of minor children present**
**Status: VIOLATION**

The narrative references a **seven-month-old child-in-common** throughout: the child was held by S1 during the assault, used as an instrument of coercion ("S1 was holding their child in-common and refusing to let V1 see him"), carried out of the home by S1, and later taken by S1 from V1's mother's home.

Despite the child's central role in the described events — including being present during the assault and being physically used in the confrontation — **the child does not appear as a listed person in the report** and is not documented by name or birth date in any field.

**This is a § 13701(c) violation.** The child was not merely "present" at the time of the officer's response (the assault was six days prior), but the child was present during the assault, which is the incident being documented. The mandatory content field for minor children requires documentation of names and ages. The child has no entry in the persons field, no name recorded, and no age beyond the narrative reference to "seven month old."

**(d) Description of any weapons on the premises**
**Status: NON-COMPLIANT / INDETERMINATE**

The Modus Operandi section contains a "Weapon Type 1" field that appears populated with redacted content ("es" — likely redacted). The narrative contains no description of weapons on the premises. Officer Mitchell did not enter the premises (V1 was TRO-restricted from entering), so independent weapon observation was not possible. However, the report does not reflect any inquiry about weapons — no statement that V1 was asked, no notation that no weapons were reported.

Whether S1 had weapons on the premises was not documented. Given that V1 described ongoing fear of S1 and S1 had a restraining order active against V1, a weapons inquiry was warranted. The absence of any weapons documentation is a **DEFICIENT** finding — the field appears in the MO section but the narrative contains no corroborating inquiry.

The narrative describes S1 pushing V1, forcing his body weight against her, locking her out of the home, and later taking the child. These are acts, not documented threats. The report does not reflect whether S1 made verbal threats during the June 5 incident or subsequently.

### STANDARD: PEN_CODE_836_ARREST_AUTHORITY

#### Element 1: Basis for No Arrest
**Status: COMPLIANT — Statutory basis adequately supported**

No arrest was made on June 11, 2009. The assault occurred June 5, 2009 — **six days prior.** S1 was not on scene. The mandatory warrantless arrest provisions of § 836(d) require that probable cause exist and that the arrest be made "as soon as probable cause arises." When probable cause arises from a victim disclosure six days after the incident with the suspect absent, warrantless arrest at that moment is not the operative obligation — the appropriate mechanism is a warrant.

The case was assigned to Detective Jew (Primary Assigned Officer), consistent with the standard practice of converting a field-generated DV report to a detective follow-up for warrant procurement.

**Note:** The case disposition is "CLEARED / COMPLAINT REFU" — indicating the case was cleared by reason of the complainant (victim) refusing to cooperate. This is a distinct clearance category from unfounded or arrest. The complaint refusal clearance does not eliminate the § 13701 report obligation (already satisfied) but is relevant to the arrest analysis.
