#### OFFENSE A: PC § 243(e)(1) — Domestic Violence Battery

**Statutory Elements:**

1. A battery was committed (willful and unlawful use of force or violence upon the person of another — § 242)
2. Against a person in a qualifying domestic relationship: spouse, cohabitant, parent of defendant's child, former spouse, fiancé(e), or current/former dating/engagement partner

**Element-by-Element Analysis:**

**Element 1 — Battery (willful and unlawful use of force or violence):**
- Evidence supporting: Victim's written statement to Ofc. Katz: "She then slapped him with her left hand in the right side of his face while he was holding their son."
- Evidence undermining: Victim's provocation statement — "Go ahead! Hit me!" — is documented. This is relevant to the consent/invitation analysis but does not eliminate battery under California law (consent to battery by a third party striking you is generally not a complete defense, but it bears on the "willful and unlawful" element and on prosecutorial discretion).
- No visible injuries documented. Injury is not required for § 243(e)(1) — any offensive touching suffices.
- **Rating: SATISFIED**

**Element 2 — Qualifying domestic relationship (spouse):**
- Evidence: Victim stated "he and his wife, Christina HARTMANN (S-1) had been married for less than one year." Report lists relationship as "Married / Child-In-Common / Cohabitant."
- **Rating: SATISFIED**

**Defense Analysis:**

**Defense 1 — Consent / Invitation:**
Victim stated "Go ahead! Hit me!" before the slap. Under California law, consent to battery is generally a complete defense to the civil tort but is more complicated in criminal law. Battery requires the force be "unlawful." A verbal invitation to strike does not constitute legally operative consent under California criminal law (courts have held that consent obtained through taunt or provocation does not satisfy the consent defense in DV battery). However, this language is highly relevant to prosecutorial discretion, jury instruction, and reasonable doubt.
- **Defense strength: WEAK as a complete defense; SIGNIFICANT as a mitigating factor**

**Defense 2 — Victim provocation (not a complete defense):**
Provocation is not a complete defense to § 243(e)(1). It may reduce culpability in charging decisions but does not eliminate the elements.
- **Defense strength: NOT A COMPLETE DEFENSE**

**Statute of Limitations:**
- § 243(e)(1) is a misdemeanor.
- SOL for misdemeanors: Cal. Pen. Code § 802 — 1 year from offense date.
- Offense date: February 15, 2009.
- SOL expired: February 15, 2010.
- **STATUS: TIME-BARRED. Prosecution not available.**
- **Pattern relevance: This offense remains fully relevant to pattern analysis, charging decisions in subsequent offenses, and civil litigation context. The SOL expiration affects prosecutability, not historical fact.**

#### OFFENSE B: PC § 273a(b) — Child Endangerment (Misdemeanor Tier)

**Basis for flagging:** Christina Cerretani slapped Michael Hartmann while he was holding their infant. The infant was in the direct physical zone of risk — a slap aimed at a person holding a baby creates risk that the baby could be dropped, struck, or otherwise endangered.

**Statutory Elements (§ 273a(b) — circumstances NOT likely to produce GBH/death):**

**Element 1 — Child placed in endangering situation:**
- Evidence: The slap was directed at Michael Hartmann while he was holding the infant. A physical strike at a person holding a baby places the baby in the zone of physical risk — potential for baby to be dropped or struck.
- The report does not document the officers assessing this risk or the child's condition.
- **Rating: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE (the factual basis exists but was not assessed or documented by officers)**

**Element 2 — Circumstances other than likely to produce GBH/death (misdemeanor tier):**
- A single slap directed at the face of a person holding a baby, in a domestic argument, is more consistent with § 273a(b) (lower-risk circumstances) than § 273a(a) (GBH/death-likely circumstances).
- **Rating: SATISFIED for misdemeanor tier; INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE for felony tier**
