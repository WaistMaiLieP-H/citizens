## Core Litigation Skills

1. **Bane Act Claim Evaluation** — Assess whether facts state a viable §52.1 claim: identify the constitutional or statutory right, the interference by threats/intimidation/coercion, and the specific intent requirement (post-Cornell v. City and County of SF). Distinguish between Bane Act claims requiring specific intent to violate rights vs. claims where the coercion is inherent in the act itself (the Reese/Cornell split).

2. **Unruh Act Claim Evaluation** — Assess whether a business establishment discriminated based on a protected characteristic. Apply the intentional discrimination standard (Harris v. Capital Growth Investors) or the ADA-incorporation provision (§51(f)).

3. **Ralph Act Claim Evaluation** — Assess violence or threats of violence motivated by protected characteristics. Distinguish from the Bane Act (Ralph requires violence/threat; Bane requires interference with rights by coercion).

4. **California Constitutional Claim Analysis** — Evaluate claims under Art. I §1 (privacy), §7 (due process/equal protection), §13 (search and seizure) as independent state grounds. Apply the independent-state-grounds doctrine to preserve state constitutional claims from federal preemption.

5. **State-Court Civil Rights Complaint Drafting** — Draft complaints meeting California pleading requirements (fact pleading, not notice pleading), with proper statutory citations, prayer for statutory damages/treble damages/civil penalties/attorney's fees under §52.

6. **Government Code §815.2 Analysis** — Evaluate public entity vicarious liability for employee civil rights violations. Apply the scope-of-employment test and distinguish from federal Monell's policy-or-custom requirement.

## Doctrinal Skills

7. **Evolution Chain Construction** — Build the complete legislative and case-law history of California civil rights statutes from origin through amendments to current state.

8. **Federal-State Cross-Reference** — Map the relationship between California civil rights claims and their federal counterparts (§52.1 ↔ §1983, Art. I §13 ↔ Fourth Amendment, Gov. Code §815.2 ↔ Monell). Identify where state law provides broader protection than federal law.

9. **Independent State Grounds Doctrine** — Analyze when California constitutional provisions provide broader protection than their federal counterparts, and how to plead state claims to survive federal preemption under Michigan v. Long.

10. **Statutory Damages Calculation** — Calculate remedies under §52 (Bane/Unruh/Ralph): minimum statutory damages ($4,000 per violation under Unruh), treble actual damages, civil penalties, and attorney's fees.
