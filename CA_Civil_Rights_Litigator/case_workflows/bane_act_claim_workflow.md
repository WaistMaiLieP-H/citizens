# Bane Act (§52.1) Claim Workflow

## Intake → Analysis → Drafting → Filing

---

### Phase 1: INTAKE

**Required elements:**
1. A person (defendant — state actor OR private actor)
2. Interfered by threat, intimidation, or coercion (or attempted to)
3. With the exercise or enjoyment of a constitutional or statutory right
4. The right was secured by federal or California Constitution or laws

**Key advantage over §1983:** "Whether or not acting under color of law" — private actors are reachable.
**Key challenge:** Cornell/Reese split on specific intent.

---

### Phase 2: ANALYSIS

**Cornell/Reese screen:**
- Was the coercion INHERENT in the constitutional violation? (Reese — coercion satisfied automatically for excessive force, unlawful detention, etc.)
- Or does the plaintiff need to show SPECIFIC INTENT to violate rights beyond the intent to commit the act? (Cornell)
- Which court will hear the case? Federal courts in CA follow Reese. State courts are split.
- Recommendation: plead both theories.

**Entity liability screen (Gov. Code §815.2):**
- Was the actor a public employee? 
- Was the act within scope of employment? (Mary M. broad test for peace officers)
- §815.2 is RESPONDEAT SUPERIOR — easier than Monell policy/custom
- §52.1(n) WAIVES Gov. Code §§821.6, 844.6, 845.6 immunities for peace officer claims

**Remedies:**
- Compensatory damages
- Statutory damages under §52 ($4,000 minimum per Unruh violation; treble actual damages)
- Injunctive relief (including pattern-or-practice under §52.1(c))
- Attorney's fees (§52.1(i))
- $25,000 civil penalty per violation (if AG/DA/city attorney action under §52.1(b))

**Government Tort Claim:**
- REQUIRED for Bane Act claims against public entities (unlike §1983)
- 6 months for personal injury, 1 year for other claims
- Late-claim relief under Gov. Code §946.6 if deadline missed
- Strategy: file both §1983 (no tort claim needed) AND Bane Act (tort claim needed)

---

### Phase 3: DRAFTING

**State court complaint (fact pleading):**
1. Caption — Superior Court, county
2. Parties — plaintiff, individual defendants, entity defendant
3. Facts — detailed factual allegations (California requires fact pleading, not notice pleading)
4. Causes of action:
   - Bane Act §52.1 against individual defendants
   - Bane Act §52.1 against entity via §815.2 respondeat superior
   - Ralph Act §51.7 (if violence motivated by protected characteristic)
   - Negligence / negligent supervision (alternative theory)
5. Prayer — compensatory, statutory (§52), treble, injunctive, fees, costs

**Or federal court (if paired with §1983):**
- Supplemental jurisdiction under 28 USC §1367
- Bane Act as a state-law claim alongside §1983

---

### Phase 4: FILING

- [ ] Government tort claim presented? (if against public entity)
- [ ] Tort claim rejected or deemed rejected? (6 months deemed rejection under Gov. Code §912.4)
- [ ] Venue: Superior Court for county where conduct occurred, OR county where defendant resides
- [ ] If filing with §1983 in federal court: verify supplemental jurisdiction
- [ ] Serve within 60 days (state court) or 90 days (federal court)
