# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Mary M. v. City of Los Angeles, 54 Cal.3d 202 (1991)
**Statute at issue:** California Government Code Section 815.2 (public entity vicarious liability)
**Version the court was reading:** Section 815.2 as enacted in the 1963 California Tort Claims Act, providing that a public entity is liable for injury proximately caused by an act or omission of an employee within the scope of employment, if the act or omission would have given rise to a cause of action against the employee.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1963/

The California Supreme Court in Mary M. read Section 815.2 in essentially its original 1963 form. The statute was part of the comprehensive Tort Claims Act that replaced California's prior patchwork of sovereign immunity rules. The version the court was reading established the basic framework: public entities are vicariously liable for their employees' torts committed within the scope of employment, on the same respondeat superior principles that apply to private employers.

This case is a landmark because the court applied the 1963 statute to hold that the City of Los Angeles was vicariously liable for a police officer's sexual assault of a woman he detained during a traffic stop. Under the version of Section 815.2 being read, the question was whether the assault fell within the "scope of employment" — and the court held that it did, because the officer used his authority as a police officer to create the opportunity for the assault. The 1963 text does not define "scope of employment," so the court applied common law respondeat superior principles and extended them to encompass willful torts committed through the misuse of job-created authority. This reading of the original statute transformed public entity liability for law enforcement misconduct in California.
