# Eastburn v. Regional Fire Protection Authority, 31 Cal. 4th 1175 (2003)

## The Case

A firefighter sexually harassed a civilian during an emergency call. The victim sued the fire protection authority under Gov. Code §815.2, arguing the entity was vicariously liable for the firefighter's conduct during the scope of employment.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **The scope-of-employment analysis under §815.2 uses the same respondeat superior framework as private employment** — was the tortious conduct "engendered by" or "arising from" the employment? The test is not whether the employer authorized the specific act, but whether the employment created the opportunity and context for the misconduct.

2. **A firefighter who commits sexual harassment during an emergency call is acting within the scope of employment** for §815.2 purposes because the employment placed the firefighter in a position of authority over the victim in an emergency context.

3. **The Mary M. v. City of Los Angeles principle — that peace officers acting under state-granted authority are within scope even when committing unauthorized acts — extends to other public safety officers with comparable authority.**

## The Rule

**Public entity vicarious liability under §815.2 extends to tortious conduct by public safety officers when the employment created the opportunity and authority context for the misconduct.** The Mary M. broad scope-of-employment test applies beyond police to other officers with state-granted authority.
