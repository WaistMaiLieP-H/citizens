# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Eastburn v. Regional Fire Protection Authority, 31 Cal.4th 1175 (2003)
**Statute at issue:** California Government Code Section 815.2 (public entity vicarious liability)
**Version the court was reading:** Section 815.2 as originally enacted in the 1963 California Tort Claims Act, providing that a public entity is liable for injury caused by an employee's act or omission within the scope of employment if the act would give rise to a cause of action against the employee.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1963/

The California Supreme Court in Eastburn read Section 815.2 in its original 1963 form — the same text Mary M. had interpreted twelve years earlier. The statute's core mechanism had not changed: public entity liability is derivative of employee liability and depends on the employee acting within the scope of employment. The court applied this to a firefighter context, examining whether a fire protection authority was vicariously liable for its firefighter's conduct.

This case matters because it tested the boundaries of "scope of employment" under the 1963 statute in a context different from law enforcement. After Mary M. expanded scope-of-employment analysis for police misconduct, the question was how far that expansion reached for other public employees. The version of Section 815.2 the court was reading provides no special rules for different categories of public employees — the same respondeat superior framework applies to firefighters, police officers, and all other government workers. The court's analysis also addressed the interaction with Section 820.2's discretionary immunity: if the employee is immune, the entity inherits that protection under Section 815.2, because the entity's liability depends on the employee's liability. This creates an important limitation — Section 815.2 only reaches employee conduct that is itself tortious and not shielded by a separate Government Code immunity.
