# Gov. Code § 815.2 — Current State (unchanged since 1963)

## Pointer

The current text of §815.2 is at `../../current/`. The statute has never been amended since its enactment as part of the 1963 Government Claims Act.

## Evolution Summary

| Stage | Year | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | 1963 | Enacted as part of Government Claims Act (Ch. 1681, Stats. 1963) |
| 02 | Current | Unchanged — same two subdivisions for 63 years |

## Why It Has Never Been Amended

Section 815.2 states a foundational principle: the public entity is vicariously liable for its employee's torts within the scope of employment. There is nothing to refine. The immunities that limit this principle live in other sections (§§ 818, 820.2, 821, etc.) — those have been amended. The vicarious liability principle itself is architecturally complete as enacted.

## Key Judicial Development (Not Legislative)

The substance of §815.2 has been shaped by case law rather than amendment:
- **Mary M. v. City of Los Angeles (1991)** — respondeat superior reaches intentional torts (sexual assault by police officer) when job authority created the opportunity
- **Eastburn v. Regional Fire Protection Authority (2003)** — if employee has statutory immunity (§820.2 discretionary immunity), entity inherits it under §815.2(b)
- **Board of County Commissioners v. Brown (1997)** — contrasts with Monell's narrower federal standard; §815.2 is automatic vicarious liability, not policy-or-custom
