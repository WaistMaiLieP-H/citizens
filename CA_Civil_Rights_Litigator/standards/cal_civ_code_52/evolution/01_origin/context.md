# Cal. Civ. Code § 52 — Origin Context

## What this statute does

Section 52 is the remedies provision for California's core civil rights statutes. It does not create rights — it provides the money damages, penalties, and enforcement mechanisms that make those rights enforceable.

## The statutes it enforces

- **Section 51 (Unruh Civil Rights Act):** Equal access to business establishments regardless of sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, sexual orientation, citizenship, primary language, or immigration status.
- **Section 51.5:** Discrimination by business establishments based on association or perceived characteristics.
- **Section 51.6:** Sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, sexual orientation, citizenship, primary language, or immigration status discrimination in pricing of services.
- **Section 51.7 (Ralph Civil Rights Act):** Freedom from violence or intimidation by threat of violence because of political affiliation or any characteristic listed in Section 51.
- **Section 51.9:** Sexual harassment in professional relationships (landlord-tenant, doctor-patient, attorney-client, etc.).

## The remedy structure

**Subdivision (a) — Unruh Act violations (Section 51, 51.5, 51.6):**
- Actual damages (special + general)
- Up to three times actual damages (treble damages)
- Minimum statutory damages of $4,000 per offense — even if actual damages are zero
- Attorney's fees as determined by the court

**Subdivision (b) — Ralph Act violations (Section 51.7) and sexual harassment (Section 51.9):**
- Actual damages
- Exemplary damages (no cap)
- Civil penalty of $25,000 per violation (for Section 51.7)
- Three-year statute of limitations for the civil penalty
- Attorney's fees

**Subdivisions (c)-(d) — Government enforcement:**
- Attorney General, district attorneys, and city attorneys can bring civil actions
- AG/DA/city attorney can intervene in federal equal protection cases of general public importance

**Subdivision (e) — Independence:**
- Section 52 remedies are independent of all other remedies — you do not have to choose between this and other laws

**Subdivision (f) — Administrative option:**
- Aggrieved persons can file with the Civil Rights Department (formerly DFEH) under Gov. Code Section 12948

**Subdivision (g) — Construction limitation:**
- Does not require physical modifications beyond what other laws already require

**Subdivision (h) — Damages definition:**
- "Actual damages" means special and general damages (declaratory of existing law)

**Subdivision (i) — Anti-waiver:**
- Subdivisions (b) through (f) cannot be waived by contract (except as provided in Section 51.7)

## Why the $4,000 minimum matters

The minimum statutory damages floor in subdivision (a) is what makes small-stakes civil rights cases viable for representation. Without it, a person denied service at a restaurant would have nominal actual damages — maybe the cost of the meal they did not get. No attorney would take that case. The $4,000 floor, combined with attorney's fees, means the case is worth bringing. This serves the same function as 42 USC Section 1988 fee-shifting in federal civil rights law — making it economically possible for attorneys to take civil rights cases that would otherwise be too small to litigate.
