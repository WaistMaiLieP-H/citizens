# Koire v. Metro Car Wash, 40 Cal. 3d 24 (1985)

## The Case

Koire challenged sex-based price discrimination — "ladies' night" promotions where businesses charged women lower prices than men. He sued car washes and nightclubs that offered discounted services to women under the Unruh Civil Rights Act and sought §52 statutory damages.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **Sex-based price discrimination by businesses violates the Unruh Civil Rights Act.** "Ladies' night" pricing treats customers differently based on sex, which is a protected characteristic under §51.

2. **Section 52 damages are available for each violation.** The statutory minimum ($250 at the time, now $4,000) is available per act of discrimination, regardless of actual damages suffered.

3. **The Unruh Act applies to all forms of sex-based differential treatment in business establishments, not just exclusions or denials of service.**

## The Rule

**Sex-based price discrimination — including promotional pricing that benefits one sex over another — violates the Unruh Act, and §52 statutory damages are recoverable per violation.**

## Citation

Koire v. Metro Car Wash, 40 Cal. 3d 24 (1985). California Supreme Court.
