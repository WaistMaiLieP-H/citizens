# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Koire v. Metro Car Wash (1985) 40 Cal.3d 24
**Statute at issue:** California Civil Code § 52 (Remedies for civil rights violations)
**Version the court was reading:** Section 52 as it existed in the mid-1980s, providing treble damages (up to three times actual damages but not less than $250) for violations of the Unruh Civil Rights Act, plus attorney fees.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin/

The California Supreme Court in Koire read Section 52 when the minimum statutory damage floor was still $250 — far lower than the $4,000 floor that later amendments would establish. The version the court was reading provided the basic remedial framework: treble actual damages with a statutory minimum, plus attorney fees, designed to make private enforcement of civil rights laws worthwhile even when individual actual damages were small.

This case is significant because the court applied the lower-floor version of Section 52 to hold that sex-based price discrimination (ladies' night promotions at car washes and other businesses) violated the Unruh Act and triggered statutory damages. Even with the modest $250 minimum, the treble-damages-plus-fees structure of this version made it possible to challenge discriminatory pricing practices that caused only minor individual harm. The case demonstrated the original design intent of Section 52: the remedy exists to enforce the principle of equal treatment, not just to compensate large losses. The Legislature later raised the minimum substantially, but the architecture the court validated in 1985 remains the same.
