KOIRE v. METRO CAR WASH
40 Cal.3d 24 (1985)
California Supreme Court
Decided: September 30, 1985

---

FACTS:

Metro Car Wash offered women discounts on car washes and free drinks at a bar on certain days ("Ladies' Days"). David Koire, a man, paid full price. He brought an Unruh Civil Rights Act (§ 51) and § 52 claim, arguing that sex-based pricing — giving women discounts not available to men — constituted sex discrimination in a business establishment.

The question: does the Unruh Act prohibit businesses from offering discounts to women if those discounts are not offered to men?

---

HOLDING:

Chief Justice Bird wrote for the Court (6-1).

SEX-BASED PRICING VIOLATES THE UNRUH ACT:
"The Unruh Act prohibits arbitrary discrimination in the provision of services and goods by business establishments on the basis of sex — including discriminating in favor of women at the expense of men."

The Act protects all persons from arbitrary sex-based discrimination in business establishments. It does not permit benevolent discrimination in one direction as a remedy for historical discrimination in another.

"The Unruh Act is not a one-way ratchet. It prohibits sex discrimination by business establishments, period — including discrimination that favors women over men."

§ 52 STATUTORY DAMAGES — CALCULATION:
"For any violation of § 51, § 52 provides:
- Up to three times actual damages, but not less than four thousand dollars ($4,000) for each offense;
- Any attorney's fees that may be determined by the court."

The $4,000 minimum applies per offense, not per plaintiff — meaning each discriminatory transaction is a separate violation supporting a separate minimum statutory damages award.

APPLICATION TO PRICE DISCRIMINATION:
Each occasion on which a man was charged full price while women were charged a discounted rate was a separate violation of § 51, supporting a separate minimum $4,000 damages award under § 52.

---

THE KOIRE FRAMEWORK FOR § 52 REMEDIES:
1. Actual damages (full compensatory loss)
2. Treble damages (up to 3× actual damages)
3. Statutory minimum of $4,000 per offense (regardless of actual damages)
4. Attorney's fees and costs

The statutory minimum is critical: a plaintiff who suffered no measurable actual damages can still recover $4,000 per violation plus attorney's fees. This makes § 51 enforcement economically viable even for small transactions.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Koire establishes:

1. **§ 52 remedy structure**: The $4,000-per-offense minimum plus treble actual damages plus fees is the remedial floor for any Unruh Act violation. Each discrete violation triggers the minimum.

2. **Sex-based discrimination**: Koire confirmed that sex-based discrimination against men is as actionable as sex-based discrimination against women under § 51. This principle applies to:
   - Government programs that systematically treat fathers differently from mothers in custody/family proceedings
   - Business policies that apply different standards to men and women without legitimate business justification

3. **Per-offense calculation**: For cases with multiple discrete violations (each false report, each retaliatory CPS filing, each discriminatory government proceeding), each separate violation is a separate "offense" for § 52 minimum damages purposes. With years of documented conduct, the aggregate statutory minimums can be substantial.
