# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Munson v. Del Taco, Inc. (2009) 46 Cal.4th 661
**Statute at issue:** California Civil Code § 52 (Remedies for civil rights violations)
**Version the court was reading:** Section 52 as it existed in the late 2000s, providing statutory damages of up to three times actual damages but not less than $4,000 for each violation of Section 51, 51.5, 51.6, 51.7, or 54.1, plus attorney fees.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/02_current/

The California Supreme Court in Munson read Section 52 in its modern remedial form, which by then had been amended several times to adjust the minimum statutory damage amount upward from the original floor. The version the court was reading set minimum statutory damages at $4,000 per violation — a figure that had been increased by the Legislature over the years to ensure the remedy remained meaningful and to incentivize private enforcement of civil rights laws.

This case matters because the court addressed what counts as a single "violation" triggering the minimum statutory damages. Under the version of Section 52 being read, each denial of equal access or accommodation is a separate violation carrying its own $4,000 minimum. The court's interpretation of the remedial statute's per-violation structure determines whether a plaintiff recovers a single $4,000 award or multiples of that amount. The minimum damages provision is the engine that makes private civil rights enforcement economically viable — without it, most Unruh Act cases would not justify the cost of litigation.
