# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Austin B. v. Escondido Union School District (2007) 149 Cal.App.4th 860
**Statute at issue:** California Civil Code § 51.7 (Ralph Civil Rights Act)
**Version the court was reading:** The Ralph Act as it existed following its 1976 enactment (Stats. 1976, ch. 1293) and subsequent amendments through the mid-2000s, which prohibited violence or intimidation by threat of violence against persons based on their actual or perceived protected characteristics.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1976/

The court in Austin B. applied Section 51.7 as it had stood since its origin — the core prohibition against violence or intimidation by threat of violence directed at a person because of their race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, political affiliation, sex, sexual orientation, age, disability, or position in a labor dispute. The version the court was reading had already been expanded beyond the original 1976 text to include additional protected categories, but the central mechanism was the same: you cannot use violence or threats of violence to interfere with someone's civil rights.

What matters about this version is that the court confirmed the Ralph Act requires actual violence or intimidation by threat of violence — not just discrimination or hostility. This is a higher bar than the Unruh Act's general anti-discrimination rule. The school district context made clear that § 51.7 reaches institutional settings where violence or credible threats occur, not just private disputes between individuals.
