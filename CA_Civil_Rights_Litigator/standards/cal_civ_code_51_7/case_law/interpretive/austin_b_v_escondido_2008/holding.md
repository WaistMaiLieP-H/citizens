# Austin B. v. Escondido Union School District, 149 Cal. App. 4th 860 (2007)

## The Case

Austin B., a student, alleged that other students committed hate-motivated violence against him at school and that the school district failed to prevent it. He sued the school district under the Ralph Act (§51.7), arguing the district was liable for failing to protect him from violence motivated by his perceived characteristics.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **The Ralph Act creates a cause of action against those who commit hate-motivated violence, but it does not create a duty for third parties (like school districts) to prevent hate violence by others.** The district is not the person who committed the violence or made the threats.

2. **A school district's liability for failing to prevent student-on-student hate violence must be established under other theories** — negligent supervision, Gov. Code §815.6 (mandatory duty), or other statutory bases — not directly under the Ralph Act.

3. **The Ralph Act targets the perpetrator of violence, not bystanders or institutions that failed to intervene.** This distinguishes it from §1985(3) (which reaches conspiracies) and from negligence theories (which reach failures to act).

## The Rule

**The Ralph Act imposes liability on perpetrators of hate-motivated violence, not on third parties who fail to prevent it. Institutional liability for failure to prevent requires other legal theories.**

## Why This Case Matters

Austin B. defines the outer boundary of the Ralph Act — it is a perpetrator-liability statute, not a failure-to-protect statute. For the steward's context, this means that Ralph Act claims against institutions (agencies, departments) that failed to prevent violence must be framed under alternative theories (negligence, Bane Act coercion by the institution itself, Gov. Code §815.6 mandatory duty failure).
