STAMPS v. SUPERIOR COURT (COUNTY OF LOS ANGELES)
136 Cal.App.4th 1441 (2006)
California Court of Appeal, Second District
Decided: January 31, 2006

---

FACTS:

Patricia Stamps was placed in a chokehold and forcibly restrained by her employer's security guards after she refused to submit to a drug test she believed was improperly ordered. She was a Black woman. She brought claims including a Ralph Civil Rights Act (§ 51.7) claim, arguing that the violence was motivated by racial animus.

The trial court sustained the employer's demurrer on § 51.7. Stamps appealed.

---

HOLDING:

Presiding Justice Turner wrote for the panel.

THE RALPH ACT — VIOLENCE MOTIVATED BY RACE, SEX, OR OTHER PROTECTED STATUS:
Section 51.7 "provides that all persons have the right to be free from any violence or threat of violence motivated by a person's membership in protected classes including race, sex, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, medical condition, marital status, and sexual orientation."

For § 51.7 purposes, "violence" includes all forms of unlawful assault and battery — including physical restraint, grabbing, pushing, choking, and similar nonconsensual physical contact.

THE STAMPS ANALYSIS — PLEADING SUFFICIENCY:
To survive demurrer on a § 51.7 claim:
1. The complaint must allege that the defendant committed or threatened to commit violent acts
2. The complaint must allege the violent acts were motivated by the plaintiff's membership in a protected class
3. Allegations of racial slurs, derogatory comments, or the disparity between treatment of similarly situated persons of different races is sufficient circumstantial evidence of racial motivation at the pleading stage

WHAT THE COURT HELD:
Stamps's allegations — that she was a Black woman who was physically assaulted by security guards, combined with the surrounding circumstances — were sufficient to allege racial motivation. The demurrer should not have been sustained.

A COMPLAINT NEED NOT ALLEGE EXPLICIT RACIAL SLURS:
Circumstantial evidence of discriminatory motivation — including that persons of other races in similar circumstances were not physically assaulted — is sufficient to plead a § 51.7 violation.

---

THE STAMPS / § 51.7 PRACTICAL FRAMEWORK:

After Stamps and Austin B.:
1. Section 51.7 is a viable alternative theory for any violence-based civil rights claim in California
2. The protected class motivation can be shown by circumstantial evidence at the pleading stage
3. Explicit statements of animus are not required — the pattern of treatment and context can establish motivation
4. The claim complements the Bane Act (§ 52.1) but has a different structure:
   - § 52.1 (Bane Act): Requires threat/intimidation/coercion + specific intent to violate constitutional rights (post-Cornell)
   - § 51.7 (Ralph Act): Requires violence + protected-class motivation (intent to harm based on class, not specifically to violate rights)

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

The § 51.7 theory is strongest where:
1. Physical force was used against the steward (June 16, 2023 arrest, any prior physical altercations with law enforcement)
2. The use of force was disproportionate compared to how similarly-situated persons would be treated
3. The surrounding circumstances suggest the targeting was based on sex (male parent in DV framework), race, or other protected characteristic
4. Contemporaneous statements or prior conduct suggests discriminatory motivation

COMBINING § 51.7 WITH OTHER CLAIMS:
Optimal pleading combines:
- § 52.1 (Bane Act) for specific-intent-to-violate-rights theory
- § 51.7 (Ralph Act) for protected-class-motivated violence theory
- § 1983 Fourth Amendment excessive force for federal constitutional theory
- Bane Act and Ralph Act remedies (treble damages, fees) as additional leverage
