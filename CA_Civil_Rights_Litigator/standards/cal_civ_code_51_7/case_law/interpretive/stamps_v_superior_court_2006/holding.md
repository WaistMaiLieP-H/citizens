# Stamps v. Superior Court, 136 Cal. App. 4th 1441 (2006)

## The Case

Stamps alleged that defendants engaged in violence and threats of violence against him because of his race. He brought claims under both the Ralph Act (§51.7) and the Bane Act (§52.1). The defendants argued the two statutes were redundant and the plaintiff had to choose one.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **The Ralph Act and the Bane Act are independent causes of action with different elements.** A plaintiff may bring both simultaneously. The Ralph Act requires violence or threat of violence motivated by a protected characteristic. The Bane Act requires interference with constitutional/statutory rights by threat, intimidation, or coercion.

2. **The Ralph Act's "violence or intimidation by threat of violence" is a higher threshold than the Bane Act's "threat, intimidation, or coercion."** The Bane Act can be triggered by coercive conduct that falls short of violence or threat of violence.

3. **When the same conduct satisfies both statutes, the plaintiff can recover under both** — but damages cannot be duplicated (the plaintiff gets the higher recovery, not both stacked).

## The Rule

**The Ralph Act (§51.7) and Bane Act (§52.1) are independent, non-duplicative causes of action. Both may be pleaded from the same facts. Ralph requires violence/threat motivated by protected characteristic; Bane requires coercive interference with rights.**
