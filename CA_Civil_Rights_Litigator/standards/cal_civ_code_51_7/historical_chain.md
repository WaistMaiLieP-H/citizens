# Cal. Civ. Code §51.7 — Historical Chain (Ralph Civil Rights Act)

---

### I. The Wound

In the 1970s, hate-motivated violence was escalating in California — physical attacks, property destruction, and threats directed at people based on race, religion, sexual orientation, and political affiliation. The Unruh Act (§51) prohibited discrimination by businesses, but it didn't address violence. There was no California statute that specifically created a civil cause of action for violence motivated by identity-based hatred.

### II. The Promise (1976)

The Ralph Civil Rights Act was enacted in 1976, predating the Bane Act by eleven years. The promise: **Every person has the right to be free from violence, or intimidation by threat of violence, committed because of who they are.**

The Ralph Act targets violence and threats of violence motivated by protected characteristics — race, religion, political affiliation, and (after amendments incorporating §51's categories) all characteristics protected by the Unruh Act.

### III. Key Features

**1. Violence or threat of violence required.** Unlike the Bane Act (which requires "threats, intimidation, or coercion" to interfere with rights) and the Unruh Act (which requires discrimination by businesses), the Ralph Act specifically targets physical violence and threats of physical violence. This is the hate-crime civil remedy.

**2. Because of protected characteristic.** The violence must be motivated by the victim's actual or perceived protected characteristic. This is a causation requirement — random violence doesn't qualify, even if the victim belongs to a protected class.

**3. False police reports as "intimidation by threat of violence."** Subdivision (b)(2) — added by amendment — defines making or threatening to make a false report to law enforcement as "intimidation by threat of violence." This provision is significant for the steward's case: weaponized 911 calls and false police reports as tools of intimidation are expressly within the Ralph Act's scope.

**4. Anti-waiver protections.** Subdivision (c) contains extensive anti-waiver provisions — a person cannot be forced to waive Ralph Act rights as a condition of any contract.

**5. Speech limitation.** Like the Bane Act, the Ralph Act protects speech — "speech alone shall not support an action" unless it threatens specific violence with apparent ability to carry it out.

### IV. The Relationship to the Bane Act

The Ralph Act and the Bane Act are siblings, not duplicates:

| Element | Ralph Act (§51.7) | Bane Act (§52.1) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Violence or threat of violence | Threat, intimidation, or coercion |
| Motivation | Because of protected characteristic | Interference with constitutional/statutory rights |
| State action | Not required | Not required |
| Causation focus | Why was the person targeted? | What right was interfered with? |

A hate-motivated police beating could support both: Ralph Act (violence because of race) and Bane Act (coercive interference with Fourth Amendment rights). The Bane Act's subdivision (h) expressly states its action is "independent of" the Ralph Act.

### V. The Statute Today

The Ralph Act is California's civil hate-crime statute. It provides damages (including §52 statutory damages), injunctive relief, and attorney's fees for violence or threats of violence motivated by identity-based hatred. The false-police-report provision makes it particularly relevant to patterns of weaponized law enforcement reports.
