# Cal. Civ. Code §51.7 — 2004 Amendment (False Police Reports)

## What Changed

The 2004 amendment added subdivision (b)(2), which defines **making or threatening to make a false report to law enforcement** as "intimidation by threat of violence" within the meaning of the Ralph Act.

## Why It Happened

A pattern had emerged: instead of committing violence directly, perpetrators were weaponizing law enforcement — calling police with fabricated reports to provoke armed responses against their targets. Swatting, false domestic violence reports, and manufactured emergency calls were being used as tools of harassment, particularly against racial minorities and other vulnerable individuals. The violence in these scenarios is indirect but real — an armed police response to a false report creates a genuine threat to the target's life and physical safety.

The 2004 amendment recognized that a false police report is not just a lie. When you file a false report against someone, you are directing the coercive power of the state — including armed officers — against that person. That is intimidation by threat of violence.

## The Text

> "(b)(2) 'Intimidation by threat of violence' also includes making or threatening to make a report to a peace officer or law enforcement agency that falsely alleges that another person has engaged in unlawful activity, if the report is made knowing it to be false or with reckless disregard for the truth of the report, and if the report is motivated by the victim's actual or perceived characteristic."

## Impact

This provision made the Ralph Act directly applicable to weaponized police reports. A person who files a false police report against someone because of their race, sexual orientation, or other protected characteristic is now civilly liable under the Ralph Act for hate-motivated intimidation — even though they never personally committed or threatened violence.

## Steward's Relevance

This provision is directly relevant to patterns alleged in the steward's case: the use of fabricated police reports and weaponized 911 calls as tools of harassment and control. If such reports were motivated by protected characteristics, they fall squarely within the Ralph Act's reach as "intimidation by threat of violence."
