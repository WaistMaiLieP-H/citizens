# Cal. Civ. Code §51.7 — Current State

## Pointer

The current text of §51.7 is at `../../current/`. The statute has been amended incrementally since 2004 (speech protections, anti-waiver provisions) but the core framework is stable.

## The Full Evolution Arc

| Stage | Year | Event | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 1976 | Ralph Civil Rights Act enacted | First CA civil hate-crime statute: violence motivated by identity |
| 02 | 1987 | Category expansion | Incorporated Unruh Act categories by reference (auto-expansion) |
| 03 | 2004 | False police report provision | Weaponized law enforcement reports = "intimidation by threat of violence" |
| 04 | Current | Stable | Full framework: direct violence + indirect violence via false reports |

## What the Evolution Shows

The Ralph Act has evolved in three stages:
1. **1976 — Direct violence.** Physical attacks and direct threats motivated by hate.
2. **1987 — Category expansion.** From fixed enumerated list to dynamic incorporation of Unruh Act categories.
3. **2004 — Indirect violence.** False police reports recognized as a form of hate-motivated intimidation.

The 2004 amendment was the most doctrinally significant: it recognized that in modern society, violence can be inflicted indirectly by weaponizing state power. You don't need to throw a punch — you can file a false police report and let armed officers deliver the violence for you. The Ralph Act now reaches both the direct and indirect forms.
