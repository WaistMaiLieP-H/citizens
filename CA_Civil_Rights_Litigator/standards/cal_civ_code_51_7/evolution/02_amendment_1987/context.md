# Cal. Civ. Code §51.7 — 1987 Amendment (Category Expansion)

## What Changed

The 1987 amendment expanded the Ralph Act's protected categories by incorporating by reference the categories protected under the Unruh Civil Rights Act (§51). Instead of maintaining a separate enumerated list, the Ralph Act now protects against violence motivated by any characteristic covered by the Unruh Act — which itself was being expanded over time.

## Why It Happened

The Bane Act (§52.1) was enacted in 1987. As the Legislature designed the broader Bane Act framework, it also modernized the Ralph Act to align its protected categories with the Unruh Act. This eliminated the need to separately amend the Ralph Act every time a new protected category was added to the Unruh Act.

## Impact

The 1987 amendment created an automatic expansion mechanism: when the Legislature adds a new category to the Unruh Act (as it did with sexual orientation in 1999, genetic information in 2005, immigration status in 2015, etc.), the Ralph Act's protections automatically extend to violence motivated by that category. This is efficient legislative design — one amendment to §51 updates protections across multiple statutes.

## Relationship to the Bane Act

The 1987 session enacted both the Bane Act (new) and the Ralph Act amendments (modernization of existing law). The two statutes were designed as complements:
- **Ralph Act** — violence motivated by identity
- **Bane Act** — interference with rights through coercion

Together they cover the full spectrum of civil rights violations by private and government actors.
