# In re Lance W., 37 Cal. 3d 873 (1985)

## The Case

After Proposition 8 (1982) added Article I, Section 28(f)(2) — the "Truth-in-Evidence" provision — to the California Constitution, the question arose whether California courts could still apply broader exclusionary rules under Article I, Section 13 than the Fourth Amendment requires.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **Proposition 8 eliminated the independent state constitutional exclusionary rule in criminal proceedings.** After Proposition 8, California courts applying the exclusionary rule in criminal cases must follow federal Fourth Amendment standards, not the formerly broader state standards.

2. **The substantive right under Article I, Section 13 still exists independently** — only the remedy (exclusion of evidence) was constrained. California's search-and-seizure provision remains an independent source of rights that may be enforced through civil actions (e.g., Bane Act claims) where the exclusionary rule is not at issue.

## The Rule

**Proposition 8 limited the exclusionary rule remedy in criminal cases to federal Fourth Amendment standards, but did NOT eliminate Article I, Section 13 as an independent source of substantive rights.** The right survives; only the criminal-proceeding remedy was narrowed.

## Why This Case Matters

In re Lance W. is the critical dividing line for Article I, Section 13:
- **Criminal cases:** exclusionary rule follows federal standards (per Proposition 8)
- **Civil cases (Bane Act, damages):** Article I, Section 13 retains full independent force. A search that might survive Fourth Amendment scrutiny could still violate the broader state right, supporting a civil claim.
