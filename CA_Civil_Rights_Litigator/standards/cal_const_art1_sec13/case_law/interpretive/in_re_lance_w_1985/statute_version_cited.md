# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** In re Lance W. (1985) 37 Cal.3d 873
**Statute at issue:** California Constitution, Article I, Section 13 (right against unreasonable searches and seizures), as modified by Article I, Section 28(d) (Proposition 8, "Truth-in-Evidence")
**Version the court was reading:** Section 13 as it existed after the 1982 passage of Proposition 8, which added Section 28(d) to Article I and required that relevant evidence not be excluded under state constitutional grounds if admissible under federal standards.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/02_current/

The California Supreme Court in Lance W. was the first case to directly confront what Proposition 8 did to the independent state grounds doctrine that Brisendine had established. The court read Section 13 alongside the newly enacted Section 28(d) and held that Proposition 8 effectively eliminated California's broader exclusionary rule. Under the version being read, California courts could no longer suppress evidence on independent state grounds if that evidence would be admissible under the Fourth Amendment as interpreted by the U.S. Supreme Court.

This is the case where the pre-Proposition 8 version of Section 13 protections died in practice. The court acknowledged that Section 13's text had not changed, but held that Section 28(d) — enacted by voters as part of a victims' rights initiative — overrode the judicial interpretation that had given Section 13 independent force. The version the court was reading is the version that still governs today: Section 13 still exists, it still says the same words, but its exclusionary rule reach has been pulled back to the federal floor. Any practitioner relying on California search-and-seizure protections must start from this case to understand that the broad Brisendine-era reading is no longer operative.
