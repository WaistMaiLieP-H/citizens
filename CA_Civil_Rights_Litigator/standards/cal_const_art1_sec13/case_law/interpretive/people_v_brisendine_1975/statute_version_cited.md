# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** People v. Brisendine (1975) 13 Cal.3d 528
**Statute at issue:** California Constitution, Article I, Section 13 (right against unreasonable searches and seizures)
**Version the court was reading:** Article I, Section 13 as adopted in the 1974 constitutional revision, before Proposition 8 (1982) limited the independent state grounds doctrine. The text guaranteed the right of people to be secure against unreasonable seizures and searches.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1974/

The California Supreme Court in Brisendine read Section 13 in its original post-1974 revision form, during the era when California courts were actively developing independent state constitutional protections that went beyond federal Fourth Amendment standards. This was the version of Section 13 at its most protective — before voters passed Proposition 8 in 1982 to constrain the state exclusionary rule.

This case is a landmark because the court explicitly held that California's search-and-seizure provision provides greater protection than the Fourth Amendment. Under the version being read, California courts could and did suppress evidence that federal courts would have admitted, based on independent state grounds. The court reasoned that California's constitutional framers intended broader protections for individual privacy. This pre-Proposition 8 reading of Section 13 established the high-water mark for state constitutional search-and-seizure protections. Any practitioner citing Brisendine must understand that the version of Section 13 the court was reading no longer operates at the same level of protection — Proposition 8 pulled the practical scope back toward federal standards in most cases.
