# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** People v. Camacho (2000) 23 Cal.4th 824
**Statute at issue:** California Constitution, Article I, Section 13 (right against unreasonable searches and seizures)
**Version the court was reading:** Article I, Section 13 as it existed after the 1974 constitutional revision and the 1982 passage of Proposition 8 (the "Truth-in-Evidence" provision, Article I, Section 28(d)), which limited the California exclusionary rule to federal constitutional floors.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/02_current/

The California Supreme Court in Camacho read Section 13 in its post-Proposition 8 form. Before 1982, California courts had interpreted Section 13 as providing broader search-and-seizure protections than the Fourth Amendment — meaning evidence that federal courts would admit could still be excluded under independent state grounds. Proposition 8 changed that by adding Section 28(d), which required that relevant evidence not be excluded based on state constitutional grounds if it would be admissible under federal standards. The version the court was reading was the original Section 13 text, now constrained by this later-enacted limitation.

This matters because the court was navigating the post-Proposition 8 landscape where the independent state grounds doctrine still existed in theory but had been significantly narrowed in practice. Under the version being read, California's search-and-seizure protections track federal Fourth Amendment standards in most situations, with only limited remaining areas where the state constitution provides independent protection. Practitioners citing this case need to understand they are working with a provision whose practical scope was reduced by voter initiative, not by any change to Section 13's own text.
