# Cal. Const. Art. I § 13 — Current Status

## Text

The text of Section 13 has never been amended. Not one word has changed since adoption on November 5, 1974 (Proposition 7). The provision reads essentially the same as the Fourth Amendment: the right of the people to be secure against unreasonable searches and seizures shall not be violated, and warrants shall issue only upon probable cause with particularity.

## Practical scope after Proposition 8

While the text is unchanged, Section 13's practical operation was fundamentally altered by Proposition 8 (1982), which added Article I, Section 28(f)(2) — the Truth-in-Evidence provision. The result is a split between the right and its remedy:

- **The right remains broader than the Fourth Amendment.** California courts have held that § 13 provides independent protections beyond the Fourth Amendment and have never reversed that holding.
- **The exclusionary rule remedy tracks federal standards.** After In re Lance W. (1985), California courts cannot exclude evidence on state constitutional grounds beyond what the Fourth Amendment requires.

## Where Section 13 still has independent force

| Context | Independent force? | Explanation |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Criminal exclusionary rule | No | Tracks federal Fourth Amendment standards under § 28(f)(2) |
| Civil suits for damages | Yes | Truth-in-Evidence applies only to criminal proceedings |
| Injunctions against future searches | Yes | Equitable relief based on § 13's independent scope |
| Warrant requirements | Yes | California can impose broader warrant requirements |
| Standing to challenge searches | Potentially | Broader standing arguments available under § 13 |
| Declaratory judgment on rights | Yes | Courts can declare § 13 scope independent of federal law |

## Evolution summary

| Stage | Date | What happened |
|-------|------|---------------|
| 01 — Origin | Nov. 5, 1974 | Proposition 7 reorganized Article I; § 13 adopted (carried over from 1849/1879 constitutions) |
| 02 — Proposition 8 | June 8, 1982 | Added § 28(f)(2); exclusionary rule narrowed to federal standards; § 13 text unchanged |
| 03 — Current | 2026 | Text unchanged since 1974; exclusionary rule still tracks federal standards; independent force survives in non-exclusionary contexts |

## Cross-references

- **Cal. Const. Art. I, § 28(f)(2)** — Truth-in-Evidence provision (Prop 8, 1982); controls exclusionary rule application
- **U.S. Const. Amend. IV** — Federal counterpart; § 13 tracks its language but was historically interpreted more broadly
- **Cal. Const. Art. I, § 1** — Privacy right; provides additional search-and-seizure protections in some contexts
- **In re Lance W. (1985) 37 Cal.3d 873** — Controlling case on Prop 8's effect on § 13 exclusionary rule
- **People v. Brisendine (1975) 13 Cal.3d 528** — Pre-Prop 8 case establishing § 13's independent broader scope
