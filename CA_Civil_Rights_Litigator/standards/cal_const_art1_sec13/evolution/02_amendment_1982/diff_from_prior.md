# Diff from Prior Stage

**Prior:** 01_origin_1974 — Section 13 adopted with independent state-grounds search-and-seizure protections broader than the Fourth Amendment
**This stage:** 02_amendment_1982 — Proposition 8 added § 28(f)(2), eliminating independent state exclusionary rule

## Key Changes

1. **New § 28(f)(2) (Truth-in-Evidence):** Relevant evidence cannot be excluded in criminal proceedings if it would be admissible under the federal Constitution
2. **Exclusionary rule narrowed:** California courts can no longer suppress evidence on independent state grounds beyond what the Fourth Amendment requires (confirmed in In re Lance W., 1985)
3. **Independent state-grounds doctrine curtailed for search/seizure:** The Brisendine/Disbrow line of cases providing broader suppression remedies was effectively overruled for exclusionary rule purposes

## What Stayed the Same

The text of Section 13 was not amended — not one word changed. The underlying right to be free from unreasonable searches and seizures remains broader than the Fourth Amendment under California law. What changed is the remedy: the exclusionary rule in criminal proceedings now tracks federal standards. Section 13 retains independent force in civil contexts, warrant requirement disputes, and non-exclusionary-rule challenges.
