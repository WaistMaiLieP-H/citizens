# Diff from Prior Stage

**Prior:** 01_origin_1974 — Section 7 adopted with due process/equal protection (a) and privileges/immunities (b)
**This stage:** 02_current — Subdivision (a) amended once by Proposition 1 (1979); no further textual changes

## Key Changes

1. **Proposition 1 (1979) — school assignment narrowing:** Added language to subdivision (a) providing that § 7 does not require school assignment or transportation based on race, creed, color, or national origin beyond what federal law requires. This brought California's equal protection standard in line with federal standards specifically and only for school desegregation remedies.
2. **Case law development (1974-present):** All other development of § 7 has been through judicial interpretation, not textual amendment. Key developments include recognition of independent state-grounds due process in family law (Salas v. Cortez), conservatorship (Conservatorship of Wendland), and privacy-adjacent claims (In re Marriage of Burkle).

## What Stayed the Same

Subdivision (b) — the prohibition on legislative favoritism — has never been amended. The core due process and equal protection guarantees of subdivision (a) remain intact and independently enforceable. Outside the school-assignment context, § 7 continues to provide protections that can exceed the Fourteenth Amendment floor.
