# In re Marriage of Burkle, 135 Cal. App. 4th 1045 (2006)

## The Case

Ronald Burkle, a billionaire, sought to seal family court proceedings during his divorce. The question was whether Article I, §7 due process and the public's First Amendment right of access required that family court proceedings remain open.

## What the Court Held

The California Court of Appeal:

1. **Family court proceedings are presumptively open to the public** under both the First Amendment and California constitutional principles. The right of access applies to family law proceedings just as it does to other civil and criminal proceedings.

2. **Sealing orders must satisfy strict scrutiny** — the party seeking closure must demonstrate a compelling interest that outweighs the public's right of access, and the closure must be narrowly tailored.

3. **The due process interests of the parties (Article I, §7) and the privacy interests of children may justify limited sealing** of specific documents, but cannot justify wholesale closure of family court proceedings.

## The Rule

**Family court proceedings are presumptively open. Sealing requires a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. Wealth and status do not justify closure.**

## Why This Case Matters

Burkle establishes that family courts are not secret courts. For the steward's context, this means that the procedural protections of due process — including public scrutiny of judicial proceedings — apply fully to family law cases. Proceedings conducted outside public view must satisfy strict scrutiny, and the presumption of openness serves as a check on judicial and agency conduct in family law matters.
