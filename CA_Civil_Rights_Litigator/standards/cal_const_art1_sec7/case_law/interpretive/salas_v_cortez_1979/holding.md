# Salas v. Cortez, 24 Cal. 3d 22 (1979)

## The Case

A father sought to establish paternity and custody rights. The question was whether due process under the California Constitution (Article I, §7) required that the alleged father be given notice and an opportunity to be heard before the court determined paternity and custody.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **The California due process clause (Article I, §7) independently protects parental rights as fundamental liberty interests.** A parent's right to the care, custody, and management of their children is a fundamental right under both the federal and state constitutions.

2. **Due process in family law proceedings requires meaningful notice and an opportunity to be heard before the state can alter parental rights.** The right to be heard includes the right to present evidence, cross-examine witnesses, and receive a decision based on the evidence presented.

3. **The state constitutional guarantee may provide broader protection than the federal due process clause** in cases involving parental rights, consistent with the independent state grounds doctrine.

## The Rule

**Article I, §7 due process protects parental rights as fundamental. Parents are entitled to meaningful notice and hearing before any adjudication affecting custody or parental status.**

## Why This Case Matters

Salas establishes that the California Constitution independently protects parental rights through its due process clause. For the steward's family law context, this means that any state action affecting custody — including reliance on unverified reports, unsupported evaluations, or procedurally deficient proceedings — must satisfy state constitutional due process requirements that may exceed federal standards.
