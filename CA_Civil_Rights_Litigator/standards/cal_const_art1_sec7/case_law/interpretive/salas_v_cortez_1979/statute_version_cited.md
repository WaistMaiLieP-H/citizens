# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Salas v. Cortez, 24 Cal.3d 22 (1979)
**Statute at issue:** California Constitution, Article I, Section 7 (due process and equal protection)
**Version the court was reading:** Article I, Section 7 as adopted in the 1974 constitutional revision, which provided: "A person may not be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law or denied equal protection of the laws."
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1974/

The California Supreme Court in Salas read Section 7 in its original post-1974 revision form, which consolidated and modernized California's due process and equal protection guarantees. The 1974 revision reorganized the state constitution but the due process concept had roots going back to the 1849 original. The version the court was reading was the first to state both due process and equal protection in a single, clean provision modeled on the Fourteenth Amendment's language but operating as an independent state constitutional guarantee.

This case is significant because the court applied the state due process clause to hold that putative fathers have a constitutionally protected interest in establishing paternity — an interest that cannot be extinguished without adequate procedural protections. The version of Section 7 the court was reading operated independently of federal due process, and the court was willing to find protected liberty interests that federal courts had not yet recognized. This independent-state-grounds approach to due process under the 1974 text established that California's due process clause is not merely a mirror of the Fourteenth Amendment but can provide broader protection in appropriate cases.
