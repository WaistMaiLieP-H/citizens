# Koebke v. Bernardo Heights Country Club, 36 Cal.4th 824 (2005)

## The Case

Birgit Koebke and Kendall French were registered domestic partners who applied for a "Family Membership" at Bernardo Heights Country Club. The club denied them, restricting family memberships to married couples and their children. Koebke sued under the Unruh Civil Rights Act, arguing the policy discriminated based on sexual orientation.

## What the Court Held

Justice Kennard, writing for the majority:

1. **The Unruh Civil Rights Act prohibits discrimination by business establishments based on sexual orientation.** Although the Act lists only "sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, or sexual orientation," the Court had previously held (In re Cox, 1970) that the Act's protections extend beyond the enumerated categories to cover all arbitrary discrimination by business establishments.

2. **A policy limiting family memberships to married couples discriminates against registered domestic partners on the basis of sexual orientation** when the practical effect is to exclude same-sex couples who cannot legally marry.

3. **A business establishment cannot use its "private club" status to avoid Unruh obligations when it operates with significant public-facing commercial characteristics.** The country club's membership practices, advertising, and scale made it a business establishment subject to the Act.

## The Rule

**The Unruh Act prohibits business establishments from using marital status as a proxy for sexual orientation discrimination. A policy that restricts benefits to married couples effectively discriminates against domestic partners when marriage is not equally available.**
