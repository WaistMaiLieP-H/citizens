# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Marina Point, Ltd. v. Wolfsen (1982) 30 Cal.3d 721
**Statute at issue:** California Civil Code § 51 (Unruh Civil Rights Act)
**Version the court was reading:** The original 1959 Unruh Act text as it stood in the early 1980s, before later amendments expanded the enumerated categories. The statute guaranteed equal access to business establishments and listed sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, and national origin as protected categories.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1959/

The California Supreme Court in Marina Point read the 1959-origin version of Section 51 and confirmed what earlier courts had suggested: the list of protected categories is illustrative, not exhaustive. The court held that a landlord's blanket refusal to rent to families with children constituted arbitrary discrimination by a business establishment, even though "familial status" was not specifically listed in the statute. The version the court was reading did not mention children or families at all — the court reached this result by interpreting the statute's purpose.

This matters because the court was working with the bare 1959 text and its open-ended structure. The ruling established that any arbitrary classification by a business establishment violates Section 51, not just classifications matching the named categories. This interpretive principle — born from the original statutory language — has driven Unruh Act jurisprudence ever since and eventually influenced the Legislature to add more categories explicitly.
