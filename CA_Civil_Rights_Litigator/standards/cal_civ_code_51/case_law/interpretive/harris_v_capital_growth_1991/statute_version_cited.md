# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Harris v. Capital Growth Investors XIV (1991) 52 Cal.3d 1142
**Statute at issue:** California Civil Code § 51 (Unruh Civil Rights Act)
**Version the court was reading:** The Unruh Act as it existed in the early 1990s, which guaranteed all persons full and equal accommodations, advantages, facilities, privileges, or services in all business establishments regardless of sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, disability, or other protected characteristics.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1959/

The California Supreme Court in Harris read Section 51 as it had evolved from its 1959 origin. The original Unruh Act listed only "sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, or national origin," but the courts had already interpreted the list as illustrative rather than exhaustive — meaning other forms of arbitrary discrimination by business establishments were also covered. The version the court was reading contained this established judicial gloss on top of the statutory text.

The significance of this version is that the court addressed whether the Unruh Act requires "intentional discrimination" or reaches disparate impact claims. The court held that the Act requires intentional discrimination in the business establishment context. Because the statute had never been amended to add a disparate impact theory, and the legislative history pointed toward intentional conduct, the version the court was reading supported that reading. This remains a foundational interpretation of what the 1959-origin statute actually demands of businesses.
