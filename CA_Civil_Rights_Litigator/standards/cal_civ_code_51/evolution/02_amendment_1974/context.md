# Cal. Civ. Code §51 — 1974 Amendment (Disability Protections)

## What Changed

The 1974 amendment added "disability" (initially termed "physical handicap") to the Unruh Act's list of protected categories. Businesses could no longer exclude or discriminate against people with physical disabilities.

## Why It Happened

Before 1974, a business could refuse service to someone in a wheelchair, deny a blind person with a guide dog entry to a restaurant, or bar a person with a mobility impairment from a hotel — with no state civil rights remedy specific to disability. The Disabled Persons Act (Civ. Code §54 et seq.) addressed some public-access issues, but the Unruh Act — the primary public-accommodations anti-discrimination statute — had no disability coverage.

The 1974 amendment was part of a broader California movement toward disability civil rights that preceded the federal Rehabilitation Act regulations (1977) by three years and the ADA (1990) by sixteen years.

## Impact

Disability became an explicitly protected category under the broadest public-accommodations statute in California. Combined with the later holding in *In re Cox* (1970) and *Marina Point v. Wolfsen* (1982) that the Unruh Act's categories are illustrative (not exhaustive), the 1974 amendment was partly symbolic — arbitrary disability discrimination was arguably already prohibited. But the explicit inclusion meant plaintiffs no longer had to argue that disability was an "arbitrary" classification; it was enumerated.
