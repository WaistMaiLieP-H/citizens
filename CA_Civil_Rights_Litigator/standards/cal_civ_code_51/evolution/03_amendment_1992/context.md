# Cal. Civ. Code §51 — 1992 Amendment (ADA Incorporation)

## What Changed

The 1992 amendment added subdivision (f), which provides that any violation of the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA) also constitutes a violation of the Unruh Civil Rights Act. This is the ADA-nexus provision.

## Why It Happened

The ADA was enacted federally in 1990. It created a new set of disability rights enforceable in federal court, but its remedies were limited — no minimum statutory damages, no treble damages, limited fee-shifting in some contexts. California's Unruh Act, by contrast, carried statutory damages under §52 (at least $4,000 per violation) and treble actual damages.

The Legislature decided to bridge the two: any ADA violation would automatically trigger Unruh liability, with Unruh's superior remedies. This meant a disability access plaintiff in California could file in state court under the Unruh Act, prove an ADA violation, and recover $4,000+ per incident — a far more powerful remedy than federal court offered.

## Impact

The ADA-incorporation provision transformed disability access litigation in California. It created a cottage industry of ADA/Unruh cases — particularly in accessibility of websites, storefronts, and parking lots — because the $4,000 statutory minimum per violation incentivized enforcement. This has been both praised (as an enforcement mechanism that makes ADA real) and criticized (as enabling serial litigants who target small businesses for minor technical violations).

## The Structural Innovation

The incorporation provision is structurally unusual: it makes a federal statute's standards automatically enforceable through a state cause of action with state remedies. A plaintiff doesn't need to bring a federal claim — they can prove the federal standard in state court and recover under state law. This is the inverse of federal preemption: state law amplifies the federal standard rather than being displaced by it.
