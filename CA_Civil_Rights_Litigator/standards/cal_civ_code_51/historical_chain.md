# Cal. Civ. Code §51 — Historical Chain (Unruh Civil Rights Act)

## The Promise, the Expansion, and the Constraint

---

### I. The Wound (pre-1959)

California businesses openly discriminated. Restaurants refused service based on race. Hotels turned away guests based on national origin. The state's common-law "right to exclude" — inherited from English property law — gave business owners broad discretion to deny service for any reason. Civil rights were for public spaces; private businesses were private.

### II. The Promise (1959)

The Unruh Civil Rights Act made the promise: **Every person in California has the right to full and equal access to every business establishment.**

Authored by Assemblymember Jesse Unruh, the 1959 Act declared all persons "free and equal" and prohibited discrimination in "all business establishments of every kind whatsoever." The original enumerated categories were sex, race, color, religion, ancestry, and national origin.

### III. The Expansion (1982)

Marina Point, Ltd. v. Wolfsen held that the enumerated categories are **illustrative, not exhaustive**. The Unruh Act protects against all arbitrary discrimination by businesses — age, family status, physical appearance, wealth, any arbitrary classification. This made the Unruh Act the broadest public-accommodations statute in the country.

### IV. The Constraint (1991)

Harris v. Capital Growth Investors LP held that the Unruh Act requires **intentional discrimination** — disparate impact alone is not enough. This constrained the expansion: while the Act reaches any arbitrary classification (Marina Point), the plaintiff must prove the defendant intended to discriminate (Harris).

### V. The ADA Incorporation

Subdivision (f) provides that any ADA violation is automatically an Unruh Act violation. This created a bridge between federal disability-access law and California's statutory damages regime ($4,000 minimum per violation under §52). The practical result: ADA plaintiffs in California can recover Unruh Act statutory damages for each accessibility violation.

### VI. The Modern Expansion

The Legislature has steadily expanded the enumerated categories: disability, medical condition, genetic information, marital status, sexual orientation, citizenship, primary language, immigration status. The CROWN Act added "protective hairstyles" to the definition of race. Gender identity and expression are included in the definition of sex.

### VII. The Standard Today

The Unruh Act is California's foundational public-accommodations statute. It guarantees full and equal access to all business establishments. Its scope is broader than federal law (any arbitrary classification, not just enumerated categories) but its proof requirement is narrower (intentional discrimination required, not just disparate impact). It provides statutory damages ($4,000 minimum per violation) and attorney's fees through §52.
