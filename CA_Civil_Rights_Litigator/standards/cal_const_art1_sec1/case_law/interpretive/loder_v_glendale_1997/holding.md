# Loder v. City of Glendale, 14 Cal. 4th 846 (1997)

## The Case

The City of Glendale required mandatory drug testing for all job applicants and for current employees seeking promotion. Loder challenged the policy as a violation of the California constitutional right to privacy (Article I, §1).

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court applied the Hill v. NCAA (1994) privacy framework:

1. **Pre-employment drug testing of all job applicants does not violate the California privacy right.** Job applicants have a diminished expectation of privacy in the employment application process, and the government's interest in a drug-free workforce is sufficient to justify the intrusion.

2. **Mandatory drug testing of current employees seeking promotion IS a violation of the California privacy right when applied without individualized suspicion.** Current employees have a greater expectation of privacy than applicants, and blanket testing without suspicion is not narrowly tailored.

3. **The Hill three-part framework governs California privacy claims:** (a) a legally protected privacy interest, (b) a reasonable expectation of privacy, (c) a serious invasion of privacy. If these are shown, the defendant must demonstrate a countervailing interest that justifies the intrusion.

## The Rule

**The California privacy right under Article I, §1 limits government drug testing programs. Pre-employment testing is permissible; blanket testing of current employees seeking promotion requires individualized suspicion.** The Hill framework governs all Article I, §1 privacy claims.
