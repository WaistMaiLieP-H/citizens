# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Loder v. City of Glendale (1997) 14 Cal.4th 846
**Statute at issue:** California Constitution, Article I, Section 1 (inalienable rights including privacy)
**Version the court was reading:** Article I, Section 1 as amended by the 1972 Privacy Initiative, with the three-part Hill v. NCAA framework already established as the governing analytical structure.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/02_privacy_amendment_1972/

The California Supreme Court in Loder read the same post-1972 text of Section 1 that Hill had interpreted three years earlier, but applied it to a specific government-employment context: mandatory drug testing of public employees. The version being read was unchanged from 1972 — the same single sentence adding "privacy" to the inalienable rights — but the court now had the Hill framework as an interpretive overlay.

This case matters because it tested the 1972 amendment's reach against a government employer's interest in a drug-free workforce. The court held that suspicionless drug testing of current city employees violated the privacy right under Section 1, while testing of job applicants did not. The version of the text the court was reading makes no distinction between applicants and employees — the court drew that line through the Hill three-part test, balancing the seriousness of the privacy invasion against the strength of the countervailing interest. This case demonstrates that the 1972 amendment's broad language requires case-by-case balancing, and that even government entities acting as employers are bound by the privacy right the voters created.
