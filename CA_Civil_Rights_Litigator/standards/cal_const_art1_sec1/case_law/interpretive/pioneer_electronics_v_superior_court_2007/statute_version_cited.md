# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Pioneer Electronics (USA), Inc. v. Superior Court (2007) 40 Cal.4th 360
**Statute at issue:** California Constitution, Article I, Section 1 (inalienable rights including privacy)
**Version the court was reading:** Article I, Section 1 as amended by the 1972 Privacy Initiative, applied through the Hill v. NCAA three-part framework to financial privacy claims.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/02_privacy_amendment_1972/

The California Supreme Court in Pioneer Electronics read the same post-1972 version of Section 1 that every privacy case since White v. Davis has relied on. The text had not changed. The court applied it to financial privacy — specifically, whether discovery of personal financial information in litigation triggers Section 1 protections and requires the Hill balancing test.

This case matters because it extended the 1972 amendment's reach into the financial domain, confirming that the privacy right the voters created covers sensitive financial and personal information, not just physical intrusions or surveillance. The version of Section 1 being read was drafted broadly — "privacy" with no limiting language — and the court honored that breadth. The practical effect is that parties seeking financial records in California litigation must justify the intrusion under the Hill framework, even when the information is otherwise relevant. The 1972 amendment continues to operate as a substantive check on information gathering, exactly as its drafters intended.
