# Cal. Const. Art. I, § 1 — Current State (unchanged since 1972)

## Pointer

The current text of §1 is at `../../current/`. The section has not been amended since the 1972 Privacy Initiative added "privacy" to the list of inalienable rights.

## Evolution Summary

| Stage | Year | Event | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 1849 | Original California Constitution | "Inalienable rights" — life, liberty, property, pursuit of happiness |
| 02 | 1972 | Proposition 11 (Privacy Initiative) | Added "privacy" to inalienable rights |
| 03 | Current | Unchanged since 1972 | All development through case law |

## What the Evolution Shows

§1 is the most structurally stable provision in the California Civil Rights Litigator's corpus. The text has changed exactly once in 177 years — when voters added one word ("privacy") in 1972. But that single word created one of the most expansive privacy rights in American law.

## Key Judicial Development (Not Legislative)

All substance since 1972 comes from case law:
- **White v. Davis (1975)** — privacy right is self-executing, enforceable against government surveillance
- **Hill v. NCAA (1994)** — three-part test (legally protected interest + reasonable expectation + serious invasion); applies to private actors
- **Loder v. City of Glendale (1997)** — state privacy right provides broader protection than federal Fourth Amendment in drug testing
- **Pioneer Electronics v. Superior Court (2007)** — financial privacy protected; discovery requests must be narrowly tailored

## Cross-References

- **Art. I, §7** — due process (often pleaded alongside §1 privacy claims)
- **Art. I, §13** — search and seizure (overlapping but distinct from §1 privacy)
- **42 USC §1983** — federal counterpart (no general federal privacy right comparable to §1)
