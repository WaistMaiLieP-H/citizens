# Cal. Const. Art. I, § 1 — Origin (1849)

## What it said

"All men are by nature free and independent, and have certain inalienable rights, among which are those of enjoying and defending life and liberty: acquiring, possessing, and protecting property: and pursuing and obtaining safety and happiness."

## Where it came from

The original California Constitution was adopted at the Monterey Convention in 1849, one year before California became a state. Article I was the Declaration of Rights — the very first article, the first section.

The language comes straight from the natural-rights tradition. John Locke's "life, liberty, and property." Thomas Jefferson's "life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness." The California framers combined both: life, liberty, property, safety, AND happiness. They gave Californians the broadest list of inalienable rights of any state constitution at the time.

## What was missing

Privacy. The word does not appear. In 1849, no one was thinking about privacy as a standalone constitutional right. That would take another 123 years.

## What stayed the same

The core structure has never changed. The list of inalienable rights has only been added to, never subtracted from. The promise that these rights exist "by nature" — not granted by government, but recognized by it — has been there from day one.
