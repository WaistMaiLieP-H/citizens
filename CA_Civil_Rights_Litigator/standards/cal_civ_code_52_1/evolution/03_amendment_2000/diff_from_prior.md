# Diff from Prior Stage

**Prior:** 02_amendment_1998 — speech protections added
**This stage:** 03_amendment_2000 — government-accountability provisions added

## Key Changes

1. **Added (m):** Bane Act violation by peace officer = duty violation
2. **Added (n):** Stripped Government Code immunities from officers sued under Bane Act
3. **Added (o):** Mandatory public entity indemnification for Bane Act judgments against officers

## Practical Impact

| Before 2000 | After 2000 |
|---|---|
| Unclear if Gov Code immunities blocked Bane Act claims against officers | Immunities explicitly stripped for Bane Act claims |
| Individual officer pays judgment (uncollectable) | Public entity must indemnify (guaranteed payment) |
| Bane Act was primarily an anti-hate-violence statute | Bane Act became a state-law §1983 alternative for police misconduct |

## Why This Matters for Practitioners

The 2000 amendment is why plaintiffs' civil rights attorneys in California now routinely plead Bane Act claims alongside §1983 claims. If qualified immunity defeats the §1983 claim in federal court, the Bane Act claim survives in state court — and the government pays the judgment either way.
