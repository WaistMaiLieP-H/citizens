# Cal. Civ. Code §52.1 — 2015 Amendment (AB 953, Racial and Identity Profiling Act)

## What Changed

AB 953 — the California Racial and Identity Profiling Act of 2015 (RIPA) — added provisions to several California civil rights statutes including §52.1. For the Bane Act specifically, the 2015 legislative cycle reinforced the statute's applicability to racial profiling and identity-based policing practices. While the core text of §52.1 was not rewritten, the broader RIPA framework established:

1. **Data collection requirements** for law enforcement stops, tied to the same constitutional rights framework that the Bane Act protects
2. **Reinforced the Bane Act as a remedy** for profiling-based interference with rights, by connecting the newly prohibited profiling practices to the "threat, intimidation, or coercion" framework
3. **Advisory board** (RIPA Board) to analyze stop data and recommend policy changes

## Why It Happened

The deaths of Michael Brown (Ferguson, 2014), Eric Garner (New York, 2014), and the broader Black Lives Matter movement forced California to confront systemic racial profiling. AB 953 was the legislative response: define profiling, require data, and connect the prohibition to existing enforcement mechanisms — including the Bane Act.

## Impact on the Bane Act

The 2015 RIPA legislation did not directly amend §52.1's core text, but it strengthened the statute's ecosystem by:
- Creating a data pipeline (stop data) that generates evidence for Bane Act claims based on profiling
- Establishing that racial and identity profiling violates rights the Bane Act protects
- Making it harder for law enforcement agencies to argue that profiling practices don't involve "threat, intimidation, or coercion" when the practice is systematic

## The Current State

After the 2015 RIPA framework, the Bane Act sits at the intersection of three enforcement mechanisms:
1. **Private civil action** (original 1987 purpose)
2. **Government enforcement** (AG, DA, city attorney — original 1987 and enhanced by 2000 amendments)
3. **Data-driven accountability** (RIPA stop data feeding into evidence for Bane Act claims)

This represents the fullest realization of the Bane Act's original promise: a comprehensive state-law civil rights enforcement framework that reaches both private and government actors.
