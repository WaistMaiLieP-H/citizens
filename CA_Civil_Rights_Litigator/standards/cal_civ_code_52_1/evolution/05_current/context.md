# Cal. Civ. Code §52.1 — Current State

## Pointer

The current text of §52.1 is at `../../current/`. The statute has not been substantively amended since the 2015 RIPA framework.

## The Full Evolution Arc

| Stage | Year | Event | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 1987 | Tom Bane Civil Rights Act enacted | Core prohibition: interference with rights through threat/intimidation/coercion |
| 02 | 1998 | AB 2719 — speech protection | Added (j)-(l): speech alone insufficient unless true threat of violence |
| 03 | 2000 | SB 2051 — government accountability | Added (m)-(o): immunity stripping, mandatory indemnification for officers |
| 04 | 2015 | AB 953 — RIPA framework | Connected profiling data to Bane Act enforcement |
| 05 | Current | Unchanged since 2015 | Full statute: subdivisions (a) through (o) |

## What the Evolution Shows

The Bane Act expanded in three waves:
1. **1987 — the right:** Anyone who uses threats/intimidation/coercion to interfere with any constitutional or statutory right is liable
2. **1998–2000 — the boundaries and teeth:** Speech protection (so the statute isn't a censorship tool) + government accountability (so the statute reaches police misconduct with real money behind it)
3. **2015 — the evidence:** Data collection framework that generates the evidence to prove Bane Act claims at scale

The unresolved question — the Cornell/Reese specific-intent split — is a judicial creation, not a legislative one. The Legislature has not intervened to resolve it. The next evolution stage for §52.1 will likely be either a California Supreme Court decision resolving the split or a legislative amendment clarifying the intent standard.

## Cross-Reference

The Bane Act works in tandem with:
- **§52** (remedies — statutory damages, attorney's fees)
- **§51.7** (Ralph Act — overlapping but narrower; requires violence/threat of violence motivated by protected characteristics)
- **§51** (Unruh — overlapping in business-establishment contexts)
- **42 USC §1983** (federal counterpart — state action required, qualified immunity applies)
