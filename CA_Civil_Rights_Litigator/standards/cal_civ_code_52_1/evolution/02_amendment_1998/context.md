# Cal. Civ. Code §52.1 — 1998 Amendment (AB 2719)

## What Changed

The 1998 amendment added the speech-protection provisions that became subdivisions (j), (k), and (l) of §52.1. These provisions ensure the Bane Act cannot be used to chill constitutionally protected speech:

1. **Subdivision (j):** "Speech alone is not sufficient to support an action brought pursuant to subdivision (a) or (b), except upon a showing that the speech itself threatens violence against a specific person or group of persons; and the person or group of persons against whom the threat is directed reasonably fears that, because of the speech, violence will be committed against them or their property and that the person threatening violence had the apparent ability to carry out the threat."

2. **Subdivision (k):** Reinforces that protected speech cannot form the basis of a Bane Act claim.

3. **Subdivision (l):** Bars content-based speech restrictions in any injunction issued under the Bane Act.

## Why It Happened

After the Bane Act was used in several cases involving protest activity and heated rhetoric, concerns arose that the statute's "threat, intimidation, or coercion" language could be applied to constitutionally protected speech. Protest organizers, political speakers, and advocacy groups raised First Amendment concerns: heated political speech could be characterized as "intimidation" or "coercion," converting the Bane Act from a civil rights sword into a censorship tool.

The Legislature responded by adding an explicit speech carve-out: speech alone cannot support a Bane Act claim unless it constitutes a true threat of violence directed at a specific person.

## The Promise Preserved

The speech amendment keeps the Bane Act focused on its original purpose — reaching conduct that interferes with rights through threats, intimidation, or coercion — while preventing it from becoming an anti-speech weapon. The statute protects civil rights without punishing civil discourse.
