# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Reese v. County of Sacramento (9th Cir. 2021) 888 F.3d 1030
**Statute at issue:** California Civil Code § 52.1 (Tom Bane Civil Rights Act)
**Version the court was reading:** The Bane Act as amended, providing a cause of action when a person interferes or attempts to interfere, by threat, intimidation, or coercion, with the exercise or enjoyment of rights secured by federal or state law.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1987/

The Ninth Circuit in Reese addressed the Bane Act in its post-amendment form, but the central operative language — "interferes by threats, intimidation, or coercion" — traced to the 1987 original. The critical issue was whether Section 52.1 requires a showing of specific intent to violate the plaintiff's constitutional rights, or whether reckless or even negligent conduct suffices. The version of the statute the court was reading does not explicitly state a mens rea standard, which created the split.

This case is significant because the Ninth Circuit's interpretation of the Bane Act diverged from some California state court readings. The federal court held that a plaintiff must show the defendant had a specific intent to violate the plaintiff's rights — that the threat, intimidation, or coercion was aimed at interfering with a known right. This "specific intent" reading came not from any amendment to the 1987 text but from judicial construction of what "interferes by threats, intimidation, or coercion" requires. The split between federal and state interpretations of this same statutory language remains a live issue for litigators choosing between state and federal court.
