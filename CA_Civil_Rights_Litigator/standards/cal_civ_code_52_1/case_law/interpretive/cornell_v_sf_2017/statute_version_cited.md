# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Cornell v. City and County of San Francisco (9th Cir. 2017) 17 Cal.App.5th 766
**Statute at issue:** California Civil Code § 52.1 (Tom Bane Civil Rights Act)
**Version the court was reading:** The Bane Act as amended through the 2010s, prohibiting interference with constitutional or statutory rights by threats, intimidation, or coercion, with remedies including actual damages, attorney fees, and civil penalties.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/02_amendments/

The court in Cornell applied the Bane Act in its modern form and squarely addressed the specific intent question that had been building since Venegas. The version of Section 52.1 being read still carried the original 1987 conduct element — "threats, intimidation, or coercion" — but the amendments had refined the remedial structure. The core interpretive question was not about any textual change; it was about what the unchanged language demands of plaintiffs.

This case matters because the court reinforced the requirement that a Bane Act plaintiff must demonstrate the defendant acted with specific intent to violate the plaintiff's rights. Under the version the court was reading, it is not enough that the defendant's conduct happened to violate a constitutional right. The plaintiff must show the defendant specifically intended to deprive the plaintiff of that right through threatening, intimidating, or coercive conduct. This specific intent requirement — read into the statute rather than written into it — has become the defining feature of modern Bane Act litigation and the primary battleground between plaintiffs and government defendants.
