# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Venegas v. County of Los Angeles (2004) 32 Cal.4th 820
**Statute at issue:** California Civil Code § 52.1 (Tom Bane Civil Rights Act)
**Version the court was reading:** The Bane Act as it existed in the early 2000s, requiring interference or attempted interference with constitutional or statutory rights through threats, intimidation, or coercion.
**Evolution stage:** evolution/01_origin_1987/

The California Supreme Court in Venegas read Section 52.1 in its post-1987 form and confronted the threshold question: what does "threats, intimidation, or coercion" actually mean in the context of a civil rights interference claim? The version the court was reading had been amended to broaden available remedies, but the core conduct element — interference through threats, intimidation, or coercion — remained as the Legislature originally wrote it in 1987.

What matters about this version is that the court established the Bane Act is not just a duplicate of Section 1983. The statute requires something beyond the constitutional violation itself: there must be an accompanying threat, intimidation, or coercion that is independently wrongful. Under the version the court was reading, a plaintiff cannot simply repackage a Fourth Amendment excessive force claim as a Bane Act claim without showing the additional coercive element. This interpretation has shaped every Bane Act case since, and it flows directly from how the 1987 text was written — as a supplement to existing civil rights protections, not a replacement.
