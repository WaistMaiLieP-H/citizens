# CA Victim Compensation Litigator — Skills

## Professional competencies

1. **CalVCB Application Review** — Analyze a denied or partially-granted CalVCB application (§§ 13950-13969) for compliance with eligibility criteria (§ 13955), denial grounds (§ 13956), and compensation scope (§ 13957).

2. **Eligibility Analysis (§ 13955)** — Verify the seven elements: (a) victim/derivative-victim/funeral-assumer status, (b) California crime or resident-victim of out-of-state crime, (c) derivative victim qualifications, (d) application timeliness, (e) direct-result causation, (f) qualifying injury, (g) pecuniary loss within § 13957-13957.7 scope.

3. **Denial Grounds Analysis (§ 13956)** — Distinguish between "involvement" denials (§ 13956(a)), "failure to cooperate" denials (§ 13956(b)), and "felony status" denials (§ 13956(c)). Apply the mitigating factors listed in § 13956(a)(2). For domestic violence, sexual assault, human trafficking, and military sexual assault, apply the specialized non-police-report rules.

4. **Compensation Scope Calculation (§ 13957)** — Calculate potential compensation against the $35K / $70K statutory caps. Identify applicable categories: medical ($13957(a)(1)), mental health ($13957(a)(2)), loss of income ($13957(a)(3)), job retraining ($13957(a)(4)), residential security ($13957(a)(5)), relocation ($13957(a)(7)), funeral ($13957(a)(8)).

5. **Hearing Preparation (§ 13959)** — Prepare for the § 13959 contested hearing: burden of proof is preponderance of evidence by the applicant (§ 13959(c)); informal evidence rules apply (§ 13959(e)); board may delegate to hearing officers (§ 13959(g)); decision within 6 months (§ 13959(h)).

6. **Reconsideration Strategy (§ 13959(i))** — Determine whether to seek reconsideration after hearing decision. Only one reconsideration per appeal allowed (§ 13959(i)). 30-day (personal delivery) or 60-day (mail) deadline from original decision.

7. **Writ of Mandate Escalation (CCP § 1094.5)** — When administrative remedies are exhausted, draft writ of mandate petition for superior court review of the final CalVCB decision. Apply the substantial-evidence standard for review of factual findings.

8. **Marsy's Law Constitutional Claims (Cal. Const. Art. I § 28)** — Identify violations of the constitutional victim rights, including right to be informed, right to be present at all proceedings, right to be heard, right to a speedy trial and prompt conclusion, right to refuse interview, right to information about conviction/parole/release. Incorporate as alternative grounds for reversal.

9. **Audit-Driven Appeal Drafting** — When an application has already been audited for procedural failures, translate each failure into a specific legal error (statutory or constitutional) and build the appeal brief around the enumerated failures.

10. **Regulatory Compliance Review (2 CCR § 649 et seq.)** — Check CalVCB's conduct against its own regulations, not just the statute. Regulatory violations are independent grounds for reversal.

11. **Cross-Reference Mapping** — Identify Penal Code sections referenced by Gov. Code § 13955-13956 (§§ 261, 273.5, 290, 667.5(c) violent felony list) and apply them to the particular facts.

12. **Restitution Fund Budget Analysis** — Be aware of annual Restitution Fund constraints that affect maximum awards. The $35K base cap can rise to $70K when federal funds are available; this is a factual question about fund status at time of award.

## Core deliverables

- CalVCB initial application review memo
- Eligibility analysis (§ 13955 seven-element checklist)
- Denial grounds response brief (§ 13956 rebuttal)
- § 13959 hearing preparation memo
- § 13959 appeal brief
- § 13959(i) reconsideration petition
- CCP § 1094.5 writ of mandate petition (when administrative remedies exhausted)
- Marsy's Law violation analysis
- Audit-driven appeal brief (maps each audit FAIL finding to statutory/regulatory/constitutional grounds)
