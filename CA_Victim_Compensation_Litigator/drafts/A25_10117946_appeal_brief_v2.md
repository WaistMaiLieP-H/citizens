## III. THE THIRTEEN REGULATORY COMPLIANCE FAILURES

### RC-1. SHORTENED DEADLINE — 10 DAYS vs. STATUTORY 30 DAYS
**Finding:** The incomplete application letter (Page 01, Form 04016, dated 8/26/2025) demands: "Please provide the requested information within 10 days."

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13952(c)(2): *"The applicant, within **30 calendar days** of being notified that the application is incomplete, may either supply the additional information or appeal the staff's determination to the board."*

**Argument:** The 10-day deadline stated by CalVCB is less than one-third of the 30-day right the Legislature enacted. A crime victim who reads the letter and believes they have only 10 days may abandon a valid claim — or fail to gather documentation in time — based on CalVCB's misstated deadline. The 30-day period is a statutory guarantee. An agency cannot shorten a statutory deadline in a form letter. Any determination based on the claimant's failure to respond within 10 days is void as a matter of law; the statutory window had not yet expired.

**Relief requested:** If any part of the denial rests on a failure to respond within the stated 10-day period, the denial is legally defective and must be reversed. At minimum, the 30-day period under § 13952(c)(2) must be applied.

---

### RC-2. FAILURE TO INFORM OF RIGHT TO APPEAL COMPLETENESS DETERMINATION
**Finding:** The incomplete application letter states only that failure to respond may result in denial. It does not advise the applicant of the right to appeal the staff's completeness determination to the Board.

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13952(c)(2): The applicant may *"appeal the staff's determination to the board, which shall review the application to determine whether it is complete."*

**Argument:** The Legislature created a specific right to challenge the staff's determination that an application is incomplete — separate from the substantive appeal of a denial. This right allows the applicant to argue that CalVCB is demanding documentation it is not entitled to require, or that the application is in fact complete. By omitting any reference to this right, CalVCB's letter extinguishes it in practice. An applicant who is not told a right exists cannot exercise it.

**Relief requested:** The Board should confirm that the applicant retains the right to challenge the completeness determination under § 13952(c)(2) and that no adverse inference arises from not having exercised a right that was never disclosed.

### RC-3. NO PSYCHOSOCIAL CIRCUMSTANCES ACCOMMODATION
**Finding:** The incomplete application letter and denial letter make no reference to the applicant's economic, psychosocial, or postcrime traumatic circumstances, and make no accommodation for barriers to documentation.

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13954(b)(2)(A): *"The board shall take the applicant's **economic, psychosocial, and postcrime traumatic circumstances** into consideration, and shall not unreasonably reject an application solely for failure to provide information."*

**Argument:** The June 16, 2023 crime occurred against a backdrop of an active criminal case, ongoing civil proceedings, communications fraud, and medical conditions including documented spinal disability. These circumstances create substantial barriers to the routine production of documentation — police reports, medical records, billing statements — within artificially shortened deadlines. Government Code § 13954(b)(2)(A) mandates that the Board consider these circumstances. There is no indication in either the incomplete application letter or the denial letter that this analysis was performed. The denial on a bare documentation ground, without any documented consideration of psychosocial circumstances, violates the statute.

**Relief requested:** The Board should conduct the § 13954(b)(2)(A) analysis before rendering a final determination and document its findings.

### RC-4. CRIME DATE DISCREPANCY — NO TIMELINESS ANALYSIS DOCUMENTED
**Finding:** CalVCB's initial correspondence used crime date 10/20/2007; the denial used 6/16/2018. Neither date is the actual qualifying crime date of 6/16/2023. No timeliness analysis was performed or documented for any of these dates.

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13953(a): Applications must be filed within seven years of the crime, or within seven years of when the victim knew or in the exercise of ordinary diligence could have discovered the injury.

**Argument:** The application references the June 16, 2023 qualifying crime. Filed in August 2025, the application is within two years of the crime — well within the seven-year window. A denial of an application for a 2018 (or 2007) crime cannot constitute a denial of an application for a 2023 crime. The Board must correct the crime date to 6/16/2023 and evaluate the application on the actual qualifying facts.

**Relief requested:** Correction of crime date in CalVCB records to 6/16/2023; re-evaluation of the application on the actual qualifying crime.

### RC-5. SINGLE-SOURCE DENIAL — NO MULTI-SOURCE VERIFICATION
**Finding:** The denial states: "Based on the documentation from ANTIOCH POLICE DEPARTMENT, we were unable to verify the victim was injured." No other sources were consulted.

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13954(a): *"The board shall verify with **hospitals, physicians, law enforcement officials, or other interested parties involved**, the treatment of the victim or derivative victim, circumstances of the crime, amounts paid or received by or for the victim or derivative victim, and any other pertinent information deemed necessary by the board."*

**Argument:** Section 13954(a) lists four categories of verification sources: hospitals, physicians, law enforcement, and other interested parties. CalVCB contacted only law enforcement. There is no indication that any hospital, emergency room, physician, or medical provider was contacted. There is no indication that the applicant was asked to provide medical records or treatment documentation. A denial based on a single police department records check — without any attempt at medical verification — fails the multi-source verification mandate. The verb is "shall verify" — not "may verify" from one source.

**Relief requested:** Verification through hospitals, physicians, and other interested parties in addition to law enforcement before any denial is rendered; applicant be given opportunity to provide supporting medical documentation.

**Argument:** The denial of an application is governed by the same obligation as the processing of an incomplete application: the Board must consider the applicant's circumstances. The denial letter — which is the final substantive determination at the staff level — contains no such analysis. The combination of RC-3 (no consideration during incompleteness phase) and RC-6 (no consideration during denial phase) demonstrates a systemic failure to apply the § 13954(b)(2)(A) mandate at any stage of the proceeding.

**Relief requested:** Documented § 13954(b)(2)(A) analysis in any reconsidered determination.

### RC-7. MISAPPLICATION OF INJURY STANDARD — § 13955(f)(3) NOT ADDRESSED
**Finding:** The denial letter cites § 13955(f)(1) (physical injury) and § 13955(f)(2) (emotional injury + threat of physical injury) but does not address § 13955(f)(3) (standalone emotional injury for qualifying crimes).

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13955(f)(3): Emotional injury alone (without accompanying physical injury or threat) qualifies for crimes including violations of Penal Code §§ 236 (false imprisonment), 207 (kidnapping), 209, 209.5, 220, 236.1, 261, 273a, 273d, and others; felony violations of §§ 187, 203, 206, 646.9; and attempted violations of § 187.

**Argument:** The qualifying crime of June 16, 2023 involved, among other things, false imprisonment (Penal Code § 236) and potentially criminal threats (Penal Code § 422) — both of which generate qualifying emotional injury independent of any physical injury. Section 13955(f)(3) expressly covers emotional injury for these crimes without requiring accompanying physical injury. The denial, citing only (f)(1) and (f)(2), simply ignores the statute's third pathway to eligibility. A denial that does not address an applicable subsection of the governing statute is legally incomplete.

**Relief requested:** Application of § 13955(f)(3) to the June 16, 2023 qualifying crime; evaluation of emotional injury as a standalone qualifying injury.

### RC-9. APPEAL DEADLINE — 45 DAYS HAS NO STATUTORY BASIS
**Finding:** The denial letter states the applicant must appeal within 45 days. The Board denial states reconsideration must be filed within 60 days.

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13959(i): *"The board shall not consider any request for reconsideration filed with the board more than **30 calendar days** after personal delivery or **60 calendar days** after the mailing of the original decision."*

**Argument:** The statute provides two timelines: 30 days for personal delivery; 60 days for mailing. The denial letter's 45-day deadline corresponds to neither. It is shorter than the mailing window (60 days) and longer than the personal delivery window (30 days). There is no statutory basis for a 45-day appeal deadline. Because the denial was presumably mailed, the applicable statutory deadline is 60 days — and any appeal filed within 60 days of the mailing of the October 8, 2025 denial is timely. If the appeal has been docketed as untimely under the 45-day rule, it must be reinstated as timely under the 60-day mailing rule of § 13959(i).

**Relief requested:** Confirmation that all filings within the 60-day statutory mailing window are treated as timely; no adverse ruling based on the non-statutory 45-day deadline.
