## PRELIMINARY STATEMENT: EQUITABLE TOLLING

Government Code § 13959(i) provides that the Board shall not consider any request for reconsideration filed more than **30 calendar days after personal delivery** or **60 calendar days after mailing** of the original decision.

The Board's denial confirmation dated December 3, 2025 stated: *"If you would like to request reconsideration, you must do so within 45 days of this letter."* No statute authorizes a 45-day reconsideration deadline. The statutory deadline is 60 days from mailing. The denial letter created a false and shorter deadline — misstating the applicant's window to act by 25%.

**Applicant files this request for reconsideration and relies on equitable tolling as follows:**

1. **Agency-created misleading deadline.** Where a government agency's own communications affirmatively misstate a deadline — giving a shorter deadline than the statute provides — the equitable tolling doctrine prevents the agency from using its own misstatement to bar the claimant's right. A victim who reads "45 days" cannot be penalized for relying on what the agency told him. *See Lantzy v. Centex Homes* (2003) 31 Cal.4th 363 (equitable tolling applies when claimant is affirmatively misled about limitations period); *Aryeh v. Canon Business Solutions* (2013) 55 Cal.4th 1185 (equitable tolling "may be applied in carefully considered circumstances to prevent the unjust technical forfeiture of causes of action").

2. **Shorter stated deadline did not bar the right to the longer period.** A victim who acted within the non-statutory 45-day window but is now past that window still has rights under the statutory 60-day mailing period. The agency cannot extend a right by misstating it as shorter and then use the passage of the stated period as a bar.

3. **Active pursuit of administrative remedies tolls any deadline.** Applicant has actively pursued all available administrative remedies, including conducting a comprehensive pre-filing audit and attempting compliance with all CalVCB communications. This diligence supports equitable tolling of any deadline.

4. **CalVCB's procedural violations independently extend the window.** The thirteen regulatory compliance failures documented herein — including failure to disclose appeal rights (RC-2), failure to notify of VWAC advocacy (RC-11), failure to provide Marsy's Law rights notice (RC-12), and the wrong crime date used throughout (RC-4) — each independently constitute grounds for equitable extension of the filing window. A victim cannot be required to perfect an appeal of a denial that evaluated the wrong crime entirely.

5. **§ 13959(i) permits good cause for late filing.** To the extent the Board construes this filing as late, Applicant asserts good cause for any delay: (a) the agency's own misstated 45-day deadline; (b) the agency's failure to provide mandatory VWAC advocacy assistance; (c) the systemic errors in processing that required comprehensive audit before the nature of the denial could be understood; and (d) the extraordinary burden on a crime victim with documented medical limitations to navigate a process riddled with procedural errors.

**Applicant requests that the Board accept and process this filing on the merits, consistent with the statutory purpose of liberal construction under Government Code § 13950.**

## II. GOVERNING LEGAL STANDARDS

### A. Liberal Construction Mandate — Government Code § 13950

Government Code § 13950 declares: *"It is the policy of this state to provide compensation... to innocent persons who suffer pecuniary loss as a result of criminal acts... This article shall be liberally construed..."*

The liberal construction mandate is not advisory — it is the legislature's direct instruction on how the Board resolves ambiguities, assesses borderline documentation, and applies eligibility standards. Every aspect of this appeal must be evaluated through a lens of liberal construction in favor of the crime victim:

- Ambiguity in documentation: resolved in Applicant's favor
- Borderline injury evidence: resolved in Applicant's favor
- Disputed filing deadlines: resolved in Applicant's favor
- Applicability of subsections (f)(1), (f)(2), (f)(3): all three must be considered, not only the most restrictive
- Grounds for denial: construed narrowly (the denial grounds are the exception; compensation is the rule)

A CalVCB determination that fails the liberal construction mandate fails on legal grounds, independently reviewable de novo by a court. *Bixby v. Pierno* (1971) 4 Cal.3d 130 (legal errors in administrative proceedings are reviewed de novo).

### B. Victim Rights Under California Constitution Art. I, § 28

California Constitution Article I, § 28, enacted by the voters as Proposition 9 (Marsy's Law, November 2008), provides in subdivision (b) seventeen specific constitutional rights for crime victims. These rights include:

- **§ 28(b)(1):** Right to be treated with fairness, respect, and dignity.
- **§ 28(b)(4):** Right to prevent the disclosure of information that could endanger the victim.
- **§ 28(b)(13):** Right to seek and obtain restitution and to have the laws of California enforced.
- **§ 28(b)(17):** Right to receive information about available victim compensation programs.

Subdivision (d) of Article I, § 28 expressly provides enforcement standing: *"A victim, the retained attorney of a victim, a lawful representative of the victim, or the prosecuting attorney upon request of the victim, may enforce the rights enumerated in subdivision (b) in any trial or appellate court with jurisdiction over the case as a matter of right."*

The California Legislature enacted the CalVCB program to implement § 28(b)(13)'s guarantee that victims' restitution rights will be enforced and § 28(b)(17)'s right to receive compensation information. Processing that denies a victim's application through procedural shortcuts, wrong dates, and single-source investigation fails the constitutional standard.

### C. Preponderance of Evidence Standard — Government Code § 13955

Government Code § 13959(c) provides: *"The applicant has the burden of establishing his or her eligibility for compensation by a preponderance of the evidence."*

The preponderance standard means more likely than not — not certainty, not corroboration by every possible source, and not proof beyond a reasonable doubt. Where CalVCB's single-source investigation failed to gather evidence from hospitals and medical providers that might establish injury by a preponderance, the Board has not discharged its § 13954(a) investigative duty before rendering a denial.

## IV. THE THIRTEEN REGULATORY COMPLIANCE FAILURES

### RC-1. SHORTENED DEADLINE — 10 DAYS vs. STATUTORY 30 DAYS
**Finding:** The incomplete application letter (Page 01, Form 04016, dated 8/26/2025) demands: "Please provide the requested information within 10 days."

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13952(c)(2): *"The applicant, within **30 calendar days** of being notified that the application is incomplete, may either supply the additional information or appeal the staff's determination to the board."*

**Argument:** The 10-day deadline stated by CalVCB is less than one-third of the 30-day right the Legislature enacted. A crime victim who reads the letter and believes they have only 10 days may abandon a valid claim — or fail to gather documentation in time — based on CalVCB's misstated deadline. Under the § 13950 liberal construction mandate, any ambiguity about deadlines must be resolved in the applicant's favor. A determination based on the claimant's failure to respond within the stated 10-day period is void as a matter of law.

### RC-5. SINGLE-SOURCE DENIAL — NO MULTI-SOURCE VERIFICATION

**Statutory violation:** Government Code § 13954(a): *"The board shall verify with **hospitals, physicians, law enforcement officials, or other interested parties involved**, the treatment of the victim or derivative victim, circumstances of the crime..."*

**Argument:** Section 13954(a) lists four categories of verification sources. CalVCB contacted only law enforcement. No hospital, emergency room, physician, or medical provider was contacted. A denial based on a single police department records check, without medical verification, fails the mandatory multi-source investigation standard. Under § 13950 liberal construction, the Board cannot deny a claim without exhausting the verification sources the Legislature mandated.

**Relief requested:** Verification through hospitals, physicians, and other interested parties before any reconsidered determination; applicant given opportunity to provide supporting medical documentation.
