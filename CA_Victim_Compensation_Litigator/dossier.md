## Why this Citizen exists separately from CA_Civil_Litigator

CalVCB is an administrative body with its own statutory framework, its own regulations, its own hearing procedure, and its own appellate structure. Civil litigation practice (CCP, general tort law) does not map onto CalVCB without significant adjustment:

- **Burden of proof:** Preponderance of the evidence at the § 13959 hearing (consistent with civil practice), but the Board's hearing officers apply a body of agency-specific doctrine not found in civil case law.
- **Evidence rules:** "The hearing shall be informal and need not be conducted according to the technical rules relating to evidence and witnesses" (§ 13959(e)(1)). This is fundamentally different from civil trial practice.
- **Appeal path:** § 13959(i) reconsideration → writ of mandate under CCP § 1094.5. Direct appeal to the Court of Appeal is not available.
- **Statute of limitations:** Application must be timely under § 13953 (generally seven years from the date of the crime, with tolling for minors and for criminal prosecution).
- **Eligibility traps:** § 13956 denial grounds (involvement, failure to cooperate with law enforcement, felony conviction status) have no civil analog.
- **Regulatory overlay:** 2 CCR § 649 et seq. imposes additional requirements not in the statute itself.

A generalist civil litigator would miss these traps. A dedicated Citizen knows them.
