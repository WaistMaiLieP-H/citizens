# Gov. Code § 13950 — Legislative Purpose

**Popular name:** CalVCB purpose statement; declaration of public interest in crime victim compensation
**Location:** Government Code, Title 2, Division 3, Part 4, Chapter 5 (Crime Victim Compensation)
**Companion sections:** §§ 13951 (definitions), 13952 (Board establishment), 13953 (application timeliness), 13955 (eligibility), 13956 (denial grounds), 13957 (compensation scope), 13959 (hearing and appeal), 13969 (Restitution Fund)

## What § 13950 does

Section 13950 is the legislative findings and declaration for California's crime victim compensation program. It announces three things:

1. **Public interest.** The Legislature declares that compensating crime victims is in the public interest. This is load-bearing because it provides the statutory policy rationale for liberal construction of the eligibility provisions in § 13955 and the strict construction of denial provisions in § 13956.

2. **Procedural framework.** This chapter (currently Chapter 5 of Part 4 of Division 3 of Title 2) "shall govern the procedure by which crime victims may obtain compensation from the Restitution Fund." The Restitution Fund is the source of payment, not the State General Fund.

3. **Citation continuity.** Subdivision (c) preserves cross-references from the pre-2002 statutory location (former Art. 1 of Ch. 5 commencing with § 13959) to the current chapter. A researcher or appellate opinion citing "former § 13959 et seq." is citing what is now § 13950 et seq.

## Why this matters for appeals

Appellate briefs challenging CalVCB decisions routinely open with § 13950 because it provides the statutory lens through which every subsequent provision should be read. When the Board narrowly interprets an eligibility provision, the appellant cites § 13950(a) for the "public interest" rule of liberal construction. When the Board broadly applies a denial provision, the appellant cites § 13950(a) to argue strict construction.

Section 13950 is not itself a basis for relief — it does not create rights or duties independent of the other sections in the chapter. It is an interpretive anchor.

## The Restitution Fund

Under § 13950(b), compensation is paid from "the Restitution Fund." The Restitution Fund is funded primarily by (a) restitution fines imposed in criminal cases, (b) federal matching funds under the Victims of Crime Act (VOCA), and (c) parole and probation penalty assessments. It is not a general-fund appropriation, which means:

- Awards are contingent on Fund solvency.
- The $35K base compensation cap (§ 13957(b)) can rise to $70K when federal funds are available — this is a fund-status question.
- The Board has some discretion to prioritize certain types of claims when Fund resources are constrained.

The Fund's annual status is published by CalVCB and is a factual matter relevant to the maximum-award analysis in any appeal.

## Relevance to the A25-10117946 appeal

Section 13950 provides the opening framework for the Hartmann appeal brief. The argument structure will be:

1. Legislature has declared it is in the public interest to compensate crime victims (§ 13950(a)).
2. The Hartmann application was denied despite meeting the § 13955 eligibility elements, OR denied on § 13956 grounds that are not supported by substantial evidence, OR denied through procedural failures violating § 13959, 2 CCR § 649, and Cal. Const. Art. I § 28.
3. Therefore the denial is contrary to the legislative purpose and must be reversed.

Section 13950 is not a standalone ground for reversal, but it frames every other ground.
