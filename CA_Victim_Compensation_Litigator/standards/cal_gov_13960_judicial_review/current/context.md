# Gov. Code § 13960 — Judicial Review of CalVCB Decisions

## What this statute does

Section 13960 is the endpoint of the CalVCB administrative appeal process — the gateway to superior court review via a writ of mandate under CCP § 1094.5. It provides critical filing deadlines, the standard of judicial review, and attorney fee provisions.

## The judicial review path

After exhausting administrative remedies (hearing under § 13959, optional reconsideration), a CalVCB applicant may seek review in superior court by petitioning for a writ of mandate. This is not a new hearing on the merits — it is judicial review of the administrative decision.

**Triggering event:** A "final decision" by the CalVCB on the application. A decision is final when:
- The Board issues its decision (if no reconsideration is sought), OR
- The Board rejects or grants reconsideration and issues a final decision

## Filing deadlines (strictly enforced)

| Scenario | Personal delivery deadline | Mailing deadline |
|---|---|---|
| No reconsideration sought | 30 calendar days | 60 calendar days |
| Reconsideration rejected | 30 calendar days | 60 calendar days |
| Reconsideration granted → final decision | 30 calendar days | 60 calendar days |

**Critical point:** The right to petition is NOT affected by failing to seek reconsideration (subd. (a)). Skipping reconsideration does not waive judicial review. However, strategically, reconsideration may be worth pursuing to build a more complete record.

**Deadline is from delivery/mailing of the Board's decision:** Count from the date the decision is personally delivered or mailed, not from when it is received.

## Standard of judicial review: CCP § 1094.5

The writ petition is governed by CCP § 1094.5, which provides two possible standards:

**1. Ordinary "abuse of discretion" review:**
- The court asks whether the findings are supported by substantial evidence in the light of the whole record
- If the Board's findings are supported by substantial evidence, the court must uphold the decision even if it would have decided differently

**2. "Independent judgment" review:**
- The court independently re-weighs the evidence and exercises its own judgment
- Applied where the administrative decision substantially affects a fundamental vested right

**Which standard applies to CalVCB appeals?** This is the critical question for A25-10117946.

The argument for **independent judgment review:**
- Victim compensation is not a gratuitous benefit — it is a statutory entitlement once eligibility is established
- Constitutional Marsy's Law rights (Cal. Const. Art. I § 28) may constitute "fundamental rights"
- The denial of compensation to a victim of violent crime may substantially affect fundamental interests in bodily integrity and financial survival

The argument for **abuse of discretion review:**
- Courts have generally applied the more deferential abuse-of-discretion/substantial evidence standard to CalVCB decisions
- The Legislature established the CalVCB as the specialized adjudicator

**Strategic note:** Even under the more deferential abuse-of-discretion standard, a CalVCB decision is reversible where the findings are not supported by substantial evidence in the record as a whole. Where the Board's decision rests on incorrect application of law (not just factual findings), the legal error is reviewed de novo regardless of which standard applies.

## Attorney fees — two tracks

**Track 1 (§ 13960(b)(1)):** If a writ issues and the court orders fees, the court may award:
- Reasonable attorney's fees, OR
- $1,000 — whichever is LESS

This cap ($1,000) makes § 13960 fee recovery largely symbolic for represented applicants. However, if the Board acts in the applicant's favor after the petition is filed but before judicial determination, the Board pays the filing costs.

**Track 2 (CCP § 1021.5):** Private attorney general fee recovery is NOT prohibited (subd. (b)(3)). CCP § 1021.5 provides fee recovery where:
- The action enforces an important right affecting the public interest
- A significant benefit conferred on the public
- The necessity and financial burden of private enforcement make fee shifting appropriate

CalVCB systemic deficiencies (violating Marsy's Law, applying wrong denial standards) may qualify for § 1021.5 attorney fees — substantially larger than the § 13960 cap.

## Relevance to A25-10117946

The § 13960 pathway is the final escalation tool:
1. If the § 13959 hearing results in denial
2. And reconsideration is denied (or waived)
3. File § 13960 petition within 30/60 days of final decision
4. Argue for independent judgment review based on the fundamental rights argument
5. Argue legal error (incorrect application of §§ 13955, 13956, Marsy's Law) reviewable de novo
6. Seek CCP § 1021.5 attorney fees in addition to § 13960 fees

**Key document to preserve:** The exact date of mailing of the Board's decision on application A25-10117946. The 60-day mailing deadline runs from that date. If reconsideration is sought and denied, the 60-day period restarts from the mailing of the reconsideration rejection.
