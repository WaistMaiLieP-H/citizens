# Bixby v. Pierno (1971) 4 Cal.3d 130

**Citation:** Bixby v. Pierno (1971) 4 Cal.3d 130, 93 Cal.Rptr. 234, 481 P.2d 242
**Court:** Supreme Court of California
**Decided:** February 1971
**Statutes construed:** CCP § 1094.5 (standard of judicial review for administrative decisions)

## Facts

Bixby was a licensee who challenged an administrative agency's revocation of his license via a petition for writ of mandate under CCP § 1094.5. The question was which standard of judicial review applied: the more deferential "abuse of discretion/substantial evidence" standard, or the more searching "independent judgment" standard.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **The standard of judicial review under CCP § 1094.5 depends on whether the administrative decision substantially affects a "fundamental vested right."** This is the *Bixby* rule — the controlling framework for California administrative law judicial review.

2. **If the administrative decision substantially affects a fundamental vested right, independent judgment review applies.** Under independent judgment review, the superior court does not merely check whether substantial evidence supports the agency's findings; the court independently re-weighs the evidence and exercises its own judgment as to what the facts and the law require.

3. **If the administrative decision does not substantially affect a fundamental vested right, substantial evidence/abuse of discretion review applies.** Under this deferential standard, the court asks only whether the agency's findings are supported by substantial evidence in the light of the whole record. If so, the decision is upheld even if the court would have decided differently.

4. **"Fundamental vested right" is a fact-sensitive inquiry.** Factors include: whether the right is a traditional common-law right, whether the right has a constitutional dimension, whether the right is highly personal (liberty, property, livelihood), and whether its deprivation causes substantial harm.

5. **License revocation typically triggers independent judgment review.** The loss of the right to practice a profession — a livelihood — is a fundamental vested right that triggers independent judgment.

## Why this is load-bearing

*Bixby* is the controlling California Supreme Court authority on the CCP § 1094.5 standard of review. Every § 13960 CalVCB writ petition must address *Bixby*: does the denial of victim compensation substantially affect a "fundamental vested right"?

**The argument for independent judgment in CalVCB cases:**
- Victim compensation is a statutory entitlement, not a gratuity — once eligibility is established, the applicant has a right to the award
- The Marsy's Law constitutional guarantees (Cal. Const. Art. I § 28) elevate victim rights to constitutional status — deprivation of those rights may constitute deprivation of a fundamental constitutional right
- Denial of compensation to a violent crime victim affects bodily integrity (by denying reimbursement for medical treatment), financial survival, and other highly personal interests

**The argument against:**
- Some courts have held CalVCB is a public benefit program, not a fundamental vested right
- Courts have applied the deferential standard to CalVCB decisions

**Strategic note for A25-10117946:** Argue for independent judgment review in the § 13960 petition. Even if the court applies the deferential standard, argue legal errors (incorrect application of §§ 13955, 13956, Marsy's Law) are reviewed de novo regardless.

## Authority status

Binding California Supreme Court precedent. Decided 1971. Still the controlling framework for CCP § 1094.5 standard-of-review analysis. Cited in every significant administrative mandamus brief in California.
