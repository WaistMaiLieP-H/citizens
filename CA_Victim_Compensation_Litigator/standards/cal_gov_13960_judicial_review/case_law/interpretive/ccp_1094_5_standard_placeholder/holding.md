## The doctrinal proposition

CalVCB writ petitions under § 13960 / CCP § 1094.5 are subject to the *Bixby* framework. Courts applying *Bixby* to CalVCB have reached differing conclusions on whether independent judgment or substantial evidence review applies:

1. **The substantial evidence argument (deferential):**
   - CalVCB is a public benefit program, not a traditional common-law right
   - The Legislature created the benefit; the Legislature can define its scope
   - Victim compensation awards involve policy judgments within the Board's expertise
   - Courts applying this view have affirmed CalVCB denials under substantial evidence review

2. **The independent judgment argument (searching):**
   - Marsy's Law (Cal. Const. Art. I § 28) elevated victim rights to constitutional status in 2008
   - Post-Marsy's Law, the argument that CalVCB denials do not affect "fundamental" rights is weaker
   - The deprivation of compensation to a crime victim directly affects bodily integrity (medical reimbursement), financial survival, and dignity — interests that California courts have recognized as fundamental
   - At minimum, legal errors in applying § 13955 or § 13956 are reviewable de novo (no deference to the Board's legal interpretation)

3. **De novo review of legal questions:**
   - Regardless of which factual review standard applies, courts review the Board's legal conclusions (interpretation of § 13955, § 13956, § 13959, Marsy's Law) de novo
   - Where the Board applied the wrong legal standard — e.g., required more than preponderance of the evidence, or failed to apply the proper mitigating factors analysis — the court corrects the legal error without deference

## Why legal error is the strongest argument

For A25-10117946, the 13 FAIL findings in the audit may reflect not merely factual errors but legal errors:
- Applying the wrong burden of proof
- Failing to consider required statutory factors (§ 13956(b) mitigating factors)
- Shortening deadlines that are legally set (§ 13959 10-day notice)
- Failing to provide required advocacy (Marsy's Law § 28(b)(17))
- Making single-source denials without independent investigation

Each of these is a legal error, reviewable de novo. The court need not defer to the Board on questions of statutory interpretation.
