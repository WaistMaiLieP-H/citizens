CALVCB JUDICIAL REVIEW — THE BIXBY APPLIED TO § 13960:

1. **Substantial evidence argument (deferential):**
   - CalVCB is a public benefit program, not a traditional common-law right
   - The Legislature created the benefit and may define its scope
   - Victim compensation awards involve policy judgments within the Board's expertise
   - Courts applying this view have affirmed CalVCB denials under substantial evidence review

2. **Independent judgment argument (searching):**
   - Marsy's Law (Cal. Const. Art. I § 28) elevated victim rights to constitutional status in 2008
   - Post-Marsy's Law, the argument that CalVCB denials do not affect "fundamental" rights is weaker
   - Deprivation of compensation directly affects bodily integrity (medical reimbursement), financial survival, and dignity
   - At minimum, legal errors in applying § 13955 or § 13956 are reviewable de novo

3. **De novo review of legal questions (always applies):**
   - Regardless of which factual review standard applies, courts review the Board's legal conclusions de novo
   - Where the Board applied the wrong legal standard — required more than preponderance of the evidence, failed to apply proper mitigating factors analysis, shortened deadlines beyond statutory authority — the court corrects the legal error without deference

---

WHY LEGAL ERROR IS THE STRONGEST ARGUMENT FOR A25-10117946:

The 13 FAIL findings in the audit reflect not merely factual errors but legal errors:
- Applying the wrong burden of proof (§ 13955 preponderance vs. higher standard)
- Failing to consider required statutory factors (§ 13956(b) mitigating factors)
- Shortening deadlines that are legally set (§ 13959 10-day notice)
- Failing to provide required advocacy (Marsy's Law § 28(b)(17))
- Making single-source denials without independent investigation

Each of these is a legal error, reviewable de novo. The court need not defer to the Board on questions of statutory interpretation.

Strategic framing: lead the § 13960 brief with the legal error arguments (de novo, always applies), then make the Bixby independent judgment argument as an alternative for the factual findings.
