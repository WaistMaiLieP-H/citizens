# Historical Chain — Gov. Code § 13955 (CalVCB Eligibility)

| Year | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1964 | UK Criminal Injuries Compensation Scheme | International model |
| 1965 | California enacts first US crime victim compensation program | Former Gov. Code § 13959 et seq.; victim-only, California-crime-only, means-tested |
| 1970s | Derivative victim and mental health counseling added | Expansion beyond direct victim |
| 1982 | Cal. Const. Art. I § 28 (Prop 8 Victims' Bill of Rights) | Constitutional recognition |
| 1984 | Federal VOCA (42 USC § 10601) | Federal matching funds |
| 1999 | Major expansion: emotional-injury-only coverage for enumerated crimes; DV non-police-report rule | Significant broadening |
| 2002 | Stats. 2002, ch. 1141 recodification | Former §§ 13959-13974 → current §§ 13950-13969; seven-element structure |
| 2008 | Marsy's Law (Prop 9) | Massive Cal. Const. Art. I § 28 expansion |
| Post-2008 | Incremental additions: human trafficking, military sexual assault, service dog injury, nonconsensual minor image distribution, derivative victim expansions, inflation adjustments to caps | Continuous expansion of who qualifies |
| 2002 → 2026 | Seven-element core structure stable | Core pleading framework unchanged |

## The chain holds

Section 13955's seven-element eligibility framework has been remarkably stable since the 2002 recodification. Every amendment since then has been additive — expanding who qualifies, expanding which crimes trigger emotional-injury coverage, raising caps. The core structure is untouched. This means an appeal brief built around the seven elements in 2026 uses the same template as an appeal brief built in 2003.

The 1965 promise — that California crime victims would have a public mechanism for recovering pecuniary losses — has been honored and progressively strengthened. Section 13955 today is a far more generous statute than its 1965 ancestor, and the constitutional overlay from Marsy's Law (2008) adds enforceable victim rights on top of the statutory framework.

For A25-10117946, the significance is that the most modern, most-expanded version of the statute applies. The appeal can invoke the full breadth of subdivisions (a) through (g), including the post-2008 additions, without fear that an older narrow version is controlling.
