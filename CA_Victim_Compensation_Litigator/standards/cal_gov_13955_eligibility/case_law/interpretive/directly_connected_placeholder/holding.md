# Doctrinal Placeholder: § 13955 Eligibility — "Directly Connected" Crime Requirement

**Note:** This folder documents the doctrinal framework for the § 13955 qualifying crime and "directly connected" victimization requirements. A specific published case citation is pending primary-source verification.

## The doctrinal proposition

Section 13955 requires a qualifying crime as the predicate for compensation. The "directly connected" doctrine governs which crimes fall within the Board's jurisdiction and whether the claimant's harm is sufficiently connected to the crime to be compensable.

### 1. Qualifying crime standard

The crime need not result in a conviction. Police reports, medical records, court filings, or other documentation establishing that a qualifying crime occurred suffice. Where a claimant was victimized but the perpetrator was not prosecuted — or where the crime was mischaracterized by law enforcement — the Board is not bound by the prosecution's characterization. The claimant can establish the qualifying crime independently.

### 2. Victim categories under § 13955

- **Direct victim**: The person who directly suffered the qualifying crime
- **Derivative victim**: Immediate family members of homicide victims; persons who witnessed a qualifying crime against a direct victim and suffered psychological injury
- **Ongoing-pattern crimes**: Domestic violence, stalking, identity theft, and similar continuing-course crimes qualify as long as each specific criminal act falls within the statute's coverage

### 3. Crime date matters

The Board's jurisdiction, the applicable filing deadline, and the governing version of the statute all turn on the correct crime date. Where CalVCB uses an incorrect date — as documented in A25-10117946 (five different crime dates across five documents) — the denial is procedurally defective because the Board never adjudicated the actual qualifying crime.

## Application to A25-10117946

The CalVCB used crime dates of: (1) BLANK, (2) 10/20/2007, and (3) 6/16/2018 across its five adjudication documents. The actual qualifying crime date is 6/16/2023. The Board denied compensation for a crime that occurred in 2007 or 2018 — crimes for which Michael never applied, which could not have been the basis for the claimed losses, and which were outside the Board's normal jurisdiction window. The directly-connected predicate was never properly evaluated because the Board substituted wrong crime dates.
