HOLDING:

§ 13955 "DIRECTLY CONNECTED" CRIME REQUIREMENT:

1. **The qualifying crime must be a crime under California law.** Section 13955 requires that the compensable victimization arise from a qualifying crime under California Penal Code — felony or misdemeanor — or, where the crime occurred out of state, a crime that would be a qualifying crime under California law.

2. **The crime does not need to result in a conviction.** CalVCB eligibility does not require that the perpetrator be arrested, charged, or convicted. A police report, medical record, or other documentation establishing that a crime occurred is sufficient predicate for the Board's jurisdiction.

3. **The claimant must be the direct victim, an immediate family member, or a derivative victim.** Section 13955 establishes three categories: (a) the person who directly suffered the crime; (b) a family member of a homicide victim; and (c) a person who suffered psychological injury from witnessing a qualifying crime against a direct victim.

4. **The "directly connected" requirement excludes collateral or speculative victimization.** The Board's jurisdiction does not extend to financial losses that are tangentially related to a crime but not "directly resulting from" the qualifying criminal act. However, where the crime is part of an ongoing pattern of criminal conduct (such as domestic violence or identity theft), each criminal act in the continuing pattern is a separately qualifying predicate crime.

5. **The qualifying crime date determines the governing statute.** Where a claim spans multiple criminal acts, each act carries its own filing deadline measured from the date of that act — not from a general "discovery" date. This matters for multi-incident applications.
