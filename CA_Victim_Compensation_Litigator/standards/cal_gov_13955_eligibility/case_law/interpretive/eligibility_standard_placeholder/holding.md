# Doctrinal Placeholder: § 13955 Eligibility — "Directly Resulting From" and the Crime-Injury Nexus

**Note:** This folder documents the doctrinal framework for the § 13955 "directly resulting from" nexus requirement. A specific published case citation is pending primary-source verification.

## The doctrinal proposition

Section 13955 requires that the compensable losses be "directly resulting from" the qualifying crime. Courts and the Board have applied this requirement as follows:

1. **"Directly resulting from" is not limited to immediate physical injury.** The statute covers not only the physical injury sustained during the crime but also consequential losses — medical expenses, mental health treatment, lost income, funeral costs, and other enumerated categories — that are causally connected to the crime.

2. **Psychological injury is compensable.** Mental health treatment necessitated by the trauma of the crime is a compensable loss "directly resulting from" the crime. This is particularly important for PTSD, anxiety, and depression claims arising from violent crimes or domestic violence.

3. **The applicant need not have suffered the crime to be eligible — witnesses and secondary victims.** Section 13955 extends eligibility to family members of homicide victims and others who suffered psychological injury from witnessing a crime against a qualifying victim.

4. **The "directly resulting from" standard is a proximate cause standard, not a but-for standard.** The crime need only be a substantial contributing cause to the loss; the applicant need not prove the crime was the sole cause.

5. **Medical necessity is not required for all treatment.** Where a treating provider documented the treatment as related to the crime's impact, the Board should accept that documentation in the absence of clear evidence to the contrary. The Board cannot second-guess a treating physician's professional judgment without expert counter-evidence.
