# Gov. Code § 13957 — CalVCB Compensation Scope (Context)

## What § 13957 does

Section 13957 specifies what CalVCB may pay for and how much. It is the rate sheet of the compensation program. Section 13955 establishes eligibility; § 13956 identifies denial grounds; § 13957 tells the applicant and the Board what compensation is actually available.

## Eleven compensation categories

The section authorizes eleven distinct categories of reimbursement in subdivision (a):

1. **Medical expenses** — subject to rate schedule in § 13957.2
2. **Outpatient mental health counseling** — capped at $10K (victim and close family of homicide victim) or $5K (other derivative victims and minor witnesses); excess permitted for dire/exceptional circumstances
3. **Loss of income/support** — subject to § 13957.5 limits
4. **Job retraining**
5. **Residential security** — $1,000 cap
6. **Residence/vehicle modification** — for permanent disability
7. **Relocation** — $3,418 base cap with expansion authority
8. **Homicide victim expenses** — medical + funeral up to $12,818
9. **Crime scene cleanup** — $1,709 cap
10. **Service dog expenses** — $10,000 cap
11. **Minor victim image distribution** — mental health counseling only

## The aggregate cap (subd. (b))

Total compensation to or on behalf of each victim or derivative victim cannot exceed **$35,000**, unless federal funds are available, in which case the cap can rise to **$70,000**. The $35K / $70K cap is the hard ceiling on per-victim recovery regardless of the number of categories claimed.

This cap is significantly lower than most civil tort recoveries would allow. CalVCB is designed as a supplement, not a substitute. An applicant with a viable civil claim should pursue both — civil damages for the full scope of loss, CalVCB compensation for the portions within the Board's jurisdiction.

## The § 13957.2 rate schedule

Section 13957.2 establishes rates for medical and mental health reimbursements. The Board publishes its current rate schedule periodically. The rate schedule often produces partial reimbursement — the victim's actual medical bills may exceed the rate-schedule amounts, leaving a portion uncompensated even within the $35K/$70K cap.

## Priority rules

Subdivision (a)(3) includes a priority rule: if the applicant requests that loss of income/support be paid first, the Board may not pay medical or mental health expenses before income/support, unless the Board determines payment will not decrease funds available for income/support. This is a usability provision — some applicants need cash more than reimbursement of medical bills that have already been paid by insurance.

## Sunset provision (subd. (c))

Subdivision (c) contains a conditional repeal provision: the section becomes inoperative July 1, 2024, if certain General Fund conditions are met. Most practitioners treat the section as in force absent formal notice of repeal. The Legislature has historically renewed or extended these sunset provisions; the statute in practice continues to operate.

## Relevance to A25-10117946

For the Hartmann appeal, § 13957 is relevant to the **remedy** side of the appeal:

1. **What can be recovered?** Identify each category of compensation applicable to the claim. Medical bills, mental health counseling, loss of income (if any), relocation costs (if any), residential security (if any).
2. **What is the maximum award?** Run the categories against the $35K / $70K cap. Is federal VOCA matching available at time of award? (Factual question about Restitution Fund status.)
3. **Partial recovery implications.** If the initial determination granted partial compensation and denied the remainder, identify which categories were granted and which denied, and appeal only the denied portions.

Section 13957 is typically NOT the battlefield on appeal — the battle is about § 13955 eligibility and § 13956 denial grounds. But once the Board is compelled to grant compensation, § 13957 governs how much.
