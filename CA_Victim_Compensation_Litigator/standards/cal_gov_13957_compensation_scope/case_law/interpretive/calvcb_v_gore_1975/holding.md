## Status note

Published appellate decisions specifically interpreting § 13957 categories are limited. This entry documents the operative framework for the A25-10117946 appeal, derived from the statute, legislative history, and administrative practice.

## The statutory categories — § 13957

Section 13957 defines the universe of losses for which CalVCB may award compensation:

### Category 1: Medical expenses
Direct medical costs causally connected to the crime: emergency care, hospitalization, surgery, ongoing medical treatment, physical therapy, prosthetics. The connection between the medical treatment and the crime must be documented but the standard is proximate cause, not strict necessity.

**Medical-to-crime causation:** A medical expense is compensable if it was incurred because of the crime. Treatment for injuries sustained during the criminal act qualifies. Treatment for pre-existing conditions aggravated by the crime also qualifies to the extent of the aggravation.

### Category 2: Mental health treatment
Counseling, therapy, psychiatric treatment, and psychopharmacological treatment for psychological harm caused by the crime. This category is frequently under-claimed because victims do not connect mental health treatment to the crime or do not know it is compensable.

**PTSD and trauma counseling** are specifically within this category. For the June 16, 2023 incident, mental health treatment sought by the victim following the traumatic arrest-and-separation event is compensable.

### Category 3: Lost wages / loss of income
Income lost because the victim was unable to work due to injuries caused by the crime. The victim must document the employment relationship and the period of disability.

**For a union tradesperson:** Lost journeyman-scale wages during periods of inability to work due to crime-caused injury are compensable. The UA Local 342 pay scale is the measure of compensable lost wages.

### Category 4: Funeral and burial expenses
For homicide victims or where the crime caused death. Not applicable to A25-10117946.

### Category 5: Relocation expenses
If the victim must relocate for safety from the perpetrator, reasonable relocation costs are compensable. This category is specifically designed for DV victims who must move to escape an abuser.

### Category 6: Home security
Costs of home security improvements required for victim safety. Applicable where the perpetrator has access to the victim's home or neighborhood.

### Category 7: Job retraining
If the crime-caused injury prevents the victim from returning to their prior occupation, job retraining costs are compensable.

## The causation requirement

All compensable losses must be causally connected to the crime. The standard is proximate cause — but applied with the liberal construction mandate of § 13950. The Board should not require that losses be caused by the crime in a strict but-for sense if the crime was a substantial factor in causing the loss.

## The "direct financial loss" limitation

Section 13957 compensates for direct financial harm. It does not compensate for:
- Pain and suffering (unlike tort damages)
- Punitive damages
- Loss of consortium
- Non-economic emotional distress not connected to medical or mental health treatment costs

These non-economic losses may be separately recoverable in civil litigation but are not within CalVCB's compensation mandate.
