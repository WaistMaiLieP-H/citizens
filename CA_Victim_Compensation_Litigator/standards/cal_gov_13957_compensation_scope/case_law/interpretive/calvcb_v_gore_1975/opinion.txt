HOLDING:

§ 13957 COMPENSABLE LOSS CATEGORIES:

1. **Medical expenses.** Direct medical costs causally connected to the crime: emergency care, hospitalization, surgery, ongoing medical treatment, physical therapy, prosthetics. The connection between treatment and crime must be documented but the standard is proximate cause, not strict necessity. Pre-existing conditions aggravated by the crime qualify to the extent of the aggravation.

2. **Mental health treatment.** Counseling, therapy, psychiatric treatment, and psychopharmacological treatment for psychological harm caused by the crime. PTSD and trauma counseling are specifically within this category. Frequently under-claimed because victims do not connect mental health treatment to the crime or do not know it is compensable.

3. **Lost wages / loss of income.** Income lost because the victim was unable to work due to injuries caused by the crime. The victim must document the employment relationship and the period of disability. For a union tradesperson, lost journeyman-scale wages during periods of inability to work due to crime-caused injury are compensable.

4. **Funeral and burial expenses.** For homicide victims or where the crime caused death.

5. **Relocation expenses.** If the victim must relocate for safety from the perpetrator, reasonable relocation costs are compensable. Specifically designed for DV victims who must move to escape an abuser.

6. **Home security improvements.** Costs of security improvements required for victim safety where the perpetrator has access to the victim's home or neighborhood.

7. **Job retraining.** If the crime-caused injury prevents the victim from returning to their prior occupation, job retraining costs are compensable.

---

THE "DIRECT FINANCIAL LOSS" LIMITATION:

Section 13957 compensates for direct financial harm. It does NOT compensate for: pain and suffering; punitive damages; loss of consortium; non-economic emotional distress not connected to medical or mental health treatment costs. These non-economic losses may be separately recoverable in civil litigation but are not within CalVCB's compensation mandate.
