# Historical Chain — Gov. Code § 13957 (CalVCB Compensation Scope)

## The arc: narrow → broad

The original 1965 program authorized compensation for a limited set of medical expenses and some loss of income, with very low caps. Over six decades of incremental amendments, § 13957 has grown to cover:

- Mental health counseling (added 1970s)
- Relocation expenses (added 1980s/1990s as DV awareness grew)
- Residential security (added for stalking/DV protection)
- Service dog injuries (added post-2000)
- Crime scene cleanup (added for homicide-survivors)
- Vehicle/residence modification (for permanent disability)
- Job retraining (for long-term earning capacity recovery)

Each addition reflected an emerging awareness of what crime victims actually need beyond bare medical costs.

## Cap adjustments

The aggregate per-victim cap has been adjusted multiple times:
- 1965 origin: lower single-digit thousands
- 1970s-1990s: incremental raises
- Post-2000: $35,000 base cap adopted
- Post-VOCA expansion: $70,000 with federal matching

The $35K/$70K structure reflects the constrained Restitution Fund and the recognition that CalVCB is a supplement, not a full-recovery mechanism.

## The chain

| Era | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1965-1970s | Medical expenses; loss of earnings; low caps |
| 1970s-1980s | Mental health counseling; derivative victim expansion |
| 1980s-1990s | Relocation; residential security; DV-specific expenses |
| 2000s | Cap raised to $35K/$70K; trauma scene cleanup added |
| 2010s-2020s | Service dog, minor image distribution, human trafficking, military sexual assault expansions |

## The promise today

Section 13957 delivers a structured compensation menu within a constrained Fund. The eleven categories cover most typical crime-victim expenses. The aggregate cap limits the program's liability but also means that applications with large economic losses must pursue additional remedies (civil suits, insurance, VOCA-funded supplementary programs).
