# Doctrinal Placeholder: § 13956 Denial Grounds — Marsy's Law Cooperation Protections

**Note:** This folder documents Marsy's Law (Cal. Const. Art. I § 28, Proposition 9, November 2008) as it constrains CalVCB's application of the § 13956 cooperation requirement and contributing conduct denial grounds. A specific published case citation is pending primary-source verification.

## The doctrinal proposition

Marsy's Law is not just a statement of aspirational values — it is an enforceable constitutional provision that limits how state agencies, including CalVCB, may treat crime victim applicants. The cooperation requirement in § 13956(a) and the contributing conduct ground in § 13956(b) must be applied consistently with Marsy's Law or the denial is constitutionally defective.

### 1. Marsy's Law rights in the CalVCB context

Article I, Section 28 guarantees:
- The right to be treated with fairness, dignity, and respect throughout the proceedings (§ 28(b)(1))
- The right to be heard at all critical stages (§ 28(b)(7))
- The right to seek and reasonably receive information about the proceedings (§ 28(b)(9))
- The right to be protected from intimidation and harm (§ 28(b)(2))

All of these rights apply to CalVCB proceedings. A denial that fails to give the applicant a meaningful opportunity to respond to adverse evidence, or that penalizes a victim for conduct attributable to their victimization, violates at minimum § 28(b)(1) and § 28(b)(7).

### 2. The constitutional floor for § 13956 mitigating factor analysis

Where § 13956(a)(2) requires the Board to consider mitigating factors, Marsy's Law raises that obligation to a constitutional floor. The Board cannot satisfy its Marsy's Law obligations by nominally "considering" mitigating factors in a boilerplate denial. The Board must actually address the applicant's specific circumstances and explain why those circumstances do not qualify as mitigating factors.

### 3. Single-source denial and the Marsy's Law fairness mandate

The most common § 13956 violation is single-source denial: the Board relies solely on a law enforcement document that characterizes the applicant unfavorably, without giving the applicant an opportunity to respond. Marsy's Law makes this practice unconstitutional — not just procedurally defective under CalVCB regulations. The applicant has a constitutional right to be heard, which requires a meaningful opportunity to contest the adverse source.

### 4. Temporal scope: only post-2008 crimes fully covered

Marsy's Law was enacted in November 2008. It applies to all crimes and all CalVCB proceedings after its enactment. For claims arising from crimes before November 2008, the pre-Marsy statutory protections apply. For claims arising from crimes after November 2008 — including the June 16, 2023 qualifying crime in A25-10117946 — Marsy's Law applies in full.
