HOLDING:

MARSY'S LAW AND THE § 13956 COOPERATION REQUIREMENT:

1. **Marsy's Law establishes affirmative victim rights that CalVCB must honor.** Article I, Section 28 of the California Constitution (enacted by Proposition 9, November 2008) gives crime victims the right to be treated with fairness, dignity, and respect; the right to be heard at all critical stages; and the right not to have their application for compensation denied without genuine consideration of their circumstances.

2. **CalVCB is a state agency bound by Marsy's Law.** As a state agency adjudicating victim compensation claims, CalVCB must apply the statutory denial grounds in § 13956 consistently with the constitutional rights established by Marsy's Law. Where a § 13956 denial would violate a victim's Marsy's Law rights — for example, by denying without hearing, by using single-source adverse evidence, or by failing to consider mitigating factors — that denial is constitutionally defective.

3. **The cooperation requirement cannot be applied to re-victimize victims who feared retaliation.** Section 13956(a)(2) lists mitigating factors for the cooperation requirement, including whether the applicant is a domestic violence victim, a sexual assault victim, or a trafficking victim whose cooperation was impaired by fear of retaliation, injury, or death. Marsy's Law reinforces these protections: a Board decision denying a DV victim for failure to cooperate — without addressing whether her cooperation was impaired by fear — violates both § 13956(a)(2) and Art. I § 28.

4. **The Board may not use the applicant's own victimization against them.** Where the § 13956(b) contributing-conduct ground would penalize a victim for circumstances arising from the crime itself (e.g., a DV victim who struck her abuser in self-defense; a crime victim whose pre-existing criminal record arose from the coercive control of her trafficker), Marsy's Law's "fairness and dignity" mandate constrains the Board's application of that ground.

5. **Marsy's Law creates a right to a meaningful response to adverse evidence.** Where the Board relies on a police report, investigation summary, or other single adverse source, Marsy's Law requires that the applicant have a genuine opportunity to contest that source. A denial based solely on a police report that the victim disputes — without investigation — violates Marsy's Law.
