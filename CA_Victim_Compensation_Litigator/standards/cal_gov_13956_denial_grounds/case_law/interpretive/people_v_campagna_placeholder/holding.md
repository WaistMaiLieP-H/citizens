## The doctrinal proposition

Section 13956 provides grounds for denial of victim compensation but is not a mandatory denial statute — most grounds are subject to mitigating factors and the Board's discretion. The key framework:

### 1. The cooperation requirement (§ 13956(a))

The Board may deny compensation where the applicant did not cooperate with law enforcement. However:

- **Mitigating factors listed in § 13956(a)(2) must be considered.** These include whether the applicant is a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or trafficking where fear of retaliation prevented cooperation; whether the applicant has a disability that impaired cooperation; and other enumerated and unenumerated factors.
- **The Board CANNOT deny for failure to cooperate without independently considering whether any mitigating factor applies.** Single-source denial without examining the applicant's circumstances is a legal error.
- **"Cooperation" is context-specific.** A victim who reported to law enforcement but did not persist in prosecution because of witness intimidation, fear, or trauma may still be "cooperating" within the meaning of the statute.

### 2. Felony or misdemeanor conduct (§ 13956(b))

The Board may deny or reduce compensation where the applicant's own criminal conduct contributed to the crime. But:

- **The denial is proportional, not absolute.** The Board may "reduce or deny" based on the applicant's conduct — it retains discretion to reduce rather than deny entirely.
- **Temporal limitation:** The Board may only consider criminal conduct that contributed to the specific victimization for which compensation is sought. A prior conviction or a separate incident is not grounds for denial unless it contributed to the instant crime.
- **Protected classes:** Domestic violence victims, sexual assault victims, trafficking victims, and military sexual assault victims are protected by additional provisions that restrict the use of their cooperation or conduct history against them (§ 13956(b)(3), (c)).

### 3. The "single source" problem

Where the Board denies based on a single adverse source (e.g., a police report that characterizes the victim unfavorably) without:
- Giving the applicant an opportunity to respond
- Independently investigating whether the characterization is accurate
- Considering the victim's account and any corroborating evidence

...the denial violates the Board's own regulations and due process. Single-source denial without independent investigation is a recurring audit finding and a cognizable appeal ground.

### 4. Mitigating factor analysis is mandatory, not permissive

Where the denial ground is "cooperation" or "contributing conduct," the Board MUST address the mitigating factors in § 13956(a)(2) and (b)(2). Failure to address these factors in the decision — even if the Board would have reached the same result after addressing them — is legal error requiring remand for proper consideration.

## Why this matters for A25-10117946

If the CalVCB denial in A25-10117946 invoked the cooperation or contributing conduct grounds, the appeal must:
1. Identify which denial ground(s) the Board invoked
2. Show that the applicable mitigating factors were not considered
3. Show that the applicant's circumstances fall within one or more mitigating factor categories
4. Show that the denial was based on single-source adverse evidence without independent investigation

Each of these is a separate reversal ground.
