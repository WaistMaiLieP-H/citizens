HOLDING:

§ 13956 DENIAL GROUNDS — FRAMEWORK:

1. **Cooperation requirement (§ 13956(a)) — mitigating factors are mandatory, not permissive.** The Board may deny compensation where the applicant did not cooperate with law enforcement. However, the mitigating factors listed in § 13956(a)(2) MUST be independently considered. These include: whether the applicant is a victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or trafficking where fear of retaliation prevented cooperation; disability impairing cooperation; and other enumerated and unenumerated factors.

2. **The Board CANNOT deny for failure to cooperate without examining whether any mitigating factor applies.** Single-source denial without examining the applicant's circumstances is a legal error requiring remand.

3. **"Cooperation" is context-specific.** A victim who reported to law enforcement but did not persist in prosecution because of witness intimidation, fear, or trauma may still be "cooperating" within the meaning of the statute.

4. **Felony or misdemeanor conduct (§ 13956(b)) — reduction is proportional, not absolute.** The Board may "reduce or deny" based on the applicant's criminal conduct. It retains discretion to reduce rather than deny entirely. The Board may only consider criminal conduct that contributed to the specific victimization — prior unrelated convictions are not grounds for denial.

5. **Protected classes restrict the use of conduct history.** Domestic violence victims, sexual assault victims, trafficking victims, and military sexual assault victims are protected by additional provisions that restrict the use of their cooperation or conduct history against them (§ 13956(b)(3), (c)).

---

THE SINGLE-SOURCE PROBLEM — REVERSIBLE ERROR:

Where the Board denies based on a single adverse source (e.g., a police report that characterizes the victim unfavorably) without: (a) giving the applicant an opportunity to respond; (b) independently investigating whether the characterization is accurate; (c) considering the victim's account and any corroborating evidence — the denial violates the Board's own regulations and due process. Single-source denial without independent investigation is a recurring audit finding and a cognizable appeal ground.
