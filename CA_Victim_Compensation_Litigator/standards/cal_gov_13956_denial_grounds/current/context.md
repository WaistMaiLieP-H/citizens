# Gov. Code § 13956 — Denial Grounds (Context)

**Popular name:** CalVCB three-track denial statute (involvement, failure to cooperate, felony status)

## Structure

Section 13956 is the affirmative-defense statute paired with the § 13955 eligibility requirements. Even when an applicant fully satisfies § 13955, the Board can still deny compensation on one of three grounds:

1. **Involvement** (subd. (a)) — discretionary ("may" deny)
2. **Failure to cooperate with law enforcement** (subd. (b)) — mandatory ("shall" deny) unless specialized rules apply
3. **Felony status** (subd. (c)) — mandatory ("shall not" be granted) until certain conditions are met

The difference between "may" (subd. (a)) and "shall" (subds. (b) and (c)) is load-bearing for appeals. An involvement denial is discretionary and reviewable for abuse of discretion. A failure-to-cooperate denial is mandatory BUT has extensive mitigating factors the Board must consider. A felony status denial is rule-bound but includes temporal carve-outs.

## Subdivision (a) — Involvement

The involvement denial is the most frequently contested. The Board can deny if the victim was "involved" in the events leading to the crime. Three factors define involvement:

- **Initiated, provoked, or aggravated** — the victim took affirmative action causing the crime
- **Reasonably foreseeable consequence** — the victim's conduct made the crime a foreseeable result
- **Committing chargeable felony** — with specific exceptions for sexual offense victims

Even when involvement is found, four mitigating factors MAY overcome it:

- Injuries significantly more serious than level of involvement predicted
- Third-party interference not reasonably foreseeable
- Victim's age, physical condition, psychological state
- Compelling health and safety concerns

The Board "shall consider" these factors — it is not optional. A denial that fails to address the mitigating factors is defective on appeal.

## Subdivision (b) — Failure to cooperate

This is the statute that produces many of the 13 FAIL findings in typical CalVCB audits. The Board cannot deny for non-cooperation without considering:

- Age, physical condition, psychological state
- Cultural or linguistic barriers
- Compelling health and safety concerns
- Reasonable fear of retaliation

Special rules exist for domestic violence victims (not deemed non-cooperative based on scene conduct; delayed reporting not dispositive), sexual assault victims (same), human trafficking victims (same, plus non-police-report alternatives), and military sexual assault victims (same, plus non-superior-officer alternatives).

The appeal theory here is straightforward: if the Board denied for non-cooperation without addressing each of the listed factors, the denial is defective and must be reversed for a proper analysis.

## Subdivision (c) — Felony status

This is a bright-line rule: convicted violent felons (PC § 667.5(c)) cannot receive compensation until discharged from all criminal-justice supervision for that specific crime. Sex offender registrants are also barred during the registration period. There are no mitigating factors.

Important nuance: the denial runs only against the person who was convicted, and only for compensation relating to events AFTER conviction. A pre-conviction victimization of a person who is later convicted of an unrelated violent felony is not automatically barred (read the temporal rule carefully).

## Relevance to A25-10117946

The 13 FAIL findings likely include some combination of:

1. **§ 13956(a) involvement denial without considering mitigating factors** — if CalVCB found involvement without addressing age, physical condition, psychological state, or compelling safety concerns, that is a § 13956(a)(2) violation.

2. **§ 13956(b) non-cooperation denial without considering factors** — if CalVCB found non-cooperation without considering fear of retaliation, psychological state, or any of the other listed factors, that is a § 13956(b)(1) violation.

3. **§ 13956(b) single-source denial** — if CalVCB relied on a single source (e.g., only the APD report) for the non-cooperation finding, without considering alternative evidence, that violates the regulatory requirement to consider all available evidence.

Each specific failure, matched to the specific statutory language the Board ignored or misapplied, is a separate ground for reversal in the appeal brief.
