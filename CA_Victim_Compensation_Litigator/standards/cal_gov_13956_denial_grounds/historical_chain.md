# Historical Chain — Gov. Code § 13956 (CalVCB Denial Grounds)

## The wound (pre-1999 and pre-2008)

The early CalVCB denial provisions were broad and largely unfettered. The Board could deny on "involvement" grounds with minimal analysis and could deny for "failure to cooperate" without considering domestic violence dynamics, psychological trauma, or fear of retaliation. Victims of domestic violence routinely had their applications denied because they recanted initial statements under pressure from abusers, because they failed to press charges, or because they delayed reporting. Sexual assault victims were denied because of the delayed-reporting pattern well-documented in trauma research. Human trafficking victims were denied because they had no police report (trafficking victims typically cannot safely approach police while under trafficker control).

The wound was **the re-victimization of victims through administrative denial mechanisms**. A statute designed to help crime victims was being used to deny them, because the program's administrative criteria encoded assumptions about "how a real victim behaves" that were contradicted by actual trauma and victimization research.

## The promise

Over a series of amendments (especially 1999 and post-2008), the Legislature added structure to § 13956 to force the Board to consider factors that had been ignored in early practice:

- **DV non-police-report rule (1999 and later):** Domestic violence applications cannot be denied solely because no police report was made. Alternative evidence (medical records, mental health records, restraining orders) can establish the crime.
- **Sexual assault parallel rule:** Same for sexual assault.
- **Human trafficking rule:** Same for trafficking, with specific alternative evidence categories (Law Enforcement Agency Endorsement, caseworker affidavit, corroborating evidence).
- **Military sexual assault rule:** Added explicitly to address pattern of non-reporting within military chain of command.
- **Mitigating factor requirements in subd. (a)(2):** Board "shall consider" age, physical condition, psychological state, and compelling safety concerns when evaluating involvement.
- **Non-cooperation mitigating factors in subd. (b)(1):** Same set of factors, plus cultural/linguistic barriers and fear of retaliation.
- **Marsy's Law overlay (2008):** Cal. Const. Art. I § 28(b) rights provide a constitutional floor beneath the statutory requirements.

The promise: **the denial provisions cannot be applied mechanically. The Board must engage with the actual circumstances of the actual victim and must document consideration of the enumerated factors.**

## The chain

| Year | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1965 | Original program; narrow denial grounds | Early administrative discretion |
| 1970s-1990s | Administrative practice of broad denials in DV and sexual assault cases | Wound developing |
| 1999 | Stats. 1999 expansion; DV non-police-report rule | First major correction |
| 2002 | Stats. 2002, ch. 1141 recodification | § 13956 enacted in current numbering |
| 2008 | Marsy's Law (Prop 9) | Constitutional overlay |
| Post-2008 | Incremental additions: sexual assault non-report rule, human trafficking rule, military sexual assault rule, mitigating factor requirements | Continuous expansion |
| 2022-2023 | Further amendments (conditional sunset provisions, mitigating factor clarifications) | Current statutory form |

## Relevance to A25-10117946

Each post-1999 protective rule that CalVCB failed to apply to the Hartmann application is a separate ground for reversal. If the denial was based on non-cooperation without considering fear of retaliation, that violates subd. (b)(1). If the denial relied on a single source, that violates subd. (b)(1)'s requirement that the Board "consider" the full set of factors. If mitigating factors were ignored, that violates the "shall consider" language.

The 13 FAIL audit findings are likely a catalog of these specific failures. The appeal brief should match each audit finding to a specific statutory provision and argue that the failure is a separate ground for reversal.
