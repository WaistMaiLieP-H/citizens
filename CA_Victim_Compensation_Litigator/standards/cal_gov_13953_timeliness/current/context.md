# Gov. Code § 13953 — CalVCB Application Timeliness

## Summary

Applications for CalVCB compensation must be filed within **seven years** of the latest of:
- The date of the crime
- Seven years after the victim turns 21 (for minor victims)
- Seven years from when the victim knew or with ordinary diligence should have known of the injury

The board may extend for good cause.

If the staff determination invoked timeliness as a denial ground, the applicant can demonstrate the application was filed well within the statutory period.

## Discovery rule

The statute provides a discovery rule: the 7-year period may run from when the victim "knew or in the exercise of ordinary diligence could have discovered" the injury. For psychological injuries that develop over time (PTSD, depression), the discovery rule potentially extends the period to 7 years from when the psychological diagnosis was made or should have been made.

## Good cause extension (subd. (b))

Even if a deadline is missed, good cause extensions are available. The board must consider:
1. Whether emotional harm or pecuniary loss is incurred during prosecution or punishment of the perpetrator
2. Whether emotional harm or pecuniary loss is incurred at parole hearings or release

These factors recognize that the criminal process itself can cause additional victimization.

## Derivative victims (subd. (c))

For derivative victims (family members of homicide victims, etc.), the filing period is tolled when the primary victim's application is accepted. This prevents derivative victims from losing their claims while the primary victim's case is processed.
