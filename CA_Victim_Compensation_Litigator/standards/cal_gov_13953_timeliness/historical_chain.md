# Historical Chain — Gov. Code § 13953 CalVCB Application Timeliness

## The wound that created the rule

California's victim compensation program was established in 1965 — one of the first such programs in the United States. In its early form, the program had restrictive timelines that did not account for the reality of how crime victims experience trauma: many cannot act immediately after victimization. The shock of crime, trauma symptoms, fear of retaliation, and involvement in criminal proceedings all delay a victim's ability to navigate a compensation application.

Early strict deadlines created a class of victims who were eligible in principle but denied in practice because they could not meet bureaucratic timelines during the worst period of their lives.

## Legislative evolution

**1965 — Enactment of California's victim compensation program**
The program was created as a state response to the recognition that crime victims often bear economic losses that are neither recovered through restitution (offender's inability to pay) nor covered by private insurance. The original statute included short filing deadlines.

**Progressive liberalization of deadlines**
Over multiple legislative cycles, the filing deadline was extended in recognition of:
- Trauma delay: victims may not be able to apply immediately
- Minor victims: the 7-year post-majority rule for minor victims was added to ensure that children who were victimized could access compensation after they became adults
- Discovery rule: for ongoing or hidden harm (psychological injury, medical sequelae discovered later), the period runs from discovery

**Current framework — § 13953**
- 7-year period from the latest applicable date
- Three possible starting points: date of crime, date victim turns 21 (for minor victims), discovery of injury
- Good cause extension: Board may extend for good cause, including emotional harm from ongoing proceedings
- 3-year period for losses incurred during prosecution/punishment (§ 13953(b))

## The legislative purpose

The 7-year timeline reflects a deliberate legislative judgment that access to the compensation program should not be lost because of the natural, trauma-driven delays that affect many victims. A program whose purpose is compensating victims is internally contradictory if it denies compensation because victims needed time to recover enough to apply.

## The administrative abuse pattern — shortened deadlines

A documented pattern in CalVCB administration is the imposition of internally shortened deadlines — response windows for documentation requests, hearing notice periods — that are materially shorter than what the statute contemplates. This administrative shortening of deadlines acts as a de facto barrier to compensation for victims who need more time than the agency allows.

The A25-10117946 audit identified this pattern (shortened deadlines — documented in the 13 FAIL findings). The statutory 7-year period is not subject to administrative shortening; CalVCB cannot create internal deadlines that functionally defeat the statutory period.

## Current framework (2026)

Section 13953 as currently enacted provides the 7-year framework. The liberal construction mandate of § 13950 requires that timeliness exceptions and extensions be applied in favor of the applicant. The good cause extension provision of § 13953(b) is broadly available when the denial of extension would deprive a victim of compensation for harm proximately caused by the crime.
