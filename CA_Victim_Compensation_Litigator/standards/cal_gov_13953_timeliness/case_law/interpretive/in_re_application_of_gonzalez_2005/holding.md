# Timeliness and Good Cause in CalVCB Applications — Framework Entry

**Citation:** [Framework entry — specific CalVCB administrative decision citation pending]
**Forum:** California Victim Compensation Board (administrative)
**Statutes construed:** Government Code § 13953 — application timeliness; good cause extension; discovery rule for delayed injury

## Status note

Published appellate case law specifically interpreting § 13953 is limited. This entry documents the administrative and doctrinal framework for CalVCB timeliness determinations, derived from the statute, legislative history, and available administrative practice.

## The doctrinal framework

### The three-trigger structure

Under § 13953, the 7-year period runs from the LATEST of:

1. **Date of crime** — the default; most applications use this trigger
2. **7 years after minor victim turns 21** — for crimes against minors; a child victimized at age 10 has until age 28 to apply, even if the crime occurred 18 years earlier
3. **Discovery date** — for injuries the victim "knew or with the exercise of ordinary diligence could have discovered" — the 7-year period runs from discovery

**The discovery trigger is underused.** Many psychological injuries (PTSD, trauma sequelae, depression causally connected to the crime) are not immediately apparent. If a victim was victimized in 2023 and did not develop a diagnosable PTSD response until 2024, the discovery trigger for the psychological injury component may start in 2024 — not 2023.

### Good cause extension — § 13953(b)

The Board has discretion to extend the application period for good cause. Good cause includes:
- Emotional harm or pecuniary loss incurred during prosecution or punishment of the perpetrator
- Losses incurred at parole hearings or proceedings related to the crime
- Any circumstance that prevented the victim from applying within the standard period through no fault of the victim

**The good cause extension is not a narrow safety valve.** It is available whenever justice requires and the ordinary period would produce an inequitable result for a victim who could not act sooner.

### Administrative deadline shortening — not authorized

Section 13953 sets the outer limit for timely application; it does not authorize CalVCB to create shorter internal deadlines for documentation production that functionally defeat the statutory period. A victim who filed a timely application under § 13953 cannot be denied compensation because internal CalVCB processing deadlines — which are not in the statute — were not met.

**This is directly relevant to A25-10117946:** If the Board shortened documentation deadlines, response windows, or hearing notice periods below what the statute and administrative procedures allow, those shortened deadlines are ultra vires and the denial based on them is reversible error.

### The A25-10117946 timeliness analysis

- **Crime date:** June 16, 2023
- **Application filed:** 2025 (A25-10117946)
