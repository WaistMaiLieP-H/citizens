HOLDING:

§ 13953 THREE-TRIGGER TIMELINESS STRUCTURE:

1. **The 7-year period runs from the LATEST of three triggers:**
   - **Date of crime** — the default; most applications use this trigger
   - **7 years after minor victim turns 21** — for crimes against minors; extends the window substantially
   - **Discovery date** — for injuries the victim "knew or with the exercise of ordinary diligence could have discovered" — the 7-year period runs from the date of discovery

2. **The discovery trigger is underused.** Many psychological injuries (PTSD, trauma sequelae, depression causally connected to the crime) are not immediately apparent. If a victim was victimized in 2023 and did not develop a diagnosable PTSD response until 2024, the discovery trigger for the psychological injury component starts in 2024 — not 2023.

3. **Good cause extension (§ 13953(b)).** The Board has discretion to extend the application period for good cause. Good cause includes: emotional harm or pecuniary loss incurred during prosecution or punishment of the perpetrator; losses incurred at parole hearings; any circumstance that prevented the victim from applying within the standard period through no fault of the victim. The good cause extension is not a narrow safety valve — it is available whenever justice requires and the ordinary period would produce an inequitable result.

4. **Administrative deadline shortening is not authorized.** Section 13953 sets the outer limit for timely application; it does not authorize CalVCB to create shorter internal deadlines for documentation production that functionally defeat the statutory period. A victim who filed a timely application cannot be denied because internal CalVCB processing deadlines — which are not in the statute — were not met.
