# Gov. Code § 13959 — Hearing and Appeal Procedure (Context)

## What § 13959 does

Section 13959 is the procedural backbone of CalVCB administrative review. It establishes the right to a hearing, the hearing conduct rules, the decision timelines, the reconsideration procedure, and (by implication) the path to superior court review via CCP § 1094.5.

## Key rights and deadlines

1. **Right to hearing** (subd. (a)) — An applicant who contests a staff recommendation to deny compensation, in whole or in part, SHALL be granted a hearing. This is mandatory, not discretionary.

2. **10-day notice of hearing** (subd. (b)) — The Board must notify the applicant at least 10 days before the hearing. Shorter notice is a procedural violation.

3. **Burden of proof** (subd. (c)) — Applicant bears the burden of establishing § 13955 eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence. This is lower than clear-and-convincing or beyond-a-reasonable-doubt, but the burden remains on the applicant throughout.

4. **Law as of application date** (subd. (d)) — The hearing officer applies the law in effect as of the date the application was submitted. This matters for applications filed before or after specific amendments — if an amendment post-dates the application, the older version controls.

5. **Informal evidence rules** (subd. (e)(1)) — The Board may rely on any evidence that "responsible persons are accustomed to rely in the conduct of serious affairs." This is an administrative-law standard lower than the California Evidence Code. Hearsay is generally admissible. Police reports are admissible. Written reports from public agencies are admissible.

6. **Service animal accommodation** (subd. (e)(2)) — Service animals accompany witnesses at hearings.

7. **Convenient location or telephone** (subd. (f)) — If the applicant's presence is required, the hearing is scheduled at a convenient location or conducted by telephone.

8. **Hearing officer delegation** (subd. (g)) — The Board may delegate appeals to hearing officers. Most appeals are heard by hearing officers, not the full Board.

9. **Six-month decision deadline** (subd. (h)) — The Board's decision must be in writing within six months of receipt of the appeal. If the Board finds the record insufficient, it must notify the applicant within six months.

10. **Reconsideration procedure** (subd. (i)) —
    - Only ONE reconsideration per decision
    - 30 days from personal delivery OR 60 days from mailing
    - Written request required
    - Board has discretion to grant or deny

11. **Board-initiated reconsideration** (subd. (j)) — At any time, at Board discretion.

12. **Post-denial evidence** (subd. (k)) — After reconsideration denial, new evidence is NOT considered unless the Board reconsiders on its own motion.

## The six-month deadline (critical)

Subdivision (h) is the **timing mechanism** of the statute. The Board must decide within six months of receiving the appeal, or must notify the applicant within six months that the record is insufficient. This is a hard deadline that provides one of the clearest appeal grounds: if the Board blows the six-month deadline without providing the insufficiency notice, the applicant has a procedural due process argument and can escalate.

## Standard of review on writ of mandate (CCP § 1094.5)

When administrative remedies under § 13959 are exhausted (initial decision + one reconsideration), the final decision is reviewable in superior court via CCP § 1094.5 writ of mandate. The standard of review depends on whether "fundamental vested rights" are at stake:

- **Fundamental vested rights** — court reviews factual findings de novo ("independent judgment")
- **Non-fundamental rights** — court reviews factual findings for substantial evidence

CalVCB eligibility determinations are often treated as non-fundamental-vested-rights decisions (substantial evidence review), but there is case law suggesting otherwise in certain circumstances. The appeal brief should argue for independent judgment review if possible.

## Informal evidence rules — both sword and shield

Subdivision (e)(1)'s informal evidence rule cuts both ways:

- **Sword for applicant:** The Board can consider hearsay, personal statements, medical records without authentication, and other evidence that would be inadmissible in a civil trial. This gives applicants flexibility to build a record without expensive authentication procedures.

- **Sword for Board:** The Board can rely on its staff report, police reports, and public agency records that the applicant may dispute. If the applicant cannot disprove the Board's reliance source, the record supports the Board's conclusion.

- **Shield for applicant:** The applicant can introduce any credible evidence, including declarations, medical narratives, mental health reports, and witness statements.

- **Shield for Board:** The Board does not have to follow formal rules.

The practical implication: build the applicant's record with multiple sources. Do not rely on a single document. Corroborate every factual claim with at least two independent pieces of evidence.

## Relevance to A25-10117946

Section 13959 is central to the procedural posture of the appeal. Every 13 FAIL finding can potentially be mapped to a § 13959 procedural rule:

1. **10-day notice failure** → § 13959(b)
2. **Six-month deadline failure** → § 13959(h)
3. **Hearing scheduling failure (location/telephone)** → § 13959(f)
4. **Evidence handling failure** → § 13959(e)(1) (Board relied on inadmissible evidence OR excluded admissible evidence)
5. **Law-as-of-application-date failure** → § 13959(d)
6. **Burden of proof confusion** → § 13959(c)
7. **Delegation-to-hearing-officer irregularities** → § 13959(g)
8. **Reconsideration denial irregularities** → § 13959(i), (k)

Each mapping is a separate ground for reversal.
