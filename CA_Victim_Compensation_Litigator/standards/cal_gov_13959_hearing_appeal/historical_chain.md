# Historical Chain — Gov. Code § 13959 (CalVCB Hearing and Appeal Procedure)

## The wound

Early CalVCB practice (1965-1990s) provided minimal procedural protection to applicants. The State Board of Control had broad discretion to deny applications. Hearings were not always granted. Decision timelines were loose. Reconsideration was rare. Writ review in superior court was the theoretical remedy, but the informal-evidence rule and deferential standard of review made reversals uncommon.

The wound was **procedural asymmetry**: the Board had near-unlimited administrative discretion, and applicants had near-unlimited burden to prove both eligibility and procedural errors, with little statutory structure to cabin Board behavior.

## The promise

Over a series of amendments, § 13959 codified specific procedural rights:

- **Mandatory hearing** on contested denials (subd. (a))
- **10-day notice** requirement (subd. (b))
- **Burden allocation** by preponderance (subd. (c))
- **Law-as-of-application-date** rule (subd. (d))
- **Informal evidence rules** with explicit standard (subd. (e))
- **Service animal accommodation** (added in 2000s/2010s)
- **Convenient location or telephone** requirement (subd. (f))
- **Six-month decision deadline** (subd. (h))
- **Reconsideration procedure** with specific deadlines (subd. (i))
- **Post-denial evidence limits** (subd. (k))

The promise: **the Board's procedural discretion is not unlimited. There are specific rules the Board must follow. Failure to follow those rules is grounds for reversal.**

## The chain

| Era | Development |
|---|---|
| 1965-1980s | Minimal procedural structure; Board Board of Control broad discretion |
| 1990s | Formalization of hearing right; notice requirements added |
| 2002 | Stats. 2002, ch. 1141 recodification; § 13959 enacted in current numbering |
| 2008 | Marsy's Law overlay — constitutional victim rights add due process floor |
| Post-2010 | Service animal, convenient location, six-month deadline refinements |
| 2022-2023 | Conditional sunset provisions; clarifications |

## The promise today

Section 13959 gives applicants a structured appeal path with specific, enforceable procedural rights. The combination of (a) mandatory hearing, (b) notice requirement, (c) burden allocation, (h) decision deadline, and (i) reconsideration procedure creates a framework within which the Board must operate. When the Board fails to honor any of these provisions, the failure is reviewable.

For A25-10117946, this means the appeal is not a one-shot "reverse the denial" argument — it is a structured attack on every procedural failure the audit identified. Each failure, tied to a specific § 13959 provision, is a separate reversal ground.
