## The § 13959 hearing framework

### Right to hearing — the threshold

Section 13959 provides that an applicant who receives an adverse determination has the right to request a hearing before the Board. The right to a hearing is not discretionary — it is a mandatory procedural protection that the Board must honor.

**Timing:** The applicant must request a hearing within a specified period after receiving notice of the adverse determination. Confirm the current regulatory deadline from CalVCB's administrative rules.

**Purpose:** The hearing is a de novo review of the staff determination. The applicant may:
- Present additional documentation
- Call witnesses
- Challenge the staff analysis
- Present legal argument on the statute

### What can be presented at hearing

The hearing is not limited to the record before the staff. An applicant who was denied because documentation was not timely produced may present that documentation at the hearing if it was not available earlier or if the denial of an extension was error.

**New evidence rule:** The hearing officer should receive any evidence relevant to the claim, subject to appropriate evidentiary standards. A denial based solely on documentation unavailable at the time of the staff decision should be reconsidered at hearing when the documentation is produced.

### Standard of review at hearing

The hearing officer conducts a de novo review on the legal and factual questions. The staff determination is not entitled to deference. The applicable standard is whether the evidence establishes by a preponderance that the applicant qualifies for compensation under §§ 13955-13957.

### The appeal brief structure

An effective § 13959 appeal brief must:

1. **State the denial grounds** — identify exactly what the staff determination said and why it denied or reduced the claim
2. **Address each denial ground** — for each ground, argue: (a) the statutory standard that applies, (b) the evidence that satisfies the standard, (c) the error in the staff's application
3. **Invoke the liberal construction mandate** — argue that ambiguous provisions must be resolved in favor of the applicant under § 13950
4. **Identify process violations** — if the CalVCB process itself was defective (inadequate notice, shortened deadlines, failure to consider evidence), document the violation and its prejudicial effect
5. **Request specific relief** — specify what compensation is sought, broken down by category

### Judicial review — § 13960

If the Board's hearing decision is adverse, § 13960 provides for judicial review by writ of mandate (Code Civ. Proc. § 1094.5). The standard of judicial review is abuse of discretion. A Board determination that misapplies the statute, fails to consider relevant evidence, or imposes requirements not in the statute is an abuse of discretion.

**The prejudicial error standard:** The court will grant the writ only if the error was prejudicial — i.e., if the Board had applied the statute correctly, the result might have been different. For most CalVCB denials based on documentation defects or shortened deadlines, this standard is easily met: if proper notice had been given, the applicant could have produced the required documentation.

**Phase 2 — § 13960 writ (if necessary):**
If the Board upholds the adverse determination despite the § 13959 hearing, § 13960 writ of mandate is available in superior court. The writ petition should argue:
- Legal error in applying §§ 13955-13957
- Abuse of discretion in applying shortened deadlines
- Failure to apply liberal construction mandate
- Procedural due process violation (inadequate notice of deadlines and requirements)
