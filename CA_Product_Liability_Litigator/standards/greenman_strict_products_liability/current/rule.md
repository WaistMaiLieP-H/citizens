## The Rule

A manufacturer (or any commercial seller in the chain of distribution) is **strictly liable** — without proof of negligence and without privity of contract — when:

1. It places a product on the market;
2. Knowing it will be used without inspection for defects;
3. The product proves to have a defect;
4. That defect causes injury to a person using the product in a reasonably foreseeable way.

Strict liability means the plaintiff does not have to prove the defendant was careless. The fact of the defect causing harm is sufficient.

---

## Three Types of Defect

Greenman established the framework. Subsequent California cases identified three defect types, each sufficient to trigger strict liability:

### 1. Manufacturing Defect
The specific unit deviated from the manufacturer's own intended design. The product came off the line wrong. Strict liability applies even if the design was perfectly safe — this particular unit was not built correctly.

**Test:** Did this product deviate from the manufacturer's intended design?

**SIRVA relevance:** If Walgreens used a bent or defective needle, that is a manufacturing defect in the device — separate from the administration negligence claim.

### 2. Design Defect
The entire product line is defective because of how it was designed. Even if perfectly manufactured, the design itself is dangerous.

**Test (Barker dual test — separate standard):**
- Consumer expectations prong: Did the product perform below the level an ordinary consumer would expect?
- Risk-utility prong: Do the risks of the design outweigh its benefits?

### 3. Failure to Warn
The product was not dangerous by design or manufacturing defect, but the manufacturer failed to provide adequate warnings about known or reasonably foreseeable risks.

**Test:** Did the manufacturer warn of all risks that were known or should have been known at the time of manufacture?

## Application to Active Cases

### SIRVA — Walgreens COVID Vaccine

**What this standard covers:**
- If the syringe or needle was defective (manufacturing defect) — applies
- If the vaccine was defectively designed (design defect) — **does NOT apply to Walgreens** (Walgreens didn't design the vaccine)
- The PRIMARY theory against Walgreens is negligent administration — see CA_Medical_Malpractice_Litigator and PL-010 skill

**VICP boundary:** Walgreens is an administering party, not a manufacturer. Greenman strict liability against the vaccine MANUFACTURER (Moderna/Pfizer) is preempted by 42 USC §300aa-11 for design defects in vaccines. Greenman strict liability against Walgreens for device defects (syringe, needle) is NOT preempted.

### RedJag 2018 Jaguar XE

**What this standard covers:**
- Vehicle sold with stripped component parts (stripped steering column, missing safety equipment) — manufacturing defect of the specific vehicle as delivered
- Cross-reference: VEH §11713 dealer prohibited acts (separate CA_Vehicle_Code_Specialist standard)

## Defenses

| Defense | Description | Strength Against Greenman |
|---|---|---|
| Product misuse | Plaintiff used product in unforeseeable way | Strong if misuse was unforeseeable |
| Comparative fault | Plaintiff's own negligence reduces recovery | Applies (Li v. Yellow Cab, 1975) — reduces, does not bar |
| Assumption of risk | Plaintiff knew of defect and voluntarily encountered it | Limited post-comparative fault |
| Learned intermediary | For drugs/medical devices: adequate warning to physician sufficient | Applies to manufacturer-physician relationship; NOT to Walgreens administration |
| VICP preemption | Manufacturer design defect claims preempted for vaccines | Applies to manufacturer only; NOT to Walgreens |
