## The Dual Test

Design defect exists if EITHER prong is satisfied:

### Prong 1 — Consumer Expectations

**Question:** Did the product fail to perform as safely as an ordinary consumer would expect when used in an intended or reasonably foreseeable manner?

**Who applies:** Jury assesses from the perspective of an ordinary consumer of that product type.

**Burden:** Plaintiff bears the full burden. Plaintiff must show the consumer expectations and the failure to meet them.

**When to use:** Products where ordinary consumers have clear, articulable safety expectations based on common experience. A chair should not collapse. A car door should not open during normal cornering. A handrail should not snap under normal body weight.

**When NOT to use (*Soule* limitation):** Complex technical products where ordinary consumers would have no reasonable expectation about the specific design feature at issue. Do not use consumer expectations prong for automobile crash-dynamics, medical device failure modes, or aircraft component specifications.

---

### Prong 2 — Risk-Utility (Burden Shifts to Defendant)

**Question:** Do the risks of the challenged design outweigh its benefits?

**Step 1 — Plaintiff's burden:** Prove the product's design caused the injury.

**Step 2 — Burden SHIFTS to defendant:** Defendant must prove the design benefits outweigh the risks, considering:

| Factor | What Defendant Must Address |
|---|---|
| Gravity of danger | How severe is the harm the design risk could cause? |
| Likelihood of injury | How probable is injury from this design? |
| Feasibility of safer design | Does a safer alternative exist that is mechanically workable? |
| Cost of safer design | What would it cost to implement the safer alternative? |
| Adverse consequences of alternative | Does the safer design compromise other functions or benefits? |

**Significance of burden shift:** The manufacturer, who designed the product and conducted (or should have conducted) testing, is in the best position to produce evidence about design choices. *Barker* places the burden there.

---

## Comparison: Design Defect vs. Manufacturing Defect

| Issue | Manufacturing Defect | Design Defect |
|---|---|---|
| What's defective | This specific unit | The entire product line / all units |
| Standard | Deviated from intended design | Failed consumer expectations OR risk > utility |
| Test | Did unit match design specs? | Barker dual test |
| Burden | Plaintiff throughout | Plaintiff (step 1) then shifts to defendant (risk-utility prong) |

## Application to Active Cases

### Spine Surgery — Dr. Wiseman Hardware

If the spinal hardware (screws, plates, rods) was defective in design — a design shared across all units of that hardware line — *Barker* risk-utility applies. Dr. Wiseman selected the hardware; the hardware manufacturer faces design defect strict liability if the design was risky relative to benefit.

**Note:** This overlaps with CA_Medical_Malpractice_Litigator's SIRVA and surgical negligence standards. The design defect theory runs against the MANUFACTURER. The surgical negligence theory runs against Dr. Wiseman. Both may be pled simultaneously against different defendants.

### RedJag 2018 Jaguar XE

**Design defect unlikely as to Walgreens (not applicable).** For the vehicle:
- If stripped parts were standard equipment that failed (e.g., stripped steering column components), consumer expectations prong applies: consumers expect steering columns not to fail.
- Risk-utility prong requires showing safer steering column designs existed and were feasible.
- More likely theories: manufacturing defect (Greenman) and dealer fraud (VEH §11713).

### COVID Vaccine — Design Defect

**BLOCKED by VICP preemption.** Any design defect claim against Moderna/Pfizer/J&J for the COVID vaccine's design is preempted under 42 USC §300aa-11 and *Bruesewitz v. Wyeth* (2011). *Barker* design defect cannot be used against vaccine manufacturers for vaccine design. See `vicp_300aa_11_preemption` standard.

**Walgreens exemption:** *Barker* design defect does not apply to Walgreens because Walgreens did not design the vaccine. The claim against Walgreens is negligent administration — a separate theory.
