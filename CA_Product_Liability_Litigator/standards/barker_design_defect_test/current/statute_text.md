# Barker v. Lull Engineering Co. — Design Defect Dual Test

**Source:** *Barker v. Lull Engineering Co., Inc.* (1978) 20 Cal.3d 413
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Author:** Chief Justice Rose Elizabeth Bird
**Retrieved:** 2026-04-13
**Type:** Case law standard (controlling precedent)
**Verified:** true (well-established Supreme Court of California precedent; codified in CACI Nos. 1203, 1204)

---

## Holding — The Dual Test for Design Defect

A product is defective in design if the plaintiff proves EITHER:

**Prong 1 — Consumer Expectations:**
> "A product may be found defective in design if the plaintiff demonstrates that the product failed to perform as safely as an ordinary consumer would expect when used in an intended or reasonably foreseeable manner."
> — 20 Cal.3d 413, 432

**Prong 2 — Risk-Utility:**
> "A product may alternatively be found defective in design if the plaintiff demonstrates that the product's design proximately caused his injury and the defendant fails to establish, in light of the relevant factors, that, on balance, the benefits of the challenged design outweigh the risk of danger inherent in such design."
> — 20 Cal.3d 413, 432

**Either prong alone is sufficient to establish design defect.**

---

## Burden of Proof — Shift Under Risk-Utility

*Barker* made a decisive allocation: under the risk-utility prong, once the plaintiff proves:
1. The product's design caused the injury, AND
2. The design was a proximate cause of the harm,

**the burden shifts to the defendant** to establish that the design's benefits outweigh its risks. This is plaintiff-favorable — the defendant must prove its design was justified.

Factors the defendant must address under risk-utility burden:
- The gravity of the danger posed by the challenged design
- The likelihood that such danger would cause injury
- The mechanical feasibility of a safer alternative design
- The financial cost of an improved design
- The adverse consequences to the product and to the consumer that would result from an alternative design

---

## Limitation — *Soule* Refinement (1994)

*Soule v. General Motors Corp.* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 548 refined the consumer expectations prong:

> The consumer expectations test is **inappropriate** when the product is sufficiently complex that ordinary consumers would have no reasonable, articulate expectations about the specific design feature at issue.

For **complex products** (automobile crash-worthiness, medical devices, aircraft), the **risk-utility prong is the primary test**. Consumer expectations prong remains available for simple products where ordinary experience gives consumers a baseline expectation.

**Practice consequence:** In cases involving complex machinery or medical devices, use risk-utility as primary and consumer expectations as alternative.
