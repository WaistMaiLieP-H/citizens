# Historical Chain — Origin: Barker v. Lull Engineering (1978)

**Standard:** barker_design_defect_test
**Era:** 1978 — Post-Greenman Design Defect Framework
**Document:** *Barker v. Lull Engineering Co., Inc.* (1978) 20 Cal.3d 413

---

## The Case

Gary Barker was injured while operating a high-lift loader manufactured by Lull Engineering. The loader tipped while Barker was working on uneven terrain. He claimed the design was defective — the loader was unstable for its intended use on sloped terrain.

The trial court gave jury instructions based on negligence principles. The California Supreme Court reversed and used the case to establish the definitive design defect standard for strict products liability.

## The 1965 Restatement Gap

RESTATEMENT (SECOND) OF TORTS §402A (1965) adopted *Greenman* strict liability but did not fully resolve the design defect analysis. The Restatement used language about products being "unreasonably dangerous" — which courts were reading as a negligence-like inquiry. This created confusion: was design defect strict liability, or was it just negligence in disguise?

*Barker* (1978) answered definitively: design defect analysis under California strict liability uses the dual test, with risk-utility burden on the defendant. It is NOT negligence.

## Chief Justice Bird's Framework

Chief Justice Rose Bird, writing for the court, saw two legitimate approaches to design defect and refused to choose between them — the dual test kept both. Her critical innovation was identifying that the BURDEN under risk-utility should rest with the defendant:

> "We recognize that in many situations it would be difficult to determine whether a product is unreasonably dangerous if we place the burden solely on the plaintiff. ... Since a manufacturer is in a far better position than an injured plaintiff to determine the feasibility of alternative designs, it seems fair to place the burden on the manufacturer in that regard once the plaintiff establishes that the product's design was a proximate cause of his or her injuries."

## Subsequent Development

| Year | Case | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 1978 | *Barker v. Lull Engineering* 20 Cal.3d 413 | **ORIGIN: Dual test established; burden shift under risk-utility** |
| 1982 | *Campbell v. General Motors Corp.* 32 Cal.3d 112 | Applied consumer expectations to seat design |
| 1994 | *Soule v. General Motors Corp.* 8 Cal.4th 548 | Refined: complex products → risk-utility primary; consumer expectations limited |
| 1997 | *Viner v. Sweet* | Further refinement of causation element |
| 2024 | CACI Nos. 1203, 1204 | Current jury instructions implementing Barker/Soule framework |
