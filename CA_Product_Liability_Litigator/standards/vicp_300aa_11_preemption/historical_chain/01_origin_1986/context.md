# Historical Chain — Origin: National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act (1986)

**Standard:** vicp_300aa_11_preemption
**Era:** 1986 — NCVIA Enactment
**Document:** Pub. L. No. 99-660, Title III (National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act), 100 Stat. 3755 (November 14, 1986)

---

## The DTP Litigation Crisis

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, the pertussis (whooping cough) component of the DTP vaccine was associated with reports of serious neurological injuries in children. Parents filed tort suits against vaccine manufacturers. Courts allowed strict liability and design defect claims under Greenman/Barker and similar state law frameworks.

By 1985-1986:
- Several manufacturers had exited the vaccine market entirely
- Vaccine prices skyrocketed as remaining manufacturers built litigation reserves into pricing
- Public health officials warned that the national childhood vaccination program was at risk

**Congress's diagnosis:** Unlimited tort liability for vaccines, which have unavoidable side effects at the population level, creates market failures that threaten public health. A better-designed compensation mechanism was needed.

## The NCVIA Compromise (1986)

Congress enacted the NCVIA as a compromise:

**For manufacturers:**
- Immunity from design defect liability for unavoidable side effects (§300aa-22(b))
- No liability for failure to warn if manufacturer warned the administering provider (§300aa-22(c))
- Pre-emption of state tort design defect claims (later confirmed by Bruesewitz)

**For injured patients:**
- No-fault compensation through the Vaccine Court (U.S. Court of Federal Claims)
- Vaccine Injury Table — presumptive causation for listed injuries
- Quick administrative process — no need to prove negligence
- Attorneys' fees paid by the program for successful petitions
- Must file VICP petition before civil suit against manufacturer (§300aa-11(a)(2))

## Post-Enactment Development

| Year | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1986 | NCVIA enacted | VICP established; manufacturer immunity codified |
| 1988 | VICP operative date | October 1, 1988 — immunity applies to vaccines administered after this date |
| 1993 | Vaccine Injury Table first published | 42 CFR §100.3 |
| 2011 | *Bruesewitz v. Wyeth LLC* 562 U.S. 223 | Design defect claims against manufacturers fully preempted |
| 2017 | SIRVA added to Vaccine Injury Table | SIRVA becomes Table Injury (presumptive causation in VICP) |
| 2022 | COVID-19 vaccines added to Table | SIRVA for COVID vaccines added; 3-year filing window applies |
