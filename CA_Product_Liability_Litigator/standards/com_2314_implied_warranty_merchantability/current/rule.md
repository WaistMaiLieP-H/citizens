# COM § 2314 Implied Warranty of Merchantability — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** com_2314_implied_warranty_merchantability
**Citizen:** CA_Product_Liability_Litigator
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

When a MERCHANT sells goods of the kind in which they deal, the law implies a warranty that the goods are MERCHANTABLE — meaning they are fit for the ordinary purposes for which such goods are used.

This warranty:
- Is implied automatically by law — it does not require express promise
- Applies only when the seller is a MERCHANT (professional dealer in that type of good)
- Does NOT require privity beyond the immediate buyer-seller relationship for property damage claims
- CAN be excluded — but only with specific conspicuous language or "as is" disclosure meeting statutory requirements

---

## Elements of a COM § 2314 Claim

1. **Merchant seller:** The seller was a merchant with respect to goods of the type sold (professional dealer, not a private individual selling a used car)
2. **Sale of goods:** The transaction was a sale of goods (not a pure service)
3. **Goods not merchantable:** The goods failed to meet at least one of the §2314(2) standards — most commonly (a) [did not pass without objection] or (c) [not fit for ordinary purposes]
4. **Causation:** The unmerchantable condition caused the plaintiff's damage
5. **Damages:** Actual damages (price difference, repair cost, consequential damages if foreseeable)

---

## Merchantability Standards — §2314(2) Analysis

The six prongs of §2314(2) are conjunctive minimums — all must be met. The most litigated:

**§2314(2)(a) — Contract description conformance:**
The goods must pass without objection in the trade under the CONTRACT DESCRIPTION. If a vehicle is sold as a "2018 Jaguar XE" and the CARFAX shows it is actually a different vehicle (different VIN, different history), the goods do not pass without objection under the contract description.

**§2314(2)(c) — Fit for ordinary purposes:**
The goods must be fit for the ordinary purposes for which such goods are used. A vehicle must be drivable, roadworthy, and have good title. A vehicle with:
- A stolen or tampered VIN (VEH §10751) — cannot be lawfully titled or registered → NOT fit for ordinary purposes
- Stripped safety components — cannot be safely operated → NOT fit for ordinary purposes
- Wrong CARFAX/accident history — arguably misrepresents safety-relevant history → NOT fit for ordinary purposes under §2314(2)(a)

**§2314(2)(f) — Label conformance:**
The goods must conform to promises or affirmations of fact on the label or container. A CARFAX report provided at point of sale is an affirmation of fact about the vehicle. A false CARFAX is a §2314(2)(f) violation.

## Exclusion of Warranty — COM § 2316

**Standard requirement (§2316(2)):** Exclusion of merchantability requires:
- Mention of the word "merchantability"
- Must be conspicuous in a writing

**"As is" exception (§2316(3)(a)):** "As is," "with all faults," or similar language excludes all implied warranties IF:
- The language calls buyer's attention to the exclusion, AND
- Makes plain that there is no implied warranty

**Critical limit on "as is" — FRAUD VITIATES THE DISCLAIMER:**
Under California law, an "as is" clause does NOT protect a seller who commits fraud. CIV §§1572/1573 (actual/constructive fraud) override contractual warranty disclaimers. A seller cannot disclaim their way out of liability for intentional misrepresentation or concealment.

*Perkins v. Superior Court* (1981) 117 Cal.App.3d 1 — "as is" clause does not protect seller who fraudulently concealed defects.
*Hines v. Brode* (1913) 168 Cal. 507 — fraud in the inducement voids the contract itself, including any disclaimer provisions.

**Examination exception (§2316(3)(b)):** No warranty for defects the buyer EXAMINED and should have discovered. BUT: this requires the buyer to have been given the actual opportunity to examine. A stripped VIN component concealed inside the vehicle is not discoverable on routine inspection.

## SOL — COM § 2725 (4-Year Warranty Period)

- **Accrual at delivery:** Warranty breach accrues when the vehicle is delivered — NOT when discovered.
- **RedJag delivery:** 2018. Four-year SOL expired approximately 2022.
- **Future performance exception:** If the dealer made any warranty about future performance of the vehicle (e.g., "passes smog," "clean title guaranteed"), accrual is when the breach was or should have been discovered.
- **Title warranty as future performance:** A warranty of good title arguably extends to future performance — the buyer relies on good title through the entire period of ownership, not just at delivery. This may extend the §2725 SOL.
- **Fraud overrides §2725:** CCP §338(d) fraud SOL (3-year from discovery) may survive even where the contractual warranty SOL has expired if the seller committed fraud in connection with the sale.

## Application to Active Cases

### RedJag 2018 Jaguar XE

**Merchant:** Auto dealer — YES. Merchants in used vehicles.

**Goods not merchantable:**
- Wrong CARFAX = §2314(2)(a)/(f) violation — description mismatch and false affirmation
- VIN tampering (VEH §10751) = §2314(2)(c) violation — not fit for ordinary purposes (cannot be lawfully operated/titled with tampered VIN)
- Stripped parts = §2314(2)(c) violation — not fit for safe operation

**"As is" defense:**
- If dealer had "as is" language: must be conspicuous; must mention merchantability OR use "as is" prominently
- Fraud override: wrong CARFAX and VIN tampering are fraudulent concealment — "as is" does not protect the dealer from fraud claims
- Examination exception: VIN tampering concealed inside vehicle structure — not discoverable on routine test drive

**SOL:** See above. §2725 4-year on its face expired 2022. Future performance (title warranty) and fraud exceptions may extend.

## Comparison: §2314 Warranty vs. Greenman Strict Liability

| Issue | COM §2314 Implied Warranty | Greenman Strict Liability |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Contract (UCC) | Tort |
| Privity | Buyer-seller | None required |
| Exclusion | Yes — "as is" with proper notice | No contractual exclusion |
| SOL | 4 years (COM §2725) | 2-3 years (CCP §335.1/§338) |
| Personal injury | Limited in warranty (COM §2719) | Full tort recovery |
| Fraud override | Yes — CIV §1572/§1573 | N/A — tort claim |
| Damages | Contract damages (price, repair, consequential) | Full tort damages |

Both theories may be pled simultaneously against the same defendant.
