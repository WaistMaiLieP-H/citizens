## The Uniform Commercial Code

The Uniform Commercial Code was drafted by the American Law Institute and the National Conference of Commissioners on Uniform State Laws between 1942 and 1952. Article 2 (Sales) included §2-314, the implied warranty of merchantability, which replaced the fragmented common law of sales warranties with a unified, buyer-protective framework.

The California Commercial Code adopted UCC Article 2 as Chapter 2 of the Commercial Code, effective January 1, 1965. COM §2314 is the direct California enactment of UCC §2-314.

## The Common Law it Replaced

Before the UCC, California sales law was governed by:
- The California Sales Act (Civil Code provisions) — which had a limited implied warranty rule
- Common law *caveat emptor* — buyer beware, applied strictly
- Express warranty requirements — only explicit promises bound sellers

The UCC adoption made merchantability the DEFAULT implied warranty for ALL merchant sellers, removing the need for buyers to negotiate basic product fitness.

## The "As Is" Exception History

The "as is" exclusion in §2316(3)(a) was included to preserve commercial flexibility — bulk sales, auctions, estate sales, and distressed-inventory transactions where all parties understand the goods are sold without warranty. The exception was NOT designed to enable retail fraud. Courts have consistently applied the fraud override to prevent sellers from weaponizing "as is" language against buyers they deceived.

| Year | Development |
