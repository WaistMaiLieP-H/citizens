# Statute of Limitations — Three Years — Property Damage and Fraud

## Text (operative provisions for product liability and fraud)

Within three years:

**(a)** An action upon a liability created by statute, other than a penalty or forfeiture.

**(b)** An action for trespass upon or injury to real property.

**(c)(1)** An action for taking, detaining, or injuring goods or chattels, including an action for the specific recovery of personal property.

[Note: §338(c)(2)-(6) address fine art theft and Holocaust-era property — not reproduced; full text at leginfo URL above.]

**(d)** An action for relief on the ground of fraud or mistake. The cause of action in that case is not deemed to have accrued until the discovery, by the aggrieved party, of the facts constituting the fraud or mistake.

---

## Key Provisions for Product Liability Context

**§338(a) — Statutory liability:** Covers claims under statutes that create liability without specifying their own limitations period. Relevant to implied warranty claims under COM §2314 and vehicle code fraud claims under VEH §11713.

**§338(c)(1) — Personal property damage:** The 3-year period for injury to goods or chattels covers property damage caused by a defective product. Distinguishes from the 2-year personal injury SOL (CCP §335.1).

**§338(d) — Fraud with discovery rule:** The cause of action does not accrue until the aggrieved party DISCOVERS the facts constituting the fraud or mistake. The word "discovery" means actual knowledge of the fraud, not constructive knowledge or the existence of suspicious circumstances that would put a reasonable person on inquiry notice — though California courts apply an objective test: when the plaintiff knew or, through reasonable diligence, should have known.
