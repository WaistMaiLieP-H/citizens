# CCP § 338 Property Damage and Fraud SOL — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** ccp_338_property_fraud_sol
**Citizen:** CA_Product_Liability_Litigator
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

Under CCP § 338, a plaintiff has **three years** to bring actions for:

1. **Property damage (§338(c)(1)):** Taking, detaining, or injuring goods or chattels (personal property)
2. **Fraud (§338(d)):** Relief on the ground of fraud or mistake — with the clock starting at DISCOVERY of the fraud, not at the time of the fraudulent act

---

## Product Liability SOL Map — Two Distinct Tracks

Product liability cases often involve two types of harm, each with a different SOL:

| Harm Type | SOL Provision | Period | Accrual Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal injury (bodily harm) | CCP §335.1 | **2 years** | Date of injury (discovery rule applies for latent injuries — *Jolly v. Eli Lilly*) |
| Property damage (damage to goods/chattels) | CCP §338(c) | **3 years** | Date of damage to property |
| Fraud/misrepresentation | CCP §338(d) | **3 years** | Date plaintiff DISCOVERS the fraud or could have discovered it with reasonable diligence |
| Breach of written warranty | CCP §337 | **4 years** | Date of breach (or discovery under Commercial Code) |
| Breach of oral warranty | CCP §339 | **2 years** | Date of breach |

**Critical distinction:** A plaintiff who suffers both personal injury AND property damage from the same defective product may have a 2-year window for bodily injury claims and a 3-year window for property damage claims running simultaneously from different trigger events.

---

## The Fraud Discovery Rule (§338(d)) — Detailed Analysis

**Standard accrual:** Causes of action normally accrue when the injury occurs.

**§338(d) exception:** For fraud, the cause of action does NOT accrue until the plaintiff discovers — or with reasonable diligence should have discovered — the facts constituting the fraud.

### What Triggers Discovery?

California courts apply an objective test: the SOL begins when the plaintiff:
1. Actually knows the facts constituting the fraud, OR
2. Has sufficient "storm warnings" that a reasonably diligent person would have made further inquiry and would have discovered the fraud.

Mere suspicion is not enough to start the clock. But willful ignorance of obvious red flags does not toll the SOL.

### Key Case Law on Discovery Rule

- *Hobart v. Hobart Estate Co.* (1945) 26 Cal.2d 412 — baseline rule
- *Jolly v. Eli Lilly & Co.* (1988) 44 Cal.3d 1103 — discovery rule in drug litigation context (personal injury, §335.1 — but the discovery rule analysis is the same)
- *Norgart v. Upjohn Co.* (1999) 21 Cal.4th 383 — plaintiff charged with knowledge of facts they would have learned through reasonable investigation

## Application to Active Cases

### SIRVA — Walgreens COVID Vaccine (~November 2021)

**Personal injury SOL:** CCP §335.1 — 2-year SOL. Injury date approximately November 2021. Surgery April 20, 2022. If personal injury claim was not filed by approximately November 2023, it may be time-barred UNLESS:
- Discovery rule applies: if the connection between the injection and the shoulder injury was not discovered until later, the clock starts at discovery
- SIRVA symptoms can be delayed — onset within 48 hours of injection is the diagnostic criterion, but medical diagnosis may come later
- **Steward note:** Verify exact injection date and when SIRVA diagnosis was made. Discovery rule tolling may be available.

**Property damage SOL:** §338(c) — 3-year period — would apply if any personal property (e.g., medical equipment) was damaged. Not the primary theory here.

**Fraud SOL:** §338(d) — if Walgreens misrepresented its administration procedures or training, fraud discovery rule extends SOL to 3 years from discovery of the misrepresentation.

### RedJag 2018 Jaguar XE

**Property damage SOL (§338(c)):** Vehicle delivered with wrong CARFAX (different VIN history), stripped parts, possibly stolen VIN. Property damage to the vehicle (as a good/chattel) — 3-year SOL. Purchase was 2018. On its face, §338(c) expired 2021. However:

**Fraud SOL (§338(d)):** The wrong CARFAX and stripped VIN were CONCEALED. Discovery rule extends the SOL to 3 years from when the fraud was discovered. If the full extent of the VIN/title fraud was not discovered until later (e.g., when VEH §10751 investigation was opened), the clock starts at discovery.

**Steward note:** Verify when the full extent of the VIN tampering and CARFAX fraud was discovered. Discovery rule tolling under §338(d) may preserve these claims.

### House Sale Fraud (2958 Honeysuckle)

**Fraud SOL (§338(d)):** $465K equity theft via 19 unsigned documents, forced sale. Discovery rule — 3 years from discovery of the fraud. If the unsigned documents and coerced sale were not fully understood until audit/investigation in 2024-2025, the SOL runs from that discovery. **Strong tolling argument.**

## Equitable Tolling Doctrines

Even where the statutory period appears expired, California recognizes:

1. **Fraudulent concealment:** If the defendant actively concealed the fraud, the SOL is tolled until the plaintiff discovers the concealment.
2. **Equitable estoppel:** If the defendant's conduct induced the plaintiff to delay filing, the defendant cannot assert the SOL as a defense.
3. **Discovery rule (statutory):** §338(d) builds the discovery rule into the statute for fraud claims.
