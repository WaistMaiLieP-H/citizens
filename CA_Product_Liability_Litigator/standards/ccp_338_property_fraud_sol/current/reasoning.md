## Why This Standard Exists in This Citizen

The statute of limitations is the procedural knife that can cut off a meritorious claim without any consideration of its merits. For this corpus, CCP §338 is included in the product liability citizen because:

1. **Property damage from defective products** follows a different SOL track than personal injury — 3 years vs. 2 years, a full additional year that can be dispositive.
2. **Product fraud (misrepresentation of product characteristics)** invokes §338(d)'s discovery rule, which is the primary mechanism for preserving stale-looking claims when the fraud was concealed.
3. **The active case record** has multiple overlapping fraud and property claims that require precise SOL calendaring — RedJag vehicle (2018), Honeysuckle house (2023), SIRVA (2021-2022).

---

## The Policy Behind the Fraud Discovery Rule

The ordinary rule — SOL begins when the injury occurs — is based on the idea that the plaintiff knows what happened and should act promptly. This assumption breaks down when:

1. The defendant CONCEALED the wrongdoing. The plaintiff cannot file a claim for something they don't know about.
2. The harm is LATENT — it does not manifest until years after the wrongful act.
3. The plaintiff is a NON-EXPERT dealing with a SOPHISTICATED fraudster who has hidden the evidence.

All three conditions apply to this case record. The concealment of the VIN tampering, the non-disclosure of the house's 19 unsigned documents, and the systematic suppression of evidence through the communication fraud and document manipulation are precisely the conditions that the fraud discovery rule was designed to address.

California courts have recognized that forcing a plaintiff to file before they can discover the fraud effectively immunizes sophisticated fraudsters who know how to hide their conduct. The discovery rule removes that immunity.

## The Distinction That Matters: Injury vs. Property Damage

Before *Greenman* (1963) and the strict liability revolution, product injury claims sounded in warranty or negligence. Warranty had a 4-year breach-of-warranty SOL (UCC/COM §2725). Negligence for personal injury had a different SOL. The creation of strict liability tort for product defects forced courts to apply the tort SOL framework — personal injury gets §335.1 (2 years), property damage gets §338(c) (3 years).

**The loss when lawyers miss this:** A plaintiff with both bodily injury and property damage claims from the same defective product files for bodily injury within 2 years but never asserts the property damage claim separately. They lose the 3-year window for property damage that would have survived even after the personal injury SOL expired.

In the RedJag case: the vehicle itself was damaged property. If the 2-year personal injury SOL has expired, §338(c) may still preserve property damage claims for the vehicle's condition. The fraud concealment further extends this via §338(d).
