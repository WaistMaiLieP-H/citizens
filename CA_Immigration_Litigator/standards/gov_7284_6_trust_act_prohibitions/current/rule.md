# Rule — California Government Code § 7284.6
## TRUST Act — Prohibitions on California Law Enforcement Participation in Immigration Enforcement

**Citation:** Cal. Gov. Code § 7284.6
**Authority:** California Values Act, Stats. 2017, ch. 495 (SB 54)

---

## The Rule

California law enforcement agencies SHALL NOT use agency money, facilities, property,
equipment, or personnel to investigate, interrogate, detain, detect, or arrest persons
for immigration enforcement purposes, including:

**(a)(1)(A)** Inquiring into an individual's immigration status

**(a)(1)(B)** Detaining an individual on the basis of a hold request

**(a)(1)(C)** Providing to a federal immigration agency:
- The release date or time of a person in custody, unless that information is publicly
  available, OR unless provided in response to a notification request where §7282.5
  criteria are met
- Personal information about an individual, including home address or work address,
  unless the information is publicly available

**(a)(1)(D)** Making or intentionally participating in arrests based on civil immigration
warrants

**(a)(1)(E)** Assisting immigration authorities in performing activities of their officers
under 8 USC §1357(a)(3)

**(a)(1)(F)** Performing the functions of an immigration officer under 8 USC §1357(g) or
any other law authorizing state/local officers to perform immigration enforcement

**(a)(2)** SHALL NOT place agents or law enforcement officers under the supervision of
federal agencies or employ agents for purposes of enforcing federal immigration law

**(a)(3)** SHALL NOT use immigration authorities as interpreters for law enforcement matters
relating to individuals

**(a)(4)** SHALL NOT detain an individual on the basis of a civil immigration warrant or
transfer an individual to immigration authorities UNLESS:
- A judicial warrant has been issued for the individual, OR
- A judicial finding of probable cause exists for the individual, OR
- The individual is described in §7282.5 (convicted of specified offenses / sex offender)

---

## The T/U Visa Exception — §7284.6(b)(4)

**AFFIRMATIVELY PERMITTED:** Agencies MAY make inquiries and perform activities necessary
for the purpose of making or requesting a certification under 8 USC §§1101(a)(15)(T)/(U)
for potential crime or trafficking victims.

**This is the victim cooperation pathway** — law enforcement can coordinate with immigration
authorities specifically to help crime and trafficking victims obtain T or U visa status.
It is a carve-out for victim protection, not a loophole for enforcement.

---

## §7284.6(c) — Transparency Requirement

Agencies participating in joint law enforcement task forces must:
- Report annually to the California Department of Justice on joint task force activities
- Those reports are public records subject to the California Public Records Act (GOV §7920 et seq.)

---

## Enforcement Pathway

§7284.6 does not create an express private right of action. Enforcement mechanisms include:
- **GOV §7284.8:** California Attorney General may bring action to enforce
- **CIV §52.1 (Bane Act):** If the TRUST Act violation occurred through force or threat,
  the Bane Act civil remedy applies
- **42 USC §1983:** If state officer's violation of §7284.6 also violated federal
  constitutional rights (due process, equal protection), §1983 applies
- **Writ of mandate (CCP §1085):** Compel agency compliance where duty is clear and ministerial

## Case Application

**APD / June 2023 incident:** If any Antioch Police Department officer communicated
Michael's personal information (address, release date) to federal immigration authorities
in connection with the June 16, 2023 incident — each such communication constitutes a
separate §7284.6(a)(1)(C) violation. The §7284.6(c) reporting requirement means any
joint task force activity involving APD and federal immigration authorities is a public
record subject to CPRA request.

**Butsaya dissolution proceedings:** If immigration enforcement threats were used against
Butsaya as a control mechanism — and California officers facilitated those threats — each
facilitation act is a §7284.6 violation. The T/U visa exception (§7284.6(b)(4)) is the
affirmative pathway: if Butsaya was a crime victim (not the DVRO perpetrator as falsely
claimed), U visa certification is available through law enforcement cooperation.
