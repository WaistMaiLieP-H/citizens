# Reasoning — California Government Code § 7284.6
## TRUST Act — Why This Standard Exists

## The Core Problem

Federal civil immigration enforcement is a civil regulatory scheme. A person who is in the
United States without authorization has violated a civil regulatory statute — not a criminal
law. Until 2017, nothing formally prevented California law enforcement agencies from acting
as an extension of federal civil immigration enforcement: honoring detainers, sharing
addresses, reporting release dates, arresting on civil warrants.

The practical effect: any interaction with California law enforcement — reporting a crime,
appearing as a witness, a traffic stop, calling 911 — could result in a person being
transferred to federal immigration custody. The chilling effect was documented and severe:
immigrants stopped reporting crimes, stopped appearing as witnesses, stopped calling for
emergency services. California communities became less safe as a direct consequence.

## What SB 54 Did

The California Values Act (SB 54, 2017) codified at GOV §§7282-7284.10 made a categorical
choice: California's resources — officers, facilities, equipment, money — will not be used
to enforce a federal civil regulatory scheme. This is a state sovereignty decision, not a
nullification of federal law. Federal immigration authorities remain free to enforce federal
law with federal resources. California simply will not provide the infrastructure.

The T/U visa exception (§7284.6(b)(4)) is the positive statement of the same principle:
California will cooperate with federal immigration authorities when that cooperation protects
crime and trafficking victims. The line is victim protection vs. enforcement.

## Why This Standard Matters for Active Cases

### The APD Forensic Audit Finding

The Antioch Police Department forensic audit documented 10 violations in connection with
the June 16, 2023 incident. If any of those violations involved communication of personal
information to federal immigration authorities — or if the June 2023 incident was
orchestrated to create a federal immigration referral — §7284.6(a)(1)(C) and (a)(1)(D)
are directly implicated.

The §7284.6(c) transparency provision gives the pathway: the joint task force reporting
requirement means any cooperation between APD and federal immigration authorities in
connection with the June 2023 incident is a public record. A CPRA request to the Antioch
PD for all joint task force reports involving federal immigration authorities covering the
period surrounding June 16, 2023 would surface this.

### Butsaya as Victim, Not Perpetrator

The source prep contains a critical inversion: the fabricated DVRO was presented as though
Butsaya was the victim and Michael was the abuser. If the actual facts show Butsaya was
used as a vehicle for the control scheme — her Thai national status exploited to isolate
her, a fabricated DVRO obtained in her name to serve other purposes, her immigration status
used as leverage — then Butsaya may herself be a T or U visa eligible victim.

§7284.6(b)(4) makes California law enforcement cooperation affirmatively permitted for T/U
visa certification. If Butsaya was a crime victim in the broader scheme, this is the
pathway that the TRUST Act specifically preserved.

## The Interpreter Rule Connection

§7284.6(a)(3) specifically prohibits using immigration authorities as interpreters for law
enforcement matters. This is not a casual prohibition — it reflects Congress's understanding
that the immigration authority plays a dual role: an interpreter who is also a federal
enforcement officer has a conflict of interest in every proceeding where the person speaking
may be subject to removal. An immigration officer serving as a court interpreter is not
§752-compliant — they cannot be sworn as a neutral interpreter when they have enforcement
authority over the witness.

This connection means: any proceeding in which a federal immigration officer served as the
interpreter for a California law enforcement matter involving Butsaya or any other party
was conducted in violation of both §7284.6(a)(3) and EVID §752.
