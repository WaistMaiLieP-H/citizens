# Rule — California Evidence Code § 752
## Interpreter for Witnesses

**Citation:** Cal. Evid. Code § 752
**Authority:** California Evidence Code

---

## The Rule

**§752(a):** When a witness is incapable of understanding the English language or of
expressing himself or herself in the English language so as to be understood directly
by counsel, court, and jury, an interpreter whom the witness can understand and who can
understand the witness shall be sworn to interpret for the witness.

**§752(b) — Compensation:**

**(1) Criminal actions and juvenile court proceedings:** The compensation of the
interpreter is a charge against the court, the county, or the state, as the case may be.

**(2) Civil actions:** Compensation of an interpreter is apportioned among the parties
as the court may determine; may be taxed as costs in the proceeding.

---

## Four Critical Rules

### Rule 1 — The Interpreter Must Be Sworn

The interpreter does not merely assist — they are **sworn**. An unsworn translation of
witness testimony is not §752-compliant. It is not evidence. A proceeding in which an
unsworn person translated for a witness was not conducted in accordance with the Evidence
Code.

**Attack theory:** If a Thai-speaking witness appeared in Butsaya dissolution proceedings
and the translation was provided by someone who was not sworn as an interpreter, the
witness's testimony is not admissible — it was not given under §752. Any findings based
on that testimony are built on inadmissible evidence.

### Rule 2 — Mutual Intelligibility Required

The interpreter must be one:
- Whom the witness can understand, AND
- Who can understand the witness

A court-appointed interpreter who does not speak the specific dialect, or who the witness
cannot comprehend, does not satisfy §752(a). This is a bilateral test — not just that
the interpreter speaks the language, but that actual mutual comprehension exists.

### Rule 3 — Criminal vs. Civil Cost Allocation

In criminal proceedings: interpreter cost is a **charge against the court** — the
defendant/witness does not pay. This is a due process protection.

In civil proceedings: cost is apportioned among parties and may be taxed as costs. A
pro se party may end up bearing a portion of interpreter cost — this is an access-to-
justice issue in Butsaya dissolution proceedings where Butsaya may have had limited
financial resources.

### Rule 4 — Immigration Officer Cannot Serve as §752 Interpreter

GOV §7284.6(a)(3) prohibits California law enforcement agencies from using immigration
authorities as interpreters for law enforcement matters. A federal immigration officer
has enforcement authority over the person they are translating for. This creates an
inherent conflict of interest — the interpreter can simultaneously gather information
for removal proceedings while purportedly helping the witness communicate.

An immigration officer serving as interpreter in a California proceeding violates:
- GOV §7284.6(a)(3) — TRUST Act prohibition on using immigration authorities as interpreters
- The neutrality requirement implicit in EVID §752(a) — the sworn interpreter must be
  one the witness can trust to translate accurately without adverse interest

## Application to Active Cases

### Butsaya Dissolution Proceedings
- Were Thai-speaking parties (Butsaya or others) provided sworn §752-compliant interpreters
  in all proceedings — pre-trial hearings, DVRO hearing, dissolution trial?
- If the DVRO hearing was conducted with an unsworn translator or no translator, Butsaya's
  participation in those proceedings was not §752-compliant — she could not meaningfully
  exercise her rights in proceedings she could not understand
- If a federal immigration officer served as interpreter at any point → dual violation:
  §7284.6(a)(3) + §752 neutrality requirement

### Thai Translation Authentication
- Documents translated from Thai must be accompanied by a sworn certified translator's
  declaration — the translator's oath is the §752 equivalent for documentary evidence
- ATA (American Translators Association) certification or Judicial Council certified court
  interpreter qualification is the relevant credential for Thai
- An unsworn, uncertified "translation" of a Thai document is not admissible evidence
  of what the Thai document says
