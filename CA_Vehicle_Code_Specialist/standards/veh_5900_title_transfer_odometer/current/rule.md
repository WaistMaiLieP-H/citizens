# Rule — VEH § 5900 Title Transfer and Odometer Disclosure

**Standard ID:** veh_5900_title_transfer_odometer
**Authority:** Cal. Veh. Code § 5900

## Core Rule

Upon sale or transfer of any registered vehicle:

1. **5-Day DMV Notification (§5900(a)):** The owner must notify DMV within 5 calendar days of:
   - Date of sale/transfer
   - Name and address of seller and buyer
   - Vehicle description (as required on DMV form)

2. **Odometer Disclosure (§5900(b)):** The owner must disclose to DMV the actual odometer reading at time of transfer. If the seller knows the displayed mileage is incorrect, the seller must provide the true mileage (if known).

3. **Fraud Threshold (§5900(b)):** Providing false or inaccurate mileage is a violation ONLY IF done with INTENT TO DEFRAUD.

## Federal Cross-Reference

§5900(b) explicitly incorporates 49 USC §32705 (Motor Vehicle Information and Cost Savings Act — federal odometer disclosure). Federal odometer fraud is a separate and concurrent cause of action:
- 49 USC §32703: Prohibits odometer tampering and misrepresentation
- 49 USC §32710: Civil penalty — $10,000 actual damages OR three times actual damages, whichever is greater, plus attorney fees
- Federal civil cause of action is PRIVATE (no need to go through DMV)

## Dealer's Obligation Under §5900

For dealer transactions, the dealer typically completes the DMV paperwork on behalf of the seller (prior owner / trade-in) and the buyer. The dealer's participation in a title transfer that contains false mileage information — even if the dealer did not personally alter the odometer — can constitute:
- Aiding/abetting odometer fraud (49 USC §32703)
- VEH §11713(l) dealer prohibited act (failure to comply with title transfer requirements)
- Criminal fraud if done with intent

## Application — RedJag

If the Jaguar XE delivered was a different vehicle than advertised (stolen/stripped with VIN issues):
- The title transfer documentation would not accurately reflect the vehicle delivered
- The odometer disclosure would be for the wrong vehicle
- The 5-day DMV notification obligation was either not met or was met with fraudulent documentation
- False mileage on a stolen/VIN-altered vehicle = §5900 violation with intent (the fraud is inherent in the substitution scheme)

49 USC §32710 private action for odometer fraud: $10,000 or 3x actual damages. In a $19,985 vehicle transaction, this could yield up to $59,955 plus attorney fees.
