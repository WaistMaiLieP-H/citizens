# Reasoning — Why Rees-Levering Exists

Rees-Levering was enacted in 1961 (Stats. 1961, Ch. 1626, AB 600 — Assemblymembers Thomas Rees and Charles Levering) to end three specific practices that had become endemic in California vehicle financing:

1. **Undisclosed finance charges** — dealers bundled finance costs into the "price" so the buyer never saw the effective APR.
2. **Mandatory add-ons** — credit life insurance, GAP, service contracts, and "paint protection" were packaged into the contract as if they were part of the purchase, not separately-priced optional products.
3. **"Yo-yo" financing (spot delivery)** — dealers delivered the vehicle before the financing was actually approved, then weeks later told the buyer the financing "fell through" and demanded either return of the vehicle (keeping the down payment and trade-in) or signing a new contract at a higher rate.

**How § 2982 fixes items 1 and 2:**
The statute mandates that every cost — every fee, every add-on, every insurance product, and the finance charge itself — appear as a separate itemized line on a single document signed by the buyer. Bundling is prohibited. Each add-on must be marked as **optional**, with a separate signature line, so the buyer knows they are choosing to buy it and are not required to.

**How § 2982.5 fixes item 3:**
If the dealer delivered the vehicle on a contract "contingent on financing" and the financing did not materialize, the statute forces the dealer to unwind the deal on the dealer's dime — return of trade-in, return of down payment, rescission of the contract. The dealer cannot threaten repossession, damage to credit, or continued use to force the buyer onto new terms.

**Application to RedJag 2018 Jaguar XE (Case #1):**

The RedJag incident is a near-textbook § 2982.5 violation:
- **Spot delivery:** Vehicle delivered before financing was finalized.
- **Financing failure:** Third-party financing did not consummate on the original terms.
- **Dealer refused to unwind:** Instead of returning trade-in and down payment and rescinding, the dealer either (a) demanded new terms, (b) retained the $10K cash down payment, or (c) asserted $19,985 in debt against the buyer.

**§ 2983 remedy:** Full rescission, return of all payments, return of trade-in value, discharge from the $19,985 debt. **§ 2984.4:** civil penalty if willful.
