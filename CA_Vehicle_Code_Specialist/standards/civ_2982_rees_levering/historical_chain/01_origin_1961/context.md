# Historical Loss — Rees-Levering 1961 Origin

**Enacting act:** Stats. 1961, Ch. 1626 (AB 600, Rees/Levering). Effective September 15, 1961.

**The specific loss this statute was written to prevent:**

California's 1950s vehicle-financing market was dominated by dealer-captive finance companies and high-rate third-party lenders. The three practices the statute attacked — undisclosed finance charges, bundled mandatory add-ons, and yo-yo financing (spot delivery) — were widespread and had generated multiple Attorney General investigations and Senate Fact-Finding Committee hearings in 1959–1961.

**Documented losses in the 1961 legislative record:**
- Consumers signing contracts showing only a "total payment" amount with no disclosed APR, effective rates exceeding 30% on what appeared to be 6% "add-on" rates;
- Credit life, disability, and property insurance packaged as mandatory, adding 8–15% to total financed amount with no consumer choice;
- Spot-delivered vehicles "unwound" weeks later with dealer retention of trade-in and down payment, forcing either loss or new contract at higher rate;
- No consolidated document — terms spread across sales slip, finance contract, and insurance endorsement, each signed separately, none showing the full picture.

**The structural fix:**
Rees-Levering imposed the **single-document rule**: every material term on one page, signed once, with each add-on separately itemized and separately signed. This mirrors (and predates by 7 years) the federal Truth in Lending Act (1968). California's statute is broader in one key respect: it provides **rescission** as a first-line remedy (not just damages) and creates a **private right of action** with **two-way fee-shifting**.

**Relation to federal Truth in Lending Act:**
When TILA was enacted in 1968 (15 USC §§ 1601 et seq., Reg Z at 12 CFR 1026), Rees-Levering was amended to conform to TILA disclosure formats while preserving California's more protective remedies. A dealer who violates TILA may face federal penalties; a dealer who violates Rees-Levering faces rescission under state law regardless of TILA compliance.
