# Historical Chain — § 11713 Dealer Prohibited Acts

## Origins

Section 11713's enumerated prohibited acts developed alongside the dealer licensing system from the mid-20th century. As California's vehicle market grew, specific fraud patterns that had recurred across transactions were enumerated as per se prohibited. The list has been expanded by the Legislature each time a new fraud pattern emerged:

- The "no downpayment" prohibition (§11713(k)) addressed a specific bait-and-switch pattern in which dealers advertised "no down" to attract buyers, then required financing of the down payment as a separate loan
- The vehicle history accuracy requirement (§11713(u)) was added as CARFAX-style reporting became standard and substitution fraud became documented
- The subscription service prohibition (§11713(v)(1)) is a recent (2022) addition responding to manufacturer attempts to charge recurring fees for features already physically installed in the vehicle (heated seats, etc.)

Each addition to the prohibited acts list represents a documented fraud pattern that the Legislature determined required specific enumeration to enable effective enforcement.

## Current State

§11713's list of prohibited acts is enforced primarily through:
1. DMV Occupational Licensing — consumer complaints; license suspension/revocation proceedings
2. New Motor Vehicle Board — franchise dealer disputes
3. Private civil action — UCL §17200 "unlawful" prong; CLRA
4. DA Consumer Protection Unit — criminal referral for egregious violations
