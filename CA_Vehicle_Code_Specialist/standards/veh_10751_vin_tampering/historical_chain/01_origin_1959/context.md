# Historical Chain — § 10751 VIN Tampering

## Origins in Vehicle Theft Suppression

Vehicle identification number requirements developed alongside the mass production of automobiles in the early 20th century. Manufacturers affixed serial numbers to enable tracking of vehicles for warranty, recall, and insurance purposes. States began requiring VIN disclosure in registration systems as a condition of operating vehicles on public roads.

As vehicle theft became a significant economic crime in the mid-20th century, California and other states enacted criminal prohibitions on VIN tampering as part of a comprehensive vehicle theft suppression framework. The California Vehicle Code's VIN provisions (§§10750-10803) reflect accumulated legislative response to documented theft and fraud patterns.

The extension of §10751 to component parts reflects the Legislature's recognition of the chop shop model — which became increasingly sophisticated in the 1970s–1990s as vehicle thefts reached peak rates in California. Chop shops that stripped vehicles for parts were often more profitable than whole-vehicle resale; §10751's component part coverage directly targets this economic model.

## Current Criminal Framework

§10751's civil seizure and hearing procedure interacts with a broader criminal framework:
- Pen. Code §10801: Operating a chop shop — felony; 2-6 years
- Pen. Code §10802: Altering VIN with intent to defraud — felony; 1-3 years
- Pen. Code §496: Receiving stolen property — felony/misdemeanor depending on value

The §10751 civil hearing determines vehicle disposition; the criminal track determines personal liability. Both run concurrently and are not mutually exclusive.
