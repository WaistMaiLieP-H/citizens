# Reasoning — Why § 10751 Exists

## Stolen Vehicle Economics

Vehicle theft in California is primarily driven by two markets: (1) whole stolen vehicles exported or sold to unwitting buyers, and (2) "chop shops" that strip vehicles for parts — high-value components (engines, transmissions, airbag modules, catalytic converters) are sold separately, and the "carcass" is either abandoned or further disguised.

VIN tampering is the tool that enables both markets. A stolen vehicle with its VIN changed can be titled and registered as if legitimate — the buyer and DMV have no way to know the vehicle is stolen without a VIN trace. Stripped vehicles assembled from components of multiple stolen cars (using one "clean" VIN plate) can be sold as a single vehicle.

§10751 directly attacks both schemes by making possession of a vehicle with a tampered VIN a criminal offense — regardless of how the possessor obtained it. The "knowingly" requirement protects innocent buyers who purchase without knowledge of the tamper; it does not protect dealers who know or should know the vehicle's VIN is irregular.

## The "Component Part" Coverage

Extending §10751 to component parts (not just the primary vehicle VIN) is the key anti-chop-shop provision. A chop shop assembled vehicle can be identified by tracing the VINs stamped on individual components (engine block serial number, airbag module, etc.). If those component VINs are removed or altered, §10751 applies to each component independently.

This means a sophisticated vehicle fraud scheme involving stolen components cannot avoid §10751 simply by replacing the primary VIN plate — every component part with a tampered identification number is a separate violation.

## Good-Faith Buyer Protection

§10751(e)(1) provides that a good-faith buyer who presents satisfactory evidence of ownership (title, purchase contract, payment records) and demonstrates the VIN was altered before the purchase can recover the vehicle or receive a DMV-assigned replacement number. The statute does not forfeit the innocent buyer's vehicle to the state — it protects the buyer from losing both the vehicle AND the purchase price.
