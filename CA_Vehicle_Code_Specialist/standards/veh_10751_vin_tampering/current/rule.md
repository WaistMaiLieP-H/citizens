# Rule — VEH § 10751 VIN Tampering

**Standard ID:** veh_10751_vin_tampering
**Authority:** Cal. Veh. Code § 10751

## Core Rule

No person may KNOWINGLY:
- Buy
- Sell
- Offer for sale
- Receive
- Possess

...any vehicle OR component part from which the manufacturer's serial or identification number (VIN, or any number used for registration) has been removed, defaced, altered, or destroyed — unless a DMV-assigned replacement number is attached.

**"Knowingly" requirement:** The statutory prohibition requires knowledge that the VIN has been tampered with. This is a criminal statute — prosecuted as a felony in California (Pen. Code §§496/10801-10803).

## Critical Coverage: Component Parts

§10751(a) is not limited to the vehicle's primary VIN plate. It covers:
- The primary VIN (dashboard, door jamb)
- Any serial or identification number affixed by the manufacturer to any component part
- Numbers used for "registration purposes" — broadly defined

This means: a stolen vehicle that has had its primary VIN replaced but retains original VINs on component parts (engine block, transmission, airbag control module) may still trigger §10751 on those components. Conversely, a vehicle assembled from stolen parts — each with VINs removed — violates §10751 for each component part.

## Seizure and Hearing Procedure (§10751(b)-(e))

When a peace officer takes custody of a §10751 vehicle:

| Step | Timeline | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Seizure notice | Within 5 days (excluding weekends/holidays) | Notice to person seized from and all DMV claimants of hearing date/time/place |
| Court hearing | Within 90 days of seizure | Superior court; limited civil case; no jury |
| Burden of proof | At hearing | Seizing agency must establish VIN was removed/altered AND no satisfactory ownership evidence |

**If ownership is established:** Vehicle returned to rightful owner; DMV assigns new identification number.
**If VIN altered and no ownership evidence:** Vehicle destroyed, sold, or disposed by court order.

## Application — RedJag Stolen/Stripped Vehicle

If the 2018 Jaguar XE delivered to Michael was:
1. A vehicle with a VIN swap (different vehicle's VIN applied) — direct §10751(a) violation (seller sold a vehicle with altered VIN)
2. Assembled from stripped/stolen components with removed VINs — §10751(a) violation for each component part
3. A "parts car" presented as a whole vehicle — each component part with removed ID number violates §10751

The dealer who sold such a vehicle "knowingly" (the substitution is not accidental) committed §10751 + criminal Pen. Code §496 (receipt of stolen property) concurrently.

**Buyer's remedy:** Michael, as a good-faith purchaser, would be entitled to return of a vehicle (or a new DMV identification number assigned) under §10751(e)(1) if satisfactory evidence of ownership is presented. The burden at the §10751 hearing is on the seizing agency — Michael's title documentation and purchase contract are the ownership evidence.

**Recovery theory:** If the vehicle delivered was not the vehicle contracted for (different vehicle with VIN swap), the entire contract is voidable for failure of consideration, in addition to vehicle code and criminal fraud claims.
