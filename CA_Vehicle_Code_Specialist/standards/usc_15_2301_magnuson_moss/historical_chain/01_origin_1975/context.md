# Historical Loss — Magnuson-Moss 1975 Origin

**Enacting act:** Pub. L. 93-637, 88 Stat. 2183 (January 4, 1975). Sponsored by Senator Warren Magnuson (D-WA) and Rep. John Moss (D-CA). Codified at 15 U.S.C. §§ 2301–2312.

**The specific federal loss this statute was written to prevent:**

In the decade preceding 1975, the Federal Trade Commission conducted extensive hearings and studies documenting systematic warranty abuse:

1. **"Warranties" that disclaimed more than they promised.** FTC study of 200 consumer-product warranties (1969) found that ~60% used language that, while appearing to grant warranty protection, actually disclaimed more implied-warranty protection than the express warranty granted. Net effect: consumer worse off for receiving the warranty.
2. **Unintelligible language.** 1972 FTC consumer-comprehension study: 78% of consumers could not correctly identify what their warranty covered after reading it.
3. **No unified federal remedy.** State UCC regimes varied; some required privity; attorney's fees unavailable; diversity jurisdiction blocked small claims.
4. **Automobile-specific abuse.** Senate Commerce Committee Report No. 93-151 (1973) identified motor vehicles as the single largest category of consumer-warranty complaints to the FTC, with "limited" warranties that excluded the very defects most likely to occur.

**The structural fix:**
Magnuson-Moss took three structural steps no state had fully taken:

1. **Federal disclosure standard** (§ 2302 + Reg Part 701) — warranty language must be simple and readable, must be pre-sale available (§ 2302(b)(1)(A) + 16 CFR § 702.3).
2. **Implied-warranty lock** (§ 2308) — the UCC default (that implied warranties may be disclaimed with "AS IS" or equivalent) is reversed whenever any written warranty is given.
3. **Federal private right of action with fees** (§ 2310(d)) — creating an economic ability to enforce warranties that had not previously existed.

**California context — relationship to Song-Beverly (1970):**
California had already enacted Song-Beverly five years before Magnuson-Moss. Magnuson-Moss expressly did not preempt (§ 2311). Effect: California consumers get both statutes' protections. The RedJag pleading should cite both — Magnuson-Moss § 2308 as the implied-warranty lock, Song-Beverly § 1793.2 as the repair-or-replace anchor with 2x civil penalty.
