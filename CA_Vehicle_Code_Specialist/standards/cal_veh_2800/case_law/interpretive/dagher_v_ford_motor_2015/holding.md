# Dagher v. Ford Motor Co., 238 Cal. App. 4th 905 (2015)

## The Case

Dagher purchased a vehicle from a Ford dealership. After delivery, the dealership told him the financing had fallen through and demanded he return the vehicle or sign a new contract at worse terms. This is the "yo-yo" or "spot delivery" scheme. Dagher sued under the Automobile Sales Finance Act (Civ. Code §2982) and the Consumers Legal Remedies Act.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **A "spot delivery" — where the dealer delivers the vehicle before financing is finalized — creates binding obligations on the dealer under Civ. Code §2982.** The dealer cannot unilaterally rescind the sale after delivery merely because the financing terms it expected did not materialize.

2. **If financing falls through after a spot delivery, the dealer must offer to unwind the transaction completely** — return the buyer's down payment and trade-in, take back the vehicle. The dealer cannot restructure the deal on less favorable terms without the buyer's knowing and voluntary consent.

3. **A dealer who pressures the buyer to accept worse terms after a spot delivery commits an unfair business practice** and may be liable for damages, restitution, and injunctive relief under the CLRA and Business and Professions Code §17200.

## The Rule

**Yo-yo financing — calling the buyer back after a spot delivery to demand worse terms — violates the Automobile Sales Finance Act. The dealer must either honor the original deal or fully unwind the transaction. Pressuring the buyer into a worse deal is an unfair business practice.**

## Why This Case Matters

The Red Jag transaction follows this pattern exactly: delivery, callback claiming financing failure, pressure to accept different terms, and ultimately the loss of both the vehicle and the down payment. Under Dagher, the dealership was obligated to either honor the original financing or return the steward's $10,000 cash and take back the car. If neither happened, the dealership violated the Automobile Sales Finance Act — and the $19,985 debt collection may be based on an unenforceable transaction.
