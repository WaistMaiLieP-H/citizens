# Historical Evolution — 1982 Tanner Amendment

**Enacting act:** Stats. 1982, Ch. 388 (AB 1787, Tanner). Effective January 1, 1983.

**What the amendment added:**
- § 1793.22(b) — the objective presumption (2 attempts for safety defects, 4 attempts for other defects, 30 cumulative out-of-service days);
- § 1793.22(e) — definitions of "new motor vehicle" and "nonconformity" with the "substantially impairs" qualifier;
- § 1793.2(d)(2)(C) — the mileage-offset formula (use prior to first repair attempt / 120,000 × price).

**Why the 1970 statute needed the 1982 amendment:**
The original 1970 § 1793.2 required "a reasonable number of attempts" but left the number to the jury. Manufacturers litigated every case on the question of whether the attempts were "reasonable" — turning a consumer-protection statute into a litigation lottery. The 1982 amendment converted "reasonable" from a jury question to a statutory presumption with counted triggers, restoring the protective function.
