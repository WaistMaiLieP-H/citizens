# Reasoning — Why § 1793.2 / Song-Beverly Exists

Song-Beverly (enacted 1970, expanded with the lemon-law presumption in 1982 by AB 1787, "Tanner Consumer Protection Act") was the Legislature's response to a pre-1970 regime in which vehicle warranties were functionally unenforceable by ordinary consumers: manufacturers could disclaim implied warranties, limit remedies to repair-or-replace at their option, and drag buyers through unlimited repair attempts while the vehicle depreciated and the consumer absorbed the loss.

**The structural problem Song-Beverly fixes:**
1. **Asymmetry of information** — the manufacturer knows whether a defect is systemic; the buyer does not.
2. **Asymmetry of resources** — the manufacturer has counsel and engineers; the buyer has a disabled vehicle and a payment book.
3. **Incentive inversion** — without a time/attempt ceiling, the manufacturer's incentive is to delay, not to fix.

Song-Beverly flips all three:
- § 1792 implies a warranty of merchantability that cannot be disclaimed in a retail sale of new consumer goods.
- § 1793.22(b) establishes an objective presumption — after N attempts or 30 days out of service, the burden shifts.
- § 1794(c) creates a 2x civil penalty for **willful** noncompliance, making delay more expensive than replacement/restitution.
- § 1794(d) awards attorney's fees to prevailing buyers only (one-way fee-shifting), making private enforcement economically possible.

**Applicability to RedJag 2018 Jaguar XE (Case #1):**
The RedJag delivery involved a vehicle that was wrong-CARFAX, stripped/stolen, and delivered with defects. Even stripped of the dealer-fraud and title-branding theories (which live in VEH §§ 11713/5900 and CIV §§ 1709/1750), Song-Beverly § 1793.2 **independently** supports replacement or restitution if the vehicle as-delivered failed to conform to the manufacturer's express warranty and the dealer or manufacturer failed to cure within a reasonable number of attempts. The § 1794(c) 2x civil penalty attaches if the failure to cure was willful — and willfulness is presumed where the defect was known at delivery (e.g., a vehicle with a falsified CARFAX, where the manufacturer/dealer knew the true history but represented otherwise).

**Why this belongs in CA_Vehicle_Code_Specialist (not Consumer_Protection alone):**
Song-Beverly is the substantive vehicle-warranty statute. While CA_Consumer_Protection_Litigator owns Rees-Levering financing violations and CLRA generally, **Song-Beverly is the single most important vehicle-specific consumer-warranty statute in California** and it interlocks with VEH §§ 5900/11713 (title/dealer fraud) and BPC § 9884.9 (repair estimates). A Vehicle Code specialist without Song-Beverly is a gap.
