# Historical Chain — § 11700 Origin

## Pre-licensing Era

Vehicle dealer licensing in California developed through the first half of the 20th century alongside the growth of the automobile industry. Early vehicle sales were largely unregulated — dealers were simply sellers. As automobiles became essential consumer goods and fraud in vehicle transactions became widespread, the Legislature developed the mandatory licensing system codified in the Vehicle Code.

The Vehicle Code itself was first enacted in 1959 (replacing the Road Vehicle Code). Section 11700's dealer licensing requirement reflects accumulated regulatory development through the mid-20th century, when:
- The used car market had become a significant source of consumer fraud
- Stolen vehicle trafficking required a registration/title system to detect
- Consumer protection required a bonded, registered seller as counterparty

## Current Framework

VEH §§11700–11740 constitute the dealer licensing article. DMV administers dealer licensing; the New Motor Vehicle Board (NMVB) handles franchise dealer disputes; BAR handles automotive repair. The three regulatory bodies have distinct but overlapping jurisdiction over vehicle transactions.

The bond requirement (§11710) and the dealer complaint mechanism (consumer complaints to DMV Occupational Licensing) are the administrative enforcement tools that flow from §11700's licensing requirement.
