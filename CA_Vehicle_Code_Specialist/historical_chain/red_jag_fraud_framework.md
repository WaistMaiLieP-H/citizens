## The Red Jag: Anatomy of Yo-Yo Financing Fraud

---

### I. What Happened

The steward purchased a 2018 Jaguar XE from a dealership. He put down $10,000 in cash. The dealership approved financing and delivered the vehicle. Then the dealership called him back — the financing "fell through." This is the yo-yo.

The CARFAX provided at the time of sale did not match the actual vehicle history. The vehicle was subsequently stripped or stolen. The steward lost his $10,000 cash down payment and received a $19,985 debt collection notice for a vehicle he no longer possesses.

### II. The Legal Framework

**Yo-Yo Financing (Civ. Code §2982):** California's Automobile Sales Finance Act regulates vehicle installment sales. A "spot delivery" — where the dealer delivers the vehicle before financing is finalized — creates specific obligations. If financing falls through, the dealer must return the buyer's down payment and trade-in. The dealer cannot restructure the deal on worse terms without the buyer's informed, voluntary consent.

**CARFAX Disclosure:** A vehicle history report that does not match the actual vehicle is fraud in the inducement. If the dealer provided a CARFAX for a different vehicle — or for a vehicle with a different history — the buyer was deceived about a material fact.

**Vehicle Code §2800 et seq.:** Registration requirements. A vehicle must be properly registered and titled. If the title was defective, the sale was defective.

**Debt Collection:** A debt collection for $19,985 on a vehicle the steward no longer possesses raises questions about the validity of the underlying obligation. If the sale was fraudulent, the debt may be unenforceable. If the vehicle was stolen while the dealership retained an interest, the dealership — not the buyer — bears the loss.

### III. The Pattern

The Red Jag transaction fits the broader pattern documented in the steward's case files: a financial transaction where the steward enters in good faith, provides value (cash, credit, labor), and exits with less than he started — while the counterparty or a third party captures the difference. The yo-yo financing scheme is a known fraud pattern that California has specifically legislated against.
