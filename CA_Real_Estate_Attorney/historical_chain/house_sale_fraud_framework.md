### II. The Legal Framework

California real property transactions are governed by multiple overlapping frameworks:

**Recording Acts (Civ. Code §1213):** Recorded instruments have priority. If a deed, lien, or encumbrance was recorded against the property, the recording determines priority — but the instrument must be authentic and properly executed.

**Transfer Disclosure (Civ. Code §1102):** Sellers must disclose material facts about the property. The transfer disclosure statement must be signed. Unsigned disclosures are defective.

**Community Property (Fam. Code §1100):** If the house was community property, both spouses must consent to the sale. A sale by one spouse without the other's consent is voidable. If a dissolution (Christina) was never finalized with a property disposition order, the community property interest may still exist.

**Escrow (Financial Code §17000):** Escrow agents have fiduciary duties. They must account for all funds. Disbursements must be authorized by all parties.

**Broker Duties (BPC §10176):** Real estate agents have duties of honest dealing. Failure to disclose material facts, participating in a fraudulent transaction, or failing to account for funds are grounds for license revocation and civil liability.

### III. The Investigation Questions

1. **The $356K gap:** Closing statement shows $605K sale. After mortgage payoff and closing costs, approximately $465K should have been available. Where did the money go between the closing and the steward's receipt of $109K?

2. **The 19 unsigned documents:** Which documents are unsigned? Disclosures? Authorizations? Deeds? Each unsigned document is an authentication defect (Records Authentication Specialist) and potentially a fraud indicator.

3. **Christina's interest:** Was the dissolution finalized with a property disposition? If not, did Christina have a community property interest that was disposed of without court authorization?

4. **The crypto and surgery:** $72K in crypto transactions and $25K in spine surgery payments appear in the financial trail but not from house proceeds. Whose money paid for these? If the steward's funds were used without authorization — or if a conservator authorized them — the accounting trail matters.

5. **Conservatorship connection:** If a conservator had authority over the steward's estate, the conservator could have authorized the sale, directed the proceeds, and signed documents on the steward's behalf. The §2620 accounting should reflect these transactions. If it doesn't, either there's no conservatorship or the conservator failed to account.
