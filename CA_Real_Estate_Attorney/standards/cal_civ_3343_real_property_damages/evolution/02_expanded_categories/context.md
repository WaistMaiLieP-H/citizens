# Amendment Context — § 3343 Expansion of Recoverable Categories

## What the amendment addressed

The original 1872 out-of-pocket measure ("additional damage arising from the particular transaction") had produced a circuit of cases debating what "additional damage" meant. Courts disagreed on whether:
- Reliance expenditures (costs incurred preparing for the transaction) were recoverable
- Lost income from property you sold in reliance on fraud was recoverable
- Lost profits from property you bought that didn't perform as represented were recoverable

The legislative expansion of § 3343 resolved these debates by codifying four specific categories. Rather than leaving the boundaries of "additional damage" to case-by-case development, the Legislature defined them.

## The four categories and what they cover

**Category (a)(1) — Reliance expenditures:**
Amounts you spent because you believed the fraud. If you hired inspectors, paid due-diligence costs, moved out of your prior home, or made deposits — all recoverable if made in reasonable reliance on the fraudulent representations.

**Category (a)(2) — Loss of use and enjoyment:**
If the fraud deprived you of use of property you owned (or should have owned), you can recover for that deprivation. In the Honeysuckle context: the loss of use of the equity proceeds from July 2023 forward.

**Category (a)(3) — Lost profits from property you were induced to sell:**
If you were fraudulently induced to SELL property and would have earned profits from it had you kept it, you can recover those reasonably expected profits. Note: this is not benefit-of-bargain; it is lost income from your actual retained use — a distinction courts draw carefully.

**Category (a)(4) — Lost profits from property you were induced to buy:**
If you were fraudulently induced to BUY property that didn't perform as represented, you can recover lost profits from the use you planned — but only with strict proof of: (i) profit-purpose acquisition, (ii) reasonable reliance, and (iii) proximate causation.

## The § 3343(b) bars — what is still prohibited

§ 3343(b) makes explicit what was already implicit:
1. **No benefit-of-bargain**: Cannot recover "represented value minus actual value." That would reward the optimistic expectation, not restore the loss.
2. **Equitable remedies preserved**: § 3343 only defines the legal damages measure. Rescission, constructive trust, accounting, and other equitable remedies are available independently.

## Why the expansion matters for Honeysuckle

§ 3343(a)(2) (loss of use) is directly applicable: the $465K in equity has been deprived from the community since July 2023. A money judgment for interest/use value from the date of deprivation through the date of award is a cognizable § 3343 claim. Combined with the § 1101(g) 50% remedy (or § 1101(h) 100% remedy), the full scope of the claim is:
- Out-of-pocket principal loss: $465K (or the measure thereof)
- Loss of use under § 3343(a)(2): ~$465K × applicable rate × time elapsed
- Reliance expenditures under § 3343(a)(1): documented costs incurred in reliance on the transaction's representations
- Attorney's fees under § 1101(g)
