STOUT v. TURNEY
22 Cal.3d 718 (1978)
California Supreme Court
Decided: November 1978

---

FACTS:

Stout purchased property from Turney in reliance on misrepresentations about the property's condition and characteristics. After closing, Stout discovered the property was worth less than represented and brought a fraud action. The trial court awarded damages using the benefit-of-the-bargain measure (difference between represented value and actual value). On appeal, the Supreme Court had to determine the correct damages measure under § 3343.

---

HOLDING:

THE OUT-OF-POCKET RULE IS MANDATORY:

1. **California applies the out-of-pocket measure for real property fraud damages.** Under § 3343, the plaintiff recovers the difference between the actual value of what the plaintiff parted with and the actual value of what the plaintiff received. The court expressly rejected the benefit-of-the-bargain measure.

2. **The out-of-pocket measure is mandatory, not optional.** "Section 3343(b)(1) expressly prohibits recovery measured by the difference between the value of property as represented and the actual value thereof." A trial court cannot award benefit-of-the-bargain damages in a real property fraud case, even if the parties would prefer that measure.

3. **Consequential damages are available in addition to the out-of-pocket baseline.** Section 3343(a)(1)-(4) permits:
   - Reliance expenditures made in preparation for the purchase
   - Costs of restoring the property to the represented condition (if practical)
   - Loss of use and enjoyment during the period of reliance
   - Other expenditures attributable to the fraud

4. **The out-of-pocket rule does not deny equitable remedies.** Section 3343(b)(2) preserves equitable remedies such as rescission and restitution. A plaintiff who prefers to unwind the transaction entirely can pursue rescission instead of damages.

---

THE STOUT FORMULA:

Out-of-pocket damages = [Actual value of what plaintiff parted with] − [Actual value of what plaintiff received]
                      + Consequential damages under § 3343(a)(1)-(4)
                      + Punitive damages under § 3294 (if fraud proven)
                      + Attorney's fees (if statutory basis)

NOT:
Benefit-of-the-bargain = [Represented value] − [Actual value] ← PROHIBITED under § 3343(b)(1)

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Stout v. Turney establishes the damages framework for the 2958 Honeysuckle complaint:

1. **Baseline out-of-pocket calculation**:
   - What the steward parted with: the Honeysuckle property, valued at $605K (sale price)
   - What the steward received: $109K net
   - Out-of-pocket damages: $605K − $109K = $496K
   - This is the § 3343 compensatory baseline

2. **Consequential damages** (§ 3343(a)(1)-(4)):
   - Reliance expenditures (costs incurred in preparing for the sale)
   - Loss of use and enjoyment of the property during the fraud period
   - Moving costs, carrying costs attributable to the fraud
   - Lost appreciation / investment value

3. **Alternative remedy — rescission under §§ 1567, 1688-1689**: If feasible, the steward may elect rescission (unwind the transaction entirely) rather than damages. Rescission restores both parties to their pre-contract positions.

4. **Family Code § 1101(h) 100% remedy operates separately**: The Stout § 3343 out-of-pocket rule governs third-party tort fraud claims. For the community property breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims against the managing spouse, the Fam. Code § 1101(h) 100%-of-value remedy is a separate and additional remedy that operates independently of § 3343.

5. **Punitive damages on top**: Stout's out-of-pocket baseline is the compensatory anchor for the punitive-to-compensatory ratio analysis under Bankhead v. ArvinMeritor.
