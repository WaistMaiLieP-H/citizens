# Alliance Mortgage Co. v. Rothwell (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1226

**Citation:** Alliance Mortgage Co. v. Rothwell (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1226, 44 Cal.Rptr.2d 352, 900 P.2d 601
**Decided:** August 1995
**Court:** Supreme Court of California
**Disposition:** Modified; clarified damages available in lender-fraud context and expanded consequential damages analysis.
**Statute construed:** Civ. Code § 3343 in conjunction with lender-fraud doctrine

## Facts

Alliance Mortgage was a mortgage lender that suffered losses as a result of fraudulent conduct by brokers and others in connection with mortgage loan transactions. The fraudulent conduct induced Alliance to fund loans that turned out to be worth less than represented. Alliance sued for fraud and sought damages under § 3343 (as a real property fraud plaintiff) and related theories.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **Section 3343 applies to defrauded lenders as well as buyers and sellers.** The "purchase, sale or exchange of property" language in § 3343(a) reaches transactions involving lenders who fund real estate acquisitions in reliance on fraud. A defrauded lender is entitled to the out-of-pocket measure under § 3343.

2. **The out-of-pocket measure for lenders is the difference between what the lender disbursed and the actual value of the security (collateral). If the security was worth less than represented, the lender's damages are the shortfall.

3. **Consequential damages under § 3343(a)(1)-(4) are available to lenders in appropriate circumstances.** The enumerated categories (reliance expenditures, loss of use, lost profits) apply to lender-plaintiffs where the facts support them.

4. **Punitive damages under § 3294 are available when the fraud is intentional.** The § 3294 punitive damages framework applies to lender fraud as it applies to buyer/seller fraud.

## Why this matters

*Alliance Mortgage* expands § 3343 beyond the buyer/seller context to reach any party defrauded in a "purchase, sale or exchange of property." This is significant because:

1. **Lender plaintiffs** — mortgage lenders, construction lenders, and private money lenders can invoke § 3343 when defrauded in loan transactions
2. **Complex transactions** — transactions involving multiple parties (buyer, seller, lender, agents, escrow) can have multiple § 3343 plaintiffs
3. **Derivative claims** — parties who provided funds in reliance on the fraud can pursue § 3343 claims even if they were not the direct buyer or seller

The case also reaffirms the broader principle that § 3343 is the governing damages measure for fraud in any real property transaction, not just a narrow subset of buyer/seller cases.

## Relevance to the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction

For Honeysuckle, *Alliance Mortgage* is relevant if any lender or third-party funder was defrauded in the 2023 transaction. If, for example, a mortgage was paid off using funds from the sale but the payoff was based on misrepresentations, the lender may have claims under § 3343 in addition to the steward's own claims.

More generally, *Alliance Mortgage* confirms the broad scope of § 3343 and establishes that the out-of-pocket rule applies regardless of which party is the plaintiff.

## Authority status

Binding California Supreme Court precedent. Still controlling. Cited alongside *Stout v. Turney* as the modern § 3343 authority.
