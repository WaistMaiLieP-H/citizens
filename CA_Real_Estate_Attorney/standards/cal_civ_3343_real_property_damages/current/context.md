# Civ. Code § 3343 — Real Property Fraud Damages

**Popular name:** Out-of-pocket damages rule for real property fraud
**Location:** Civil Code, Division 4, Part 1, Title 2, Chapter 2 (Measure of Damages for Particular Wrongs)

## What § 3343 does

Section 3343 establishes California's **out-of-pocket** measure of damages for real property fraud. This is different from the common-law **benefit-of-the-bargain** measure used in some other jurisdictions and in other contexts. Under § 3343:

**Measure:** "the difference between the actual value of that with which the defrauded person parted and the actual value of that which he received"

**Plus consequential damages** in four enumerated categories (subd. (a)(1)-(4)):
1. Amounts expended in reliance
2. Loss of use and enjoyment
3. Lost profits from property the defrauded party sold (for sellers defrauded)
4. Lost profits from property the defrauded party purchased (for buyers defrauded)

## The out-of-pocket vs. benefit-of-the-bargain distinction

**Out-of-pocket (§ 3343):** Plaintiff recovers the difference between what they gave and what they got. Example: Seller parts with a house worth $605K and receives $109K in cash. Out-of-pocket damages = $605K - $109K = $496K.

**Benefit-of-the-bargain (not California rule for real property):** Plaintiff recovers the difference between what was promised and what was delivered. Example: Buyer was promised a house worth $800K but received a house worth $605K. Benefit-of-the-bargain damages = $800K - $605K = $195K.

California explicitly chose the out-of-pocket rule in § 3343 and affirmatively rejected the benefit-of-the-bargain rule in subd. (b)(1): "Nothing in this section shall... Permit the defrauded person to recover any amount measured by the difference between the value of property as represented and the actual value thereof."

## The four consequential damage categories

Beyond the out-of-pocket baseline, § 3343(a)(1)-(4) adds specific consequential damages:

**(1) Reliance expenditures** — amounts actually spent in reliance on the fraud (e.g., inspection costs, moving expenses, improvements made in reliance on false representations)

**(2) Loss of use and enjoyment** — compensation for the lost use of the property (rental value of a home the buyer couldn't occupy; business interruption for a commercial property)

**(3) Lost profits on property SOLD by defrauded party** — if a seller was defrauded into parting with property, they can recover profits they would have earned by keeping it

**(4) Lost profits on property PURCHASED by defrauded party** — if a buyer was defrauded into acquiring property, they can recover anticipated profits the property would have generated — but only under strict conditions (subd. (a)(4)(i)-(iii)): acquisition for profit, reasonable reliance, and proximate causation

## Subdivision (b) — preservation of other remedies

Subdivision (b)(2) is critical: "Nothing in this section shall... Deny to any person having a cause of action for fraud or deceit any legal or equitable remedies to which such person may be entitled." This means § 3343 does not foreclose:

- **Rescission** under Civ. §§ 1567, 1688-1689
- **Restitution** upon rescission
- **Punitive damages** under Civ. § 3294
- **Equitable remedies** (constructive trust, specific performance, etc.)
- **Statutory remedies** under Civ. § 1102.4 or other disclosure statutes

In practice, a plaintiff defrauded in a real estate transaction pleads:
1. § 1572 / § 1709-1710 fraud (establishing liability)
2. § 3343 out-of-pocket damages (the compensatory baseline)
3. § 3343(a)(1)-(4) consequential damages (specific categories)
4. § 3294 punitive damages (for fraudulent conduct)
5. Rescission with restitution (alternative remedy)

## Relevance to the Honeysuckle transaction

For Honeysuckle, § 3343 provides the damages calculation framework:

**Seller defrauded analysis:**
- What was the actual value of what the steward parted with? (the house)
- What was the actual value of what the steward received? ($109K net)
- Difference: $605K (sale price as actual value of house) − $109K = $496K out-of-pocket damages

**Plus consequential damages:**
- Reliance expenditures on the transaction
- Lost use and enjoyment of the property (if the steward would have kept it)
- § 3343(a)(3) lost profits on property sold

**Plus punitive damages under § 3294** for the fraud conduct (fraud track, no despicability threshold).

**Plus attorney's fees** if available under a specific statute (e.g., Fam § 1101(g) for the community property breach claim).

**Alternative: rescission** — unwind the sale, return the parties to their pre-sale positions. This may be difficult or impossible if the property has been transferred multiple times, but preserving the alternative preserves remedies.
