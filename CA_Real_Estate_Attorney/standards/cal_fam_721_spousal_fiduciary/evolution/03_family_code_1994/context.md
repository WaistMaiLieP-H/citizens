# Amendment Context — Family Code Recodification (1994)

## What changed

Stats. 1992, ch. 162 (AB 1650) enacted the California Family Code, effective January 1, 1994. The Family Code reorganized all California family law that had been scattered across the Civil Code (the Marriage Title, spanning Civil Code §§ 4000–5500) into a single, purpose-built code.

**For § 721 specifically:**
- Civil Code § 5103 → Family Code § 721 (substantively unchanged)
- Civil Code § 5125 → Family Code § 1100 (equal management of community personal property)
- Civil Code § 5127 → Family Code § 1102 (both spouses required for community real property)
- Civil Code § 5125.1 → Family Code § 1101 (statutory remedy for breach of fiduciary duty)

## Why the recodification matters

The 1994 recodification was not just housekeeping. It:

1. **Established family law as its own field** — no longer nested inside the Civil Code, where it competed with contracts and torts for structure and interpretation. Family law now had its own division with its own interpretive framework.

2. **Created the Division 4 structure** — Part 1 of Division 4 (§§ 700–754) governs community property management during marriage. § 721 sits in Chapter 1 ("General Provisions"). This structural positioning means § 721 is the foundational governance rule that all of Part 1 operates under.

3. **Clarified the Probate Code carve-out** — The new § 721(a) lead clause ("Except as provided in Sections 143, 144, 146...") was added or clarified to address the interface between spousal fiduciary duties and testamentary and trust-related transactions. This was a practical fix, not a substantive change.

## What did NOT change

The 1994 recodification expressly preserved the substance of the 1975 equal management reform. The "highest good faith and fair dealing" language and the "neither shall take any unfair advantage" prohibition carried over word-for-word. The Legislature's intent was reorganization, not reform, at this stage.

## The subsequent partnership amendment

The most significant post-1994 change came sometime in the late 1990s or early 2000s, when the Legislature added the Corporations Code cross-references (§§ 16403, 16404, 16503) and the three specific obligations (books access, information on request, accounting/trustee). That amendment — addressed in stage 04 — made the fiduciary duty operational in a way the 1994 recodification did not.
