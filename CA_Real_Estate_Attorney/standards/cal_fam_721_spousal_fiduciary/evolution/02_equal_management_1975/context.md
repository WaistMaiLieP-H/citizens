# Amendment Context — Equal Management Reform (1975)

## What changed

The 1969–1975 reform package was the most significant change to California community property law in a century. The key statute was SB 364 (Stats. 1975, ch. 1272), which:

1. **Abolished husband-only management of community personal property** — §§ 5125 and 5127 (later §§ 1100 and 1102 of the Family Code) now gave each spouse equal management and control.

2. **Elevated "confidential relation" to enforceable fiduciary duty** — § 5103's "duty of the highest good faith and fair dealing" language replaced the nominal coverture-era confidential-relation reference with an actual fiduciary standard. The phrase "neither shall take any unfair advantage of the other" was new.

3. **Created a real remedy** — For the first time, a spouse had a statutory basis to challenge transactions made without consent during the marriage. This was not just a dissolution-era remedy; it was a live duty.

## Why 1975

The federal Equal Credit Opportunity Act (Pub. L. 93-495, 1974) prohibited discrimination in credit on the basis of sex or marital status. California could not maintain a community property system where husbands unilaterally mortgaged property — wives needed the legal standing to verify and challenge transactions. SB 364 was the legislative response.

Simultaneously, the women's rights movement's legal arm was litigating spousal property rights cases nationwide. The California Commission on the Status of Women had been lobbying for equal management since 1968. SB 364 was the culmination of seven years of legislative work.

## The diff from prior

| Before 1975 | After 1975 (§ 5103) |
|---|---|
| Husband exclusive management of community personal property | Equal management for each spouse |
| "Confidential relation" as label only | "Highest good faith and fair dealing" as enforceable duty |
| No statutory remedy during marriage | Breach actionable during marriage |
| Wife joins husband's instrument for real property | Both spouses must join (§ 5127 / later § 1102) |

## Historical significance

In re Marriage of Rossin (2009) described this reform as creating a "partnership" standard before the Corporations Code cross-references made it explicit. The 1975 language — "highest good faith and fair dealing" — is the foundational promise. Every subsequent amendment built on this 1975 bedrock.
