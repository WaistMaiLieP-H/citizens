# Representative authority: Fam. § 721 partnership-level fiduciary duty

**Note on case selection:** Scaffolded for a Court of Appeal case illustrating how partnership fiduciary duty principles (via Corp. Code §§ 16403-16503) apply to spousal transactions under Fam. Code § 721(b). Specific case citation pending primary-source verification.

## The doctrinal proposition

Fam. Code § 721(b) expressly incorporates partnership fiduciary duty by cross-reference to Corp. Code §§ 16403, 16404, and 16503. This means the well-developed body of partnership law on fiduciary duty applies to spousal transactions. Key partnership duties that thus apply to spouses:

1. **Duty of loyalty** (Corp. Code § 16404(b)) — the managing spouse must (a) account for property, profit, or benefit derived in conduct of the marriage's economic affairs, (b) refrain from dealing with the community on behalf of a party whose interests are adverse to the community, and (c) refrain from competing with the community in the conduct of its affairs.

2. **Duty of care** (Corp. Code § 16404(c)) — the managing spouse must refrain from engaging in grossly negligent or reckless conduct, intentional misconduct, or knowing violations of law.

3. **Duty of good faith and fair dealing** (Corp. Code § 16404(d)) — the managing spouse must discharge their duties and obligations with good faith and fair dealing.

4. **Duty to render information** (Corp. Code § 16403) — the managing spouse must furnish, without demand, any information concerning the community's affairs reasonably required for the other spouse to exercise their rights and duties.

## Why this matters

The incorporation of partnership fiduciary duty by reference in § 721(b) gives the spousal fiduciary duty the substantive weight of a fully-developed body of law. Instead of having to rebuild fiduciary doctrine from scratch in the spousal context, courts can apply the partnership case law directly. This is particularly valuable because partnership fiduciary duty is one of the most demanding in California law — stronger than ordinary trustee duty, nearly as strong as attorney-client duty.

The duty to "render information... without demand" is particularly important. Under partnership law, a managing partner cannot wait to be asked — they must volunteer material information. Applied to spouses under § 721(b), this means the managing spouse cannot defeat a disclosure claim by pointing out that the non-managing spouse never specifically asked about the transaction. The duty is affirmative and proactive.

## Relevance to the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction

Applying the partnership fiduciary duty to Honeysuckle:

1. **Duty of loyalty:** The managing spouse must account for property, profit, or benefit derived in the Honeysuckle transaction — the $496K unaccounted gap is squarely within this duty.

2. **Refraining from adverse-interest dealings:** If the managing spouse was dealing with the community property on behalf of an adverse party (e.g., transferring proceeds to accounts held by relatives or third parties), the duty of loyalty was breached.

3. **Duty to render information without demand:** The managing spouse should have volunteered detailed information about the transaction's structure, timing, and disposition of proceeds. Silence or waiting-to-be-asked is a breach.

4. **Duty of care:** Grossly negligent handling of the community's economic affairs — allowing 19 unsigned documents, losing track of $496K in proceeds — may constitute a duty-of-care breach independent of fraud.
