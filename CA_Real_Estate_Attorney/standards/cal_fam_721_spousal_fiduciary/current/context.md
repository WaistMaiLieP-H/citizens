# Fam. Code § 721 — General Spousal Fiduciary Duty

**Popular name:** Spousal fiduciary duty general rule; highest-good-faith rule
**Location:** Family Code, Division 4 (Rights and Obligations During Marriage), Part 1 (Competency), Chapter 1 (General Provisions)
**Companion sections:** §§ 1100 (community personal property duty), 1101 (remedy), 1102 (community real property dual-consent); Corp. Code §§ 16403, 16404, 16503 (partnership fiduciary duties incorporated by reference)

## What § 721 does

Section 721 is the **general fiduciary duty statute** for California spouses. It establishes the duty that § 1100(e) and § 1101 enforce. Section 721 provides the doctrinal foundation; §§ 1100 and 1101 provide the specific applications and remedies.

## Three key propositions

1. **Spouses can transact with each other and with third parties.** Subd. (a) establishes the baseline — married persons have the same contractual capacity as unmarried persons, subject to subd. (b)'s fiduciary overlay.

2. **Interspousal transactions are subject to the "highest good faith and fair dealing" standard.** Subd. (b) imports the common-law confidential relationship doctrine: "neither shall take any unfair advantage of the other." This is the strongest fiduciary standard in California law — stronger than ordinary business fiduciary duty, stronger than trustee duty, equivalent to the partnership duty under the Corporations Code partnership statutes.

3. **The duty includes three specific obligations (§ 721(b)(1)-(3)):**
   - **Access to books.** Each spouse must provide the other "access at all times to any books kept regarding a transaction" for inspection and copying.
   - **True and full information.** Upon request, each spouse must render "true and full information" about transactions concerning community property.
   - **Accounting and constructive trust.** Any benefit or profit derived without the other spouse's consent is held as a trustee — the non-consenting spouse can sue for an accounting and constructive trust of the benefit.

## The incorporation of partnership law

Section 721(b)'s reference to Corp. Code §§ 16403, 16404, and 16503 is critical. Those sections define the fiduciary duty of partners in a general partnership, which is one of the highest fiduciary standards in California law. By incorporating partnership fiduciary duty by reference, § 721 signals that spouses owe each other the same level of duty as business partners, not merely the level of duty that arms-length contracting parties owe.

This matters in practice because partnership fiduciary duty case law is well-developed and provides a substantial body of doctrine that can be applied to spousal transactions. A spouse who would not be allowed to take an opportunity from a partnership without disclosure and consent cannot take a community asset from the other spouse without disclosure and consent.

## The "highest good faith and fair dealing" standard

The statutory language "duty of the highest good faith and fair dealing" is deliberately strong. It is not the ordinary contractual duty of good faith (which is a lower standard). It is not the fiduciary duty of an ordinary trustee (which is strong but not "highest"). It is the "highest" level of duty California law recognizes — a level reserved for relationships where one party is especially dependent on the honesty and competence of the other.

Case law applying § 721 has emphasized this high standard:
- *In re Marriage of Rossin* (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 725 — "highest character" fiduciary duty
- *In re Marriage of Feldman* (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1470 — affirmative disclosure obligation
- *In re Marriage of Margulis* (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 1252 — disclosure during the divorce process

## Relationship to §§ 1100(e) and 1101

Section 721 is the general duty. Section 1100(e) is the specific application to community property management — it expressly references § 721 as the source of the fiduciary obligation. Section 1101 is the remedy — it expressly references "Sections 721 and 1100" as the duty source for the 50%/100% remedy structure.

The three sections read together form a coherent framework:
- **§ 721** — general duty and its content (access, information, accounting)
- **§ 1100(e)** — specific application to community personal property management
- **§ 1101** — remedies (50% default, 100% when breach falls within § 3294 fraud/oppression/malice)

For community real property, add **§ 1102** (dual-consent requirement).

## Relevance to the Hartmann matter

Section 721 provides the doctrinal foundation for all spousal claims related to the Honeysuckle transaction and the broader community property dispossession pattern. Specific applications:

1. **Access to books** — Were the steward's access rights under § 721(b)(1) honored? If documentation about the Honeysuckle sale, the crypto transfers, the surgery payments, and related transactions was kept by the other spouse, the steward had a right to access those records at any time.

2. **True and full information** — Upon request, the other spouse had a duty to provide true and full information about the community property transactions. Failure to respond, misleading responses, or partial responses are actionable breaches.

3. **Accounting and constructive trust** — Benefits derived from community property without the steward's consent are held in trust for the community. The steward can sue for an accounting and constructive trust of those benefits.

4. **Partnership-level duty** — Because § 721(b) incorporates partnership fiduciary duty, the steward can invoke the full body of partnership law (including the duty to account for usurped opportunities, the duty to refrain from competing, and the duty to disclose material information) as analogues for the spousal duty.
