# In re Marriage of Feldman (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1470

**Citation:** In re Marriage of Feldman (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1470, 64 Cal.Rptr.3d 29
**Decided:** 2007
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District, Division One
**Disposition:** Affirmed sanctions against non-disclosing spouse; clarified the scope of the § 1101 disclosure duty.
**Statutes construed:** Fam. Code §§ 721, 1100, 1101

## Facts

In the Feldmans' dissolution proceedings, the wife obtained substantial sanctions against the husband for repeated failures to disclose community property assets. The husband had failed to identify, value, and disclose multiple community assets (including complex financial instruments and business interests) during the disclosure process. The trial court imposed sanctions including attorney's fees and a monetary penalty. The husband appealed, arguing the sanctions were disproportionate and that some of the undisclosed items were not material.

## Holding

The Court of Appeal affirmed and articulated key principles:

1. **The disclosure duty under §§ 721 and 1100(e) is comprehensive, not selective.** A managing spouse must disclose ALL community assets and their values, not merely those the spouse considers material or those the other spouse specifically asks about. The statutory language "full disclosure of all material facts and information" means full, not partial.

2. **Sanctions under § 1101 are substantial and cumulative.** The 50% remedy under subd. (g) or 100% remedy under subd. (h) can be applied cumulatively across multiple asset categories. A spouse who fails to disclose multiple assets faces sanctions for each.

3. **The "actual knowledge" limitation on the 3-year SOL is narrow.** Under § 1101(d)(1), the SOL runs from "actual knowledge." This is not "constructive knowledge" or "should have known." The plaintiff's subjective actual awareness of the transaction is what starts the clock. Plaintiffs frequently have an extended window because they did not actually know what was happening until they started investigating.

4. **Attorney's fees and costs under § 1101(g) are mandatory, not discretionary, upon a finding of breach.** Once the court finds a § 1100(e) breach, the 50% remedy is automatic and attorney's fees follow. The court cannot decline to award fees based on equitable considerations.

5. **The scope of the § 721 fiduciary duty extends to complex financial instruments.** The court rejected the argument that only simple, obvious community assets are subject to the disclosure rule. Hedge fund interests, partnership interests, executive compensation, stock options, and other sophisticated instruments are all within the disclosure obligation.

## Why this is load-bearing

*Feldman* is the modern appellate authority on the scope and sanctions of the § 1101 remedy framework. It is routinely cited for:

1. The comprehensiveness of the disclosure duty (not selective)
2. The narrow "actual knowledge" SOL rule
3. The mandatory nature of attorney's fees upon breach finding
4. The extension of disclosure duty to sophisticated financial instruments

*Feldman* is particularly useful for plaintiffs because it makes clear that the § 1101 remedy is substantial, cumulative, and automatic. A managing spouse cannot escape sanctions by arguing "I didn't think that was material" or "I forgot to disclose that one item."

## Relevance to the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction

For Honeysuckle, *Feldman* supports the plaintiff's theory in several ways:

1. **Comprehensive disclosure required:** The managing spouse had a duty to disclose ALL material facts about the Honeysuckle transaction, including the community property status, the destination of proceeds, and any side agreements or related transactions.

2. **SOL analysis:** Under *Feldman*'s narrow reading of "actual knowledge," the 3-year SOL ran from when the steward actually learned of the transaction's specific irregularities — which may be recent, even though the sale occurred in 2023.

3. **Mandatory fees and sanctions:** If the plaintiff prevails on the § 1100(e) breach claim, *Feldman* supports automatic attorney's fees and costs under § 1101(g) — the court cannot deny them on equitable grounds.

4. **Extension to complex transactions:** The Honeysuckle transaction had multiple layers (house sale, crypto transfers, surgery payments, unsigned documents). Under *Feldman*, all of these are within the disclosure obligation — the managing spouse could not selectively disclose only the house sale while omitting the related transactions.

## Authority status

Published, binding as persuasive appellate authority. Still controlling as of 2026-04-11. Cited frequently in family law property division cases.
