IN RE MARRIAGE OF FELDMAN
153 Cal.App.4th 1470 (2007)
California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District, Division One
Decided: 2007

---

FACTS:

In the Feldmans' dissolution proceedings, the wife obtained substantial sanctions against the husband for repeated failures to disclose community property assets. The husband had failed to identify, value, and disclose multiple community assets (including complex financial instruments and business interests) during the disclosure process. The trial court imposed sanctions including attorney's fees and a monetary penalty. Husband appealed, arguing the sanctions were disproportionate and that some undisclosed items were not material.

---

HOLDING:

FIVE FOUNDATIONAL PROPOSITIONS ON § 1101 SCOPE AND SANCTIONS:

1. **The disclosure duty under §§ 721 and 1100(e) is comprehensive, not selective.** "A managing spouse must disclose ALL community assets and their values, not merely those the spouse considers material or those the other spouse specifically asks about. The statutory language 'full disclosure of all material facts and information' means full, not partial."

2. **Sanctions under § 1101 are substantial and cumulative.** The 50% remedy under subd. (g) or 100% remedy under subd. (h) can be applied cumulatively across multiple asset categories. A spouse who fails to disclose multiple assets faces sanctions for each.

3. **The "actual knowledge" limitation on the 3-year SOL is narrow.** Under § 1101(d)(1), the SOL runs from "actual knowledge." This is NOT "constructive knowledge" or "should have known." The plaintiff's subjective actual awareness of the transaction is what starts the clock. Plaintiffs frequently have an extended window because they did not actually know what was happening until they started investigating.

4. **Attorney's fees and costs under § 1101(g) are mandatory, not discretionary, upon a finding of breach.** "Once the court finds a § 1100(e) breach, the 50% remedy is automatic and attorney's fees follow. The court cannot decline to award fees based on equitable considerations."

5. **The scope of the § 721 fiduciary duty extends to complex financial instruments.** Hedge fund interests, partnership interests, executive compensation, stock options, and other sophisticated instruments are all within the disclosure obligation — the argument that the non-managing spouse "wouldn't understand complex instruments anyway" is not a defense.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Feldman supports the plaintiff's theory for the Honeysuckle transaction in several ways:

1. **Comprehensive disclosure required**: The managing spouse had a duty to disclose ALL material facts about the Honeysuckle transaction — community property status, destination of proceeds, side agreements, related transactions. "Selective" disclosure covering only the house sale while omitting crypto transfers and surgery payments is a breach.

2. **SOL analysis — "actual knowledge" rule**: Under Feldman's narrow reading, the 3-year SOL ran from when the steward actually learned of the transaction's specific irregularities. If discovery of the $496K gap occurred recently, the SOL may still be open even though the sale occurred in 2023.

3. **Mandatory fees and sanctions**: If the plaintiff prevails on the § 1100(e) breach claim, Feldman supports automatic attorney's fees and costs under § 1101(g). The court cannot deny them on equitable grounds.

4. **Complex transaction extension**: The Honeysuckle transaction had multiple layers (house sale, crypto transfers, surgery payments, unsigned documents). Under Feldman, all of these layers are within the disclosure obligation — no selective disclosure escape.

5. **Cumulative sanctions**: If multiple community assets were concealed (house proceeds, crypto, other transfers), Feldman supports cumulative sanctions for each concealment.
