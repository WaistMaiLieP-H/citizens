# Amendment Context — Family Code Recodification (1994)

## What changed

Stats. 1992, ch. 162 (AB 1650) moved Civ. Code § 5125.1 to Fam. Code § 1101 without substantive change. The reorganization brought § 1101 into a coherent relationship with:

- **§ 721** (fiduciary duty source) — the breach that § 1101 remedies
- **§ 1100** (equal management of community personal property) — the companion management right
- **§ 1102** (both-spouses-must-join for community real property) — the specific requirement § 1101 enforces in the real property context

The structural relationship between §§ 721, 1100, 1101, and 1102 within Division 4, Part 1 of the Family Code is the framework that makes them collectively enforceable as an integrated community property management system. § 1101(g) references "Sections 721 and 1100" explicitly — the cross-reference structure was formalized at recodification.

## What remained as outstanding

The § 1101(h) 100%-remedy provision — for breaches "within the ambit of Section 3294 of the Civil Code" (despicable conduct) — was not yet part of § 1101. The 50% remedy of § 1101(g) was the maximum available at this stage.

## Significance

The 1994 recodification consolidated the remedy structure and made the cross-references explicit. It did not substantively change the rights or remedies. The critical gap — the absence of a super-remedy for egregious breaches — was addressed by the stage 03 amendment.
