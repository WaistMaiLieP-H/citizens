# Statute version cited in Robinson v. Grossman (1997)

*Robinson v. Grossman* applied Civ. Code § 1102 et seq. (the TDS statute) as it existed in 1997. The core TDS framework was enacted by AB 1448 (Stats. 1985, ch. 1574) and has been the governing residential disclosure regime since January 1, 1987.

**Relevant statutory text (1997 ≈ 2026):**
The court applied § 1102.6 (the TDS form) and § 1102.4 (strict liability for omissions). Both provisions have been amended to expand disclosure categories since 1997 — including natural hazard disclosures, death disclosure, and other addenda — but the core framework construed in *Robinson* (seller's disclosure of known material conditions, strict liability for omissions, concurrent fraud theory) remains in force without material drift.

**Consistency check:** *Robinson*'s holdings on the scope of the disclosure duty (historical conditions, remediated defects) and the layered strict-liability/fraud structure apply to current § 1102 et seq. without amendment drift concerns. The 1997 text analyzed by the court is substantively identical to current § 1102 and § 1102.4 for purposes of this doctrine.

**Application to Honeysuckle (October 2023 sale):** The version of § 1102 in effect for the 2023 sale is the current version. *Robinson*'s analysis applies in full.
