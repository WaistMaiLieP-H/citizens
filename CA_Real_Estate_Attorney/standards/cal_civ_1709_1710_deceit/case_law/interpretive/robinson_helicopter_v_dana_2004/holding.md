# Robinson Helicopter Co. v. Dana Corp. (2004) 34 Cal.4th 979

**Citation:** Robinson Helicopter Co., Inc. v. Dana Corp. (2004) 34 Cal.4th 979, 22 Cal.Rptr.3d 352, 102 P.3d 268
**Decided:** December 2004
**Court:** Supreme Court of California
**Disposition:** Affirmed judgment for plaintiff; established the economic loss rule exception for intentional fraud.
**Statute construed:** Civ. Code §§ 1709-1710 (deceit) in the context of the economic loss rule

## Facts

Robinson Helicopter manufactured helicopters that required specific clutch assemblies manufactured by Dana Corporation. Dana supplied non-conforming clutches over a period of time, misrepresenting them as meeting contract specifications. When Robinson discovered the non-conformance, it had to recall and repair helicopters already sold to customers. Robinson sued Dana for breach of contract AND for fraud (intentional misrepresentation). Dana moved to dismiss the fraud claim under the "economic loss rule," which generally bars tort recovery for economic losses arising from breach of contract.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **The economic loss rule generally bars tort recovery for economic losses arising from breach of contract.** This is the baseline rule: when parties are in contract, and the only losses are economic (as opposed to physical injury to person or property), tort claims are generally unavailable — the remedy is breach of contract.

2. **BUT the economic loss rule does NOT bar intentional fraud claims where the fraudulent conduct is independent of the contract.** If the defendant committed fraud that is distinct from the mere breach of contractual promises, the fraud claim survives. The court identified several categories of fraud that satisfy the "independent" requirement:
   - Fraud in the inducement (false statements that induced entry into the contract)
   - Affirmative misrepresentations during contract performance (not merely failure to perform)
   - Fraudulent concealment of breach or defects

3. **Dana's conduct was independent fraud.** Dana did not merely fail to deliver conforming goods — it affirmatively represented that non-conforming goods WERE conforming. This was a fraud separate from and in addition to the breach of contract.

4. **Tort remedies, including punitive damages, are available for independent fraud.** Because the fraud claim survived, Robinson was entitled to tort damages and the possibility of § 3294 punitive damages, not just contract damages.

## Why this is load-bearing

*Robinson Helicopter* is THE modern California authority on the economic loss rule and its exceptions. Before *Robinson Helicopter*, California case law was inconsistent on whether fraud claims could survive the economic loss rule. After *Robinson Helicopter*, the rule is clear:

- **Economic loss rule bars fraud claims that are merely recast breach of contract claims** ("you promised X, you delivered Y, you committed fraud"). Not allowed.
- **Economic loss rule does NOT bar fraud claims where the fraud is independent** (affirmative misrepresentation, concealment, fraud in the inducement). Allowed.

This distinction is critical in real estate fraud cases where the parties are in contract (the purchase agreement). Plaintiffs must plead fraud as an independent claim, not merely as a breach of contract theory.

## Relevance to the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction

*Robinson Helicopter* is directly relevant to the Honeysuckle complaint. The buyer and seller are in contract (the purchase agreement), and the economic losses are the $496K gap between sale price and net proceeds. Under the economic loss rule, some courts might dismiss a fraud claim as merely a breach of contract claim.

*Robinson Helicopter* defeats that argument. The Honeysuckle fraud theories are independent:

1. **Fraud in the inducement** — misrepresentations about the community property status, the authority to sell, or the destination of proceeds that induced the parties to enter the contract
2. **Affirmative misrepresentations** — statements on the closing statement, TDS, or other documents that were affirmatively false
3. **Fraudulent concealment** — suppression of material facts about the transaction

All three categories satisfy *Robinson Helicopter*'s independence requirement. The fraud claims survive the economic loss rule, and the plaintiff is entitled to tort damages and punitive damages under § 3294.

## Authority status

Binding California Supreme Court precedent. Still controlling as of 2026-04-11. Cited as the leading economic loss rule exception case in every modern California commercial and real estate fraud opinion.
