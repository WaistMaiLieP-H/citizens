# Historical Chain — Civ. Code §§ 1709-1710 (Tort of Deceit)

| Year | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1872 | Civ. Code §§ 1709-1710 enacted | Codification of common-law deceit tort |
| 1872 → 2026 | **Text unchanged** | 154 years of textual stability |
| 1941 | *Seeger v. Odell*, 18 Cal.2d 409 | Modern articulation of suppression deceit |
| 1985 | *Tenzer v. Superscope*, 39 Cal.3d 18 | Circumstantial-evidence rule for promissory deceit |
| 1996 | *Lazar v. Superior Court*, 12 Cal.4th 631 | Promissory fraud in tort; tort-independent-of-contract rule |
| 2004 | *Robinson Helicopter Co. v. Dana Corp.*, 34 Cal.4th 979 | Economic loss rule exception for intentional fraud |
| 2026 | In force, unchanged | Active tort doctrine |

## The wound and promise

Like § 1572, §§ 1709-1710 address the wound of pre-1872 California's fragmented and inconsistent fraud doctrine. The 1872 codification provided uniform rules: specific acts, specific elements, uniform application across all 58 counties.

The promise has held for 154 years. The Legislature has had multiple opportunities to modify §§ 1709-1710 and has chosen not to. The underlying doctrine has been refined by case law (especially *Seeger*, *Tenzer*, *Lazar*, *Robinson Helicopter*) but the statutory text has remained stable.

## Relationship to § 1572

§ 1572 and §§ 1709-1710 are often pleaded together because they cover overlapping ground with subtly different scope:

- § 1572 applies to fraud in the formation of a contract (contract context)
- §§ 1709-1710 apply to deceit generally (tort context, not dependent on contract)

Both have four (or five, for § 1572) alternative acts and both have been textually stable since 1872. Case law interpreting one is generally transferable to the other, with appropriate context adjustments.
