# Historical Chain — Fam. Code § 1100 (Community Property Management)

## The wound

Before January 1, 1975, California community property law subordinated married women. Under former Civ. Code § 5125 (enacted 1872, derived from Spanish-Mexican civil law traditions imported at statehood), the husband had exclusive management and control of community personal property. A wife who worked, inherited, or earned income saw those funds fall into a community estate she could not legally manage. She could not sign deeds, could not open bank accounts with community funds in her own name, could not enter binding contracts. Her husband, in theory, was supposed to act in the community's interest; in practice, many husbands dissipated community assets, sold them without notice, or transferred them to relatives and friends to shield them from future division at dissolution.

The wound was **structural: the law assumed one spouse was the economic actor and the other was not, and when that assumption failed, the non-managing spouse had no remedy except after-the-fact equitable claims for damages she could rarely prove.**

## The promise

Three successive promises built the current § 1100:

1. **1975 — Equal management and control.** SB 364 (Stats. 1973, ch. 987), effective January 1, 1975, abolished the husband's-management rule. Either spouse has full authority over community personal property, subject to written-consent requirements for gifts and family-dwelling dispositions. The promise: no spouse's economic authority is subordinated to the other's.

2. **1986-1991 — Fiduciary duty codification.** Stats. 1986, ch. 1091 and Stats. 1991, ch. 1026 added fiduciary-duty language to former Civ. Code § 5125, requiring spouses to act "in accordance with the general rules governing fiduciary relationships" and to make "full disclosure" of all material facts about community assets. The promise: disclosure is affirmative, not reactive, and the standard is the "highest character" fiduciary duty.

3. **1992-1994 — Family Code recodification.** Stats. 1992, ch. 162, effective January 1, 1994, recodified the family-law sections of the Civil Code into the new Family Code. Civ. Code § 5125 became Fam. Code § 1100. The substantive rules were preserved; the language was modernized. Paired enforcement: Fam. Code § 1101 provides the remedy structure (50% community share for ordinary breaches; 100% of value for fraudulent/oppressive/malicious breaches under the § 3294 definition).

## The promise today

Section 1100, paired with § 1101 and § 721, delivers on all three of the original promises:

- Equal management and control — neither spouse is legally subordinated.
- Affirmative fiduciary disclosure — silence or incomplete response is a breach.
- Punitive remedy for fraudulent breaches — 100% of the asset's value, not just the 50% community share, plus attorney's fees.

The continuous-duty rule (the duty runs from marriage through separation through division) is what makes the framework usable in the Honeysuckle fact pattern. Without that rule, a managing spouse could exploit the separation period to conceal assets with impunity. With it, the duty survives the separation and becomes the vehicle for the plaintiff's recovery.

## Why this chain matters for 2958 Honeysuckle

Honeysuckle presents every failure pattern the 1975-1994 reforms were designed to address:

1. Community property acquired during marriage
2. Disposition without proper consent and/or disclosure
3. Concealment of material facts about where proceeds went
4. A dissolution that was not finalized with proper property division
5. A multi-year window in which the continuous fiduciary duty was in force but not honored

Under *Walker* (2012), the remedy is § 1101(h) 100% of the value of the undisclosed/wrongfully-disposed asset. For a $605K sale with $496K unaccounted, that is a meaningful recovery. Combined with § 1572 tort fraud claims against third parties (brokers, escrow, title, buyer), § 3294 punitives, and § 1101(g) attorney's fees, the pleading package is substantial.

The chain holds. The promise delivers.
