# Statute version cited in In re Marriage of Lucas (1980)

*Lucas* was decided under the former Civil Code sections that have since been recodified into the Family Code. The operative authorities were:

- **Former Civ. Code § 4800** (distribution of community property at dissolution) — now Fam. Code § 2550
- **Former Civ. Code § 5110** (community property presumption for property acquired during marriage) — now Fam. Code § 760
- **Former Civ. Code § 5125** (management and control — as of 1980, reflecting the 1975 equal-management-and-control amendment) — now Fam. Code § 1100

**Post-Lucas codifications affecting § 1100 relevance:**
- Stats. 1983, ch. 342 (amended former § 4800.2) — codified the reimbursement rule for separate property contributions
- Stats. 1984, ch. 1671 (further amendments) — strengthened the written-agreement requirement
- Stats. 1992, ch. 162 (recodification) — moved all of the above to the Family Code

**Consistency with current § 1100:** The joint-title presumption that was the heart of *Lucas* is now codified at Fam. Code § 2581 (general joint title presumption at dissolution) and § 2640 (reimbursement for separate contributions). The management-and-control rule that was former § 5125 is now § 1100. The *Lucas* analysis of the relationship between characterization and management is still good law: characterization determines who has management authority under § 1100.

**Drift analysis:** *Lucas* did not construe the fiduciary duty language that was later added to former § 5125 (in 1986/1991). Its holdings are about characterization and the joint-title presumption, not about the § 1100(e) duty directly. So *Lucas* is relevant to § 1100 primarily as a characterization authority, not as a fiduciary-duty authority. For fiduciary-duty cases, see Rossin and Walker in this same folder.
