# In re Marriage of Rossin (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 725

**Citation:** In re Marriage of Rossin (2009) 172 Cal.App.4th 725, 91 Cal.Rptr.3d 427
**Decided:** March 23, 2009
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, Division Eight
**Disposition:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part; upheld trial court's finding of breach of fiduciary duty and its disclosure-related sanctions.
**Statutes construed:** Family Code §§ 721, 1100, 1101

## Facts

During marriage, husband engaged in business transactions that involved community property. At dissolution, wife alleged husband had failed to disclose material facts about certain community investments and had effectively used his management authority over community property to advance his own interests. The trial court found breach of fiduciary duty, ordered sanctions, and required full disclosure of the previously concealed information. Husband appealed, arguing the disclosure obligation was overbroad and that the sanctions were not supported by adequate findings.

## Holding

The Court of Appeal affirmed the fiduciary duty findings and articulated three propositions:

1. **The § 1100(e) / § 721 fiduciary duty is the highest duty known to law.** Spouses stand in a fiduciary relationship "of the highest character," requiring good faith and full disclosure, not merely absence of bad faith. (*Id.* at 734, citing § 721(b).)

2. **Concealment of material facts is a breach regardless of motive.** The duty is affirmative. A managing spouse who fails to disclose a material fact breaches even if the failure was not intended to harm the other spouse. The standard is objective: would a reasonable spouse in the same position have disclosed? (*Id.* at 735.)

3. **The duty continues from marriage through separation through division.** There is no interruption. A spouse cannot take advantage of the separation period to conceal or dispose of community assets. The duty runs continuously from the commencement of marriage through the final division of community property. (*Id.* at 738.)

## Why this matters

*Rossin* is the standard-setting articulation of the spousal fiduciary duty in the modern California Family Code. It is routinely cited for three propositions:

1. That the duty is the "highest character" — stronger than ordinary business fiduciary duties.
2. That disclosure is affirmative, not merely honest response to direct questions.
3. That the duty operates continuously through separation.

Together these make the § 1100(e) duty extraordinarily hard for a managing spouse to satisfy without active disclosure.

## Relevance to Honeysuckle

*Rossin*'s three propositions map directly onto the Honeysuckle facts:

1. **Highest character duty.** The person managing the Honeysuckle transaction owed the other spouse a duty "of the highest character." Ordinary arms-length transaction practices (disclose only what was asked about) are insufficient.

2. **Affirmative disclosure.** The duty was affirmative. The managing spouse had to disclose, without being asked, all material facts about the transaction — who the buyer was, what price was being received, where proceeds were going, who was signing what.

3. **Continuous through separation.** Even if Michael and Christina were separated at the time of the Honeysuckle sale, the § 1100(e) duty was in force. Separation does not end it; only division ends it.

*Rossin* is the case to cite when the defense argues "they were separated, so the fiduciary duty didn't apply." Wrong — it applied, and it applied at its full strength.

## Authority status

Published, binding as persuasive appellate authority. Not reviewed by Supreme Court. Cited frequently in family-law property division cases. No subsequent negative treatment.
