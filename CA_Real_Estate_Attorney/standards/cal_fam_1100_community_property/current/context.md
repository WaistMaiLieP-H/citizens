# Fam. Code § 1100 — Current Context

**Popular name:** Community personal property management and control; spousal fiduciary duty
**Location:** Family Code, Division 4 (Rights and Obligations During Marriage), Part 4 (Management and Control of Marital Property), Chapter 2 (Community Property)
**Companion sections:** § 721 (spousal fiduciary duty general rule), § 761 (community property held in revocable trust), § 1101 (spousal claim for breach of fiduciary duty), § 1102 (community REAL property — requires both spouses' consent), § 1103 (mental incapacity exception), § 2550 (equal division at dissolution)
**Verification status:** Current text retrieved via VernenLegal MCP from leginfo 2026-04-10

## What § 1100 does

Section 1100 governs which spouse has authority to manage, control, and dispose of community personal property during marriage. It operates alongside § 1102 (which governs community REAL property) and § 721 (which imposes the general spousal fiduciary duty). The five subdivisions create a tiered rule:

1. **Subdivision (a) — General rule: equal management and control.** Either spouse has full authority to manage and dispose of community personal property. Each spouse has the same power over community property that he or she would have over separate property.

2. **Subdivision (b) — Gift restriction.** Neither spouse may make a gift of community personal property or dispose of it for less than fair and reasonable value without the written consent of the other.

3. **Subdivision (c) — Family dwelling and household furnishings restriction.** Neither spouse may sell, convey, or encumber community personal property used as the family dwelling, household furniture/furnishings/fittings, or clothing of the other spouse or minor children, without the written consent of the other. Note: this covers the dwelling as personal property (e.g., a mobile home characterized as personalty), household furnishings, and certain family personal property. The family REAL estate (fee simple house) is governed by § 1102, which has its own written-consent rule.

4. **Subdivision (d) — Business management exception.** A spouse operating a business that is all or substantially all community property has primary management and control, subject to a prior-written-notice requirement for major transactions. Failure to give notice does NOT invalidate the transaction but can trigger § 1101 remedies.

5. **Subdivision (e) — Fiduciary duty.** Each spouse owes the other a duty "in accordance with the general rules governing fiduciary relationships" (§ 721). This duty includes full disclosure of all material facts about community assets and debts, equal access to all records, and is enforceable until the assets and liabilities have been divided.

## The fiduciary duty has teeth

Subdivision (e) is the most load-bearing provision for real estate litigation arising from community property disputes. It creates a **statutory fiduciary duty** that operates between spouses during marriage and survives into the dissolution process until division. The enforcement mechanism is § 1101, which allows a spouse to sue for:

- 50% of the value of the asset undisclosed or wrongly disposed of (§ 1101(g))
- 100% of the value if the breach was fraudulent or malicious (§ 1101(h))
- Attorney's fees and costs
- Punitive damages in appropriate cases

The fiduciary duty is continuous — it does not stop when the spouses separate. It continues until "the assets and liabilities have been divided by the parties or by a court." This is the critical rule for a dissolution that was never finalized with proper division: the duty to disclose and the right to sue under § 1101 remain in force indefinitely.

## Why this is Citizen-relevant

The Honeysuckle facts present a direct § 1100(e) / § 1101(h) theory:

1. **Marriage status.** Michael and Christina were married at the time of the 2958 Honeysuckle acquisition. The house was presumptively community property under Family Code § 760 (property acquired during marriage).

2. **Dissolution status.** The steward's memory documents that the Christina dissolution (rf09470833) was never finalized with a proper disposition judgment. This means the community property was NEVER judicially divided. The fiduciary duty under § 1100(e) continues in force today.

3. **Breach theory.** If Christina made representations or took actions concerning the house disposition without the steward's full consent, or if she concealed material facts about the sale proceeds, she breached the § 1100(e) duty. The remedy under § 1101(h) is 100% of the asset value (not 50%) because the breach was fraudulent or malicious.

4. **Conservatorship overlay.** If the steward was subject to a conservatorship (per project_conservatorship_discovery.md), the conservator's role in directing or ratifying dispositions is a separate inquiry — but the spousal fiduciary duty in § 1100(e) is independent of the conservatorship and can be asserted by the steward in his own capacity.

## Relationship to § 1572 fraud

A § 1100(e) breach of fiduciary duty is conceptually adjacent to § 1572(3) suppression fraud. When the spouse with community property authority conceals material facts from the other spouse, both statutes are violated. Plaintiffs routinely plead both — § 1100(e) / § 1101 for the inter-spousal claim, and § 1572 for the tort claim against third parties (the broker, escrow officer, title company, buyer) who participated in the concealment.

The § 1101(h) 100%-of-value remedy is especially powerful: instead of suing for the out-of-pocket difference between sale price and proceeds received, the steward can sue for the full community-share value of the property as of the date of the undisclosed disposition.
