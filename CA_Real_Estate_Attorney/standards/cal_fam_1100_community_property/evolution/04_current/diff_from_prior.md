# § 1100 — Diff from 1975 origin to 2026 current

## Major changes

### 1986: Fiduciary duty language added to former Civ. Code § 5125

Stats. 1986, ch. 1091 added fiduciary-duty language requiring spouses to act "in accordance with the general rules governing fiduciary relationships which control the actions of persons having relationships of personal confidence as specified in Section 5103" (later Fam. Code § 721). The explicit full-disclosure obligation — requiring a spouse to disclose "all material facts and information regarding the existence, characterization, and valuation of all assets in which the community has or may have an interest and debts for which the community is or may be liable" — was strengthened further in 1991.

### 1991: Explicit full-disclosure obligation

Stats. 1991, ch. 1026 added the current subdivision (e) language requiring each spouse to make "full disclosure" of all material facts and provide "equal access to all information, records, and books" about community assets and debts. This is the language that is now in force in Fam. Code § 1100(e).

### 1992-1994: Recodification from Civil Code to Family Code

Stats. 1992, ch. 162 (effective 1994-01-01). Civ. Code § 5125 was moved to Fam. Code § 1100. The substantive language was preserved with minor technical modernization. The 1986/1991 fiduciary-duty amendments were carried forward in full.

### 1993-2001: Technical amendments

Several minor amendments to the business-management subdivision (d) and to cross-reference numbering. No substantive change to the core rules.

## What has NOT changed since 1991

The critical fiduciary-duty language in subdivision (e) has been textually stable for 35 years. The core (a)-(d) management rules have been stable since the 1975 original, with only the 1992 recodification's technical modernization affecting the surface text.

## Practical effect

A modern plaintiff suing under Fam. Code § 1100(e) for breach of spousal fiduciary duty has the benefit of:
- 35 years of stable statutory text
- 35 years of accumulated case law interpreting the full-disclosure obligation
- Paired enforcement under Fam. Code § 1101 (which provides the 50%/100% remedy structure and was added in the same 1991 legislation)
- The general § 721 fiduciary duty as an interpretive anchor

The "undivided community property" window — the period between separation and judicial division — is when these claims are most commonly litigated. A failure to finalize dissolution with proper property division leaves the § 1100(e) fiduciary duty continuously in force until division occurs. This is the critical timing rule for the Honeysuckle facts.
