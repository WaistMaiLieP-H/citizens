# Fam. Code § 1102 — Community Real Property Consent Requirement

**Popular name:** Community real property dual-consent rule
**Location:** Family Code, Division 4, Part 4, Chapter 2 (Community Property)
**Companion sections:** §§ 760 (community property presumption), 761 (revocable trust exception), 1100 (community personal property), 1101 (remedy), 1103 (mental incapacity exception), 2033 (attorney's fee lien exception)

## What § 1102 does

Section 1102 is the **dual-consent rule** for community real property. It is the most important statute in California real estate law when community property is involved. The core rule:

**Both spouses must join in executing any instrument that leases community real property for more than one year, sells it, conveys it, or encumbers it.**

This is a mandatory requirement. Unlike § 1100 (community personal property, where either spouse has management authority subject to specific consent requirements), § 1102 requires **both spouses** to sign every meaningful real estate instrument involving community real property.

## The bona fide purchaser exception (subd. (c))

Subdivision (c) creates a critical exception: if the non-record-title spouse's name is not on the title, and a purchaser/lender/lessee acted in good faith without knowledge of the marriage, the sole-spouse instrument is **presumed valid**. This protects third parties who had no reason to know community property was involved.

The practical consequence: a spouse who wants to protect their community property interest must be on the record title. If the other spouse holds sole record title, a bona fide purchaser dealing with that spouse can rely on the title appearing in that spouse's name and is protected.

## The one-year SOL for avoidance (subd. (d))

Subdivision (d) imposes a strict **one-year statute of limitations** from the date of recording to challenge a sole-spouse instrument executed on property held in that spouse's record title alone. This is much shorter than the general fraud SOL (3 years under CCP § 338(d)) or the § 1101 fiduciary breach SOL (3 years from actual knowledge).

**The one-year rule matters for Honeysuckle:** If the Honeysuckle transaction was executed solely by one spouse with the other spouse not on record title, the one-year clock ran from the date the deed was recorded. This could foreclose § 1102 avoidance even if other fraud claims (§ 1572, § 1101, § 1102.4) remain viable.

Critical exception: the one-year rule applies to "an action to avoid an instrument" — it limits the remedy of avoidance (rescission of the instrument), not the remedy of damages. A plaintiff can still sue for damages under other theories (§ 1572 tort fraud, § 1101 fiduciary breach remedy, § 1102.4 TDS strict liability) after the one-year avoidance window has closed.

## Subd. (b) — interspousal exception

Subdivision (b) exempts transactions between spouses (husband selling to wife, or vice versa). The rationale: § 1102's protective purpose is to prevent one spouse from disposing of community property without the other's knowledge; interspousal transactions presumptively have both spouses' knowledge.

## Relevance to the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction

Section 1102 is the most direct statute governing the Honeysuckle sale. Critical factual inquiries:

1. **Was the property community property?** — Apply Fam. § 760 presumption (acquired during marriage = community). If the steward was married to Christina at the time of acquisition, presumption attaches.

2. **Was title held in both names or one?** — Look at the actual deed. If held in one name alone, the bona fide purchaser exception in subd. (c) may apply; if held in both names, both signatures were required.

3. **Did both spouses sign the sale documents?** — If not, and title was held in both names, § 1102 is violated per se. If title was held in one name and the buyer acted in good faith without knowledge of the marriage, the sale may be presumed valid under subd. (c).

4. **When was the deed recorded?** — The one-year SOL runs from recording. If more than one year has passed, the avoidance remedy is foreclosed but damages remedies remain.

5. **Did the 19 unsigned documents relate to § 1102 requirements?** — If any of the unsigned documents required both-spouse signatures under § 1102, those signatures were missing, creating per se § 1102 violation.

## Interaction with § 1100

Section 1100 governs community personal property (management by either spouse with specific written consent requirements for gifts, family dwelling, and business dispositions). Section 1102 governs community REAL property (requiring both spouses' execution for leases over 1 year, sales, conveyances, and encumbrances).

For the Honeysuckle transaction (a fee simple house sale), § 1102 is the operative rule. Furnishings, personal property, and household effects within the home are governed by § 1100. The sale of the house itself is a § 1102 transaction.
