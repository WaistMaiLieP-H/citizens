# Amendment Context — Equal Management for Community Real Property (1975)

## What changed

Stats. 1975, ch. 1272 (SB 364) replaced "husband manages, wife joins" with "both spouses must join." The new Civil Code § 5127 established:

1. **Equal management rights** — "Either spouse has the management and control of the community real property." Either spouse can manage; neither has a superior position.

2. **Mandatory joint execution** — For any sale, lease exceeding one year, conveyance, or encumbrance of community real property, BOTH spouses must execute. There is no "primary" signer.

3. **Symmetrical good-faith purchaser rule** — The good-faith buyer protection that previously protected only buyers from husbands acting alone was expanded: "the sole lease, contract, mortgage, or deed of either spouse" to a good-faith purchaser without knowledge of the marriage relation is valid. Now protects buyers from either spouse acting alone.

## The key phrase: "both spouses... are required to join"

This is the operative provision. It means:
- The instrument is VOID (not merely voidable) without both signatures
- There is no waiver by conduct — one spouse's failure to object does not cure the missing signature
- The one-year SOL under current § 1102(d) applies to instruments affecting record-title property, but that limitation does not apply to actions against the signing spouse for breach of fiduciary duty

## Why § 1102's requirement was not sufficient in the Honeysuckle case

The "both spouses must join" requirement was satisfied on paper at Honeysuckle — signatures appeared on instruments. The § 1102 violation is not about missing signatures but about what constitutes genuine informed joint execution:
- Were the 19 unsigned documents reviewed before signing?
- Was the net-proceeds calculation disclosed?
- Was the sale conducted in a manner consistent with joint management?

§ 1102 is one layer of the claim. The § 721 fiduciary duty, § 1101 remedy, and § 1572 fraud claims provide the additional layers.
