# Amendment Context — Family Code Recodification (1994)

## What changed

The 1994 recodification brought Civil Code § 5127 into the Family Code as § 1102 with several structural clarifications:

1. **Explicit exception for spouse-to-spouse transactions** (§ 1102(b)) — A sale or transfer between spouses does not require both to join, since one party IS both sides of the transaction.

2. **Subdivision (c) explicitly separates** pre-January 1, 1975 and post-January 1, 1975 good-faith purchaser rules, clarifying the transitional boundary.

3. **One-year SOL** (§ 1102(d)) — codified the limitations period for actions to void instruments affecting record-title property executed by one spouse alone. This was a practical protection for title certainty.

4. **Attorney's fees carve-out** (§ 1102(e)) — New. Either spouse can unilaterally encumber their interest in community real property to pay reasonable attorney's fees in dissolution, nullity, or legal separation proceedings. This prevents the wealthier spouse from using § 1102's joint-execution requirement to cut off the other's access to legal representation.

## Why § 1102(e) matters

§ 1102(e) was a significant policy addition. Without it, the "both spouses must join" rule could be weaponized: a spouse controlling the community assets could refuse to join any encumbrance, leaving the other spouse unable to finance legal representation. The (e) carve-out ensures access to justice cannot be blocked by the very property control the dissolution proceeding is meant to adjudicate.

## No substantive change to the core rule

The core holding — both spouses must jointly execute any instrument by which community real property is sold, conveyed, or encumbered — was not changed by the recodification.
