DOCTRINAL PROPOSITION — FAM. CODE § 1102 AND DISSOLUTION REMEDIES:

Modern California Court of Appeal decisions have addressed several recurring issues in Fam. Code § 1102 litigation:

INTERACTION WITH DISSOLUTION PROPERTY DIVISION:
When a § 1102 violation occurs pre-dissolution, the remedy can be pursued either as a stand-alone § 1101 action (which includes § 1102 violations within its scope) or as part of the dissolution property division. The choice of forum affects strategy and timing.

THIRD-PARTY PURCHASER PROTECTION:
Third parties who received community real property through a unilateral sole-spouse transaction — but who would not qualify under the bona fide purchaser exception — are subject to being ordered to return the property or pay equivalent value. Courts consider: the third party's knowledge of the marriage, their good faith, and equitable considerations.

REMEDIES BEYOND AVOIDANCE:
Even when the one-year avoidance SOL has closed, plaintiffs can pursue damages remedies under:
- § 1101(g): 50% of asset value + attorney's fees
- § 1101(h): 100% for fraudulent breach + attorney's fees
These are not foreclosed by the one-year avoidance SOL.

CONSTRUCTIVE TRUST AS EQUITABLE REMEDY:
When the defendant spouse received proceeds from a § 1102 violation and those proceeds can be traced, a constructive trust on the proceeds is available as an equitable remedy. Particularly valuable when the defendant has transferred or dissipated the direct asset.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

For the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction:

1. **One-year avoidance SOL has closed** (likely, for a 2023 sale): Avoidance under § 1102(d) is unavailable. However, damages remedies remain fully available.

2. **Damages under § 1101(g) or (h)**: 50% or 100% of the community asset value, calculated at the highest of (a) breach date, (b) sale date, or (c) award date. For a $605K house: 100% remedy = $605K plus attorney's fees.

3. **Constructive trust on traceable proceeds**: If any of the $496K unaccounted proceeds can be traced to identifiable accounts (crypto wallets, bank accounts, specific real property purchases), a constructive trust can be imposed on those funds.

4. **Attorney's fees under § 1101(g)**: Mandatory upon breach finding, in addition to the § 1101(h) remedy.

5. **Parallel tracks**: The § 1102 and § 1101 remedies run parallel to the tort fraud claims against third parties under §§ 1572, 1709-1710, 3294. Both tracks should be pursued simultaneously.
