CARLETON v. TORTOSA
14 Cal.App.4th 745 (1993)
California Court of Appeal, Third Appellate District
Decided: 1993

---

FACTS:

A real estate broker represented a buyer in a transaction while having an undisclosed financial interest in the seller entity. The buyer alleged the broker concealed a material conflict of interest, failed to disclose the dual financial relationship, and collected compensation from both sides of the transaction without the buyer's informed written consent.

---

HOLDING:

FIDUCIARY DUTY AND § 10176 VIOLATIONS:
"A real estate broker stands in a fiduciary relationship with the client. The broker owes the highest duty of good faith, loyalty, and disclosure. Non-disclosure of a conflict of interest is per se actionable."

A broker who fails to disclose a financial interest on the other side of the transaction — or who collects compensation from both sides without informed written consent — violates both § 10176(d) (undisclosed dual agency) and § 10176(g) (secret profit).

ELEMENTS OF THE CIVIL ENFORCEMENT FRAMEWORK:
1. The client need not prove actual financial harm from the conflict to establish liability — the fiduciary breach itself supports rescission and fee disgorgement
2. BPC § 10176 violations support civil liability independent of contract claims
3. A broker who violates § 10176 by taking a secret profit forfeits ALL right to compensation for the transaction — not just the undisclosed portion

THE CARLETON CIVIL ENFORCEMENT TABLE:

| Violation Type | § 10176 Subdivision | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Misrepresentation to principal | (a) | Rescission + damages |
| Deceptive material inducement | (b) | Same |
| Secret compensation | (g) | Disgorgement; license discipline |
| Commingling funds | (h) | Rescission + damages |
| Undisclosed dual agency | (d) | Full compensation forfeiture |

DISGORGEMENT WITHOUT PROOF OF HARM:
"A broker who violates the fiduciary duty by concealing a conflict need not have caused provable economic harm — the breach itself entitles the client to disgorgement and rescission."

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

For the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction, the § 10176 / Carleton framework applies directly:

1. **Undisclosed relationships**: If the listing broker or buyer's broker had undisclosed relationships to the seller entity or to any parties who benefited from the transaction, the $465K equity disappearance and the $72K crypto diversion both warrant broker investigation under this standard.

2. **19 unsigned documents**: If the broker allowed the transaction to close without proper completion of required disclosures, this is a § 10176(a)/(b) violation.

3. **Secret compensation**: Any secret or undisclosed compensation arrangement between the broker and any related party (escrow, title, other participants) triggers § 10176(g) and the disgorgement remedy.

4. **Commission forfeiture**: If the broker representing Christina received any benefit from the equity diversion, the broker's entire commission is forfeitable under Carleton.
