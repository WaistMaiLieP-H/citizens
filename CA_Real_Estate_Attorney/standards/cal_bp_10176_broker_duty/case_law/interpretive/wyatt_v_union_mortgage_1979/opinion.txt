WYATT v. UNION MORTGAGE CO.
24 Cal.3d 773 (1979)
California Supreme Court
Decided: August 1979

---

FACTS:

The Wyatts sought a mortgage loan through Union Mortgage Co., a licensed real estate broker acting as mortgage loan broker. Union Mortgage assisted in arranging the loan but failed to disclose material information about the loan's structure, the lender's identity, and compensation arrangements that affected the borrowers' interests. The Wyatts sued, alleging breach of fiduciary duty and statutory violations of BPC §§ 10176 and 10177.

---

HOLDING:

THE FIDUCIARY DUTY IN MORTGAGE BROKERAGE:
"A real estate broker acting as a mortgage loan broker owes a fiduciary duty to the borrower. When the broker represents the borrower in seeking financing, the broker is the borrower's agent and owes the full range of common-law fiduciary duties — loyalty, disclosure, due care, accounting."

THE THREE FOUNDATIONAL WYATT PROPOSITIONS:

1. **Brokers owe fiduciary duties to their principals**: This is the starting point for every broker-liability analysis in California real estate law.

2. **Disclosure is affirmative, not passive**: "The fiduciary duty includes affirmative disclosure of material facts. The broker cannot wait to be asked. Material facts about the loan terms, the lender's identity, fees and commissions, and any potential conflicts of interest must be disclosed proactively."

3. **Undisclosed compensation is per se wrongful**: "A broker who accepts compensation from any source without disclosing it to the principal has breached the duty of loyalty. The amount matters, the source matters, and non-disclosure is always wrongful." — Consistent with BPC § 10176(g).

REMEDIES:
"Damages include actual losses and disgorgement of undisclosed compensation. The remedy for breach includes not only the plaintiff's actual damages but also disgorgement of any undisclosed compensation the broker received — effectively restoring the plaintiff to a position as if the breach had not occurred."

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Wyatt is the California Supreme Court's foundational statement on broker fiduciary duty in lending and brokerage contexts. For the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction:

1. **Identify all broker principals**: Determine who was the broker and who was the broker's principal in the 2023 transaction.

2. **Apply the affirmative disclosure rule**: What material facts should have been disclosed? Apply the affirmative disclosure rule to each transaction participant — not just what was asked, but what should have been volunteered.

3. **Trace all compensation**: Was any compensation received from any source? Check for undisclosed commissions, referral fees, kickbacks, or any other payment to or from the broker.

4. **§ 10176(g) — "full amount" required**: Per § 10176(g), the "full amount" of compensation from any source must be disclosed. Any departure from full disclosure is a breach of the fiduciary duty and a statutory violation.

5. **Disgorgement as additional remedy**: If undisclosed compensation is identified, Wyatt supports disgorgement of that compensation in addition to actual damages — a significant recovery tool.
