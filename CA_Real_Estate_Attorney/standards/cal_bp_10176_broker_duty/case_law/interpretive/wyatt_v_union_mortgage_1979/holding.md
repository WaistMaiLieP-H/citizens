# Wyatt v. Union Mortgage Co. (1979) 24 Cal.3d 773

**Citation:** Wyatt v. Union Mortgage Co. (1979) 24 Cal.3d 773, 157 Cal.Rptr. 392, 598 P.2d 45
**Decided:** August 1979
**Court:** Supreme Court of California
**Disposition:** Affirmed judgment for plaintiffs; established broker fiduciary duty to principals in mortgage loan transactions.
**Statutes construed:** Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 10176, 10177; common-law broker fiduciary duty

## Facts

The Wyatts sought a mortgage loan through Union Mortgage Co., a licensed real estate broker acting as mortgage loan broker. Union Mortgage assisted in arranging the loan but failed to disclose material information about the loan's structure, the lender's identity, and compensation arrangements that affected the borrowers' interests. The Wyatts later sued, alleging breach of fiduciary duty and statutory violations.

## Holding

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **A real estate broker acting as a mortgage loan broker owes a fiduciary duty to the borrower.** When the broker represents the borrower in seeking financing, the broker is the borrower's agent and owes the full range of common-law fiduciary duties — loyalty, disclosure, due care, accounting.

2. **The fiduciary duty includes affirmative disclosure of material facts.** The broker cannot wait to be asked. Material facts about the loan terms, the lender's identity, fees and commissions, and any potential conflicts of interest must be disclosed proactively.

3. **Secret or undisclosed compensation is a per se breach of fiduciary duty.** Consistent with BPC § 10176(g), a broker who accepts compensation from any source without disclosing it to the principal has breached the duty of loyalty. The amount matters, the source matters, and non-disclosure is always wrongful.

4. **Damages include actual losses and disgorgement of undisclosed compensation.** The remedy for breach includes not only the plaintiff's actual damages but also disgorgement of any undisclosed compensation the broker received — effectively restoring the plaintiff to a position as if the breach had not occurred.

## Why this is load-bearing

*Wyatt* is the California Supreme Court's foundational statement on broker fiduciary duty in lending and brokerage contexts. It establishes three propositions that apply across real estate broker cases:

1. **Brokers owe fiduciary duties to their principals** — this is the starting point for every broker-liability analysis
2. **Disclosure is affirmative** — brokers must volunteer material information, not wait to be asked
3. **Undisclosed compensation is per se wrongful** — the duty of loyalty requires full disclosure of all compensation, from all sources

These propositions are codified in part in § 10176 (particularly subd. (g) on secret compensation) and underlie the Real Estate Commissioner's disciplinary jurisprudence.

## Relevance to the 2958 Honeysuckle transaction

For Honeysuckle, *Wyatt* is relevant if any broker, mortgage broker, loan officer, or similar agent was involved in the 2023 transaction. The analysis:

1. **Who was the broker and who was the broker's principal?** — determine the agency relationships
2. **What material facts should have been disclosed?** — apply the affirmative disclosure rule
3. **Was any compensation received from any source?** — check for undisclosed commissions, referral fees, kickbacks, or any other payment
4. **Was the full scope of compensation disclosed?** — per § 10176(g), the "full amount" of compensation must be disclosed

If undisclosed compensation is identified, *Wyatt* supports disgorgement of that compensation in addition to actual damages. This can be a significant recovery.

## Authority status

Binding California Supreme Court precedent. Still controlling as of 2026-04-11. Cited as the foundational broker fiduciary duty case in California real estate law.
