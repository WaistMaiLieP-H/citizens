CALIFORNIA BUSINESS AND PROFESSIONS CODE § 10176
Real Estate License Disciplinary Grounds — Origin (1943)

SOURCE: Real Estate Dealers License Act, Stats. 1943 / California Real Estate Act
Predecessor: California Real Estate Act (1917), as substantially amended and reorganized
REQUIRES_PRIMARY_SOURCE_VERIFICATION for exact 1943 text.

--- Summary of 1943/1950s BPC § 10176 predecessor text ---
The commissioner may investigate and may temporarily suspend or permanently revoke a real estate license where the licensee has been guilty of:

(a) Making any substantial misrepresentation.
(b) Making any false promises of a character likely to influence, persuade, or induce.
(c) A continued and flagrant course of misrepresentation or making of false promises through licensees.
(d) Acting for more than one party in a transaction without the knowledge or consent of all parties thereto.
(e) Commingling with his or her own money or property the money or other property of others which is received and held by him or her.

NOTE: The original disciplinary grounds (a)–(e) formed the core of the broker-conduct statute. Later amendments added subdivisions (f) through (m) covering: exclusive agreements without termination dates (f), undisclosed compensation (g), option-to-purchase abuse (h), catchall fraud/dishonesty (i), unauthorized solicitation of purchase agreements (j), failure to disburse committed mortgage funds (k), intentional closing delay (l), and cross-licensing law violations (m).
