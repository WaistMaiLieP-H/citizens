BANKHEAD v. ARVINMERITOR, INC.
205 Cal.App.4th 68 (2012)
California Court of Appeal, First Appellate District, Division Four
Decided: April 3, 2012

---

FACTS:

Plaintiff Gordon Bankhead contracted mesothelioma from occupational exposure to asbestos-containing brake linings manufactured by ArvinMeritor (formerly Rockwell International). He obtained a compensatory award and a punitive award. The defendant challenged the punitive award under the federal constitutional proportionality rules from BMW of North America v. Gore (1996) 517 U.S. 559 and State Farm Mutual Auto. Ins. Co. v. Campbell (2003) 538 U.S. 408, which hold that punitive-to-compensatory ratios greater than 9:1 or 10:1 are presumptively unconstitutional absent exceptional facts.

---

HOLDING:

THE BANKHEAD FRAMEWORK — FEDERAL AND CALIFORNIA LIMITS SYNTHESIZED:

1. **The three Gore guideposts are the starting point**:
   (1) Degree of reprehensibility of defendant's conduct
   (2) Disparity between actual or potential harm and the punitive award (the ratio)
   (3) Difference between the punitive award and civil penalties authorized in comparable cases

2. **Reprehensibility is the most important guidepost.** Factors that increase reprehensibility:
   - Health and safety violations (not merely economic harm)
   - Vulnerable victims
   - Repeated misconduct over time (not a single incident)
   - Intentional malice or deceit (rather than accidental harm)
   - Financial motive
   - Large number of victims

3. **Ratios above 9:1 are permitted for extraordinary reprehensibility.** The 9:1 ratio cap is not absolute. In cases of extreme reprehensibility, repeated misconduct, or where the compensatory award is small but the harm large, higher ratios can be upheld.

4. **Defendant's wealth remains relevant in California.** Although federal due process does not require a wealth-based adjustment, California law (Adams v. Murakami) does — the financial-condition evidence requirement is independently required.

5. **Potential harm, not just actual harm, can anchor the ratio.** Where a defendant's conduct caused $1 actual harm to a specific plaintiff but had the potential to cause millions in harm, the ratio calculation can reference the potential harm.

---

THE BANKHEAD REPREHENSIBILITY PROFILE:

Courts consider multiple reprehensibility factors; each adds to the case for a higher ratio:

| Factor | Present? | Effect on Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Intentional fraud (not accidental) | Yes | Maximum |
| Multi-year pattern (not single incident) | Yes | Elevated |
| Vulnerable victim | Fact-dependent | Elevated if proven |
| Financial motive | Yes | Maximum |
| Institutional sophistication | Yes | Elevated |

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

The 2958 Honeysuckle transaction presents a favorable reprehensibility profile under Bankhead:

- **Intentional fraud** (not accidental): Maximum reprehensibility factor
- **Multi-year pattern** (not single incident): Elevates reprehensibility
- **Vulnerable victim** (if conservatorship is proven): Elevates reprehensibility
- **Financial motive** (dispossession for financial gain): Maximum reprehensibility factor
- **Institutional sophistication** (multi-party coordinated transaction): Elevates reprehensibility

**Damages calculation**:
- If compensatory damages = $500K (out-of-pocket equity loss), a 9:1 ratio = $4.5M punitive
- Higher ratios (15:1 or 20:1) can be justified on this reprehensibility profile = $7.5M-$10M punitive
- Alternative: anchor to **potential harm** — the full pattern of dispossession (house + crypto + surgery + Treasury + pension) totals several million dollars, permitting a larger award without exceeding ratio guidelines

**Bankhead is the authority** when defendants argue the punitive award is disproportionate. It provides the analytical framework for justifying an above-9:1 ratio based on extraordinary reprehensibility.
