# § 3294 — Diff from 1980 version to 1987 amendment

## Changes in subdivision (a)

**Before (1980):** "...guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice, express or implied..."
**After (1987):** "...guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice..."
**Change:** Deleted "express or implied" — the phrase became redundant with the new statutory definitions in subdivision (c).

## Unchanged in subdivision (b)

Subdivision (b) — the employer vicarious liability rule — was preserved verbatim from the 1980 text. No changes.

## New in subdivision (c)

Three new definitions. The critical drafting choice: "despicable" was added to malice (second prong only) and oppression, but NOT to fraud.

- **Malice (c)(1):** Intent-to-injure prong (no despicable) OR willful-conscious-disregard prong (with despicable).
- **Oppression (c)(2):** Despicable conduct causing cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of rights.
- **Fraud (c)(3):** Intentional misrepresentation/deceit/concealment with intent to deprive of property or legal rights. NO despicable requirement.

## New in subdivision (e)

Transitional provision confirming that the 1987 changes apply to cases in which trial had not begun by January 1, 1988. Effective date marker.

## NOT ADDED in 1987

- Subdivision (d) — the homicide-based wrongful death provision — was NOT part of the 1987 tort reform amendment. It was added separately in 1988 by AB 3158 (Stats. 1988, ch. 938) after the *Allen v. Toledo* controversy.

## Practical effect

The 1987 amendment is the dividing line for modern California punitive practice:

- Pre-1988: Common-law jury instruction on malice/oppression/fraud; plaintiff-friendly.
- Post-1988: Statutory definitions with despicable-conduct threshold for malice/oppression; plaintiff must prove more; fraud path largely unchanged.

*College Hospital v. Superior Court* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 704 is the leading Supreme Court case interpreting the 1987 amendment's "despicable" standard — it defines despicable conduct as "so vile, base, contemptible, miserable, wretched or loathsome that it would be looked down upon and despised by ordinary decent people." This high bar is why modern malice/oppression claims are harder to sustain than pre-1988.
