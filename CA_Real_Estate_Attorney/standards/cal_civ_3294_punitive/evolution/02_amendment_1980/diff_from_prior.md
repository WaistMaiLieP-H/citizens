# § 3294 — Diff from 1872 origin to 1980 amendment

## Structural changes

- 1872: Single unnumbered paragraph.
- 1980: Split into subdivisions (a) and (b). Subdivision (a) contains the original 1872 text verbatim. Subdivision (b) is new — the employer vicarious liability restriction.

## Textual additions

NEW — subdivision (b) (quoted in 02_amendment_1980/text.txt):

> An employer shall not be liable for damages pursuant to subdivision (a), based upon acts of an employee of the employer, unless the employer had advance knowledge of the unfitness of the employee and employed him or her with a conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others or authorized or ratified the wrongful conduct for which the damages are awarded or was personally guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice. With respect to a corporate employer, the advance knowledge and conscious disregard, authorization, ratification or act of oppression, fraud, or malice must be on the part of an officer, director, or managing agent of the corporation.

## Textual deletions

NONE. The original 1872 language was preserved as subdivision (a) without modification.

## Practical effect

Before 1981: Vicarious punitive liability governed by general respondeat superior and California common-law agency principles.

After 1981: Four-step proof required for punitive damages against a corporate employer — predicate wrong + managing-agent OR advance-knowledge OR authorization/ratification + clear-and-convincing-evidence standard on all elements.

## Unchanged

The three predicate wrongs (oppression, fraud, malice) remained defined only by common law until the 1987 amendment. The "express or implied" phrase for malice was retained in 1980 and deleted in 1987.
