# § 3294 — Diff from 1987 amendment to 2026 current

## Single change: subdivision (d) added in 1988

Stats. 1988, ch. 938, § 1 (effective January 1, 1989). Added a new subdivision (d) reading:

> Damages may be recovered pursuant to this section in an action pursuant to Chapter 4 (commencing with Section 377.10) of Title 3 of Part 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure based upon a death which resulted from a homicide for which the defendant has been convicted of a felony, whether or not the decedent died instantly or survived the fatal injury for some period of time. The procedures for joinder and consolidation contained in Section 377.62 of the Code of Civil Procedure shall apply to prevent multiple recoveries of punitive or exemplary damages based upon the same wrongful act.

## Why

Before 1989, punitive damages were not available in wrongful death / survival actions because Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 377.34 (formerly § 573) barred them. This resulted in the anomaly that a defendant who killed the plaintiff paid LESS than a defendant who merely injured the plaintiff — because killing cut off the punitive remedy entirely. The 1988 amendment carved out a narrow exception: when the death was caused by a homicide for which the defendant has a felony conviction, punitives may be sought by the decedent's survivors/estate.

The conviction requirement is critical. Without a felony homicide conviction, punitives remain barred in wrongful death. *Rufo v. Simpson* (2001) 86 Cal.App.4th 573 (O.J. Simpson civil judgment) applied this rule: because Simpson had been acquitted of the homicide, § 3294(d) did not apply, and the $25 million punitive award had to be justified through alternative doctrinal routes.

## Changes after 1988

**None.** From 1988-01-01 through 2026-04-10, § 3294 has been textually stable. 38 years. No amendments.

## Cumulative diff from 1872 origin to 2026 current

- 1872 → 1980: Added subdivision (b) employer vicarious liability rule.
- 1980 → 1987: Added subdivision (c) statutory definitions, subdivision (e) effective date; deleted "express or implied" from (a).
- 1987 → 1988: Added subdivision (d) homicide-based wrongful death exception.
- 1988 → 2026: Unchanged.

## Plaintiff's doctrinal map

To prevail on a § 3294 claim in 2026, a plaintiff must:
1. Have a tort (not contract) cause of action
2. Prove by clear and convincing evidence one of: oppression (despicable), fraud (intent to deprive), or malice (intent to injure OR despicable + conscious disregard)
3. If defendant is a corporate employer, tie the wrong to an officer/director/managing agent OR prove advance knowledge + disregard OR prove authorization/ratification
4. At the punitive phase of trial, introduce evidence of the defendant's financial condition
5. Accept the BMW/State Farm constitutional ratio cap (typically 9:1 or less)
