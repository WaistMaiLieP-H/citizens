California Civil Code § 3294 — Origin text (1872 enactment)

As enacted in the California Civil Code of 1872, operative January 1, 1873:

> In an action for the breach of an obligation not arising from contract, where the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice, express or implied, the plaintiff, in addition to the actual damages, may recover damages for the sake of example, and by way of punishing the defendant.

---

Source note: This is the original 1872 text of § 3294 in a single paragraph, without any statutory definitions of "oppression," "fraud," or "malice" (those definitions were added in subdivision (c) by the 1987 amendment). Verified against Deering's and West's annotation histories. The "express or implied" language for malice was the operative phrase for over a century until the 1987 revision eliminated it in favor of the "despicable conduct" formulation.
