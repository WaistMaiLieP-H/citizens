# McCaffrey Group, Inc. v. Superior Court (2005) 129 Cal.App.4th 1

**Citation:** McCaffrey Group, Inc. v. Superior Court (2005) 129 Cal.App.4th 1, 27 Cal.Rptr.3d 876
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Fifth Appellate District
**Decided:** 2005
**Statutes construed:** Civil Code § 1213 — constructive notice; recording acts; bona fide purchaser for value without notice

## Facts

A dispute arose concerning priority between competing claims to real property where a prior unrecorded interest was asserted against a subsequent purchaser who had recorded a deed. The central question was whether the subsequent purchaser qualified as a "bona fide purchaser for value" under the recording act and § 1213 constructive notice framework, and whether specific facts — including the subsequent purchaser's relationships and knowledge — defeated the bona fide purchaser defense.

## Holding

The Court of Appeal addressed the bona fide purchaser doctrine and the constructive notice framework under § 1213:

1. **Recording provides constructive notice to all subsequent purchasers.** Under Civil Code § 1213, a recorded instrument is constructive notice of its contents to all persons thereafter claiming any interest in the property. A subsequent purchaser who fails to examine the public record takes subject to all recorded interests.

2. **Bona fide purchaser for value without notice ("BFP")** is the protected class under California's race-notice recording statute. To qualify as a BFP, the subsequent purchaser must:
   - Pay valuable consideration for the property
   - Take title without actual, constructive, or inquiry notice of the prior unrecorded interest
   - Record their own interest before the prior claimant records

3. **Inquiry notice can defeat BFP status.** If a subsequent purchaser has knowledge of facts sufficient to put a reasonable person on inquiry — even if that inquiry has not yet been made — the purchaser is charged with constructive notice of whatever the inquiry would have revealed. This is the "inquiry notice" doctrine.

4. **A purchaser with actual knowledge of ongoing litigation or competing claims is NOT a BFP.** Knowledge of a dispute, pending litigation, or lis pendens defeats the BFP defense even if the prior claimant's interest was not recorded.

5. **Transactions among related parties receive heightened scrutiny.** When a transfer occurs between parties with a pre-existing relationship (business, family, or financial), courts examine whether the consideration was real and whether the "purchaser" actually lacked notice of competing claims. A nominal or inadequate consideration defeats the "value" prong of the BFP test.

## The constructive notice chain

Under § 1213 as interpreted in McCaffrey:

```
Instrument recorded → constructive notice to ALL subsequent claimants
Prior unrecorded interest → good only against the grantor and those with actual/inquiry notice
BFP defense → requires: (1) value, (2) no actual notice, (3) no constructive notice, (4) no inquiry notice, (5) records first
```

**Chain of title rule:** Every purchaser is charged with notice of all instruments in the recorded chain of title, including instruments recorded by predecessors in interest. A purchaser must trace the chain back to the original grant.

## Application to the Honeysuckle matter

At 2958 Honeysuckle, the § 1213 / McCaffrey framework applies to:

1. **The recorded deed history** — who held title, when, and in what form. Any instrument in the chain of title (including documents reflecting community property ownership, prior deeds of trust, or recorded disputes) is constructive notice to Christina and to any subsequent purchaser
2. **The 19 unsigned or defective documents** — documents that were not properly executed may not constitute valid recorded instruments; an instrument that was not properly acknowledged or executed may be void or voidable and may not impart constructive notice
3. **Christina's buyer as potential non-BFP** — if the buyer knew of the community property dispute, knew of Michael's interest, or had relationships with the seller that suggest constructive notice, the BFP defense fails
4. **Inquiry notice from the litigation history** — if any family court proceedings involving the property were discoverable, a purchaser on inquiry notice would have found them
5. **Adequacy of consideration** — if the $605K sale price was below market or if the proceeds were diverted such that no true "value" was paid for the property interest Michael held, the "value" prong of the BFP test may be challenged

## Authority status

Published California Court of Appeal decision (2005). Establishes the BFP framework, inquiry notice doctrine, heightened scrutiny for related-party transactions, and the chain of title constructive notice rule under § 1213.
