# Triple A Management Co. v. Frisone, 69 Cal. App. 4th 520 (1999)

## The Case

A dispute arose over conflicting interests in real property. One party had an unrecorded interest; the other had a recorded interest. The question was whether the recording acts (Civ. Code §1213 et seq.) protected the recorded interest against the unrecorded one.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **California's recording acts protect bona fide purchasers for value who record first.** Under the race-notice system, a subsequent purchaser who (a) pays value, (b) acts in good faith without notice of the prior interest, and (c) records first, prevails over an earlier unrecorded interest.

2. **"Notice" includes actual knowledge, constructive notice from recorded documents, and inquiry notice from facts that would put a reasonable person on notice.** A purchaser who ignores red flags cannot claim bona fide purchaser status.

3. **The recording system is designed to protect reliance on the public record.** Parties who search the record and find no competing interests are entitled to rely on that search — provided they are not on inquiry notice of unrecorded claims.

## The Rule

**Under California's race-notice recording system, a subsequent bona fide purchaser who records first prevails over an earlier unrecorded interest — but only if they had no actual, constructive, or inquiry notice of the prior interest.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's house sale: if there were unrecorded interests in the property (Christina's community property interest from an unfinalized dissolution, a conservator's authority over the estate, liens or encumbrances not in the public record), the recording acts govern who has priority. The 19 unsigned documents may represent interests that were never properly recorded — and the question of who benefited from the $356K gap may turn on what was recorded and what wasn't.
