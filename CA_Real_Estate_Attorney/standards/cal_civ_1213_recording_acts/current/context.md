# Context — Civil Code § 1213 Recording Acts and Constructive Notice

## The wound and the promise

**The wound:** Without a recording system, sellers could sell the same property to multiple buyers; lenders could take security in property already encumbered; fraud was endemic in real property transfers. Title to California land was functionally unknowable without a public record system.

**The promise:** Civil Code § 1213 promises that a person who diligently searches the public record will be protected against undisclosed prior interests. It also promises that those who fail to record their interests — or who take interests with knowledge of prior claims — will not be protected against those they failed to disclose to or had notice of.

## How § 1213 works in practice

### Layer 1: The recording trigger

When an instrument affecting real property (deed, deed of trust, easement, lis pendens, abstract of judgment, notice of default) is:
1. Properly executed and acknowledged
2. Presented to the county recorder
3. Stamped, assigned a document number, and indexed

...it becomes part of the public record. From that moment, § 1213 deems all subsequent claimants to have constructive notice of its contents.

### Layer 2: The three types of notice

| Notice Type | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Actual notice** | Direct personal knowledge | Defeats BFP status conclusively |
| **Constructive notice** | Recorded instrument in chain of title | Same effect as actual notice; charged to all who search or should search |
| **Inquiry notice** | Knowledge of facts sufficient to put a reasonable person on inquiry | Charged with whatever inquiry would have disclosed |

### Layer 3: The BFP doctrine

A **bona fide purchaser for value without notice** takes free of prior unrecorded interests if they:
1. Pay valuable consideration (not nominal or inadequate consideration)
2. Take without actual, constructive, or inquiry notice of the prior interest
3. Record their own deed or interest before the prior claimant records

**All three elements must be satisfied.** Failure of any one defeats BFP status.

**Related-party transactions:** Courts scrutinize transfers between spouses, family members, business partners, or other related parties. The "without notice" element is harder to establish when the parties have a pre-existing relationship that would ordinarily put a reasonable person on inquiry about the other party's claims.

### Layer 4: Chain of title

A purchaser is charged with constructive notice of all instruments appearing in the recorded chain of title — every prior conveyance, encumbrance, or instrument affecting the property from the original patent grant through the current transaction.

This creates the "chain of title" obligation: before purchasing, a buyer (or title company on the buyer's behalf) must trace every link in the chain of recorded instruments.

### Layer 5: Defective instruments

Not every recorded instrument imparts constructive notice:

- **Forged instruments:** A deed that bears a forged signature is void, not merely voidable. A forged deed passes no title and imparts no constructive notice. No bona fide purchaser can take good title through a forged deed.
- **Improperly acknowledged instruments:** An instrument that was not properly notarized or acknowledged may not be entitled to recordation; if recorded anyway, it may not impart constructive notice.
- **Misfiled/misindexed instruments:** An instrument recorded but not properly indexed in the grantor-grantee index may not impart constructive notice as to parties who search the index and do not find it.

## Application to the Honeysuckle matter — priority analysis

**The community property character of 2958 Honeysuckle** was established through the recorded deed at the time of acquisition. All subsequent claimants — including any buyer in the $605K sale — were on constructive notice that:

1. The property was community property owned jointly by Michael and Christina
2. Alienation required both spouses' joinder under Fam. Code § 1102
3. Any family court proceedings that generated recorded instruments (abstract of judgment, lis pendens) were in the public record and constituted constructive notice of the disputes

**The 19 unsigned documents** — if required instruments were not signed or were improperly executed, the chain of instruments in the Honeysuckle transaction may be defective. Defective instruments do not pass good title and may not impart constructive notice.

**The buyer's BFP status** requires examination:
- Was the buyer related to or connected with Christina?
- Did the buyer pay the full $605K, and where did the money go?
- Did the buyer have any relationship to any party who had knowledge of Michael's claims?
- Were there any recorded instruments (lis pendens, notice of claim) that would have put a reasonable buyer on inquiry?

## Bilateral analysis

**As complainant (Michael):**
- Michael's community property interest was in the recorded chain of title; constructive notice ran to all buyers
- Any buyer who had inquiry notice of Michael's claims — through family court records, recorded instruments, or known relationships — took subject to those claims
- A void transaction (if any document was forged) is wholly ineffective against Michael regardless of BFP defenses

**As respondent (if any party asserts BFP against Michael's claims):**
- The burden is on the party claiming BFP status to prove all three elements: value, no notice, and first recording
- Inadequate consideration, related-party relationships, or recorded competing instruments defeat the defense
