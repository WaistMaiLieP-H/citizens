# § 1572 — Diff from 1872 origin to 2026 current

**Textual changes:** NONE.

**Stability window:** 154 years (1872-03-21 to 2026-04-10), still in force.

**Significance:** California Civil Code § 1572 is one of the handful of sections in the 1872 Civil Code that has never been textually amended. Its case-law body has grown enormously — there are thousands of published opinions interpreting it — but the legislature has never touched the text. This places § 1572 in the same "stable origin" category as Civil Code § 1708 (general duty of care) and §§ 1709–1710 (deceit), which also survive in their 1872 form.

**Practical consequence for litigation:** When citing § 1572, counsel does NOT need to specify a version — the statute has only ever had one version. This is unusual in California practice and simplifies the cross-reference layer. It also means that 1880s and 1890s California Supreme Court opinions construing § 1572 remain authoritative as to the statute's plain meaning, and later opinions layer on application without competing with a changed text.

**Case-law revival patterns:** Though the text is unchanged, the doctrinal emphasis has shifted. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, California courts focused on subdivisions (1) and (2) (active misrepresentation) because tort remedies for suppression were still constrained. Since *Seeger v. Odell* (1941) 18 Cal.2d 409, courts have given full force to subdivision (3) (suppression), provided a duty to disclose exists. Since *Tenzer v. Superscope* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 18, subdivision (4) (promissory fraud) has become a heavily litigated theory, particularly in employment and contract-performance cases. The Honeysuckle facts sit squarely in the modern subdivision (3) and (4) pattern.

**No sunset, no rollback, no narrowing amendment.** The legislature has had 154 years to narrow § 1572 if it wished. It has not.
