California Civil Code § 1572 — Origin text (1872 enactment)

As enacted in the original California Civil Code, adopted by the Legislature on March 21, 1872, operative January 1, 1873. The Civil Code of 1872 was drafted by the Code Commissioners under statute authorizing a comprehensive codification (1870 Stats. ch. 516). The 1872 text of § 1572 is substantively identical to the current text and has never been textually amended since enactment — 154 years stable.

Original text:

> Actual fraud, within the meaning of this Chapter, consists in any of the following acts, committed by a party to the contract, or with his connivance, with intent to deceive another party thereto, or to induce him to enter into the contract:
>
> 1. The suggestion, as a fact, of that which is not true, by one who does not believe it to be true;
>
> 2. The positive assertion, in a manner not warranted by the information of the person making it, of that which is not true, though he believes it to be true;
>
> 3. The suppression of that which is true, by one having knowledge or belief of the fact;
>
> 4. A promise made without any intention of performing it; or,
>
> 5. Any other act fitted to deceive.

---

Source note: The Civil Code of California of 1872 (official published volume), § 1572. The code was based substantially on Field's Draft Civil Code of New York (David Dudley Field's 1865 draft), which was never enacted in New York but adopted in modified form by California. Field's § 1572 in turn traced to the English and continental civilian definitions of dolus, and to the 18th-19th century common-law treatises (Pothier, Story on Equity Jurisprudence § 187).
