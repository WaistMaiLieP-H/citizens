TENZER v. SUPERSCOPE, INC.
39 Cal.3d 18 (1985)
California Supreme Court
Decided: July 18, 1985

---

FACTS:

Leon Tenzer, a real estate broker, introduced Superscope to a property owner for purposes of a potential sale. Before the introduction, Tenzer and Superscope allegedly reached an oral agreement that if a sale closed, Superscope would pay Tenzer a finder's fee. Superscope bought the property. Superscope then refused to pay, claiming the oral agreement was unenforceable under the broker commission statute of frauds (Civ. Code § 1624).

Tenzer sued for (a) breach of contract, (b) promissory fraud, and (c) quantum meruit. The trial court granted summary judgment for Superscope on all counts — the contract claim because of the statute of frauds, and the fraud claim on the theory that you cannot bootstrap a statute-of-frauds-barred contract into a fraud claim merely by alleging the defendant never intended to perform.

---

HOLDING:

FOUR FOUNDATIONAL DOCTRINAL POINTS ON PROMISSORY FRAUD:

1. **A promissory fraud claim survives the statute of frauds.** "The plaintiff is not suing to enforce the oral contract; the plaintiff is suing for the tort of deceit. The damages flow from the fraudulent misrepresentation of intent-to-perform, not from the unenforceable contract itself."

2. **Present intent not to perform is a question of fact, provable by circumstantial evidence.** "Something more than nonperformance is required" — but "something more" can be shown by:
   - Hasty repudiation of the promise
   - Failure to attempt performance
   - Promises of performance after the time for performance has passed
   - Continuing assurances after it became obvious performance would not occur
   - "Any fact or circumstance" from which intent-at-time-of-promise can be inferred

3. **The circumstantial-evidence rule is necessary or fraud would be unprovable.** Chief Justice Bird: "A contrary rule would be most unfortunate: The language of the law has long spoken of the 'secret and unfathomable workings of the human mind,' but if such workings were truly unfathomable few fraud claims could be proved."

4. **Summary judgment on intent is disfavored.** Because the question is the promisor's state of mind at the time of the promise — and state of mind is almost always circumstantial — summary adjudication rarely resolves promissory fraud.

---

WHY TENZER MADE PROMISSORY FRAUD VIABLE IN CALIFORNIA:

Before Tenzer, defendants routinely defeated promissory-fraud claims on summary judgment by arguing that the plaintiff's only evidence was non-performance, and non-performance alone cannot prove original intent. After Tenzer, plaintiffs need only point to any additional circumstance from which a reasonable jury could infer contrary intent — and that circumstance can be as subtle as the timing of the subsequent conduct.

Every California promissory-fraud opinion since 1985 cites Tenzer on the circumstantial-evidence point. Lazar v. Superior Court (1996) adds Supreme Court reaffirmation; Tenzer remains the foundational articulation.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

The circumstantial evidence of fraudulent intent at the Honeysuckle closing is abundant, mapping directly onto Tenzer's enumerated categories:

1. **19 unsigned documents** — a pattern inconsistent with a good-faith transaction. Under Tenzer, this alone is enough to infer the promisor knew more than they represented.

2. **$496K gap** — where closing statement line items do not account for the missing funds. Tenzer's "failure to attempt performance" factor.

3. **Pre-positioned crypto and surgery accounts receiving funds immediately post-closing** — Tenzer's "hasty repudiation / actions inconsistent with the promise" factor.

4. **Pre-planned conservatorship structure** — Tenzer's "any fact or circumstance" catchall.

All four of Tenzer's enumerated inference-supporting circumstances are present. A Tenzer-compliant complaint lists these circumstances and pleads the inference that, at the time of each promise embedded in the closing documents, the promisor had no intent to perform.
