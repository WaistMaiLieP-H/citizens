# Statute version cited in Tenzer (1985)

At the time of *Tenzer*, Civil Code § 1572 read exactly as it reads today (2026) and as it was enacted in 1872. No amendments.

**Text as cited by the court (1985) = current text:**

> Actual fraud, within the meaning of this Chapter, consists in any of the following acts, committed by a party to the contract, or with his connivance, with intent to deceive another party thereto, or to induce him to enter into the contract:
>
> (1) The suggestion, as a fact, of that which is not true, by one who does not believe it to be true;
> (2) The positive assertion, in a manner not warranted by the information of the person making it, of that which is not true, though he believes it to be true;
> (3) The suppression of that which is true, by one having knowledge or belief of the fact;
> (4) A promise made without any intention of performing it; or,
> (5) Any other act fitted to deceive.

The court's analysis focused on **subdivision (4)** — "a promise made without any intention of performing it." The court treated that subdivision as self-executing: the only element not plain on the face of the statute is the evidentiary standard for proving intent, which the court supplied by holding that circumstantial evidence is sufficient.

**Consistency check:** The *Tenzer* opinion at 39 Cal.3d 30-31 quotes subdivision (4) verbatim. The quotation matches both the 1872 original and the 2026 current text. No textual drift.
