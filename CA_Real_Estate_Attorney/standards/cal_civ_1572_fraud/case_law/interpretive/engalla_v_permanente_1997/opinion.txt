ENGALLA v. PERMANENTE MEDICAL GROUP, INC.
15 Cal.4th 951 (1997)
California Supreme Court
Decided: June 30, 1997

---

FACTS:

Wilfredo Engalla was a patient in Kaiser Permanente's HMO. He was diagnosed with lung cancer that Kaiser allegedly should have detected years earlier. His family filed a medical malpractice claim, which Kaiser required be submitted to binding arbitration described in Kaiser's member handbook as fast, neutral, and efficient.

In reality:
- Kaiser's internal data showed the mean time from claim filing to selection of a neutral arbitrator was 863 days
- Of roughly 1,100 claims filed in 1989, only one had a neutral arbitrator selected within the 60-day period the handbook described as normal
- Kaiser used delay as a litigation tactic — terminally ill claimants often died before their arbitration was decided, eliminating non-economic damages

Plaintiff's family alleged Kaiser fraudulently induced the arbitration agreement by misrepresenting the speed and neutrality of the process.

---

HOLDING:

FRAUDULENT INDUCEMENT DEFEATS AN ARBITRATION AGREEMENT:
"If a party proves that the adhesive arbitration clause was induced by fraud under § 1572, the arbitration clause is voidable and a court can refuse to enforce it."

PATTERN-BASED FRAUD — THE ENGALLA FRAMEWORK:
1. **Fraud can be shown by a pattern of false statements in form contracts and marketing materials.** The court accepted: (a) specific statements in the handbook about arbitration speed; (b) Kaiser's internal data contradicting those statements; (c) an inference that Kaiser knew the statements were false when made; (d) Kaiser's intent that claimants rely on those representations; and (e) damages flowing from delayed arbitration.

2. **Waiver of arbitration through bad faith delay.** Kaiser's conduct during arbitration — manipulating delays to let the claimant die before resolution — constituted waiver of the right to compel arbitration.

3. **Reaffirmation of the Tenzer/Lazar circumstantial-evidence framework.** "Fraudulent intent can be inferred from a pattern of conduct inconsistent with the representations. The 863-day-average contradiction of the '60-day-normal' representation was that pattern."

---

THE ENGALLA PRINCIPLE — FORM DOCUMENTS ARE SUBJECT TO § 1572:

"Pre-printed form documents are still subject to § 1572 fraud analysis, and the operative question is whether the representations in those forms were false and whether the preparer knew they were false."

THE MAGNITUDE-OF-CONTRADICTION INFERENCE:
Where a party makes a representation in a printed document and that representation is contradicted by the party's own internal data, a factfinder can infer fraudulent intent from the magnitude of the contradiction. The larger the gap between the representation and the internal reality, the stronger the inference.

Kaiser: "60-day normal" vs. 863-day average = 14× shortfall → fraudulent inference upheld.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Engalla is directly relevant to the 2958 Honeysuckle closing document fraud:

1. **Form closing documents**: The Honeysuckle closing used standard CAR purchase agreement, standard escrow instructions, and standard deed forms. Under Engalla, pre-printed form documents are subject to § 1572 fraud analysis. The question is whether the representations in those forms were false and whether the preparer knew they were false.

2. **Magnitude of contradiction as fraud inference**: The closing statement represented a disposition of proceeds contradicted by the actual flow of funds. $605K → $109K is an 82% shortfall. Engalla's magnitude-of-contradiction inference applies directly. Where the gap between representation and reality is this large, fraudulent intent can be inferred.

3. **Contemporaneous internal knowledge**: Whoever prepared the closing documents had access to the actual fund flow. If those persons knew the representations about proceeds destination were false, Engalla provides the inference framework.
