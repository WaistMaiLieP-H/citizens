# Statute version cited in Engalla (1997)

Civil Code § 1572 in 1997 was identical to the 1872 text and to the 2026 text. No amendments.

The *Engalla* court applied the full five-subdivision structure of § 1572. The operative subdivisions for the fraudulent inducement holding were:

- **§ 1572(1)** — "suggestion as a fact of that which is not true" (Kaiser's representations about arbitration speed)
- **§ 1572(2)** — "positive assertion in a manner not warranted" (Kaiser's claims about neutrality, not warranted by its internal data)
- **§ 1572(3)** — "suppression of that which is true" (Kaiser's failure to disclose the 863-day average)

The court read the subdivisions together rather than picking one, because Kaiser's conduct arguably satisfied all three. This is a useful drafting pattern for plaintiffs: a well-pleaded § 1572 complaint can invoke multiple subdivisions alternatively or cumulatively.

**Consistency check:** The *Engalla* opinion does not quote § 1572 verbatim in the published text but cites the section and applies its elements. The court's treatment is consistent with the 1872/2026 stable text.
