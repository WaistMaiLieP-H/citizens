# Statute version cited in Lovejoy (2004)

Civil Code § 1572 in 2004 was identical to the 1872 original and the 2026 current text. The operative subdivision in *Lovejoy* was **§ 1572(3) — suppression of that which is true, by one having knowledge or belief of the fact**.

The *Lovejoy* court did not write out the full five-subdivision statute; it focused on subdivision (3) and incorporated by reference the common-law duty-to-disclose framework from *Warner Construction*. That framework overlays § 1572(3) and identifies when the "having knowledge or belief of the fact" element is accompanied by a legal duty to act on that knowledge.

**Key doctrinal addition:** *Lovejoy* does not amend or reinterpret § 1572(3) — it catalogs when the prerequisite duty to disclose exists. Without a duty to disclose, mere silence (even knowing silence) is not fraud. With a duty, silence is fraud under § 1572(3).

**Consistency check:** The *Lovejoy* analysis is fully compatible with the statutory text. A plaintiff pleading § 1572(3) after *Lovejoy* must allege (a) the defendant knew a material fact, (b) the defendant had a duty to disclose (from one of the four *Lovejoy* categories), (c) the defendant suppressed the fact, (d) the plaintiff was unaware and would have acted differently, (e) damages.
