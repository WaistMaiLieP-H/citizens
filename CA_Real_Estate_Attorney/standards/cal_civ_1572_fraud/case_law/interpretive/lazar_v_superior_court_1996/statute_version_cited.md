# Statute version cited in Lazar (1996)

The *Lazar* court applied California Civil Code § 1572 as it existed in 1996. Because § 1572 has never been textually amended since its 1872 enactment, the version cited in *Lazar* is identical to the version in force today (2026) and identical to the 1872 original.

**Statute text in force at time of decision (1996) — identical to current:**

> Actual fraud, within the meaning of this Chapter, consists in any of the following acts, committed by a party to the contract, or with his connivance, with intent to deceive another party thereto, or to induce him to enter into the contract:
>
> 1. The suggestion, as a fact, of that which is not true, by one who does not believe it to be true;
> 2. The positive assertion, in a manner not warranted by the information of the person making it, of that which is not true, though he believes it to be true;
> 3. The suppression of that which is true, by one having knowledge or belief of the fact;
> 4. A promise made without any intention of performing it; or,
> 5. Any other act fitted to deceive.

The subdivision construed in *Lazar* is **subd. (4)** — promissory fraud. The court's reasoning does not depend on any amendment history because there is none. The opinion's holdings apply with identical force to current § 1572(4).

**Consistency check:** The *Lazar* opinion quotes § 1572(4) verbatim at page 638. The verbatim quotation matches the 1872 text and the 2026 text. Internal consistency verified.
