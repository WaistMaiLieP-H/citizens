## INTRODUCTION

1. This action arises from the June 2023 sale of real property at 2958 Honeysuckle [Street/Drive/Road], [City], Contra Costa County, California (the "Property"), a community property asset of the marriage of Plaintiff Michael Hartmann and Defendant Christina Marie Cerretani.

2. The sale generated gross proceeds of approximately $605,000. The Property carried equity of approximately $465,000. Plaintiff received approximately $109,000 — less than one-half of his community property share — while approximately $72,000 in cryptocurrency and $25,000 in spine surgery costs were diverted or offset against Plaintiff's share without his prior written consent or informed agreement.

3. The sale was conducted without the lawful dual consent required by **Family Code § 1102** for the disposition of community real property. Nineteen (19) transaction documents bore Plaintiff's purported signature but were not signed by Plaintiff, demonstrating that the transaction was processed through the use of forged or unauthorized instruments.

4. Material disclosures required by **Civil Code §§ 1102 through 1102.17** (Transfer Disclosure Statement) and **Civil Code §§ 1709–1710** (deceit) were not made or were made fraudulently.

5. The real estate broker(s) involved in the transaction owed Plaintiff fiduciary duties under **Business and Professions Code § 10176**, and violated those duties by failing to disclose conflicts of interest, compensation arrangements, and material facts, and by facilitating or failing to prevent the use of unauthorized signature instruments.

6. Plaintiff brings this action for:
   (a) Actual fraud — Civil Code § 1572;
   (b) Deceit — Civil Code §§ 1709–1710;
   (c) Violation of community real property dual-consent requirement — Family Code § 1102;
   (d) Breach of spousal fiduciary duty — Family Code §§ 721 and 1100(e);
   (e) Statutory 100% remedy for fraudulent breach of spousal fiduciary duty — Family Code § 1101(h);
   (f) Real property fraud damages — Civil Code § 3343;
   (g) Broker prohibited conduct — Business and Professions Code § 10176;
   (h) Transfer Disclosure Statement violations — Civil Code §§ 1102 through 1102.17; and
   (i) Punitive damages — Civil Code § 3294.

### FIFTH CAUSE OF ACTION
**100% Statutory Remedy for Fraudulent Breach of Fiduciary Duty — Family Code § 1101(h)**
*(Against Defendant Cerretani)*

58. Plaintiff incorporates by reference all foregoing paragraphs.

59. **Family Code § 1101(a)** provides that a spouse has a claim against the other spouse for any breach of the fiduciary duty owed under § 721, including for impairment of the claimant spouse's present undivided one-half interest in community estate assets and liabilities.

60. **Family Code § 1101(h)** provides that if a court finds that a spouse has breached a fiduciary duty that results in impairment to the claimant spouse's present undivided one-half interest in community estate, the court shall order an **accounting of the community estate** and shall award the claimant spouse **50 percent, or an amount equal to 50 percent, of any asset undisclosed or transferred in breach of the fiduciary duty.**

61. Further, where the breach of fiduciary duty involves **fraud**, Family Code § 1101(h) authorizes the award of **100 percent of any asset subject to the breach** — not merely 50 percent.

63. The asset subject to the fraudulent breach is the community property interest in the Property and its equity proceeds — the approximately $465,000 in net equity at the time of sale.

64. Plaintiff is entitled, under Family Code § 1101(h), to **100% of the community property equity** — approximately $465,000 — as the remedy for Defendant Cerretani's fraudulent breach of spousal fiduciary duty.

65. The § 1101(h) remedy is in addition to, and not in lieu of, any other damages, disgorgement, or equitable relief available under other causes of action.
