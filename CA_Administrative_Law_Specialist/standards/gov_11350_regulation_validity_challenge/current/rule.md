# GOV § 11350 Regulation Validity Challenge — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** gov_11350_regulation_validity_challenge
**Citizen:** CA_Administrative_Law_Specialist
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

Any interested person may bring a superior court action for declaratory relief challenging a regulation's validity. The court may declare the regulation invalid if: (1) there was a substantial failure to comply with the APA rulemaking chapter; (2) for emergency regulations, the emergency facts do not constitute an actual emergency; or (3) the necessity determination is not supported by substantial evidence (§11350(b)(1)); or (4) the agency declaration under §11346.5(a)(8) conflicts with substantial evidence in the record.

---

## Standing — "Any Interested Person"

The standing requirement is permissive: any *interested* person. This is broader than the traditional injury-in-fact requirement for other forms of judicial review. A person who is regulated by the challenged regulation, or who is affected by its application to others, qualifies.

The failure to first petition the agency or seek reconsideration does NOT affect the right to judicial determination (§11350(a)). There is no exhaustion prerequisite for §11350 challenges.

---

## The Three Challenge Grounds

### Ground 1 — Substantial APA Process Failure (§11350(a))
The regulation failed to comply with the APA rulemaking chapter (GOV §§11340-11356). "Substantial" failure — minor technical deviations do not void regulations. The court looks to whether the APA's core procedural requirements were met:

- Was the regulation subject to OAL review? Was OAL review conducted?
- Was the rulemaking file complete under §11347.3?
- Was the necessity finding made and supported?
- Was the notice-and-comment period provided?
- Was the final statement of reasons prepared?

**Omission as evidence (§11350(d)(3)):** An item *required* to be in the rulemaking file but *missing* from it is evidence of the omission — introduced for the sole purpose of proving the omission. A missing required element of the rulemaking file is itself the evidence that the APA was not followed.

### Ground 2 — Emergency Regulation: Facts Don't Constitute Emergency (§11350(a))
Emergency regulations bypass the normal notice-and-comment process. They are valid only if the agency made a finding that an emergency exists. If the recited facts do not constitute an "emergency" within GOV §11346.1's definition, the emergency regulation is invalid and the normal rulemaking process was required.

### Ground 3 — Necessity Determination Not Supported by Substantial Evidence (§11350(b)(1))
Even if APA procedures were followed, the regulation may be invalid if the agency's determination that it is "reasonably necessary to effectuate the purpose of the statute" being implemented is not supported by substantial evidence. The evidentiary universe is the rulemaking file.

---

## The Evidentiary Universe — §11350(d)

§11350 actions are decided on a closed record: only the rulemaking file (§11347.3), the emergency finding (if applicable), items required but omitted (to prove omission), and evidence of whether the regulation was required to be adopted under the APA. No general evidence. No new fact-finding.

**The missing-item rule (§11350(d)(3)):** If the rulemaking file is missing a required element, that missing element can be proved by showing it was required but absent. The absence is the evidence.

**OAL approval is not dispositive (§11350(c)):** The court does not defer to OAL's approval or the Governor's overruling of OAL. The court makes its own determination from the record.

## Application to Active Cases

### CalVCB Deadline Compression — Potential Regulatory Challenge

The CalVCB audit (A25-10117946) documented shortened deadlines. If CalVCB imposed shortened deadlines through a regulation (rather than by statute), and if that regulation was adopted without the required APA rulemaking process (no OAL review, no rulemaking file, no necessity finding), §11350 provides a vehicle to challenge the regulation itself.

**The audit trail approach:**
1. Identify the specific CalVCB regulatory provision imposing the shortened deadline
2. Request the rulemaking file under GOV §11347.3 for that provision
3. If the rulemaking file is missing required elements, §11350(d)(3) allows proof of the omission
4. If OAL review was not conducted, or if the necessity determination is missing, §11350(a) substantial APA failure applies

**Note:** §11350 is a regulation-validity challenge, not a case-specific appeal. It would invalidate the regulation prospectively, potentially affecting all CalVCB applicants — not just A25-10117946. The case-specific remedy is §1094.5 mandamus; §11350 is the systemic remedy.

### CDI / DMHC Regulatory Challenges

Insurance and health plan regulations that are applied in enforcement actions may be challenged under §11350 if the rulemaking process was deficient. The CA_Insurance_Compliance_Litigator and CA_Healthcare_Fraud_Litigator share jurisdiction with this Citizen on regulatory validity challenges to their governing regulations.

## §11350 vs. §1094.5 — When to Use Which

| Challenge | Vehicle | When |
|---|---|---|
| Agency's decision in your specific case | CCP §1094.5 writ of mandate | Final adjudicative decision after required hearing |
| The regulation itself is invalid | GOV §11350 declaratory relief | Regulation's validity challenged, regardless of any specific case |
| Agency failed to adopt required regulation | CCP §1085 ordinary mandamus | Ministerial duty to adopt — no discretion |
| Emergency regulation issued without emergency | GOV §11350 | Emergency finding facts don't support emergency |
