# GOV § 11340 APA Purpose — Operative Rule

## The Rule

GOV §11340 is the interpretive foundation of California's APA. It establishes that the APA chapter (GOV §§11340-11529) exists to correct identified problems with California's regulatory system: volume, clarity, necessity, proportionality, central review, and small-party burden. Every APA requirement is interpreted in light of these findings.

---

## How §11340 Is Used

### 1. Interpretive Canon — APA Provisions Are Construed to Serve the §11340 Findings

When an APA procedural requirement is ambiguous — does this type of agency action require rulemaking? does this notice satisfy the APA's requirements? — the court interprets the ambiguity by asking which reading better serves the §11340 findings:
- The reading that promotes clarity (§11340(b)) over obscurity
- The reading that requires necessity findings (§11340(c)) over discretionary omission
- The reading that protects small parties (§11340(g)) over imposing complex compliance burdens

### 2. Necessity Finding Attacks — §11340(c) as Substantive Standard

§11340(c) specifically identifies adoption of unnecessary regulations as a problem. When an agency has not made a finding of necessity, or when the necessity finding is not supported by substantial evidence (the basis for §11350(b)(1) regulation invalidity), §11340(c) provides the substantive rationale: the Legislature found that unnecessary regulations are a specific harm that the APA exists to prevent.

### 3. Plain Language Requirement — §11340(b) as Clarity Standard

§11340(b)'s finding that regulations are "frequently unclear and unnecessarily complex" justifies the OAL's plain language review and the APA's requirement that regulations be comprehensible to regulated parties. A regulation that a regulated party could not reasonably be expected to understand fails the §11340(b) standard — which the OAL is charged with enforcing.

### 4. Small Business / Pro Se Parties — §11340(g) as Equity Principle

§11340(g) specifically identifies that regulatory complexity "put[s] small businesses, which do not have the resources to hire experts to assist them, at a distinct disadvantage." This finding extends beyond small businesses: it applies to any regulated party without professional representation. Pro se applicants navigating CalVCB, individuals facing agency enforcement without counsel, and communities affected by agency action without sophisticated advocates — all are within the equity concern GOV §11340(g) documents.

**Application to CalVCB:** The CalVCB audit findings of shortened deadlines and single-source denials are exactly the problems §11340(g) identifies. A pro se victim compensation applicant — who "does not have the resources to hire experts" — is placed at a "distinct disadvantage" by deadline compression and inadequate notice. The §11340(g) finding is the legislative backdrop that gives context to why §1094.5 review of CalVCB's manner violations matters.

## §11340's Role in Each APA Standard

| §11340 Finding | What It Justifies |
|---|---|
| (a) Unprecedented growth | OAL central review; rulemaking file requirement |
| (b) Unclear language | Plain language requirement; OAL clarity review |
| (c) Unnecessary regulations | Necessity finding requirement; §11350(b)(1) necessity challenge |
| (d) Prescriptive vs. performance standards | OAL proportionality review |
| (e) No central review office | OAL establishment (1979) |
| (f) Legislature + executive involvement | Joint oversight of rulemaking |
| (g) Small business disadvantage | Access to administrative process; pro se equity principle |

## Application to Active Cases

### CalVCB A25-10117946

§11340(g) is the equity backdrop for every CalVCB APA challenge: the Board's procedures must be comprehensible and accessible to unrepresented applicants. Shortened deadlines that a represented applicant might navigate with professional assistance become fatal traps for pro se victims. When CalVCB's procedures fail these applicants, they fail the Legislature's own declared purpose.

### OAL Review as Evidence

If a CalVCB or other agency regulation was reviewed by OAL and approved, that approval reflects OAL's determination that the regulation met the §11340 standards (authority, necessity, clarity, consistency). But as §11350(c) establishes, OAL approval is not dispositive in a court challenge — the court makes its own determination. The OAL approval is relevant evidence that the §11340 process was followed, not a conclusive finding that the regulation is valid.
