# GOV § 11340 APA Purpose — Reasoning and Historical Loss Analysis

## Why This Standard Exists — The Legislature's Self-Indictment

GOV §11340 is unusual: it is the Legislature documenting its own failure. The findings in §11340(a)-(g) are not abstract policy aspirations — they are the Legislature's acknowledgment that California's regulatory system had grown out of control, that regulations were unclear and unnecessary, and that no one in state government was checking whether regulations were authorized, comprehensible, or needed.

This self-indictment is the interpretive key to the entire APA. Every requirement — rulemaking files, necessity findings, OAL review, plain language — exists because the Legislature found that without these requirements, agencies were making law badly. The APA is the Legislature's correction.

---

## The Seven Findings as a Diagnostic Framework

The seven §11340 findings can be read as a diagnostic:

**(a) Volume:** The regulatory state expanded faster than accountability could follow. More regulations = more opportunities for non-compliance, inconsistency, and contradiction.

**(b) Clarity:** Regulations written for bureaucratic convenience rather than regulated-party understanding. Complexity serves regulators (who know the system) at the expense of regulated parties (who don't).

**(c) Necessity:** Regulations adopted without asking whether they were needed. The absence of necessity discipline means agencies can expand their jurisdiction through rulemaking without statutory basis.

**(d) Proportionality:** Prescriptive standards (do it exactly this way) imposed where performance standards (achieve this result) would work equally well. Prescriptive standards favor large regulated parties who can build compliance infrastructure; performance standards allow innovation.

**(e) Central review gap:** No OAL before 1979. Agencies reviewed their own regulations — a structural conflict of interest equivalent to a litigant serving as their own judge.

**(f) Accountability gap:** The Legislature needed to be more directly involved. Agencies had been legislating through regulation without the political accountability that makes legislative power legitimate.

**(g) Representational inequity:** Small businesses and unrepresented parties bear the highest burden from regulatory complexity. They cannot hire experts to navigate the system. The APA's clarity and necessity requirements are, among other things, an equity measure.

## The Historical Loss — The Pre-APA Reform Regulatory State

Before the OAL reform and the modern APA chapter, California's regulatory system had produced exactly the harms §11340 documents. The typical regulated party in the pre-OAL era:

- Faced regulations they could not understand
- Had no reliable way to know whether a regulation was actually authorized by the statute it purported to implement
- Could not challenge a regulation's necessity without facing the agency's own review of its own rulemaking
- Received no guaranteed notice that a regulation affecting them was being proposed

The historical loss is cumulative: unclear regulations produce compliance failures that harm the regulated party; unnecessary regulations impose costs without benefit; unauthorized regulations bind parties to obligations the Legislature never intended. The APA is the remedy — but the remedy only works if its procedural requirements are enforced.

## Application to the Case Record

The CalVCB A25-10117946 audit demonstrates §11340's relevance in the specific context:

- **§11340(b) — Clarity:** Were CalVCB's appeal procedures (including deadline rules) written in plain language comprehensible to a pro se victim?
- **§11340(c) — Necessity:** Were CalVCB's deadline compression rules necessary, or were they adopted without necessity findings?
- **§11340(g) — Equity:** A pro se victim compensation applicant is exactly the type of party §11340(g) identifies as most harmed by regulatory complexity and inaccessibility

The §11340 findings do not create an independent cause of action — they are interpretive tools. But they make the §1094.5 challenge and the §11350 regulation validity challenge more than procedural technicalities: they situate those challenges in the Legislature's own declared understanding of what the APA is for.
