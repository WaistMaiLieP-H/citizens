## Why This Standard Exists — The SLAPP Problem

A SLAPP (Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation) is a lawsuit filed not to win, but to silence. The mechanism: file a lawsuit against someone who has spoken out on a public issue, filed a complaint with a government agency, or participated in official proceedings. The lawsuit itself — regardless of its merits — imposes litigation costs, discovery burdens, and reputational harm on the target. The target either falls silent or depletes their resources fighting the case. The SLAPP filer wins by filing.

California's legislature found in 1992 that this pattern was occurring at a "disturbing" rate. The anti-SLAPP statute was the response: a mechanism to identify these lawsuits early, strike them before discovery commences, and shift the cost to the filer through mandatory attorney fee recovery.

---

## The Policy Structure

**Early resolution:** The special motion to strike must be heard before discovery begins. The discovery stay ensures the SLAPP target is not drained by the discovery process before the court determines whether the claim has merit.

**Burden shifting:** The defendant does the first work (showing the claim arises from protected activity), then the burden shifts to the plaintiff to show they actually have a case. This prevents plaintiffs from surviving anti-SLAPP motions on the strength of their pleadings alone — they must show evidence.

**Mandatory fees:** A prevailing defendant gets attorney's fees as of right. This is the deterrent. A plaintiff who files a SLAPP claim knowing it will be struck faces a mandatory fee award — not a discretionary one. The deterrent must be mandatory to work.

**Broad construction:** §425.16(a) requires the statute to be "construed broadly." The legislature recognized that narrow construction would recreate the SLAPP problem it was designed to solve.

## The Historical Loss — What SLAPPs Cost

Before §425.16, a person who:
- Filed a complaint with the Medical Board against a negligent physician
- Reported suspicious activity to law enforcement
- Testified truthfully in family law proceedings
- Filed a CalVCB application documenting their victimization
- Wrote to their city council about a developer's project

...could be sued for that protected activity. The lawsuit would name defamation, malicious prosecution, intentional interference, or other torts. The legal theories were often thin — but litigation is expensive. The target either settled (effectively paying to be allowed to exercise their constitutional rights) or spent years and resources defending the suit.

The historical loss was not just individual — it was systemic. When participation in official proceedings and public discourse becomes a financial risk, people stop participating. Witnesses become reluctant. Complainants become silent. The democratic and legal systems that depend on citizen participation are degraded.

## Application to the Case Record

The pattern documented in this case record — counter-filings in family law proceedings, claims arising from reports to OPD, assertions arising from statements made in court proceedings — is the SLAPP pattern precisely. The anti-SLAPP statute is the tool for early identification and termination of those claims.

The discovery stay is particularly important here: if any counter-claim has been filed arising from protected petition activity, the stay prevents the counter-claimant from using the discovery process as the weapon — forcing production of private documents, creating deposition costs, and distracting from the underlying merits.
