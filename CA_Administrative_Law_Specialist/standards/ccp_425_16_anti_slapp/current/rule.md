# CCP § 425.16 Anti-SLAPP — Operative Rule

**Citizen:** CA_Administrative_Law_Specialist
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

## The Rule

CCP §425.16 provides a special motion to strike causes of action arising from protected petition or free speech activity in connection with a public issue. The motion imposes a two-step burden-shifting test: (1) the defendant must show the challenged cause of action arises from protected activity; (2) if shown, the plaintiff must demonstrate a probability of prevailing. If the plaintiff cannot show probability of prevailing, the motion is granted and the cause of action is stricken with mandatory attorney fee recovery by the defendant.

Discovery is stayed upon filing until the ruling. The statute is construed **broadly**.

---

## The Two-Step Test

### Step 1 — Defendant's Burden: Protected Activity

The defendant must show the cause of action arises from an act in furtherance of the right of petition or free speech in connection with a public issue. The four categories (§425.16(e)):

1. **Official proceeding statements:** Any statement made before a legislative, executive, or judicial proceeding, or other official proceeding authorized by law
2. **Official body connection:** Statements made in connection with an issue under consideration by a legislative, executive, or judicial body, or official proceeding
3. **Public forum + public interest:** Statements in a public forum in connection with an issue of public interest
4. **Catch-all:** Any other conduct in furtherance of constitutional petition or speech rights in connection with a public issue

**Key:** The four categories are disjunctive — one is enough. The focus is on the **act** underlying the cause of action, not on the legal theory pleaded. If the cause of action is substantially based on protected activity, Step 1 is met.

### Step 2 — Plaintiff's Burden: Probability of Prevailing

If Step 1 is met, the burden shifts to the plaintiff to demonstrate a **probability** that it will prevail on the challenged claim. "Probability" means a prima facie case — enough evidence to show the claim has minimal merit if believed. The court does not weigh the evidence; it determines if the plaintiff has presented evidence that, if believed, would constitute a prima facie case.

**The standard is not high for the plaintiff, but it is real:** The plaintiff must present competent, admissible evidence on each element of the claim. A legally sufficient complaint without evidentiary support fails Step 2.

---

## Discovery Stay — The SLAPP Weapon

The discovery stay (§425.16(g)) is automatic upon filing. This is the mechanism that makes anti-SLAPP strategically significant:

- The plaintiff cannot conduct discovery to build their case after the motion is filed
- The plaintiff must oppose the motion with evidence they already have
- If the plaintiff's case depends on information that only discovery would reveal, the stay effectively forces the plaintiff to oppose the motion with inadequate evidence

Good cause exception: on noticed motion, the court may order specified discovery despite the stay.

---

## Mandatory Attorney Fees — The Deterrent

A prevailing defendant is entitled to attorney's fees as a matter of right (§425.16(c)(1)). This is not discretionary. The deterrent effect: plaintiffs filing claims that arise from protected activity knowing they will lose the anti-SLAPP motion face mandatory fee liability.

**Frivolous motion exception (§425.16(c)(1)):** If the court finds the motion itself was frivolous or solely intended to delay, the plaintiff prevailing on the motion recovers fees under §128.5. This prevents weaponization of the anti-SLAPP motion as a delay tactic.

---

## The Public Prosecutor Exemption (§425.16(d))

The AG, CDI Commissioner, DA, and city attorney acting as a public prosecutor are exempt from anti-SLAPP. Their enforcement actions in the name of the People cannot be struck under §425.16. This does not protect private parties suing in similar subject areas — only public prosecutors acting in their official enforcement capacity.

## Application to Active Cases

### Protective Shield — Petition Activity

If any of the civil claims against parties in this case record arise from the filing of this litigation, petitions to government agencies, statements made in court proceedings, or reports made to law enforcement — those claims may be subject to anti-SLAPP.

**The retaliation pattern (family law + criminal case):** Claims or counterclaims filed in response to:
- Reports to OPD (official proceedings — §425.16(e)(1))
- Petitions to the court (official proceedings — §425.16(e)(1))
- Statements made in family law proceedings (official proceedings — §425.16(e)(1))
- CalVCB application statements (official proceeding authorized by law — §425.16(e)(1))

These activities are protected. A tort claim premised on any of these (e.g., malicious prosecution, defamation) must survive the anti-SLAPP two-step.

### Anti-SLAPP as Sword — Frivolous Claims Against Petitioner

If any party has filed a claim against Michael H. that arises from his exercise of petition rights (filing court papers, reporting to agencies, testifying), that claim is a SLAPP target. The 60-day filing window from service of complaint applies; good cause for late filing may extend it.

### CalVCB Application — Official Proceeding

A CalVCB application is a statement made in connection with an official proceeding authorized by law (§425.16(e)(1)/(2)). Any tort claim arising from the content of the application (e.g., claiming statements in the application were defamatory) would be subject to anti-SLAPP challenge.

## Anti-SLAPP Checklist

| Issue | Analysis |
|---|---|
| What is the specific act underlying the claim? | Identify the act, not the legal theory |
| Does the act fall into one of §425.16(e)'s four categories? | Official proceeding? Public issue? Catch-all? |
| Is it in connection with a "public issue"? | Broadly construed; not limited to traditional public affairs |
| Was the motion filed within 60 days of service? | Count from service of complaint/cross-complaint |
| What evidence does plaintiff have NOW to show probability of prevailing? | No new discovery after stay |
| Is the defendant a public prosecutor? | If yes, §425.16(d) exempts the claim |
