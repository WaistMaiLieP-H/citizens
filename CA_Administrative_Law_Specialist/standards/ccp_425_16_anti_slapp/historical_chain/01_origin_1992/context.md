# Historical Chain — Origin: Anti-SLAPP Statute (1992)

**Standard:** ccp_425_16_anti_slapp
**Era:** 1992 — California Anti-SLAPP Act

---

## The SLAPP Problem — Pre-1992

By the late 1980s and early 1990s, California courts were seeing an increasing pattern of lawsuits targeting individuals and organizations for participating in official proceedings and public discourse. The typical SLAPP:
- Sued a neighborhood activist who had opposed a development project at public hearings
- Sued a consumer who had filed a complaint with a regulatory agency
- Sued a witness or complainant for statements made in official proceedings
- Sued a journalist or community advocate for statements on matters of public concern

The legal theories were typically defamation, tortious interference, or malicious prosecution. The merits were often thin or nonexistent. But the strategic purpose was clear: make the cost of public participation so high that targets would withdraw their petitions, retract their statements, or simply stop participating.

## The Legislative Response — Stats. 1992, ch. 726

California enacted CCP §425.16 in 1992 (Stats. 1992, ch. 726). The statute was designed with three key mechanisms:
1. A special motion to strike — available before answer, at the pleadings stage
2. A discovery stay — automatic upon filing the motion
3. Mandatory attorney fees for prevailing defendants

The 1992 enactment has been amended multiple times to clarify the definition of protected activity, refine the public prosecutor exemption, and address particular contexts (open meeting laws, CPRA). The core structure has remained unchanged.

## The Scope of "Public Issue" — Broadening Over Time

Courts have broadly construed "public issue" as required by §425.16(a). The issue need not be a traditional political or civic matter — commercial disputes, professional regulation, and proceedings before administrative bodies have been recognized as involving "public issues" or being conducted in connection with "official proceedings authorized by law."
