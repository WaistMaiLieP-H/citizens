# CCP § 1094.5 Administrative Mandamus — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** ccp_1094_5_administrative_mandamus
**Citizen:** CA_Administrative_Law_Specialist
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

CCP §1094.5 provides judicial review of final administrative orders where: (1) a hearing was required by law; (2) evidence was taken; and (3) discretion was vested in the agency. The reviewing court does not retry the case — it reviews whether the agency abused its discretion on the administrative record.

**Three grounds for relief (§1094.5(b)):**
1. Respondent acted without or in excess of jurisdiction
2. Petitioner was denied a fair trial
3. Prejudicial abuse of discretion

**Abuse of discretion exists if:**
- Agency did not proceed in the manner required by law
- The order or decision is not supported by the findings
- The findings are not supported by the evidence

---

## The Two Standards of Review — This Is the Tactical Divide

### Substantial Evidence Standard (default — §1094.5(c))
Applies in all cases UNLESS the court is authorized by law to exercise independent judgment. The court asks: is there substantial evidence in the light of the whole record to support the agency's findings? The agency's finding stands if any substantial evidence supports it — even if the court might have decided differently.

### Independent Judgment Standard (vested rights — §1094.5(c))
Applies when: (1) the statute expressly authorizes independent judgment; OR (2) the petitioner had a **fundamental vested right** at stake. Under independent judgment, the court re-weighs the evidence itself. Abuse of discretion exists if findings are not supported by the **weight** of the evidence — the court's conclusion, not the agency's, controls.

**The vested rights doctrine (Bixby v. Pierno):** When an administrative decision threatens a fundamental vested right — not just an expectancy, but an established right such as a license already issued, a property right, or a liberty interest — the petitioner is entitled to independent judgment review. The court steps in as a genuine tribunal, not a rubber stamp.

**Why this matters:** CalVCB appeal applicants who had an established award later modified or denied, and professional license holders whose licenses are revoked, are in the vested rights category. The difference between substantial evidence (agency nearly always wins) and independent judgment (genuine re-trial on the record) is often the difference between winning and losing.

## Prerequisites for §1094.5 — All Four Required

| Element | What it means | Failure = |
|---|---|---|
| Final administrative order | The agency has issued its last word; no pending reconsideration | Petition dismissed (not ripe) |
| Hearing required by law | The statute creating the agency proceeding mandated a hearing | Ordinary mandamus (§1085) instead |
| Evidence taken | Sworn testimony, documents submitted to the agency | Ordinary mandamus (§1085) instead |
| Discretion vested | Agency had discretion to decide — not a purely ministerial act | Ordinary mandamus (§1085) instead |

## The Three-Question Inquiry (§1094.5(b))

**Question 1 — Jurisdiction:**
Did the agency act within its legal authority? Jurisdictional errors are the strongest ground: if the agency had no power to make the order it made, or exceeded the scope of its statutory authority, the writ issues regardless of the merits of the underlying decision.

**Question 2 — Fair Trial:**
Was the hearing procedurally fair? Applies the APA hearing rights (GOV §11513): oath-based testimony, cross-examination rights, privilege recognition, hearsay limitations. A hearing that denied cross-examination, admitted constitutionally barred evidence, or was conducted by a conflicted ALJ is grounds for relief on this basis.

**Question 3 — Abuse of Discretion:**
The workhorse ground. Three sub-grounds (§1094.5(b)):
- **Manner:** Did the agency proceed in the manner required by law? (APA procedural compliance)
- **Decision-findings link:** Is the decision supported by the findings? (The agency's own stated reasons must logically support its conclusion)
- **Findings-evidence link:** Are the findings supported by the evidence? (Under the applicable standard of review)

---

## Stay Pending Judgment

**General stay (§1094.5(g)):** Court may stay the administrative order pending judgment unless against the public interest. The burden is on the party opposing the stay to show public interest harm.

**Enhanced stay standard (§1094.5(h)):** For APA-governed agency decisions (GOV §§11500 et seq.) and BPC Division 2 licensee decisions: stay requires BOTH (1) likelihood of success on merits AND (2) no public interest harm. Harder standard to meet.

**Critical:** The penalty does not become moot if the petition was filed while the penalty was in effect, even if the penalty has since been served or completed (§1094.5(g)).

## Application to Active Cases

### CalVCB Appeal — A25-10117946

**Proceeding type:** CalVCB is a state agency. Victim compensation awards involve a hearing right under GOV §§13950-13969. The Board's denial of an application is a final administrative order after a proceeding in which evidence is taken.

**Standard of review analysis:**
- Has CalVCB made final determination? Denial letter = final order → §1094.5 ripe
- Was a hearing required by law? GOV §13960 requires CalVCB to provide opportunity for hearing → hearing required
- Evidence taken? CalVCB considers police reports, medical records, application forms → evidence taken
- Discretion vested? CalVCB determines eligibility, amount, covered expenses → discretion vested

**→ §1094.5 applies. Petition for writ of mandate in Superior Court is the correct vehicle.**

**Standard of review:** Is the applicant's claim to victim compensation a "fundamental vested right"? The Bixby vested rights analysis applies differently to new claims vs. established entitlements. CalVCB initial denial is likely **substantial evidence** review. However: the 13 FAIL findings in the A25-10117946 audit include APA procedural failures (shortened deadlines, single-source denial, failure to consider all evidence) that go to the **manner** ground — agency did not proceed in the manner required by law. That ground triggers §1094.5(b) regardless of the standard of evidence review.

**The "manner" attack on CalVCB:**
- Shortened deadlines without statutory authority → not proceeded in manner required by law
- Single-source denial (police report only, no independent investigation) → if GOV §13955 requires Board to consider available evidence, failure to do so = manner failure
- Deadline compression cutting off applicant's opportunity to submit additional evidence → fair trial violation

**The decision-findings link attack:**
- If the Board's findings cite the police report without addressing contradictory evidence (medical records, witness statements), the decision is not supported by the findings
- If the Board did not state findings at all, the Topanga doctrine applies: an agency that makes no findings has per se abused its discretion on the decision-findings link

### OAH Proceedings — Dr. Wiita

If the PC §1368 competency evaluation generated any OAH proceeding, the fair trial ground (§1094.5(b)(2)) is available: was the hearing conducted with the procedural protections of GOV §11513? Was there adequate cross-examination? Was hearsay used as the sole basis for a finding without satisfying the civil admissibility threshold?

## Practical Mandamus Filing Checklist

| Step | Action | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Confirm finality | Verify agency has issued final decision; no pending reconsideration | — |
| Identify proceeding type | APA (GOV §§11500+)? Special agency? CalVCB? Determines hearing requirements | — |
| Determine standard | Vested rights = independent judgment; all others = substantial evidence | — |
| File verified petition | Superior Court; name agency + official in charge as respondent | 30 days (many agencies); CCP §1094.6 for local agencies; check specific agency statute |
| Request administrative record | Include in petition or serve separate demand | Concurrent with filing |
| Apply for stay if needed | §1094.5(g) or (h) depending on agency type; serve with proof | Promptly after filing |
