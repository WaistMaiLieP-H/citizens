# Historical Chain — CAL_PROB_1801_CONSERVATORSHIP_APPOINTMENT
## Origin: Probate Code Recodification — Stats. 1990, Ch. 79

## The Pre-1990 Problem

California's conservatorship law before 1990 was fragmented across multiple chapters with inconsistent standards. Petitioners could exploit ambiguity in the grounds for appointment. Court oversight was minimal — hearings were often perfunctory, conservatees were rarely represented, and the court investigator function was underdeveloped.

The Britney Spears conservatorship later revealed to the public what advocates had documented for decades: the system was vulnerable to exploitation by family members seeking control of another person's assets or life decisions. The same exploitation was documented in cases involving elderly people, disabled people, and young adults with developmental disabilities.

## The 1990 Recodification

Stats. 1990, Ch. 79 enacted the modern Division 4 of the Probate Code — the comprehensive framework governing guardianship, conservatorship, and protective proceedings. The 1990 Act:
- Unified the grounds for appointment (§1801) with a clear element structure
- Established the clear-and-convincing evidence standard (§1801(e))
- Added the isolated-incidents exclusion (§1801(b))
- Created the court investigator function as a mandatory checkpoint (§1826)
- Established the periodic review obligation (§1851)

## The Limited Conservatorship Framework

§ 1801(d) reflects the state's policy on developmental disability from WIC § 4501 — developmentally disabled citizens shall receive services resulting in more independent, productive, and normal lives. The limited conservatorship was designed to be a protective mechanism, not a control mechanism. The Legislature explicitly rejected the presumption of incompetence for developmentally disabled adults.

## The Wound

The wound that §1801 tries to close is the one that opened decades before it: the power to take away a person's liberty under cover of protection. Every procedural requirement in §1801 — the burden of proof, the element structure, the isolated-incidents exclusion, the limited conservatorship protections — exists because without it, the system becomes an instrument of control rather than protection.

A conservatorship established without clear-and-convincing evidence, relying solely on isolated incidents, without the investigator's report, without proper notice, is not a protective conservatorship. It is a court-sanctioned deprivation. Every element the statute requires to be proved is an element that was routinely not proved before 1990.
