# Probate Code §1801 — Historical Chain

## The Power to Take Away a Person's Life

---

### I. The Wound

Conservatorship is the most powerful legal tool a court can use against a living person. It strips an adult of legal capacity — the right to make their own decisions about money, property, medical care, residence, and daily life. A conservator appointed by the court steps into the conservatee's shoes and makes those decisions instead.

The wound is not that conservatorships exist — some people genuinely need protection. The wound is that **the system operates with minimal accountability and is vulnerable to abuse.** Conservators have filed for appointment over people who were capable, seized assets that should have remained under the person's control, made medical decisions against the person's will, isolated people from family and friends, and done all of this under the cover of court authority with limited oversight.

The Britney Spears conservatorship (terminated 2021) brought national attention to the problem, but conservatorship abuse has been documented for decades — particularly against elderly people, disabled people, and people targeted by family members or professional conservators seeking access to their assets.

### II. The Framework

California's conservatorship statute (Probate Code Division 4) provides for:

- **General conservatorship** (§1801(a)-(c)) — for adults who are unable to provide for their personal needs or manage their financial resources. Applies to elderly, incapacitated, or disabled adults who need assistance.
- **Limited conservatorship** (§1801(d)) — for developmentally disabled adults. Designed to be less restrictive than general conservatorship, preserving as much autonomy as possible.
- **LPS conservatorship** (Welfare & Institutions Code §5000 et seq.) — for people who are gravely disabled due to mental illness. Separate statutory framework with different procedures.

### III. The Procedural Protections (and Their Failures)

The statute includes protections against abuse:

- **Petition, notice, and hearing** (§§1810-1835) — the proposed conservatee must be notified and has the right to attend the hearing
- **Court investigation** (§1826) — a court investigator must interview the proposed conservatee, evaluate the need for conservatorship, and report to the court
- **Appointment of counsel** (§1828.5) — the proposed conservatee has the right to counsel; the court must appoint counsel if requested
- **Periodic review** (§1851) — the court investigator must review the conservatorship periodically and report to the court
- **Accounting** (§2620) — the conservator must file regular accountings of the estate

The problem is enforcement. Court investigators are overworked and underresourced. Accountings may not be filed or may not be reviewed carefully. Periodic reviews may be cursory. The conservatee — who has been declared unable to manage their own affairs — is in the weakest possible position to challenge the conservatorship or report abuse.

### IV. The Steward's Discovery

The steward's investigation uncovered evidence suggesting a conservatorship may have been established in secret — potentially when the steward was a minor (age 14). If this is true, it would explain the pattern documented across multiple case files: property taken without consent, medical decisions made by others, communications intercepted, financial accounts controlled, identity documents held by others, and systematic isolation from support networks.

A secret conservatorship — one established without proper notice to the conservatee, or in a jurisdiction the conservatee doesn't know about — is a violation of every procedural protection the statute provides. It converts the most powerful protective tool in the law into an instrument of control.

### V. The Standard This Citizen Carries

This Citizen carries §1801 not as a neutral overview of conservatorship law but as an investigative framework: **Was a conservatorship established? Was it properly established? Is it being properly administered? Is it the mechanism enabling the documented pattern of control?** Every element of the statute — grounds, petition, notice, investigation, hearing, appointment, powers, duties, accounting, review, termination — is an investigative checkpoint.
