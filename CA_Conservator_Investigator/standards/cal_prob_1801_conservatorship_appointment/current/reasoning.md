# Reasoning — CAL_PROB_1801_CONSERVATORSHIP_APPOINTMENT

## Why Clear and Convincing Evidence

§ 1801(e) imposes the second-highest civil burden of proof in California because conservatorship is a fundamental deprivation of liberty and autonomy. The California Supreme Court recognized in Conservatorship of Wendland (2001) that a conservatorship depriving a conservatee of the right to refuse medical treatment requires more than a preponderance — the fundamental rights at stake require an elevated standard.

Clear and convincing evidence means the factfinder must have a high degree of probability that the fact is true — not just more-likely-than-not. For a conservatorship, this means the petitioner must present strong, unambiguous, contemporaneous evidence of the grounds — not equivocal, not remote, not secondhand.

## The Isolated Incidents Exclusion

§ 1801(b) contains a statutory anti-abuse provision: substantial inability to manage finances may NOT be proved solely by isolated incidents of negligence or improvidence. This exclusion was added because petitioners routinely relied on one or two bad financial decisions to argue for estate conservatorship. The legislature recognized that a single bad decision (investing in a bad stock, forgetting a bill) is not evidence of substantial inability — it is evidence of ordinary human fallibility.

For the steward's investigation: if a conservatorship of the estate was established, the supporting petition must show a PATTERN of financial incapacity, not isolated incidents. A petition relying on isolated incidents fails the §1801(b) test as a matter of law.

## The Limited Conservatorship Protective Framings

§ 1801(d) creates a protective wall around developmentally disabled adults:
1. Conservatorship must encourage self-reliance — not dependency
2. Ordered only to the extent necessitated by PROVEN limitations
3. Conservatee is NOT presumed incompetent
4. Conservatee retains ALL rights not specifically and individually designated to the conservator

This means a limited conservatorship cannot function as a blanket deprivation. Each right transferred must be specifically named. A conservatee under limited conservatorship retains every right not on the specific list — including rights to contract, to communicate, to associate, to access their own records.

## The "Subject to §1800.3" Gateway

§ 1801 opens with "Subject to Section 1800.3" — the least restrictive alternative requirement. The court must make an affirmative finding that less-restrictive alternatives (supported decision-making, powers of attorney, health care surrogates) have been considered and found inadequate before appointing a conservator. The least restrictive alternative finding is a prerequisite to §1801 appointment — not an optional finding.
