# Rule — CAL_PROB_1801_CONSERVATORSHIP_APPOINTMENT

**Citation:** Cal. Prob. Code § 1801  
**Standard:** Conservatorship Appointment — Grounds and Burden of Proof

## The Rule

Subject to Section 1800.3 (least restrictive alternative requirement), a court may appoint a conservator under the following conditions:

**§ 1801(a) — Conservator of the person:**  
A conservator of the person may be appointed for a person who is unable to provide properly for personal needs for physical health, food, clothing, or shelter.

**§ 1801(b) — Conservator of the estate:**  
A conservator of the estate may be appointed for a person who is substantially unable to manage financial resources or resist fraud or undue influence. **Statutory exclusion:** Substantial inability may NOT be proved solely by isolated incidents of negligence or improvidence.

**§ 1801(c) — Combined person and estate:**  
A conservator of the person and estate may be appointed for a person meeting both (a) and (b).

**§ 1801(d) — Limited conservatorship (developmentally disabled adults):**  
A limited conservatorship may be utilized only as necessary to promote and protect well-being; shall be designed to encourage maximum self-reliance and independence; shall be ordered only to the extent necessitated by proven mental and adaptive limitations. The conservatee SHALL NOT be presumed incompetent and retains all legal and civil rights except those specifically designated as legal disabilities and specifically granted to the limited conservator.

**§ 1801(e) — BURDEN OF PROOF:**  
The standard of proof for appointment is **clear and convincing evidence.**

## Operative Elements for Each Track

**Person conservatorship (§1801(a)):**
1. Proposed conservatee is unable (not merely less able) to provide for personal needs
2. Inability extends to physical health, food, clothing, or shelter
3. Proved by clear and convincing evidence

**Estate conservatorship (§1801(b)):**
1. Proposed conservatee is substantially unable to manage financial resources OR resist fraud or undue influence
2. Substantial inability MAY NOT be proved solely by isolated incidents
3. Proved by clear and convincing evidence
