# Conservatorship of Wendland, 26 Cal. 4th 519 (2001)

## The Case

Robert Wendland was severely brain-injured in a car accident. He was not in a persistent vegetative state — he had some consciousness and could perform limited tasks. His wife, as conservator, sought court authority to withdraw his feeding tube. His mother opposed.

The question was what standard of proof applies when a conservator seeks to withdraw life-sustaining treatment from a conservatee who is conscious but incapacitated.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **Clear and convincing evidence is required before a conservator can withdraw life-sustaining treatment from a conscious conservatee.** The conservator must prove by clear and convincing evidence either that (a) the conservatee would have wanted treatment withdrawn under the circumstances, or (b) withdrawal is in the conservatee's best interest.

2. **The conservatee's right to life is a fundamental constitutional interest.** When a conservatee is conscious, the presumption is in favor of continued life. The burden is on the person seeking to end treatment.

3. **A conservator's power over the conservatee's person does NOT include the unreviewable authority to end the conservatee's life.** Judicial oversight is required for decisions of this magnitude.

## The Rule

**A conservator cannot withdraw life-sustaining treatment from a conscious conservatee without clear and convincing evidence that the conservatee would have wanted it or that it is in the conservatee's best interest. The court must review the decision.**

## Why This Case Matters

Wendland establishes the outer boundary of conservator power: the conservator controls many aspects of the conservatee's life, but the most consequential decisions — life and death — require judicial review and the highest civil standard of proof. This principle extends beyond medical treatment to any decision that fundamentally alters the conservatee's life: the conservator's power is not unlimited, and the court retains oversight authority.
