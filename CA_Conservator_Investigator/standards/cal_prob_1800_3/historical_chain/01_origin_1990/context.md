# Historical Chain — CAL_PROB_1800_3_MINOR_CONSERVATEE
## Origin: 1990 Probate Code — Least Restrictive Alternative Framework

## Pre-1990: The Absence of Scope Limits

Before 1990, California conservatorship law did not have a clear least-restrictive-alternative requirement codified as a prerequisite to appointment. Courts had discretion to appoint conservators whenever they found the grounds were met — without independently assessing whether less restrictive measures could achieve the same protective goals.

## The 1990 Recodification — §1800.3 as the Gateway

Stats. 1990, Ch. 79 added §1800.3 as the gateway section preceding §1801. The placement is deliberate: before the court can even reach the §1801 grounds analysis, it must make an affirmative §1800.3(b) finding that conservatorship is the least restrictive alternative.

The minor conservatee limitation in §1800.3(a) reflects the Legislature's recognition that minors are already subject to comprehensive legal frameworks (parental authority, guardianship, juvenile court) that are more appropriate than general probate conservatorship. General conservatorship for an unmarried minor would be redundant with guardianship and would lack the developmental support framework the Legislature provided for the developmental disability track.

## The Supported Decision-Making Addition

California added supported decision-making agreements to §1800.3(c)'s list of alternatives in recent years, reflecting the national and international movement toward supported decision-making as a preferred alternative to guardianship and conservatorship for people with disabilities. The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (Article 12) recognizes supported decision-making as the preferred approach — substituted judgment (conservatorship) should be the last resort, not the default.

## The Wound

The wound is the gap between the least-restrictive-alternative requirement and its enforcement. The §1800.3(b) mandatory finding is often made in a cursory sentence in the order of appointment — "the court finds conservatorship is the least restrictive alternative" — without a meaningful inquiry into what alternatives were considered or why they were found inadequate. A finding made without inquiry is a checkbox finding, not a constitutional determination.
