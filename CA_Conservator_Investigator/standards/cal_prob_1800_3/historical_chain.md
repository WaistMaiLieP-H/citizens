# Probate Code §1800.3 — Historical Chain

## The Least Restrictive Alternative: The Constitutional Floor

---

### I. What This Section Does

Section 1800.3(b) is the single most important sentence in California conservatorship law:

**"A conservatorship of the person or of the estate shall not be granted by the court unless the court makes an express finding that the granting of the conservatorship is the least restrictive alternative needed for the protection of the conservatee."**

This is not discretionary language. It is mandatory. The court SHALL NOT grant a conservatorship unless it EXPRESSLY FINDS it is the LEAST RESTRICTIVE alternative. Every other arrangement must be considered and rejected first.

### II. What "Least Restrictive" Means

Section 1800.3(c) lists the alternatives the court must consider:
- **Supported decisionmaking agreements** — a person helps the individual make decisions without taking away their legal capacity
- **Powers of attorney** — the individual voluntarily delegates specific authority while retaining capacity
- **Health care surrogates** — a designated person makes medical decisions
- **Advance health care directives** — the individual's own instructions govern future medical decisions

The court must consider these alternatives "with current and possible supports." This means the court cannot just look at the person as they are — it must ask: with appropriate support, could this person manage their own affairs without a conservatorship?

### III. Why This Is the Constitutional Floor

The least-restrictive-alternative requirement is not just a statutory preference — it is a due process requirement. Taking away a person's legal capacity is a deprivation of liberty under both the Fourteenth Amendment and California Constitution Article I, §7. Due process requires that the deprivation be no greater than necessary to achieve the legitimate purpose (protection of the person).

A conservatorship imposed when a less restrictive alternative would serve is a due process violation — it deprives the person of more liberty than the Constitution permits.

### IV. For the Steward's Case

If a conservatorship was imposed:
- Did the court make an EXPRESS FINDING that conservatorship was the least restrictive alternative? If not, the appointment violates §1800.3(b).
- Were alternatives considered? Power of attorney? Supported decisionmaking? If not, the analysis was incomplete.
- Is the conservatorship STILL the least restrictive alternative? A person who works, parents, litigates, and builds a company is a person for whom less restrictive alternatives obviously exist.

The least-restrictive-alternative requirement applies at appointment (§1800.3), at every periodic review (§1851), and at termination hearings (§1863). It is a continuing obligation, not a one-time finding.
