# Reasoning — CAL_PROB_1800_3_MINOR_CONSERVATEE

## The Least Restrictive Alternative as a Constitutional Requirement

§ 1800.3(b) is not merely a policy preference — it is a constitutional requirement encoded in statute. The Supreme Court recognized in Zinermon v. Burch (1990) that deprivations of liberty by the state require procedural due process proportionate to the deprivation's severity. Conservatorship is one of the most severe deprivations of liberty available in civil law.

The least restrictive alternative requirement operationalizes the constitutional principle: if a person can have their needs met with less restrictive means (a power of attorney, a supported decision-making agreement), then the more restrictive conservatorship is not constitutionally justified. The §1800.3(b) mandatory finding is the court's certification that the constitutional threshold has been met.

## Why the Minor Conservatee Rule Matters for the Steward's Investigation

§ 1800.3(a) limits Probate Code conservatorship of a minor to married minors or minors whose marriages have been dissolved. An unmarried 14-year-old cannot be subject to a general Probate Code conservatorship under §1801.

This means: if a conservatorship was established over the steward when he was 14 and unmarried, it was not a general Probate Code conservatorship. The mechanism must have been either:
1. Guardianship (different statutory framework, different rights)
2. LPS conservatorship (WIC §5350+ — requires psychiatric basis, county initiation)
3. Some other proceeding — developmental disability track (Dept. of Developmental Services involvement)

This is a critical investigative finding: the type of proceeding determines what court records exist, in what county, and what due process protections were required.

## The Supported Decision-Making Alternative

§ 1800.3(c) requires consideration of supported decision-making agreements. This is a relatively new addition to California law, reflecting the broader movement away from substituted judgment (conservator decides for conservatee) toward supported judgment (conservatee decides with assistance).

A conservatorship established without considering supported decision-making — especially one established years ago when supported decision-making frameworks were less developed — may no longer satisfy the least restrictive alternative analysis as of today. The annual §1851 review must revisit whether the conservatorship remains the least restrictive alternative given current options.
