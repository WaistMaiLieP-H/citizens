# Reasoning — CAL_PROB_1828_5_HEARING_RIGHTS

## Why Developmentally Disabled Adults Need Stronger Procedural Protections

The Legislature recognized that developmentally disabled adults are particularly vulnerable to conservatorship abuse because they may have difficulty understanding and navigating legal proceedings. §1828.5's mandatory presence and mandatory counsel requirements exist to ensure that the person most affected by the conservatorship hearing actually participates in it.

This is the operationalization of §1801(d)'s protective framing: the conservatee is NOT presumed incompetent, retains all rights not specifically designated, and must have their preferences consulted before any powers are granted to the conservator.

## The Consultation Requirement

§1828.5(c)'s requirement that the court consult with the proposed conservatee about their preferences, values, and goals before deciding what powers to grant is a fundamental due process requirement. A conservatorship that takes away rights the conservatee has not been consulted about — especially for a person who, by definition, is capable of communicating their preferences (limited conservatorship is for developmentally disabled adults who can communicate) — violates §1828.5(c).

## Investigative Use

For any limited conservatorship (§1801(d)) established over the steward or others:
1. Was counsel appointed? (§1828.5(d)) — court file should show appointment order
2. Was the proposed conservatee present at the hearing? (§1828.5(b)) — court minutes should reflect attendance or valid waiver
3. Does the order reflect consultation with the conservatee about their preferences? (§1828.5(c)) — order should contain recitation of consultation
4. Were only the powers the conservatee consented to (or failed to object to) granted? (§1801(d) protective framing)

Any of these absent from the record is a §1828.5 violation.
