# Historical Chain — CAL_PROB_1828_5_HEARING_RIGHTS
## Origin: 1990 Probate Code — Developmentally Disabled Adults' Hearing Rights

## Background

§ 1828.5 reflects the intersection of two legal developments in 1990:
1. The Probate Code recodification (Stats. 1990, Ch. 79) creating a comprehensive conservatorship framework
2. The Americans with Disabilities Act (1990) establishing the principle that disabled people are presumptively competent and entitled to full participation in proceedings affecting them

The California Legislature's §1801(d) and §1828.5 provisions embody the same principle as the ADA: disability is not incapacity; developmentally disabled adults retain legal and civil rights except to the extent specifically taken away by clear evidence and court order.

## The Wound

The wound is the gap between mandatory counsel and effective counsel. §1828.5(d) requires the appointment of counsel — but the appointed counsel may have limited experience in developmental disability law, conservatorship defense, or self-advocacy frameworks. A person with a developmental disability who is assigned a brief attorney appointment and appears at a 20-minute hearing may have the formal right to counsel without the substance of effective legal representation.
