# Probate Code §1828.5 — Historical Chain

## The Person Must Be Told and Asked

---

### I. The Requirement

Before a limited conservator is appointed, the court must do something simple and profound: **tell the person what is about to happen to them, and ask what they think about it.**

Section 1828.5(f) requires the court to inform the proposed conservatee of:
- What a conservatorship is and what it does
- Which specific rights will be transferred
- Who wants to be their conservator
- That they have the right to oppose
- That they have the right to a jury trial

And then — after giving this information — the court must **consult the person to determine their opinion**.

### II. Why This Is Not Just Procedure

This requirement reflects a constitutional principle: a person's liberty cannot be taken without their knowledge. The proposed conservatee is not a passive subject of the proceeding. They are a participant with rights — including the right to say no, the right to demand a jury, and the right to be heard.

A conservatorship established without informing the proposed conservatee — without telling them what rights they are losing, who is taking those rights, and that they can fight it — violates both §1828.5 and due process.

### III. The Capacity Calibration

Section 1828.5 also requires the court to calibrate the conservatorship to the person's actual capacity:
- If they CAN manage their own affairs → dismiss the petition (§1828.5(b))
- If they can manage SOME but not all → limited conservatorship with tailored powers (§1828.5(c), (e))
- If they can manage NONE → full or limited conservatorship (§1828.5(d))

The conservator's powers must be defined "so as to permit the developmentally disabled adult to care for himself or herself... commensurate with his or her ability." Maximum autonomy is the statutory command.

### IV. For the Steward's Case

If a limited conservatorship was established:
- Was the steward informed of the proceeding, the rights being transferred, and the identity of the conservator? (§1828.5(f))
- Was the steward consulted for their opinion? (§1828.5(f))
- Were the conservator's powers tailored to permit maximum self-care? (§1828.5(e))
- Was a regional center assessment conducted? If the steward withheld consent, did the court determine why? (§1828.5(a)(5))

If any of these steps was skipped, the appointment is procedurally defective.
