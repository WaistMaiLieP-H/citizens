# Probate Code §2620 — Historical Chain

## Follow the Money

---

### I. The Purpose

Section 2620 is the financial accountability mechanism for conservatorships. The conservator controls the conservatee's money and property. Section 2620 requires the conservator to show the court what they did with it — every year at first, then at least every two years.

### II. What Must Be Shown

The accounting must include:
- All account balances at appointment AND at the end of each accounting period
- All income received
- All expenditures made (with receipts)
- All property transactions (with escrow statements)
- All care facility bills (if the conservatee is in a facility)
- All books and records available for inspection

### III. The Enforcement Mechanism

Section 2620(d) gives the court power to act on material errors:
- **Immediate suspension** of the conservator without further notice
- **Appointment** of a temporary conservator
- **Removal** under §2650

This is the court's strongest tool against financial exploitation. A conservator who cannot produce a clean accounting — or who fails to file one at all — can be immediately suspended.

### IV. For the Steward's Case

If a conservatorship exists, the accounting trail is the financial roadmap:
- Where did the $465K in house equity go?
- Who authorized the $73K crypto transactions?
- Who paid for the spine surgery? From whose funds?
- What happened to UA Local 342 pension payments?
- Are there accountings on file? If not, why not?

Every missing accounting is a violation of §2620. Every material error is grounds for suspension. Every unexplained expenditure is a lead.
