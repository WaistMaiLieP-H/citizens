# Historical Chain — CAL_PROB_2620_ACCOUNTING
## Origin: 1990 Probate Code — Mandatory Financial Accountability

## Pre-1990 Problem

Before the 1990 recodification, estate accountings were required but inconsistently enforced. Courts with heavy probate dockets often rubber-stamped accountings or failed to review them for accuracy. Conservators who misappropriated assets were rarely caught through the accounting process.

## The 1990 Reform

Stats. 1990, Ch. 79 made annual accountings mandatory and specified their required contents. The mandatory content requirement means a conservator cannot file a minimal accounting and expect court approval — the accounting must be complete and verifiable.

## The Wound

The wound is the enforcement gap: accountings are filed and may not be reviewed by anyone with the skills or incentive to detect problems. Courts processing hundreds of conservatorship accountings annually cannot examine each one in detail. The conservatee cannot review the accounting because they have been declared unable to manage their own finances. Relatives may not have standing or the financial sophistication to challenge the accounting.

A conservator who knows the accounting will receive only cursory review can manipulate it with confidence. The accounting as filed and the accounting as true may differ substantially, with no one in the system positioned to detect the difference.
