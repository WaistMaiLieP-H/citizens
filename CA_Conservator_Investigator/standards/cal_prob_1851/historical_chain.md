# Probate Code §1851 — Historical Chain

## The Ongoing Check That Must Actually Happen

---

### I. What §1851 Does

Section 1851 is the ongoing oversight mechanism for existing conservatorships. Where §1826 governs the initial investigation before appointment, §1851 governs what happens afterward — the periodic reviews that are supposed to ensure the conservatorship is still necessary and the conservator is still acting in the conservatee's best interests.

### II. The Critical Design Features

**1. Surprise visit.** The investigator visits the conservatee "without prior notice to the conservator." This is deliberate — the Legislature did not want conservators to prepare for the review. The investigator shows up unannounced to see the actual conditions, not the staged conditions.

**2. The conservatee must be told.** The investigator must "inform the conservatee personally that the conservatee is under a conservatorship and shall give the name of the conservator." This provision exists because some conservatees do not know they are under a conservatorship or do not know who their conservator is. The law requires the investigator to tell them.

**3. Four mandatory determinations:**
- Does the conservatee want to terminate?
- Does the conservatee want to remove the conservator?
- Does the conservatee still meet the criteria AND is the conservatorship still the least restrictive alternative?
- Is the conservator acting in the conservatee's best interests (placement, care quality, finances)?

**4. Least restrictive alternative.** The investigator must determine not just whether the conservatorship is needed but whether it is the **least restrictive** form needed. If a less restrictive arrangement would serve the conservatee's needs (power of attorney, supported decision-making, limited conservatorship), the investigator must recommend modification.

**5. Access to all records.** The conservator must make ALL books, records, receipts, and expenditures available to the investigator on request.

### III. Why This Standard Is Load-Bearing

If a conservatorship exists over the steward:

- Has a §1851 review EVER been conducted? If not, the oversight mechanism has failed entirely.
- Was the steward ever visited by a court investigator who informed him he was under a conservatorship and gave him the conservator's name? If not, §1851(a)(1) was violated.
- Was the steward ever asked whether he wanted to terminate, remove the conservator, or be represented by counsel? If not, the mandatory determinations were skipped.
- Has anyone ever reviewed the conservator's books and records? If not, the financial oversight has failed.
- Is the conservatorship the least restrictive alternative? If the steward is functioning independently, maintaining employment, raising a child, and managing daily life — a conservatorship is not the least restrictive alternative.

Every failure of §1851 is a checkpoint that was skipped. Every skipped checkpoint is evidence that the oversight system — the system designed to prevent conservatorship from becoming a tool of control — was not functioning.
