# Rule — CAL_PROB_1851_ANNUAL_REVIEW

**Citation:** Cal. Prob. Code § 1851  
**Standard:** Court Investigator Periodic Review — Conservatorship Oversight

## The Rule

After a conservatorship is established, the court investigator must conduct periodic reviews. The review must occur at least every two years. For limited conservatorships of developmentally disabled adults, the review must occur within one year of appointment and at least every two years after.

**Without prior notice to the conservator** (unless the court orders notice to prevent harm to the conservatee), the court investigator must visit the conservatee and determine all of the following:

**(A)** Whether the conservatee wishes to petition the court to terminate the conservatorship.

**(B)** Whether the conservatee wishes to petition the court to remove the conservator.

**(C)** Whether the conservatee still satisfies the criteria for appointment under §1801, and whether the conservatorship remains the LEAST RESTRICTIVE ALTERNATIVE for meeting the conservatee's needs.

**(D)** Whether the conservator is acting in the best interests of the conservatee, including:
- Quality of care provided
- Appropriateness of placement
- Physical and mental health treatment received
- Whether the conservatee has adequate access to their finances
- Whether the financial resources are being appropriately managed

**Document access obligation:** The conservator must make all books, records, receipts, and expenditures available to the court investigator upon request.

**Written findings:** The investigator must certify findings to the court at least 15 days before the scheduled review hearing. Copies must be delivered to the conservatee, the conservator, their attorneys, and the conservatee's closest relatives.

## What Absence of Compliance Records Proves

If a conservatorship has operated for multiple years without §1851 review records in the court file:
- Each year without a review is a separate §1851 violation
- The cumulative absence of reviews is evidence that the conservatorship operated entirely outside judicial oversight
- The absence of records is itself evidence — it proves the reviews never happened
