# Reasoning — CAL_PROB_1851_ANNUAL_REVIEW

## Why Ongoing Review Is Required

A conservatorship established at one point in time may not be appropriate years later. The conservatee may recover capacity. The conservator may become an abuser. The conservatee's needs may change. The circumstances that justified the appointment may no longer exist.

§ 1851 exists because the Legislature recognized that a conservatorship appointment is not a permanent determination of incapacity — it is a snapshot of the conservatee's condition at the time of appointment, subject to change. The periodic review requires the court to revisit that determination on a regular basis, using an independent investigator who visits the conservatee without the conservator's presence or prior knowledge.

## The Surprise Visit

§ 1851 specifically requires that the review occur WITHOUT prior notice to the conservator (unless the court orders otherwise). This is the Legislature's recognition that the conservator has a conflict of interest — the conservator controls the conservatee's environment and could stage a favorable presentation if given notice.

The surprise visit allows the investigator to see the conservatee's actual condition, not a curated version. A conservatorship characterized by isolation, inadequate care, or financial abuse may look very different when the investigator arrives unannounced.

## The Termination Question

§ 1851(a)(A) requires the investigator to ask the conservatee every review period whether they wish to petition to terminate the conservatorship. This is a mandatory question, not an optional one. The investigator must document the conservatee's answer.

For the steward's investigation: if a conservatorship has operated for years and the conservatee's repeated statements that they wish to terminate the conservatorship do not appear in the §1851 review records — either those records never existed, or the investigator failed to ask, or the answer was suppressed. All three outcomes are §1851 violations.

## The Least Restrictive Alternative Revisit

§ 1851(a)(C) requires the investigator to revisit the least restrictive alternative analysis at every review. If less restrictive alternatives have become available (e.g., supported decision-making, technology-assisted financial management), the conservatorship may no longer be justified. The failure to revisit this analysis at each review is a §1851 violation.

## Investigative Framework for the Steward

The §1851 review records are the audit trail of a conservatorship's existence. For any conservatorship spanning multiple years:

1. Request the complete court file — it should contain a §1851 review report for each review period
2. Count the number of review periods vs. the number of actual reviews
3. For each review that occurred: does the report reflect a genuine visit? Does it document the conservatee's answers to the mandatory questions?
4. Cross-reference the review records against the conservatee's actual life circumstances during that period

The absence of review records spanning years is the strongest possible evidence that the conservatorship operated as an instrument of control rather than protection.
