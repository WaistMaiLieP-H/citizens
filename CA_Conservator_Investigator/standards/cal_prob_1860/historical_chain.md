# Probate Code §§1860/1863 — Historical Chain

## The Exit That Must Be Available

---

### I. The Design

The termination framework has a critical structural feature: **the burden is on the conservatorship to justify its continuation, not on the conservatee to justify termination.**

Section 1863(c) says it plainly: "Unless the court determines, on the record and by clear and convincing evidence" that (1) the conservatee still meets the criteria AND (2) the conservatorship is the least restrictive alternative — "the court shall enter judgment terminating the conservatorship."

This means:
- The standard of proof for CONTINUING a conservatorship is clear and convincing evidence — the second-highest standard in civil law
- The default is TERMINATION — the conservatorship ends unless the court affirmatively finds it should continue
- The conservatee has the right to a JURY TRIAL on the question of termination
- The conservatee must be PRODUCED at the hearing (not just represented — physically present)

### II. Why This Matters

A conservatorship that cannot be terminated is not a protective arrangement — it is a cage. The Legislature designed §1863 to ensure that every conservatee has a path out:

1. **Any interested person can petition** (§1861) — the conservatee, the conservator, a friend, a relative
2. **The burden is on the conservatorship** — clear and convincing evidence, on the record
3. **Jury trial available** — the conservatee can demand that a jury, not a judge, decide
4. **Least restrictive alternative review** — even if criteria are met, the court must consider whether a less restrictive arrangement would serve

### III. For the Steward's Case

If a conservatorship exists:
- The steward can petition for termination under §1861
- At the hearing, the conservator must prove by clear and convincing evidence that the conservatorship is still needed AND is the least restrictive alternative
- The steward can demand a jury trial
- The steward must be produced at the hearing
- If the conservator cannot meet the burden, the court MUST terminate

A person who works, raises a child, manages daily life, navigates complex legal proceedings pro se, and builds a technology company is not a person who meets the criteria for conservatorship. The least-restrictive-alternative analysis alone should be dispositive.
