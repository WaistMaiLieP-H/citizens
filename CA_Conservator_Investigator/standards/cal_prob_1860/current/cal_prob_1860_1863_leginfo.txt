California Probate Code §§ 1860, 1863 — Termination of Conservatorship

§ 1860. Duration and Termination

(a) A conservatorship continues until terminated by the death of the conservatee or by order of the court pursuant to Section 1863.
(b) At a hearing under Section 1850 or on a petition to terminate under Section 1861, the court shall proceed as provided in Section 1863.
(c) If a conservatorship is established for the person of a married minor, the conservatorship does not terminate automatically if the marriage is dissolved.
(d) This section does not apply to limited conservatorships.

§ 1863. Termination Hearing

(a) The court shall hear and determine the matter according to the law and procedure relating to the trial of civil actions, INCLUDING TRIAL BY JURY IF DEMANDED BY THE CONSERVATEE.

(b) The conservatee shall be produced at the hearing, except:
  (A) When out of state and not the petitioner.
  (B) When unable to attend by reason of medical inability (must be established by licensed medical practitioner affidavit).
  (C) When the court investigator reports the conservatee expressly does not wish to attend, does not contest, and does not object to the conservator.

(3) Emotional or psychological instability is NOT good cause for absence unless attendance would cause serious and immediate physiological damage.

(c) UNLESS THE COURT DETERMINES, ON THE RECORD AND BY CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE, that:
  (1) the conservatee still meets the criteria for appointment under §1801(a) or (b); AND
  (2) a conservatorship remains the LEAST RESTRICTIVE ALTERNATIVE needed for protection under §1800.3(b);
THE COURT SHALL ENTER JUDGMENT TERMINATING THE CONSERVATORSHIP.

(d) If the court finds criteria still met by clear and convincing evidence, it shall determine whether to MODIFY existing powers to ensure least restrictive alternative.

(g) Termination does not preclude a new proceeding on the same or other grounds.

(h) If uncontested and both conservator and conservatee wish to terminate, the court may terminate WITHOUT AN EVIDENTIARY HEARING.
