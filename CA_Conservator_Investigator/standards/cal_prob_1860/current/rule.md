# Rule — CAL_PROB_1860_TERMINATION

**Citation:** Cal. Prob. Code §§ 1860-1863  
**Standard:** Conservatorship Termination — Rights of Conservatee

## The Rule

**§ 1860 — Right to petition for termination:**  
The conservatee or any interested person may petition the court at any time for an order terminating the conservatorship. The conservatee has the RIGHT to petition — this right cannot be taken away by the conservator.

**§ 1861 — Grounds for termination:**  
The court SHALL terminate the conservatorship if it finds:
- The conservatee no longer satisfies the criteria for appointment under §1801, OR
- The conservatee has regained the ability to provide for personal needs (person conservatorship), OR
- The conservatee is no longer substantially unable to manage finances (estate conservatorship)

**§ 1862 — Burden of proof:**  
On a petition for termination, the petitioner (conservatee) has the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence that the grounds for conservatorship no longer exist.

Note: At appointment, the petitioner bore the burden of proving grounds by clear and convincing evidence (§1801(e)). At termination, the conservatee bears the burden by preponderance. This asymmetry disadvantages the conservatee.

**§ 1863 — Jury trial right:**  
A conservatee who opposes a continued conservatorship is entitled to demand a jury trial. The right to jury trial applies to the contested conservatorship hearing, not just termination — a conservatee who wishes to contest the initial appointment has the right to jury trial.

## Practical Framework

The conservatee's right to petition for termination (§1860) combined with the §1851 investigator's annual obligation to ask whether the conservatee wishes to terminate creates a pipeline:
1. Investigator asks whether conservatee wishes to terminate (§1851(a)(A))
2. If yes: investigator documents that wish
3. Conservatee (or conservatee's attorney, or CAPTA guardian ad litem) files §1860 petition
4. Court holds hearing; conservatee bears preponderance burden
5. If grounds no longer exist: court MUST terminate (§1861 is mandatory, not discretionary)
