# Historical Chain — CAL_PROB_1860_TERMINATION
## Origin: 1990 Probate Code — Termination as Conservatee's Right

## The Pre-1990 Problem

Before 1990, termination of a conservatorship was treated as a judicial discretion question — not as a right the conservatee could assert. Courts were reluctant to question their own prior appointments. Conservatees who had recovered capacity had no clear statutory mechanism to compel termination.

## The 1990 Reform

Stats. 1990, Ch. 79 established §1860-1863 as a clear statutory framework: the conservatee has the right to petition, the court must terminate if grounds are gone, and the conservatee has the right to jury trial. These provisions shifted the termination analysis from judicial discretion to statutory mandate.

## The Wound

The wound is the access problem: the right to petition exists, but the ability to exercise it requires awareness of the right, access to counsel, and the physical and psychological capacity to pursue legal proceedings. A conservatorship that isolates, medicates, and controls a conservatee eliminates the practical ability to exercise the §1860 right even while leaving the formal right intact.

The formal right to petition for termination, surrounded by practical barriers to exercising it, is the modern form of a conservatorship that perpetuates itself through the passivity of its subject.
