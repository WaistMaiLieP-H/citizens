# Historical Chain — CAL_PROB_1826_INVESTIGATOR_PRE_APPOINTMENT
## Origin: 1990 Probate Code Recodification — Court Investigator Function

## The Pre-Investigation Era

Before the 1990 recodification established §1826 as a mandatory investigator function, California conservatorship hearings often proceeded with nothing more than the petitioner's declaration and a cursory court appearance. Proposed conservatees — often confused, isolated, or unaware of what was happening to them — appeared at hearings without knowing they had the right to oppose, without independent counsel, and without anyone in the room who had spoken to them independently about their wishes.

The result was predictable: conservatorships established over people who did not need them, did not want them, and were never told they could say no.

## The 1990 Reform

Stats. 1990, Ch. 79 made the court investigator mandatory for all conservatorship proceedings. The §1826 duties — personal interview, rights notification, family contact, medical record review, written report — were the Legislature's attempt to create an independent voice for the proposed conservatee within a system that had been dominated by petitioners.

## The Failure Mode: Checkbox Compliance

The §1826 investigation can be performed in form without substance. An investigator who visits a proposed conservatee for 15 minutes, reads from a script, and files a boilerplate report has technically complied with §1826 while defeating its purpose. Real compliance requires actual engagement — understanding the proposed conservatee's wishes, finding out whether they want to oppose, genuinely assessing whether the grounds are met.

The failure mode is not usually outright non-compliance. It is checkbox compliance: the report exists, but its contents are predetermined. For the steward's investigation, the question is not just whether the report exists — but what it says, whether it reflects genuine investigation, and whether the conservatee's expressed wishes appear in the record.

## The Wound

The wound is this: §1826 gives the proposed conservatee a voice. If the investigation was conducted without the conservatee's knowledge — if they were never interviewed, never informed of their rights, never told a petition was pending — then §1826 was violated in its most fundamental form. The gatekeeper did not protect the person it was designed to protect.
