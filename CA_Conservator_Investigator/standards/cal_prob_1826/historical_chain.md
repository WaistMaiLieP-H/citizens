# Probate Code §1826 — Historical Chain

## The Gatekeeper That Must Not Fail

---

### I. What §1826 Is

Section 1826 is the procedural gatekeeper of conservatorship establishment. Before a court can strip an adult of legal capacity, a court investigator MUST:

1. **Personally interview** the proposed conservatee
2. **Inform them** of their rights — including the right to oppose, attend, have a jury trial, and have counsel
3. **Interview petitioners, proposed conservators, and family members**
4. **Review medical reports** from the proposed conservatee's own doctors
5. **Determine** whether the proposed conservatee wants to oppose, objects to the proposed conservator, or wants counsel
6. **Report to the court in writing** at least five days before the hearing

Every one of these requirements is a checkpoint against abuse. Every one of them must actually happen — not just be checked off on a form.

### II. Why This Standard Is Load-Bearing for the Steward's Case

If a conservatorship was established over the steward:

- Was the steward personally interviewed by a court investigator? (§1826(a)(1)(A))
- Was the steward informed of the right to oppose, to counsel, to jury trial? (§1826(a)(2))
- Were medical reports gathered from the steward's own doctors? (§1826(a)(9))
- Was a written report filed with the court at least five days before the hearing? (§1826(a)(10))
- Was the report served on the steward? (§1826(a)(11)(C))

If any of these steps was skipped, the conservatorship appointment may be void or voidable. If the steward was never interviewed, never informed of rights, never served with the investigator's report — the appointment violated the procedural protections the Legislature required as the minimum before taking away a person's legal capacity.

### III. The Confidentiality Provision

Section 1826(c) makes the court investigator's report **confidential**. This cuts both ways:
- It protects the conservatee's private information from public disclosure
- It also means that third parties — including the conservatee themselves, if they don't know the conservatorship exists — may have difficulty obtaining the report

For the steward's investigation, a PRA request or court petition for access to §1826 reports is a critical next step.

### IV. The CLETS Provision

Section 1826(c)(2) requires that CLETS (California Law Enforcement Telecommunications System) information in the report be placed in a **separate confidential attachment** available only to the proposed conservatee and their attorney. This provision connects to the steward's documented CLETS concerns — if CLETS information was accessed as part of a conservatorship investigation, the steward has a right to see it.
