# Rule — CAL_PROB_1826_INVESTIGATOR_PRE_APPOINTMENT

**Citation:** Cal. Prob. Code § 1826  
**Standard:** Court Investigator Duties — Pre-Appointment Investigation

## The Rule

Before any conservatorship can be established, the court investigator MUST perform ALL of the following:

**§ 1826(a)(1) — Mandatory interviews:**
- (A) The proposed conservatee personally
- (B) All petitioners and all proposed conservators who are not petitioners
- (C) Spouse/RDP and first-degree relatives; if none, second-degree relatives to greatest extent possible
- (D) Second-degree relatives, neighbors, and close friends to greatest extent practical

**§ 1826(a)(2) — Mandatory rights notification to proposed conservatee:**
Must inform the proposed conservatee of: the contents of the petition and citation; the nature, purpose, and effect of the proceeding; the right to oppose the petition; the right to attend the hearing; the right to jury trial; the right to legal counsel; the right to court-appointed counsel if unable to retain counsel.

**§ 1826(a)(3)-(8) — Required determinations:**
- Whether proposed conservatee can attend the hearing and whether willing to attend
- Whether grounds exist (referencing §1821 supplemental information and §811 mental function deficits)
- Whether proposed conservatee wishes to oppose the conservatorship
- Whether proposed conservatee objects to proposed conservator or prefers another
- Whether proposed conservatee wishes to be represented by legal counsel
- Whether proposed conservatee is incapable of communicating a desire to vote (Elections Code §2208)

**§ 1826(a)(9) — Medical record review:**
Must gather and review relevant medical reports from the proposed conservatee's primary care physician and other health care providers.

**§ 1826(a)(10)-(11) — Report and delivery:**
Must report to the court in writing at least 5 days before the hearing. Must deliver the report at least 5 days before the hearing to: petitioner's attorney, proposed conservatee's attorney, proposed conservatee, and specified relatives.

**§ 1826(c) — Confidentiality:**
The report is confidential. Confidential medical information and CLETS information shall be in a separate attachment available only to the proposed conservatee and their attorney.

## Per Se Defect Rule

Failure to perform any mandatory §1826(a) duty is not a curable procedural irregularity — it is a jurisdictional defect in the appointment. An appointment made without the §1826 investigation violates the fundamental due process rights of the proposed conservatee.
