# Rule — CAL_PROB_811_CAPACITY_DETERMINATION

**Citation:** Cal. Prob. Code § 811  
**Standard:** Mental Function Deficits — Capacity Assessment Framework

## The Rule

For purposes of determining whether a person has the capacity to make any particular decision or perform any particular act, the court shall consider all of the following:

**§ 811(a) — Mental function deficits:**  
The deficit must involve one or more of the following mental functions:
1. Alertness and attention (including consciousness, orientation to time/place/person/reason, ability to attend/concentrate)
2. Information processing (including short/long-term memory, comprehension, reasoning, planning, sequencing complex tasks)
3. Thought processes (including delusions, hallucinations, disorganized thinking, thought disturbances)
4. Ability to modulate mood and affect

**§ 811(b) — Significant impairment:**  
A deficit in the above functions must be shown to significantly impair the person's ability to understand and appreciate the consequences of decisions, and to communicate in any way.

**§ 811(c) — Basis of judgment:**  
Capacity determination shall be based on evidence of substantially impaired cognitive ability — not solely on a diagnosis.

**§ 811(d) — Medical or mental health opinions:**  
Evidence must include evidence of specific functional deficits; capacity is NOT determined by diagnosis alone.

## The Diagnosis ≠ Incapacity Rule

§ 811 expressly prohibits determining incapacity based solely on a diagnosis. A diagnosis of dementia, schizophrenia, developmental disability, or any other condition does not by itself establish incapacity. The court must find specific functional deficits AND that those deficits significantly impair the specific ability at issue.

This means: a person with a developmental disability diagnosis retains capacity for decisions the disability does not functionally impair.
