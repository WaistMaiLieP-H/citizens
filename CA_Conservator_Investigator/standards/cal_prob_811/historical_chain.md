# Probate Code §811 — Historical Chain

## The Standard That Defines What "Incapacity" Actually Means

---

### I. Why §811 Is the Foundation

Before a conservatorship can be established, someone must determine that the proposed conservatee lacks capacity. Section 811 defines what that means — and more importantly, what it does NOT mean.

### II. The Four Mental Functions

Section 811 lists four categories of mental function. A capacity determination must identify a deficit in at least one:

1. **Alertness and attention** — consciousness, orientation, concentration
2. **Information processing** — memory, communication, recognition, reasoning, planning
3. **Thought processes** — disorganized thinking, hallucinations, delusions
4. **Mood and affect** — inappropriate emotional states

### III. The Two Critical Safeguards

**Safeguard 1: Correlation required (§811(a)).** It is not enough to show a mental function deficit exists. The deficit must CORRELATE with the specific decision or act in question. A person who has memory problems may still have capacity to make medical decisions if the memory deficit does not impair their ability to understand treatment options. Capacity is decision-specific, not global.

**Safeguard 2: Diagnosis alone is insufficient (§811(d)).** "The mere diagnosis of a mental or physical disorder shall not be sufficient in and of itself." A diagnosis of dementia, schizophrenia, depression, or any other condition does NOT automatically mean the person lacks capacity. The question is functional: can the person understand and appreciate the consequences of the specific decision?

### IV. For the Steward's Case

If a conservatorship was imposed based on a capacity determination:

- **Which mental function deficit was identified?** Was it documented with evidence, not just a diagnosis?
- **Was the correlation shown?** Between the deficit and the specific decisions the conservator was empowered to make?
- **Who made the determination?** Was it based on medical reports from the proposed conservatee's own doctors (§1826(a)(9)), or from a doctor chosen by the petitioner?
- **Was the Wiita CST report the basis?** If so, that report reviewed only two sources — a capacity finding based on two sources may not satisfy §811's evidence requirements.

Section 811 is the standard against which any capacity finding must be measured. A finding that doesn't identify a specific §811 deficit, doesn't show correlation to specific decisions, and relies on diagnosis alone rather than functional assessment fails the statute.
