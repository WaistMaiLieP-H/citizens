# CA Conservator Investigator — Citizen-Level Historical Chain

## The Investigative Framework: Eight Checkpoints

---

This Citizen carries eight standards. Together they form a chain of investigative checkpoints — each one is a question the law requires to be answered before, during, and at the end of a conservatorship. If any checkpoint was skipped, the conservatorship is procedurally defective. If multiple were skipped, the conservatorship may be void.

### The Chain

```
§811 (Capacity)
  ↓ Does the person actually have a mental function deficit?
  ↓ Does the deficit correlate to the specific decisions at issue?
  ↓ Diagnosis alone is never sufficient.
  
§1800.3 (Least Restrictive Alternative)
  ↓ Is conservatorship the LEAST restrictive option?
  ↓ Were alternatives considered? (POA, supported decisionmaking, surrogates)
  ↓ This question must be answered at appointment, review, AND termination.

§1801 (Appointment Grounds)
  ↓ Clear and convincing evidence that the person:
  ↓   (a) Cannot provide for personal needs, OR
  ↓   (b) Cannot manage finances / resist fraud (NOT from isolated incidents), OR
  ↓   (d) Is developmentally disabled (limited conservatorship — maximum autonomy required)

§1826 (Court Investigator — Initial)
  ↓ Was the person PERSONALLY INTERVIEWED?
  ↓ Was the person INFORMED of rights (oppose, attend, jury, counsel)?
  ↓ Were MEDICAL REPORTS from the person's OWN doctors gathered?
  ↓ Was a WRITTEN REPORT filed 5 days before hearing?

§1828.5 (Hearing Rights)
  ↓ Was the person TOLD what is happening?
  ↓ Was the person TOLD which rights are being transferred?
  ↓ Was the person ASKED what they think?
  ↓ Right to jury trial? Right to counsel?

§1851 (Periodic Review)
  ↓ SURPRISE VISIT (no notice to conservator)?
  ↓ Was the person TOLD they are under conservatorship?
  ↓ Does the person want to TERMINATE?
  ↓ Does the person want to REMOVE the conservator?
  ↓ Is conservatorship STILL the least restrictive alternative?
  ↓ Is the conservator acting in the person's BEST INTEREST?

§2620 (Accounting)
  ↓ Has the conservator filed ANNUAL/BIENNIAL accountings?
  ↓ Are all ACCOUNT STATEMENTS attached?
  ↓ Are all EXPENDITURES documented with receipts?
  ↓ Has the court REVIEWED the accountings?
  ↓ Where did the MONEY go?

§1860/1863 (Termination)
  ↓ BURDEN IS ON THE CONSERVATORSHIP to justify continuation
  ↓ Clear and convincing evidence REQUIRED to continue
  ↓ If criteria not met → court MUST terminate
  ↓ Conservatee has right to JURY TRIAL
  ↓ Conservatee must be PRODUCED at hearing
```

### The Cross-Citizen Connections

When the conservatorship investigation intersects other Citizens' domains:

- **Family law intersection:** If the conservator's authority affects custody (the conservatee is a parent), the Family Law Litigator's §3011 best-interest standard applies alongside the conservatorship framework.

- **Federal civil rights intersection:** If state actors (judges, investigators, public conservators) participated in an improper conservatorship, the US Federal Civil Rights Litigator's §1983 reaches them for due process violations.

- **State civil rights intersection:** If anyone — state actor or private person — interfered with the conservatee's rights through threats, intimidation, or coercion, the CA Civil Rights Litigator's Bane Act (§52.1) provides a cause of action.

- **Financial fraud intersection:** If the conservator used their authority to misappropriate assets, the accounting trail under §2620 provides the documentary evidence, and the civil litigation framework (Tort Claims Act, mandamus) provides the procedural tools.

### The Single Question

All eight standards reduce to one question:

**Was this conservatorship properly established, properly maintained, and still necessary — or is it an instrument of control operating without the safeguards the law requires?**

Every checkpoint that was skipped is evidence of the answer.
