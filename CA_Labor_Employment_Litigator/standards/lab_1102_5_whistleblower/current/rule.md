## Governing Provision

Cal. Lab. Code § 1102.5 (Stats. 1984, Ch. 1538; substantially amended through Stats. 2013, Ch. 781)

## The Three Prohibitions

### § 1102.5(a) — No Suppression Policy

Employer cannot adopt or enforce any rule **preventing** an employee from disclosing information to:
- A government or law enforcement agency
- A person with authority over the employee
- Another employee with investigative authority

**Trigger:** Employee has reasonable cause to believe the information discloses a violation of state or federal statute, or any rule or regulation — regardless of whether disclosure is part of their job duties.

This prohibits gag rules, confidentiality agreements that prevent reporting illegal conduct, and policies that discourage reporting.

### § 1102.5(b) — No Retaliation for Disclosure

Employer cannot retaliate against an employee for:
- Disclosing information (actual disclosure)
- Because employer **believes** the employee disclosed or **may disclose** (anticipated disclosure)

The anticipated disclosure protection is critical: retaliation can occur before the employee actually reports. An employer who fires a worker because they suspect the worker is about to report a violation has violated §1102.5(b) even if no report was ever made.

### § 1102.5(c) — No Retaliation for Refusal

Employer cannot retaliate against an employee for **refusing to participate** in an activity that would violate state or federal statute or any rule or regulation.

An employee who refuses to falsify records, refuses to participate in fraud, or refuses to follow an illegal order is protected — independently of whether they made any disclosure.

## Additional Provisions

**§ 1102.5(d) — Prior Employment Protection:**
Cannot retaliate for disclosures or refusals in **any former employment**. Whistleblower protection extends across job changes.

**§ 1102.5(h) — Family Member Protection:**
Cannot retaliate against an employee because a **family member** exercised protected rights. This prevents retaliating against one family member to punish another.

## Remedies

| Remedy | Type | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Civil penalty | $10,000 per employee per violation | Mandatory upon violation finding |
| Attorney fees | Reasonable fees + costs | Awarded to successful plaintiff |
| Other remedies | Reinstatement, back pay, damages | Available under other Labor Code provisions |

**The $10,000 per-violation penalty is awarded directly to the employee** — not to the state. Multiple violations = multiple penalties.

## The "Reasonable Cause to Believe" Standard

The employee does not need to be correct that a violation occurred. "Reasonable cause to believe" is an objective standard: would a reasonable person in the employee's position believe the disclosed information showed a legal violation? Actual violation is not required for protection.
