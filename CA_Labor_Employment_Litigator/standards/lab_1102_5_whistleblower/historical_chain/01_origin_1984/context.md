## Origin: Stats. 1984, Ch. 1538

§1102.5 was enacted in 1984 as California's general whistleblower protection statute. The 1984 version covered disclosures to government agencies only. The key expansions came later:

**2003 Amendment:** Extended protection to disclosures to persons with authority over the employee (internal reporting protected, not just external agency reporting).

**2013 Amendment (Stats. 2013, Ch. 781):** Major expansion — added §1102.5(b) anticipated disclosure protection ("because the employer believes that the employee disclosed or may disclose"); added §1102.5(h) family member protection; added $10,000 per-violation civil penalty.

The 2013 amendment was the transformative one: it changed §1102.5 from a backward-looking protection (retaliation for past disclosure) to a forward-looking one (retaliation for anticipated future disclosure). This matched emerging evidence of how sophisticated retaliation actually operates.

## Historical Gap This Filled

Before §1102.5, California employees who disclosed employer violations had:
- Common law wrongful discharge (Tameny tort) — required public policy violation, hard to establish for regulatory disclosures
- Federal sector-specific whistleblower statutes — narrow coverage
- No general protection for disclosing any state regulatory violation

§1102.5 created that general protection. The 2013 amendments made it effective against the most common retaliation pattern: preemptive action against suspected future whistleblowers.
