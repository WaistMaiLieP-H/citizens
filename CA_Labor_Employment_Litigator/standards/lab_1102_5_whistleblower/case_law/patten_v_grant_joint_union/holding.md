# Patten v. Grant Joint Union High School District (2005) — § 1102.5 Case Law

**Citation:** Patten v. Grant Joint Union High School District (2005) 134 Cal.App.4th 1378
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Third District
**Status:** UNVERIFIED — steward must confirm at Westlaw

## Holding (from training knowledge — UNVERIFIED)

The Court of Appeal has held that §1102.5 protects disclosures to supervisors within the employing entity, not only to external government agencies. The "reasonable cause to believe" standard is assessed objectively — the employee need not prove the violation actually occurred, only that they reasonably believed it had occurred. Causation requires showing that the protected disclosure was a "contributing factor" in the adverse employment action.

---

## Key Doctrine

1. **"Reasonable cause" is objective:** Courts measure from the perspective of a reasonable employee with the same information. The employer's claim that no violation occurred is irrelevant to the §1102.5 analysis if the employee's belief was objectively reasonable.
2. **Contributing factor causation:** The employee need not show the disclosure was the sole reason for adverse action — only that it was a contributing factor. Burden then shifts to employer to prove it would have taken the same action absent the protected disclosure.
3. **Internal reporting protected:** Disclosure to supervisors and internal investigators constitutes protected disclosure under §1102.5.
