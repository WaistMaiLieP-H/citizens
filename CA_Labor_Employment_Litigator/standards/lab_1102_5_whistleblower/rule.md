# LAB § 1102.5 — Whistleblower Protection: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Labor_Employment_Litigator / lab_1102_5_whistleblower
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## What § 1102.5 Prohibits

Three independent prohibitions:

**§1102.5(a) — No anti-disclosure policy:** An employer cannot make, adopt, or enforce any rule, regulation, or policy that prevents an employee from disclosing information to government agencies, law enforcement, supervisors, or any person authorized to investigate violations — if the employee has reasonable cause to believe the information discloses a violation of any statute or regulation.

**§1102.5(b) — No retaliation for disclosure:** An employer cannot retaliate against an employee for disclosing, or because the employer believes the employee may disclose, a violation to any of the above recipients.

**§1102.5(c) — No retaliation for refusal:** An employer cannot retaliate against an employee for refusing to participate in an activity that would result in a violation of law.

---

## The "Reasonable Cause to Believe" Standard

The employee need not prove that a violation actually occurred. They need only show they had "reasonable cause to believe" the disclosed information showed a violation. This is an objective standard measured from the employee's perspective — did a reasonable employee in that position have reason to believe the law was being violated?

This is the same standard used for probable cause determinations in criminal law. It is a low bar that protects the reporting, not the underlying truth of the allegation.

---

## Covered Recipients of Disclosure

§1102.5 protects disclosures to:
- Any government agency
- Any law enforcement agency
- A person with authority over the employee
- Another employee who has authority to investigate, discover, or correct the violation
- Any public body conducting an investigation, hearing, or inquiry

**Key expansion:** Unlike some whistleblower statutes, §1102.5 covers internal disclosures — reporting to a supervisor or internal investigator is protected. The employee does not need to go external to be protected.

---

## Family Member Extension — § 1102.5(h)

An employer cannot retaliate against an employee because the employee is a **family member** of a person who engaged in protected disclosures. This extends protection horizontally — if a family member of the disclosing worker is employed by the same employer, retaliating against that family member is an independent §1102.5(h) violation.

---

## Remedies

| Remedy | Basis |
|---|---|
| $10,000 civil penalty per employee per violation | §1102.5(f)(1) |
| Actual damages | General civil action |
| Reinstatement | Available in civil action |
| Attorney's fees | §1102.5(j) — court authorized to award to successful plaintiff |

The $10,000 penalty is per employee per violation — not per lawsuit. Multiple violations against one employee = multiple penalties.

---

## Procedure

**Track 1 — Labor Commissioner:** Administrative complaint to the Labor Commissioner. Commissioner investigates and assesses civil penalty.

**Track 2 — Civil action in superior court:** Plaintiff files directly in superior court. Attorney's fees available under §1102.5(j).

**SOL:** California Labor Code §1102.5 claims generally governed by CCP §338(a) — 3-year SOL for statutory penalties. Confirm with superior court civil action practice.
