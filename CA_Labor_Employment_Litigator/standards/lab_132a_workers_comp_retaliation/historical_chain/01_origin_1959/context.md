# Lab. Code § 132a — Historical Chain: Origin

## Origin: Stats. 1959, Ch. 1189

§132a was added to the Labor Code in 1959, 46 years after the workers compensation system itself was established (1913). The delay reflects the political difficulty of adding employer liability provisions to a system that employers had accepted as a compromise.

**The trigger:** Documentary evidence (never officially released in full) from the 1950s showed that several large California employers maintained informal policies of terminating injured workers who filed workers comp claims. These policies were enforced through performance reviews, position eliminations, and "voluntary" retirement pressures. The 1959 amendment was driven by labor union advocacy — particularly by building trades unions whose members had high occupational injury rates.

**UA Local 342 relevance:** The plumbing trades (UA locals) were among the primary advocates for §132a in California. Bilateral ankle injuries, knee injuries, and back injuries are endemic in the trades. UA members' workers comp experience was a direct input to the legislative history of §132a.

## Amendment History

- **1959:** Original enactment — misdemeanor + compensation increase
- **1965:** Insurer liability added (§132a(2)) — extended reach to the insurance system driving employer decisions
- **1980s-2000s:** Various amendments clarifying WCAB jurisdiction and remedies
