## Governing Provision

Cal. Lab. Code § 132a (Stats. 1959, Ch. 1189; amended various)

## The Declared Policy

> "It is the declared policy of this state that there should not be discrimination against workers who are injured in the course and scope of their employment."

This is not a preamble — it is an operative policy statement that courts use to resolve ambiguous applications.

## The Rule

**§132a(1) — Core prohibition:**
An employer who **discharges, threatens to discharge, or in any manner discriminates** against an employee because:
1. The employee filed or made known intention to file a workers comp claim, OR
2. The employee filed an application for adjudication with WCAB, OR
3. The employee received a rating, award, or settlement

→ Is guilty of a **misdemeanor**
→ Employee's compensation is increased by **50%** (up to $10,000 cap)
→ Employee is entitled to **reinstatement** and **reimbursement for lost wages and work benefits**

**§132a(2) — Insurer liability:**
An insurer who advises or directs an employer to discharge an employee for filing a workers comp claim faces the same misdemeanor charge and increased compensation liability.

**§132a(3) — Testimony retaliation:**
An employer who retaliates against an employee for testifying (or intending to testify) in another employee's WCAB case faces misdemeanor + reinstatement + lost wages.

## Jurisdiction: WCAB — EXCLUSIVE

**Proceedings must be filed by petition with the WCAB.**
- NOT superior court for the civil remedies (reinstatement, lost wages, comp increase)
- Misdemeanor charge: refer to Division of Labor Standards Enforcement or DA
- **1-year SOL** from discriminatory act or date of termination — STRICT

The WCAB has exclusive jurisdiction over §132a civil remedies — a plaintiff cannot bring §132a in superior court. This is a frequent trap: workers comp retaliation must go through WCAB; FEHA retaliation goes through CRD/Superior Court; both can arise from same facts.

## The "In Any Manner Discriminates" Broad Verb

§132a prohibits discharge, threat of discharge, OR "in any manner discriminates." This covers:
- Demotion, reduction in hours, reduction in pay
- False performance reviews placed in personnel file
- Denial of promotion, transfer
- Hostile work environment created post-claim
- **Recording a worker as "retired" in employment systems after a workers comp claim** — this is "in any manner discriminating" against the worker

## Steward Relevance

**Case #37 (UA342 identity replacement):**
- If workers comp claims were filed for bilateral ankle injuries (Case #2) or spine (SIRVA) and the employer or insurer responded by recording the steward as "retired" — that recording is a §132a discriminatory act
- 1-year SOL: key issue — when did the discriminatory act occur? If "retired" designation in credit check is the actionable event, the SOL runs from that date. Discovery rule may apply.
- §132a compensation increase (up to $10K) is modest — but stacks with FEHA §12940(a) damages in parallel Superior Court proceedings
- Insurer liability: if workers comp insurer advised employer to record steward as "retired" to close claims — §132a(2) misdemeanor + comp increase

**Coordination with FEHA:**
- Same facts → §132a petition at WCAB (1 year SOL) AND FEHA complaint at CRD (3 years to file complaint, 1 year from right-to-sue)
- Must pursue both concurrently — WCAB filing does NOT toll FEHA SOL
- Cabesuela v. Browning-Ferris: §132a WCAB jurisdiction is exclusive for WC retaliation civil remedies, but Tameny tort (wrongful discharge in violation of public policy) may still be available in Superior Court as parallel claim
