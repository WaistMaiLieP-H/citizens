# LAB § 132a — Workers' Compensation Retaliation: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Labor_Employment_Litigator / lab_132a_workers_comp_retaliation
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## What § 132a Prohibits

Section 132a makes it a misdemeanor for any employer to discharge, threaten to discharge, or in any manner discriminate against an employee because the employee:

1. Filed a workers' compensation claim
2. Made known their intention to file a workers' compensation claim
3. Filed an application for adjudication
4. Received a workers' compensation rating, award, or settlement

The trigger is the claim or the signal of an intent to claim — not the resolution of the claim. An employer who fires a worker the day they mention a workplace injury has violated §132a before any formal claim is filed.

---

## Who Can Violate § 132a

**Employers (§132a(1)):** Any employer — direct discharge, threat of discharge, or any discrimination in employment.

**Insurers (§132a(2)):** Any workers' compensation insurer that advises, directs, or threatens an employer to discharge a worker because of a claim is independently liable — the insurer is not shielded by the employer's act. This is the critical provision when insurance carriers are involved in employment decisions. Carrier communications recommending discharge of an injured worker are discovered through subpoena of claims-handling records.

---

## Remedies Under § 132a

| Remedy | Amount |
|---|---|
| Increased compensation | 50% of awarded compensation, up to $10,000 |
| Costs and expenses | Up to $250 |
| Reinstatement | Yes — mandatory if sought |
| Reimbursement for lost wages and work benefits | Yes — full make-whole |

The 50% increase is on TOP of the workers' compensation award, not in lieu of it. The employee receives the underlying compensation award PLUS up to $10K additional.

---

## Jurisdiction and Procedure

**Forum:** Workers' Compensation Appeals Board (WCAB) — NOT superior court.

Filing a §132a claim in superior court is a jurisdictional error. The WCAB has exclusive jurisdiction over the petition for increased compensation, reinstatement, and lost wages. The superior court or district attorney handles the misdemeanor criminal charge — the WCAB cannot try misdemeanors.

**Procedure:** File a petition with the WCAB.

**SOL:** One year from the discriminatory act or date of termination. This is a hard one-year limit — shorter than most California employment SOLs. SOL management is critical.

---

## The "In Any Manner Discriminates" Standard

"In any manner discriminates" is broader than discharge alone. It includes:
- Reduction in hours or pay
- Demotion
- Hostile work environment following claim filing
- Transfer to less desirable position
- Failure to promote
- Any adverse employment action temporally connected to the claim

## Application to This Citizen's Cases

### UA342 Pension Identity Replacement

If Michael Hartmann filed a workers' compensation claim in connection with bilateral ankle injuries or any other occupational injury BEFORE the pension was frozen or the employment status was altered to "retired" — the adverse employment action following a WC claim is the exact §132a pattern.

**Key question for steward:** Identify the date of any workers' compensation claim or formal injury report made through UA342 or any employer. Then compare to date of pension freeze / "retired" credit report entry. Temporal sequence is the core of the §132a case.

### Bilateral Ankles / SIRVA

Both injuries are occupational injury candidates. If claims were filed or signaled, and adverse employment consequences followed, §132a applies to each independently.

---

## One-Year SOL Warning

The one-year SOL from discriminatory act means that if the pension freeze or identity replacement occurred more than one year ago, the WCAB petition for §132a relief is time-barred UNLESS:
- The discriminatory act was concealed (potential discovery rule tolling)
- There was a continuing violation

**Steward must determine the exact date of the discriminatory act.** If it is within one year of today (2026-04-13), file immediately. If over one year, evaluate tolling arguments before the WCAB.
