## Holding (from training knowledge — UNVERIFIED)

California courts have consistently held that the temporal connection between a workers' compensation claim and an adverse employment action raises a rebuttable inference of discrimination. The employer bears the burden of establishing a non-discriminatory reason for the adverse action. The WCAB applies a burden-shifting framework analogous to McDonnell Douglas in FEHA cases, though §132a does not expressly codify this.

---

## Key Doctrine

1. **Temporal proximity:** Close timing between claim filing and adverse action is circumstantial evidence of the discriminatory motive required by §132a.
2. **"In any manner discriminates" broadly construed:** Courts have applied §132a to a wide range of adverse actions beyond termination — including schedule changes, demotions, and hostile work environment following claim filing.
3. **Insurer liability is independent:** An insurer who directed the discharge can be joined as a direct §132a defendant; the insurer's liability does not depend on the employer's technical employment decision.
