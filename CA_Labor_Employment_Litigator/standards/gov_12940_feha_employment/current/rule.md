## Governing Provision

Cal. Gov. Code § 12940 (Fair Employment and Housing Act; Stats. 1980, Ch. 992; substantially amended through Stats. 2023)

## The Core Prohibition (§12940(a))

An employer cannot, because of a protected characteristic, **refuse to hire, discharge, or discriminate** in compensation or terms, conditions, or privileges of employment. Protected characteristics include physical disability, mental disability, medical condition, age, sex, race, national origin, gender identity, and veteran/military status.

**The "False Retirement" Theory:** If an employer (or entity acting as an employer) records a worker as "retired" in employment systems when the worker did not voluntarily retire, that false designation functions as a constructive discharge under §12940(a) — if the false designation was motivated by the worker's physical disability, medical condition, or age. The false record deprives the worker of continued employment and all its associated benefits.

## The Accommodation Triad (§§12940(m) + 12940(n))

### § 12940(m) — Failure to Make Reasonable Accommodation

Employer must make reasonable accommodation for the **known** physical or mental disability.
- "Reasonable" means effective — not merely token
- No obligation if accommodation would cause undue hardship
- SEPARATE cause of action from §12940(a) discrimination — a worker can be treated equally but still be denied accommodation
- Requesting accommodation is independently protected under §12940(m)(2)

### § 12940(n) — Failure to Engage in Interactive Process

Employer must engage in **timely, good-faith, interactive process** to determine effective accommodations.
- Triggered by request for accommodation OR employer's knowledge of disability
- Failure to respond to accommodation request = stand-alone §12940(n) violation even if no accommodation was needed
- "Timely" — unreasonable delay in engaging is itself the violation
- An employer who says nothing and does nothing when it knows an employee has a disability violates §12940(n) per se

**Identity Replacement Application:** If the steward's disability (spine, ankles, SIRVA) was known to an employer, and the employer responded by recording the worker as "retired" instead of engaging in the interactive process — that is a §12940(n) violation AND a §12940(m) failure AND (if disability-motivated) a §12940(a) discriminatory discharge.

## The Retaliation Provision (§12940(h))

Cannot discharge or discriminate against any person because the person:
- Opposed practices forbidden under FEHA
- Filed a complaint, testified, or assisted in any FEHA proceeding

**Application:** If any employment adverse action occurred after the steward sought workers comp, filed a disability claim, or opposed discriminatory treatment — §12940(h) retaliation analysis applies.

## Protected Characteristics Relevant to Cases

| Characteristic | Basis |
|---|---|
| Physical disability (spine, ankles, shoulder/SIRVA) | §12940(a) + §12940(m) |
| Medical condition (spine surgery, SIRVA) | §12940(a)(2) |
| Age (if "retired" designation was age-motivated) | §12940(a)(5) |
| Veteran/military status | §12940(a) |
