# Gov. Code § 12940 — Historical Chain: Origin

## Origin: Stats. 1980, Ch. 992 — Fair Employment and Housing Act

The Fair Employment and Housing Act consolidated California's prior fair employment statutes (the California Fair Employment Practice Act of 1959 and the Rumford Fair Housing Act of 1963) into a single comprehensive framework administered by the Department of Fair Employment and Housing (DFEH, now Civil Rights Department — CRD after Stats. 2021, Ch. 357).

**Key design principle:** The Legislature intentionally made FEHA broader than Title VII and the ADA. This was a state sovereignty statement: California workers would have stronger protections than the federal floor.

**The Physical/Mental Disability Framework:** The 1992 California ADA-equivalent amendments brought disability discrimination into FEHA's framework with a broader definition of "disability" than the federal ADA (Gov. Code §12926) — California's definition does not require the "major life activity" limitation that federal ADA requires. Any physical or mental impairment that limits a major life activity OR that an employer regards as limiting is covered.

**The Accommodation Mandate:** §12940(m) and §12940(n) were specifically added and strengthened in response to documented employer practices of ignoring disabled workers' needs, then documenting performance issues (which the disability caused) as the basis for termination. The interactive process requirement was designed to create a paper trail that exposes this pattern.

## CHRONICLE Flag

No CHRONICLE restriction. FEHA's history includes protection of all groups including historically discriminated against communities — the statute is remedial and inclusive. No discriminatory content in the historical chain. Full documentation maintained.
