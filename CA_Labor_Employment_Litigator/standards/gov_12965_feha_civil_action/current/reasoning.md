# Gov. Code § 12965 — FEHA Civil Action Procedure — Reasoning

**Standard ID:** `gov_12965_feha_civil_action`
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Date:** 2026-04-12

---

## Why Mandatory Exhaustion Exists

§12965 requires CRD complaint filing as a precondition (exhaustion of administrative remedy) before a civil action can be filed. This exhaustion requirement reflects the Legislature's preference for agency investigation and conciliation as primary resolution mechanisms — consistent with the California view that regulatory agencies are equipped to handle discrimination patterns that courts see only as individual cases.

The practical consequence: a worker who discovers FEHA violations and immediately files in Superior Court (without the CRD complaint) will have the case dismissed for failure to exhaust. The exhaustion requirement is jurisdictional — courts cannot exercise jurisdiction over an unexhausted FEHA claim.

## The 2019 SOL Extension — Why It Matters for Old Injuries

Stats. 2019, Ch. 709 extended the CRD complaint filing SOL from 1 year to 3 years from the discriminatory act. This was a significant expansion driven by the recognition that many victims (especially those subject to control and isolation) cannot access legal help within 1 year of the discriminatory act.

**For the steward's cases:** The 3-year SOL from the discriminatory act means that any FEHA violation that occurred within the past 3 years from the CRD complaint filing date is preserved. Employment discrimination that occurred more than 3 years ago may require discovery rule tolling arguments (the act was not discoverable until recently) or continuing violation doctrine arguments (the discrimination is ongoing).

**Continuing violation doctrine:** A pattern of discriminatory acts that continues to the present may be treated as a single ongoing violation — with the SOL running from the last act in the pattern, not the first. If the false "retired" designation continues to affect employment records today (it appears in credit checks, pension records, etc.), each day the employer fails to correct it may be a continuing discriminatory act.

## The Asymmetric Fee Standard as Strategic Design

The §12965(c)(6) asymmetric fee standard (plaintiff may get fees, defendant gets fees only for frivolous actions) is a deliberate legislative choice to make FEHA a viable plaintiff-friendly forum. Without asymmetric fees, the risk of paying the employer's attorney fees deters valid claims — especially by workers with limited resources against large institutional defendants.

The asymmetry means: a worker with a good-faith FEHA claim can litigate without fear that losing will result in a catastrophic fee award against them. This is the same design philosophy as §15657.5 (elder financial abuse mandatory fees) and §1102.5 (attorney fees to successful plaintiffs) — the Legislature uses fee structures as enforcement mechanisms.

## The Mandatory Dispute Resolution Step as Opportunity

§12965(a)(2) requires mandatory dispute resolution before CRD files a civil action. This is not merely a delay — it is a free mediation opportunity. For the steward's employment claims:
- CRD mandatory dispute resolution is at no cost
- The employer must participate
- A successful resolution here avoids years of litigation
- An unsuccessful resolution creates a clear record that the employer refused to remedy the violation — which is relevant evidence in the subsequent civil action

## The Right-to-Sue Notice as the Trigger for Damages

The right-to-sue notice is not just a procedural formality — it is the event that starts the 1-year civil action SOL and opens the door to full FEHA remedies in Superior Court (back pay, front pay, emotional distress, punitive damages, attorney fees). Missing the 1-year right-to-sue deadline is fatal to the civil claim and cannot be cured. This deadline must be calendared from the moment the notice is received.
