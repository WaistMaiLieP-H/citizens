# Gov. Code § 12965 — FEHA Civil Action Procedure — Rule

**Layer:** 1 — Current Operative Rule
**Date:** 2026-04-12

## Governing Provision

Cal. Gov. Code § 12965 (Stats. 1980, Ch. 992; last amended Stats. 2022)

## The FEHA Litigation Pathway — Three Steps

### Step 1: File Verified Complaint with CRD

File a verified complaint with the Civil Rights Department (formerly DFEH).
- **3-year SOL** from date of discriminatory act (Stats. 2019, Ch. 709 extended from 1 year)
- Complaint must be verified (signed under penalty of perjury)
- Complaint triggers CRD investigation

### Step 2: Mandatory Dispute Resolution

Before CRD files a civil action, all parties participate in **mandatory dispute resolution** — free of charge — in CRD's internal dispute resolution division. This is not optional for the department.

**Tolling:** The one-year right-to-sue SOL is tolled during mandatory dispute resolution proceedings.

### Step 3: Right-to-Sue Notice + Civil Action

**150-day trigger:** If CRD does not bring a civil action within 150 days, complainant may request a right-to-sue notice.

**Right-to-sue notice:** CRD issues notice; complainant has **1 year from the date of the notice** to file in Superior Court.

**Venue:** Superior Court of any county where:
- Unlawful practice was committed
- Employment records are maintained
- Complainant would have worked
- Defendant's residence or principal office

## The SOL Framework — Nested Deadlines

| Limitation | Period | Triggers |
|---|---|---|
| File CRD complaint | 3 years from discriminatory act | Discriminatory act date |
| CRD bring action | Within 1-2 years of complaint | CRD decision deadline |
| Request right-to-sue | After 150 days if no CRD action | Or earlier if CRD decides not to proceed |
| File civil action | 1 year from right-to-sue notice | Notice issuance date |
| Tolling (dispute resolution) | During CRD dispute resolution | Automatic |
| Tolling (concurrent EEOC) | During EEOC investigation | §12965(e)+(f) |

**For identity replacement/false retirement cases:** The 3-year complaint SOL runs from the discriminatory act — which may be the date the employer recorded "retired" in employment systems. If that date is unknown, the discovery rule tolls until the worker knew or should have known. The credit check that first revealed "retired at 44" may be the discovery date.

## Attorney Fees — Asymmetric Standard (§12965(c)(6))

**Plaintiff's attorney fees:** Discretionary — court "may" award fees to prevailing plaintiff.
**Defendant's attorney fees:** Available ONLY if the court finds the action was "frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless."

This asymmetric standard means:
- A plaintiff who wins may get attorney fees
- A plaintiff who loses does NOT automatically pay defendant's fees
- Defendant must prove the case was frivolous to get fees — a high bar

This asymmetry is why FEHA is attorney-friendly for plaintiff's counsel: the downside of losing is generally no fee award against the plaintiff.

**Note:** Compare with §15657.5 (elder financial abuse) where attorney fees for successful plaintiff are mandatory; under FEHA §12965(c)(6) they are discretionary. FEHA cases must be large enough in damages to make attorney fee award likely.

## Available Relief (§12965(d))

In FEHA civil actions, courts may award:
- Back pay and front pay
- Emotional distress damages
- Punitive damages (no MICRA-style cap for FEHA)
- Injunctive relief (reinstatement, policy changes, training requirements)
- Attorney fees + expert witness fees

No damages cap applies to FEHA claims. City of Moorpark v. Superior Court (1998) confirmed full damage availability.

## Steward Relevance

**The critical first step:** Any FEHA claim (§12940 violation) is barred without a CRD complaint filed within 3 years of the discriminatory act. For the false "retired" designation in the credit check — the 3-year clock runs from the date that designation was created (or discovered). If that date is within the past 3 years, a CRD complaint should be filed immediately to preserve the claim. If the discovery was recent but the act was older — discovery rule tolling analysis required.

**Concurrent EEOC filing:** If FEHA and ADA Title I claims are being pursued simultaneously (EBRPD is a covered employer under ADA), a concurrent EEOC charge filing triggers §12965(e)+(f) tolling — extending the right-to-sue period during EEOC investigation.

**The right-to-sue request strategy:** CRD can be requested to issue right-to-sue after 150 days. For time-sensitive cases, requesting early right-to-sue gets the claim into superior court faster.
