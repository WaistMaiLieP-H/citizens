# McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green (1973) / Harris v. Forklift Systems (1993)
## FEHA § 12940 Case Law — Key Doctrine

**Primary citation:** McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792 (1973) [federal, but California courts apply analogous framework to FEHA]
**Secondary citation:** Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17 (1993) [harassment standard]
**California FEHA application note:** California courts routinely apply McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting in FEHA cases; California standard is sometimes broader.

---

## McDonnell Douglas Burden-Shifting Under FEHA

**Step 1 — Prima facie case (plaintiff):**
- Plaintiff is a member of a protected class
- Plaintiff was qualified for the position or performing competently
- Plaintiff suffered adverse employment action
- Circumstances suggest discriminatory motive (including temporal proximity to protected characteristic disclosure)

**Step 2 — Articulate legitimate reason (employer):**
- Employer articulates a legitimate, non-discriminatory reason for the adverse action
- This is a burden of production, not persuasion — employer only needs to state a reason

**Step 3 — Pretext (plaintiff):**
- Plaintiff shows employer's stated reason is pretextual
- Pretext = false reason OR reason not actually motivating the employer
- Plaintiff does not need to disprove employer's stated reason — only show it is unworthy of belief

**California distinction:** Under the California Supreme Court's Harris v. City of Santa Monica (2013), even mixed-motive cases (discrimination was a motivating factor among others) can survive summary judgment, though remedies may be limited.

---

## Harris — Hostile Work Environment Standard

For a harassment claim under §12940(j), the conduct must be:
1. Based on a protected characteristic
2. Severe or pervasive enough to alter conditions of employment and create an abusive work environment
3. Subjectively perceived as abusive by the victim
4. Objectively perceived as abusive by a reasonable person

California courts apply this federal standard but may interpret it more broadly.
