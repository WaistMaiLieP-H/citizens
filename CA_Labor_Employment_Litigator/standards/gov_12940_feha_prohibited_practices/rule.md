# GOV § 12940 — FEHA Prohibited Employment Practices: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Labor_Employment_Litigator / gov_12940_feha_prohibited_practices
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## What FEHA § 12940 Prohibits — Key Subdivisions

### § 12940(a) — Discrimination Based on Protected Class

An employer cannot discriminate against any person in hiring, discharge, compensation, or terms and conditions of employment because of protected characteristics, including:

- **Physical disability** — directly applicable to occupational injuries, bilateral ankle injuries, SIRVA
- **Mental disability** — directly applicable to Dr. Wiita evaluation, competency proceedings
- **Medical condition** — includes cancer, physical conditions arising from injury
- **Age** — applicable to union member benefit eligibility scenarios
- **Veteran or military status** — flagged for potential nexus

The employer cannot refuse to hire, bar from employment, discharge, or discriminate in terms of employment on any of these bases.

### § 12940(h) — Retaliation for FEHA Protected Activity

An employer cannot retaliate against any person because:
1. The person **opposed** any FEHA-prohibited practice, OR
2. The person **filed a complaint, testified, or assisted** in any FEHA proceeding

This is the broadest retaliation provision in FEHA. "Opposition" is not limited to formal complaints — informal objections to discriminatory practices are covered. Temporal connection between the opposition and adverse action raises the inference of retaliation.

### § 12940(j) — Harassment

An employer or covered entity cannot harass an employee because of any protected characteristic. Personal liability attaches to the individual harasser under §12940(j)(3) regardless of employer knowledge. The employer is separately liable for harassment by supervisors and for the failure to take all reasonable steps to prevent it.

### § 12940(k) — Failure to Prevent Discrimination and Harassment

An employer must take "all reasonable steps necessary" to prevent discrimination and harassment. This is an independent claim — even if the underlying discrimination or harassment claim fails, the failure to prevent is independently actionable if the employer's prevention measures were inadequate.

### § 12940(m) — Failure to Accommodate

An employer cannot fail to make reasonable accommodation for a **known** physical or mental disability. Key elements:
1. Employee has a physical or mental disability (or medical condition)
2. Employer **knows** about the disability
3. Employer failed to provide reasonable accommodation

"Reasonable accommodation" is what effectively enables the employee to perform the essential functions of the job. It does not include accommodations that cause undue hardship — but the employer bears the burden of proving undue hardship.

### § 12940(n) — Failure to Engage in Interactive Process

Independent of whether accommodation is ultimately provided, the employer must engage in a "timely, good faith, interactive process" when an accommodation request is made. Failure to engage in this process is an independent violation — even if the employer claims it would have been unable to provide any accommodation.

## Critical Distinctions for This Citizen's Cases

**Physical disability from occupational injury:**
A UA342 plumber with bilateral ankle injuries or SIRVA has a physical disability within FEHA's definition if the condition limits a major life activity. The employer's obligation to accommodate arises from the disability, not from whether the injury was work-related. Workers' compensation addresses the work-relatedness; FEHA addresses the employment discrimination.

**§12940(h) retaliation — parallel to §132a:**
If the retaliation was triggered by a workers' compensation filing (§132a) AND constituted discrimination based on physical disability (§12940(a)/(h)), both claims run simultaneously. Different elements, different forums for initial processing, different remedies — but both claims apply.

## Application to UA342 Cases

**Identity replacement / pension freeze:**
If the UA342 employment status was altered to "retired" without legitimate basis, and if this alteration was connected to a physical disability (occupational injury) — FEHA §12940(a) disability discrimination applies. The "retired" status is adverse employment action; the disability is the nexus.

**Failure to accommodate and interactive process:**
If Michael Hartmann had occupational injuries that required accommodation (modified duty, adjusted hours) and UA342 or an employer failed to engage in the interactive process or provide accommodation, §12940(m)/(n) apply as independent claims.
