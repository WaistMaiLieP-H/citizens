# Gov. Code § 12965 — FEHA Civil Procedure — Reasoning

**Standard ID:** `gov_12965_feha_civil_procedure`
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Date:** 2026-04-15

---

## Why § 12965 Is the Choke Point

FEHA's civil remedy is unenforceable without § 12965. The substantive right created by § 12940 does not self-execute in superior court; it requires an administrative gateway. Miss the gateway, and the claim is time-barred, defectively filed, or dismissed for failure to exhaust. The California Supreme Court has treated administrative exhaustion under FEHA as jurisdictional in effect, though the modern characterization is "mandatory prerequisite." *Rojo v. Kliger*, 52 Cal.3d 65 (1990) preserved common-law tort claims (like *Tameny* wrongful discharge) that can proceed independently of FEHA exhaustion — but any claim pleaded under § 12940 itself must go through CRD.

The gateway itself is not hostile to the claimant — it is a set of numeric deadlines that, once satisfied, open every FEHA remedy. The issue for the injured worker is always temporal: by the time the worker realizes that the adverse action has occurred, the SOL clock may have partially run. This standard exists to map the clocks so that filings can be sequenced correctly.

---

## The Three-Clock System

Three statute-of-limitations clocks run simultaneously under FEHA:

1. **CRD complaint clock — 3 years (§ 12960).** The FEHA administrative complaint must be filed within 3 years of the last discriminatory act. This was extended from 1 year by AB 9 (Stats. 2019) — a critical expansion. Acts prior to 2019 may still be subject to the old 1-year deadline if the underlying conduct pre-dated the amendment.
2. **Administrative hold — 150 days.** After filing, CRD has 150 days before the claimant may demand a right-to-sue letter. CRD may proceed with investigation; if it opts not to issue findings or file its own civil action, the claimant can request right-to-sue at any time after 150 days.
3. **Civil action clock — 1 year from right-to-sue (§ 12965(c)(1)(C)).** Once the right-to-sue issues, the plaintiff has 1 year to file the civil complaint in superior court. This clock is tolled during mandatory dispute resolution (§ 12965(c)(1)(D)) and during EEOC concurrent processing.

Missing any of these three deadlines is a complete bar. A claim could be meritorious on every substantive ground and still fail procedurally because the right-to-sue notice was 366 days old.

---

## Why the Right-to-Sue Letter Must Be Requested, Not Waited For

The default scenario — waiting for CRD to issue findings — can take 9-12 months and still not produce a right-to-sue letter. Experienced FEHA practitioners request the right-to-sue letter at day 151 of the administrative process. This puts the claim on a predictable timeline: file CRD complaint on Day 0, request right-to-sue Day 151, receive right-to-sue approximately Day 160-180, file civil action within 365 days after receipt. This compresses the pre-litigation phase from an unknown duration to approximately 6-18 months.

For time-critical cases (like pension-freeze patterns where evidence is actively being destroyed), the right-to-sue request should be made at Day 151 and the civil complaint drafted in parallel with the administrative process.

## The Asymmetric Fee-Shifting Rule

§ 12965(c)(6) implements a one-way fee-shift that follows the civil-rights model from *Christiansburg Garment Co. v. EEOC*, 434 U.S. 412 (1978). Prevailing plaintiffs routinely recover fees; prevailing defendants recover fees only when the action was "frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless, or ... the plaintiff continued to litigate after it clearly became so." This asymmetry is the practical reason FEHA claims are economically viable for plaintiffs' counsel on contingency — the fee award does not depend on the damages award and can exceed the compensatory damages where liability is clear but damages are modest.

For plaintiffs with modest economic losses but clear discrimination (common in accommodation-denial cases where the worker ultimately found other work), the § 12965 fee award is the main financial engine of the case.

## EEOC Concurrent Filing — Why Every FEHA Case Files Federal Too

Any FEHA claim that parallels a federal protected category (Title VII race/sex/national origin/religion/color, ADA disability, ADEA age, GINA genetic information) should be filed simultaneously with the EEOC. The effects are:

1. **Tolling.** The 1-year civil-action SOL is tolled during EEOC processing (§ 12965(c)(1)(D) effectively, through the work-sharing agreement).
2. **Federal jurisdiction option.** The EEOC right-to-sue preserves the federal forum for claims the plaintiff may later want to move out of state court.
3. **Dual remedy preservation.** FEHA and Title VII/ADA damages caps differ. California has no cap; federal Title VII caps at $300,000 for large employers. Filing both preserves both remedial frameworks.

The CRD/EEOC work-sharing agreement means that filing with either agency is treated as a filing with both for administrative purposes, but the right-to-sue letters are separate and each controls its own civil-action clock.

---

## Continuing Violation — The Rescue Doctrine

When discriminatory conduct is ongoing or consists of a series of related acts, the continuing-violation doctrine can toll the 3-year CRD clock to the date of the last act in the series. *Richards v. CH2M Hill, Inc.*, 26 Cal.4th 798 (2001) adopted the doctrine for FEHA and defined its elements: the employer's conduct must be (1) similar in kind, (2) reasonably frequent, and (3) not yet acquired a degree of permanence that would put a reasonable person on notice of the need to file. This doctrine is essential for cases where the discrimination is systemic and the worker cannot point to a single triggering date.

For the UA342 identity-replacement pattern, continuing violation applies: each pay period in which the "retired" designation persists is an act of continuing disability discrimination; each dispatch denial is a new adverse action; each failure to respond to an accommodation request is a fresh § 12940(n) violation.
