# GOV § 12965 — FEHA Civil Procedure: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Labor_Employment_Litigator / gov_12965_feha_civil_procedure
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Mandatory Prerequisite

A FEHA civil action in superior court **requires a prior CRD/DFEH complaint**. There is no exception. Filing suit without the right-to-sue notice is a fatal procedural defect. This is not a technicality — the California Supreme Court has held the administrative exhaustion requirement is jurisdictional.

**Step 1:** File a verified complaint with the Civil Rights Department (CRD, formerly DFEH).
**Step 2:** Wait for the right-to-sue notice (or request it after 150 days).
**Step 3:** File civil action within 1 year of the right-to-sue notice.

---

## SOL Architecture — Three Dates to Track

| Date | Event | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Date of last discriminatory act | SOL runs on the underlying FEHA violation | 3 years to file CRD complaint (Cal. Gov. Code §12960) |
| Date of CRD complaint | Administrative process begins | 150 days before right-to-sue can be requested |
| Date of right-to-sue notice | Civil action SOL begins | 1 year to file civil action in superior court |

**Critical:** If the 3-year CRD complaint SOL has expired, the right-to-sue analysis is moot — the underlying claim is gone. The 3-year period runs from the date of the discriminatory act.

---

## Requesting Right-to-Sue

After filing a CRD complaint:
- **At any time after 150 days:** Claimant may request right-to-sue notice
- **Department must issue** within one year of complaint filing if not already issued
- Once issued: **1 year to file civil action in superior court**

The right-to-sue request is the lever that moves the case from administrative to judicial. Use it when settlement through CRD is unlikely or the claimant needs to reach court quickly.

---

## Attorney's Fees — Asymmetric Standard

**Prevailing plaintiff:** Court may award attorney's fees and costs at its discretion.

**Prevailing defendant:** Cannot receive attorney's fees UNLESS the court finds the action was "frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless when brought." This asymmetric standard means a defendant who wins cannot routinely recover fees — only in egregious cases.

This asymmetry is intentional: Congress and the Legislature designed it to encourage meritorious FEHA claims without deterring plaintiffs by the risk of a fee-shifting loss.

---

## EEOC Concurrent Filing — Tolling

If a complaint is filed **concurrently** with both the CRD and the EEOC, and the CRD defers to the EEOC, the 1-year civil action SOL is **tolled** until the federal right-to-sue period expires. This means the claimant has the longer of the two windows.

**When to file concurrently:** Any case involving a federal parallel claim (Title VII, ADA, ADEA) should be filed with both agencies simultaneously to preserve both federal and state tracks.

---

## Application to This Citizen's Cases

### UA342 Pension Identity Replacement / FEHA Discrimination

Before any FEHA civil complaint is filed in connection with disability discrimination or retaliation in UA342 employment, a CRD complaint must be filed. The 3-year SOL runs from the last discriminatory act.

**Steward must determine:**
1. Date of last discriminatory act (pension freeze, "retired" entry, failure to accommodate, or any other FEHA-covered adverse action)
2. Whether that date is within 3 years of today (2026-04-13) — if yes, CRD complaint can still be filed
3. Whether a concurrent EEOC filing is needed for parallel Title I ADA or ADEA claims

**SOL warning:** If the last discriminatory act is outside 3 years, the FEHA administrative route is closed. Evaluate whether continuing violation doctrine applies.
