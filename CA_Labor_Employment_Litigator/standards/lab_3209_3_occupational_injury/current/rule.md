## Governing Provision

Cal. Lab. Code § 3208 (Workers Compensation Act; Stats. 1913, Ch. 176; current form)

## The Definition

> "Injury" includes any **injury or disease arising out of the employment**.

This is a broad, inclusionary definition. Key points:

### "Arising Out of the Employment" (AOE)

The injury must have a causal connection to the employment. Not every injury that occurs during work qualifies — there must be a nexus between the employment activities and the injury. But the nexus need not be the primary cause; employment being a contributing cause is sufficient.

**Trade worker injuries (UA Local 342 plumbers):**
- Bilateral ankle injuries from repetitive heavy trade work → presumptively AOE
- Back injuries from lifting, bending, pipe handling → presumptively AOE
- Shoulder injury (SIRVA) if vaccine mandated by or strongly encouraged by employer → arguable AOE (employer-mandated vaccination in the course of employment creates AOE nexus)

### "Injury or Disease" — Broad Coverage

§3208 covers:
- **Specific injuries** — single incident (fall, acute strain)
- **Cumulative injuries** — repetitive trauma over time (§3208.1(b)) — critical for trade workers whose bilateral ankle injuries developed over years of repetitive heavy labor
- **Occupational diseases** — conditions caused by workplace exposures, conditions, or activities

### The "In the Course of Employment" (COE) Element

§3600 adds the COE requirement: the injury must arise "in the course of employment." This covers:
- On-the-job hours and location
- Travel for work purposes
- Activities incidental to employment at employer-assigned or reasonably contemplated locations

**SIRVA analysis:** If the COVID vaccine was administered as a condition of continued employment, or as part of an employer-organized vaccination program, or at an employer-directed location — the vaccination event occurred "in the course of employment" even if not at the worksite.

## The Cumulative Injury SOL — Critical for Trade Workers

For cumulative injuries (§3208.1(b)), the SOL for filing a workers comp claim runs from the **date of disability** or the **date the employee knew or should have known** that the injury was work-related — whichever is later. This discovery-based SOL means:
- A UA Local 342 plumber who develops bilateral ankle degeneration over 20 years
- Is not barred by a SOL running from the first day of ankle pain
- The SOL runs from when the injury caused disability or when the plumber knew/should have known the ankle condition was occupationally caused

**Application:** If the steward's bilateral ankle injuries are cumulative injuries (repetitive trade work over many years), and the steward only recently understood they were occupationally caused — the workers comp SOL may not have run.

## Steward Relevance
