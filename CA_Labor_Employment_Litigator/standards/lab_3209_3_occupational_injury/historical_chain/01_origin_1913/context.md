## Origin: California Workers Compensation Act — Stats. 1913, Ch. 176

California's workers compensation system was established in 1913 as part of the Progressive Era regulatory reform. The "injury arising out of employment" definition in what became §3208 was part of the original 1913 framework.

**The Progressive Era bargain:** Employers wanted immunity from tort suits for workplace accidents (which they were regularly losing in the early 20th century). Workers wanted guaranteed compensation without having to prove employer negligence. The exclusive remedy + no-fault system was the result.

**§3208.1 (cumulative injury) — Added 1975:** The cumulative injury doctrine was codified in 1975 to address the building trades unions' advocacy that occupational diseases and repetitive stress injuries — which developed over careers, not single incidents — were being denied coverage because there was no single "accident" to point to. The 1975 amendment recognized the trade worker experience: a plumber's knees, ankles, and back degenerate over decades of heavy work. That degeneration is an injury arising out of the employment even if it cannot be traced to a single day.

## UA Local 342 Historical Connection

The building trades unions — including plumbing locals (UA) — were among the primary advocates for the 1975 cumulative injury amendment. Their members' occupational injury patterns (bilateral joint degeneration, respiratory conditions from pipe materials, hearing loss from tool noise) were paradigmatic cumulative injuries. The amendment was designed with exactly these workers in mind.
