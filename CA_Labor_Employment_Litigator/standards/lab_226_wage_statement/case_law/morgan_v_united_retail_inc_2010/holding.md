# Morgan v. United Retail Inc., 186 Cal.App.4th 1136 (2010)

**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second District
**Year:** 2010
**Holding reference:** § 226(a)(9) — accurate itemization of hourly rates and hours at each rate

---

## Key Holding

An employer's itemized wage statement must accurately show all hourly rates in effect during the pay period and the corresponding number of hours worked at each rate. A wage statement that shows only a total number of hours with a single rate, when multiple rates applied, fails § 226(a)(9) and is actionable under § 226(e).

The court rejected the employer's argument that total-hours + total-wages was "substantial compliance." The statute requires the specific line-item breakdown; substantial compliance is not a defense.

---

## Why This Case Matters for This Citizen

For the apprenticeship advancement fraud pattern and the identity-replacement pattern: the wage statement must show hours at each rate. If an apprentice is advanced prematurely to a higher step rate, the wage statement must accurately reflect the hours-at-rate. If the rate shown does not correspond to the apprentice's actual DAS-certified step, or if the hours shown do not correspond to actual hours worked, *Morgan* controls: the statement is defective on the face of the record, and the employee does not need to prove independent harm beyond the defect itself.

For Michael Hartmann's UA342 pattern: if any post-2020 wage statement shows hours at any rate when Michael Hartmann did not work those hours, the statement violates § 226(a)(2) and (a)(9) simultaneously — one hour worked when none was actually worked is a per se defect.
