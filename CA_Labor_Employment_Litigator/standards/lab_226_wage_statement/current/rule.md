# LAB § 226 — Itemized Wage Statement: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Labor_Employment_Litigator / lab_226_wage_statement
**Build date:** 2026-04-15

---

## What § 226 Requires

Every employer in California must, at the time each payment of wages is made, furnish each employee with an **accurate itemized statement in writing** showing nine categories of information (§ 226(a)):

1. Gross wages earned
2. Total hours worked (for nonexempt employees)
3. Piece-rate units and rate (if applicable)
4. All deductions (written authorization required; deductions may be aggregated)
5. Net wages earned
6. Inclusive dates of the pay period
7. The employee's name and either the last four digits of SSN or an employee ID
8. Name and address of the legal entity that is the employer
9. All applicable hourly rates in effect and corresponding hours worked at each rate

For piece-rate employees and prevailing-wage workers (apprentices, journeymen on public works), additional line items apply under subdivisions (a)(9), (e)(2)(B)-(C), and Lab. Code §§ 226.2 and 1776.

---

## The "Accurate" Requirement Is the Core Duty

The operative word is **accurate**. A wage statement that contains false information on any of the nine required items is a § 226 violation even if every number adds up correctly. A wage statement that:

- shows hours worked that were not actually worked ("ghost hours"),
- shows the employee's name as a person who did not in fact earn those wages,
- shows an apprenticeship classification the worker does not hold,
- shows a prevailing-wage rate different from the rate actually paid or certified,
- shows the employer's name as an entity that did not actually pay the wages, or
- omits required information (hours, rates, deductions)

is inaccurate within the meaning of § 226(a) and triggers the § 226(e) remedy framework.

---

## Damages Under § 226(e)(1)

An employee who suffers injury as a result of a knowing and intentional failure to comply may recover:

| Element | Amount |
|---|---|
| Initial pay period violation | $50 |
| Each subsequent pay period | $100 |
| Aggregate cap per employee | $4,000 + attorney's fees + costs |

The employee must show the failure was (1) knowing and intentional and (2) caused injury. "Injury" is presumed when the statement fails to provide required information preventing the employee from promptly and easily determining gross wages, hours, deductions, or the identity of the employer — so injury is near-automatic when a category is wholly missing or demonstrably false (§ 226(e)(2)).

---

## Civil Penalties Under § 226.3 and PAGA (§ 2699)

**§ 226.3 direct penalty:** For willful failure to provide an accurate itemized statement, the Labor Commissioner may assess **$250 per employee per violation** for the initial violation and **$1,000 per employee per violation** for each subsequent violation. This is civil penalty, separate from § 226(e) damages.

**PAGA (Lab. Code § 2699):** An aggrieved employee may recover civil penalties on behalf of themselves and other aggrieved employees — including § 226 penalties — in a Private Attorneys General Act representative action. 75% of the penalties go to the Labor & Workforce Development Agency; 25% to the aggrieved employees. PAGA also provides attorney's fees to the prevailing plaintiff.

---

## Statute of Limitations

- **§ 226(e) damages:** 1 year (CCP § 340(a) — statutory penalty).
- **§ 226 / § 226.3 civil penalties / PAGA:** 1 year (CCP § 340(a)), with a mandatory 65-day LWDA notice period under PAGA that tolls the SOL.
- **§ 226 underlying wage claim (if the inaccuracy masks unpaid wages):** 3 years for statutory wage claims (CCP § 338); 4 years under UCL (§ 17200) for unfair-competition theory.

---

## Employer Recordkeeping (§ 226(b))

Employers must keep wage records for at least **three years** at the place of employment or a central location. Current or former employees have a right to inspect and receive copies within **21 calendar days** of request. Failure to comply is a **$750** penalty (§ 226(f)).

This is the document-production mechanism by which fraudulent wage statements are surfaced. A Request for Wage Records under § 226(b) triggers the 21-day clock; a failure to produce is itself independently penalized.

## Application to UA342 Apprenticeship Advancement Fraud (Case #37)

### Ghost Wage Statements After 2020 Injury

Per steward memory (`project_post_injury_work_reality.md`), Michael Hartmann has worked exactly once (~4 days in 2021) since the 1/6/2020 L5/S1 injury. Any UA342 / contributing-employer W-2, pay stub, or itemized wage statement showing hours, wages, or apprenticeship hours earned by Michael Hartmann after that date is a per se **inaccurate wage statement** under § 226(a).

**Per-pay-period damages calculation:**
- If ghost wage statements were issued bi-weekly across any year of the post-2020 period, the § 226(e) cap is reached ($4,000) after approximately 40 pay periods — roughly 1.5 years.
- Multiple employers / multiple contributing entities = parallel § 226 claims, each with its own cap.
- § 226.3 civil penalty: $250 first + $1,000 per subsequent = a single employer issuing 52 false statements per year = $51,250 in civil penalties per year.
- PAGA overlay: if any other apprentice in the Steve Silber coordinator lane was similarly subjected to advancement-hour falsification, a PAGA representative action multiplies the penalty base across all aggrieved apprentices.

### Apprenticeship Advancement Fraud Nexus

The Steve Silber coordinator pattern (per memory) involved advancement hours recorded against apprentices who did not earn them. Each such false entry reflected in a wage statement is a § 226 violation against the apprentice whose name appears on the statement. The § 226 case is simultaneously a Cal. Apprenticeship Council / DAS compliance case (8 CCR §§ 205-230) and a potential federal wire-fraud predicate (18 USC § 1343) when the false statements are transmitted electronically.

### Inaccurate Employer Identity

§ 226(a)(8) requires the **legal name and address of the employer**. When the identity-replacement pattern inserts a different worker on UA342 dispatches while the pension / wage statements continue to show Michael Hartmann as the worker, the statement is inaccurate on § 226(a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(7), and potentially (a)(8). Each of these is an independent § 226(a) defect.
