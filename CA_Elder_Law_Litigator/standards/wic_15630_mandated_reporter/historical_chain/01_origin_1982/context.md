# WIC § 15630 — Historical Chain: Origin

**Stage:** 01 — Origin
**Year:** 1982
**Legislation:** Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act, Stats. 1982, Ch. 1557

---

## The Wound

The mandated reporter obligation for elder abuse was not a precautionary measure. It was a response to a documented and growing pattern: elderly people were being financially and physically abused inside institutions and in their homes, and the professionals closest to them — nurses, administrators, social workers, physicians — were staying silent.

The silence was not always cowardice. It was institutional. Nursing home administrators told staff not to report because reports meant inspections. Financial managers who suspected fraud in elder accounts were told the client relationship was more important than reporting. Physicians deferred to family members. The common denominator was that every person who could have acted chose not to because there was no personal penalty for silence.

The legislature answered with a personal, individual, non-delegable reporting duty with criminal penalties attached.

---

## The 1982 Structure

When the Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act was enacted in 1982, it established:
1. A defined class of mandated reporters tied to professional role, not institutional status
2. An immediate reporting obligation upon reasonable suspicion — not certainty, not proof
3. Criminal penalties for failure to report
4. Protection for reporters — immunity from civil or criminal liability for good-faith reports

The critical design choice was making the duty **individual**: no institutional instruction or organizational policy could discharge the individual's obligation or shield them from the criminal penalty for silence.

---

## Why Financial Abuse Was Included

The original Act addressed physical abuse primarily, but financial abuse was understood from the beginning as a parallel threat. Elders were targets for financial exploitation precisely because they often depended on the same professionals who controlled their finances. Adding financial abuse to the reporting trigger closed the loop: a nursing home administrator who witnessed a family member draining a resident's bank account had the same obligation as a nurse who witnessed physical striking.

---

## Amendments (key evolution points)

- **1991:** Article 8.5 enacted, strengthening enhanced civil remedies and cross-linking the mandated reporter obligation to the civil remedy system
- **2005:** Financial institutions added more explicitly to the mandated reporter framework — responding to evidence that bank tellers and account managers were seeing elder financial abuse patterns but had no clear institutional obligation to report
- **Ongoing (through 2023):** Continuing expansion of covered categories, coordination with federal Elder Justice Act (2010), internet reporting tools authorized by §15658

---

## What the 1982 Legislature Promised

Society promised that professionals who stand between an elder and their abuser will not be permitted to look away. The profession creates the duty. The knowledge creates the obligation. Silence creates the crime.

That promise is as current as the 2023 amendment.
