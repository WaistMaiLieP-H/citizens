# Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 15610.30 — Rule

## Three Pathways to Financial Abuse

### Pathway 1: Wrongful Use [(a)(1)]
Takes/secretes/appropriates/obtains/retains property for wrongful use OR with intent
to defraud (or both).

**"Wrongful use" defined [(b)]:** The person knew or should have known that their
conduct was likely to be harmful to the elder or dependent adult. This is a negligence-
adjacent standard for the underlying act — not just intentional wrongdoing.

**Implication:** A trustee who manages trust assets in ways that deplete them, knowing
or having reason to know the depletion harms the elder beneficiary, commits financial
abuse under (a)(1). No need to prove subjective intent to defraud.

### Pathway 2: Assistance [(a)(2)]
Assists another person or entity in the wrongful use or intent-to-defraud taking.

**Implication:** The financial institution that processes fraudulent transactions,
the insurance agent who structures a policy that strips elder assets, and the attorney
who drafts instruments achieving the transfer — all can be "assistants" liable under
(a)(2) if they knew or should have known the conduct was harmful.

### Pathway 3: Undue Influence [(a)(3)]
Takes/secretes/appropriates/obtains/retains property by undue influence (§15610.70).

No intent to defraud needed. No wrongful use needed. If the property was obtained
through excessive persuasion overcoming free will → financial abuse by definition.

## The Breadth of "Property" [(c)]

Financial abuse covers deprivation of ANY property right — not just cash or real estate:
- Agreements (contracts signed under undue influence)
- Donative transfers (gifts obtained through pressure)
- **Testamentary bequests** (will changes made at the influencer's direction)
- Property held by a representative (trustee, conservator) — the representative's
  mismanagement of the elder's assets is covered directly

## "Representative" Liability [(d)]

Conservators, trustees, estate representatives, and attorneys-in-fact are
**"representatives" who are direct perpetrators** — not merely agents whose liability
depends on a separate principal's wrongdoing.

If a trustee depletes trust assets for wrongful use, the trustee committed financial
abuse directly. If a conservator diverts conservatorship assets, the conservator
committed financial abuse directly.

## Application: Ann Hillberg / Northern Trust / State Farm

**The Northern Trust trustee:**
- Is a "conservator, trustee, or other representative" under §15610.30(d)(1)
- The Northern Trust variable product trust is the vehicle; the trustee manages it
- If the trust is being administered in ways that deplete Ann's assets while
  generating fees or benefiting others → wrongful use under (a)(1)
- If the HILLBERGMANN compound identity created trust instruments that Ann did not
  authorize or understand → undue influence under (a)(3)

**The State Farm agent:**
- Issued a policy under conditions that may involve identity merger (HILLBERGMANN)
- If the policy was structured to benefit a third party at Ann's expense → wrongful use
- If Ann's insurance coverage was obtained under conditions of pressure or deception →
  undue influence
- State Farm as an institution falls under §15630 mandated reporter duties —
  financial institution with obligations to report known/suspected financial abuse

**The §15610.30(b) "should have known" standard:**
Both Northern Trust and State Farm, as sophisticated financial institutions, are held
to the "should have known" standard. Professional knowledge of elder financial abuse
patterns means they cannot claim ignorance of obvious exploitation signals.

**Prob. Code §859 multiplier:**
If the financial abuse was in bad faith — and a compound identity scheme creating
fictitious financial instruments IS bad faith — Prob. Code §859 provides TWICE THE
VALUE of property taken as damages.
