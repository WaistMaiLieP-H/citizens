# §15610.30 Case Law — Holdings

**UNVERIFIED CITATIONS — Verify before filing reliance**

## Rule 1 (Mack v. Soung): The harm standard is objective and prospective
- Does not require actual harm — "likely to be harmful" is the test
- Defendant's actual knowledge OR what a reasonable person should have known
- Cannot be defeated by showing no harm actually resulted

## Rule 2 (Teselle v. McLoughlin): "Wrongful use" requires benefit to defendant or third party
- Negligent management ≠ financial abuse (no wrongful use element)
- Exploitative management (draining assets that flow to defendant/third party) = financial abuse
- The HILLBERGMANN compound identity scheme: who receives the benefit of the
  Northern Trust products and State Farm premiums? Follow that money.

## Application Strategy
The two-step analysis for Hillberg case:
1. **Who received the benefit?** If assets flowed from Ann Hillberg's estate to Northern
   Trust fees, State Farm premiums, or to persons in the HILLBERGMANN compound identity
   → wrongful use established
2. **Did they know or should have known?** Northern Trust and State Farm are
   sophisticated institutions — "should have known" is easily established by their
   professional obligation to know elder financial abuse patterns
