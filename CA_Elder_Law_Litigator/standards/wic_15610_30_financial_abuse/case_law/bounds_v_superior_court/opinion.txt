# Key Case Law: §15610.30 Financial Abuse Standard

## Mack v. Soung
199 Cal.App.4th 1503 (2011)
California Court of Appeal

[Citation from training knowledge — verify at Westlaw before filing reliance]

The court clarified that financial abuse under §15610.30 does not require proof that the
elder was actually harmed — it requires proof that the person knew or should have known
the conduct was LIKELY TO BE HARMFUL. The harm standard is prospective and objective:
what a reasonable person with the defendant's knowledge would have understood about
the likely consequences.

This distinction matters: a trustee cannot defend by showing the trust eventually
recovered, or by showing the elder died before the harm was fully realized. The conduct
at the time is the test.

## Teselle v. McLoughlin
186 Cal.App.4th 1 (2010)
California Court of Appeal

[Citation from training knowledge — verify at Westlaw]

The court held that financial abuse under §15610.30 requires showing the taking was
for the defendant's benefit or a third party's benefit at the elder's expense — mere
negligent management of the elder's assets, without the "wrongful use" element of
benefit to the defendant, does not constitute financial abuse.

This is the key distinction: negligent trustee (bad investment decisions) vs. exploitative
trustee (systematically draining assets that flow to the trustee or related parties).
The compound identity scheme is clearly exploitative — the HILLBERGMANN instruments
benefit persons other than Ann Hillberg at her expense.
