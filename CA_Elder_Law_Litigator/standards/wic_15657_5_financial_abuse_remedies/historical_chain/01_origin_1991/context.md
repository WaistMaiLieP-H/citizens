# WIC § 15657.5 — Historical Chain: Origin Context

**Standard ID:** `wic_15657_5_financial_abuse_remedies`
**Layer:** 3 — Historical Chain
**Subdirectory:** 01_origin_1991
**Date:** 2026-04-12

## Origin: 1991 Act + 2008 Financial Abuse Amendment

§15657.5 was part of the original Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act (Stats. 1991, Ch. 774). The financial abuse enhanced remedies were designed in parallel with §15657 (physical abuse enhanced remedies) from the beginning.

**Key design decision:** The Legislature set the attorney fee threshold at preponderance for financial abuse — lower than the C&C + recklessness threshold for physical abuse. This was a deliberate policy choice based on the recognition that financial exploitation is routinely carried out through legal instruments that conceal the abuse.

**2008 Amendment (Stats. 2008, Ch. 475):** When §15610.70 was added to define undue influence, §15657.5 was amended to ensure the undue influence pathway to financial abuse (§15610.30(a)(3)) was expressly covered by the enhanced remedies. The judgment labeling requirement (§15657.5(e)) was also added or clarified in this amendment period.

## The Historical Gap This Filled

Before §15657.5, financial exploitation of elders was litigated as:
- Breach of fiduciary duty (available only against express fiduciaries)
- Fraud (required C&C proof of specific intent)
- Constructive trust / unjust enrichment (equitable; no attorney fees)

None of these triggered mandatory attorney fee shifting at preponderance. The combination of high proof burden and no fee shifting meant that most financial elder abuse cases were economically impossible to litigate unless the amounts involved were substantial. §15657.5 broke this barrier.

## CHRONICLE Flag

No CHRONICLE restriction. Statute is protective and remedial. Historical record discusses financial exploitation of elders — painful but not discriminatory or offensive in the CHRONICLE sense. Full documentation maintained.
