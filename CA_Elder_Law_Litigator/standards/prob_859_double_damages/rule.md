# PROB § 859 — Double Damages for Bad Faith Taking: Rule Summary

**Standard:** CA_Elder_Law_Litigator / prob_859_double_damages
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## What § 859 Does

Section 859 authorizes a court to award **twice the value** of property wrongfully taken from a protected person when the taking was in bad faith. It is the Probate Code's blunt answer to elder financial exploitation: if you steal from an elder or dependent adult, you do not just return what you took — you pay double.

---

## Three Pathways to Double Damages

### Pathway 1: Bad Faith Wrongful Taking, Concealment, or Disposal
- **Property belonging to:** conservatee, minor, elder, dependent adult, trust, or decedent estate
- **Conduct:** wrongfully taken, concealed, or disposed of
- **Mental state:** in bad faith
- **Result:** liability for **twice the value** of the property

### Pathway 2: Undue Influence in Bad Faith
- **Conduct:** taken, concealed, or disposed of property
- **Method:** through undue influence (as defined in §15610.70 — the four-factor test)
- **Mental state:** in bad faith
- **Result:** liability for **twice the value**

### Pathway 3: Elder or Dependent Adult Financial Abuse Under §15610.30
- **Conduct:** any taking, concealing, or disposing of property that meets the §15610.30 definition
- **WIC §15610.30 definition:** taking, secreting, appropriating, obtaining, or retaining real or personal property of an elder or dependent adult to a wrongful use or with intent to defraud; OR assisting in such; OR taking by undue influence
- **Mental state:** the bad faith element is embedded in the §15610.30 financial abuse definition
- **Result:** liability for **twice the value**

## Attorney's Fees Under § 859

Attorney's fees under §859 are **discretionary** ("may, in the court's discretion, be liable for reasonable attorney's fees and costs") — UNLESS WIC §15657.5 applies.

**Critical distinction:**
- **§859 fees:** Discretionary
- **§15657.5 fees:** MANDATORY if financial abuse proven by preponderance

In practice, where WIC §15657.5 applies (elder or dependent adult financial abuse), the mandatory fee provision governs and the §859 discretionary provision is superseded. This means:

In elder financial abuse cases, attorney's fees are mandatory (§15657.5), and the double damages multiplier (§859) applies on top. The §859 text expressly preserves §15657.5 as controlling: "except as otherwise required by law, **including Section 15657.5**."

---

## The "Twice the Value" Calculation

The measure is twice the value of the **property recovered by an action under this part** — meaning the starting point is the property the court is ordering returned or compensating. Calculation:

1. Determine the fair market value of the wrongfully taken property at time of taking (or highest value during concealment period — disputed; steward verification needed)
2. Double that figure = §859 damages
3. Add §15657.5 mandatory attorney's fees and costs on top
4. Add §3294 punitive damages (separate track — requires employer ratification or §3294(b) finding)

---

## Additive Nature

The statute is explicit: "The remedies provided in this section shall be in addition to any other remedies available in law."

This means §859 double damages stack on:
- Restitution / return of property (the base)
- §15657.5 mandatory attorney's fees
- Civil Code §3294 punitive damages
- Equitable remedies (constructive trust, accounting, injunction)
- Tort damages for financial elder abuse

## Bad Faith Standard

"Bad faith" under §859 is not separately defined in the Probate Code. Courts have interpreted it to require:
- Knowledge that the taking was wrongful, OR
- Subjective intent to deprive the rightful owner, OR
- Reckless disregard for the rights of the protected person

This is a lower bar than fraud under §15657.5(b) (which requires C&C showing of recklessness, fraud, or malice for the enhanced damage track). Bad faith by preponderance is sufficient for the §859 double damages.
