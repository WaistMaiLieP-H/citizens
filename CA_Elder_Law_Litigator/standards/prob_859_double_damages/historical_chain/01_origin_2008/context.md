# PROB § 859 — Historical Chain: Origin

**Stage:** 01 — Origin (current form)
**Year:** 2008 (modernized form); prior versions existed as part of the Probate Code bad faith taking framework
**Legislation:** Stats. 2008 — expanded to elder and dependent adult financial abuse via WIC §15610.30 cross-reference

---

## The Wound

Elder financial abuse in California followed a consistent pattern before §859: a trusted person — a conservator, trustee, attorney-in-fact, family member — would drain an elder's assets over months or years. When caught, they would return what remained. The return was the entire remedy. There was no penalty for the period of abuse, no consequence for the concealment, no cost for the years of exploitation.

The legislature recognized that a remedy requiring only return of property created a risk-free exploitation model: steal, invest the stolen funds, and if caught, return the principal while keeping the gains. The answer was a multiplier. Take it in bad faith, pay back double.

---

## The 2008 Expansion

Section 859 was expanded to explicitly incorporate WIC §15610.30 (elder or dependent adult financial abuse) as a standalone predicate. Before this, the bad faith taking provision existed in the Probate Code but was not expressly linked to the elder abuse statutory framework. The 2008 expansion:

1. Made elder/dependent adult financial abuse (as defined in §15610.30) an explicit Pathway 3 to double damages
2. Cross-linked the civil penalty system of WIC Chapter 11 with the Probate Code's property recovery framework
3. Ensured that every WIC §15610.30 financial abuse finding automatically carried the potential for double damages under the Probate Code

This integration was the legislature's recognition that elder financial abuse happens overwhelmingly in fiduciary relationships — conservators, trustees, attorneys-in-fact — who are subject to Probate Code jurisdiction. Linking the two codes closed the gap.

---

## What the Legislature Promised in § 859

Society promised that stealing from people who depend on legal fiduciaries — elders, conservatees, minors, trust beneficiaries — is not a risk-free enterprise. The multiplier is the promise: the cost of bad faith taking is more than restitution. It is punishment.

The "in addition to any other remedies" clause promises that §859 does not replace other remedies — it adds to them. The elder financial abuse victim collects: double damages + mandatory attorney's fees (§15657.5) + punitive damages (§3294) + restitution + equitable relief.

The design is intentional accumulation.
