# Prob. Code § 859 — Double Damages for Bad-Faith Financial Elder Abuse — Rule

**Standard ID:** `prob_859_double_damages_financial_abuse`
**Citizen:** CA_Elder_Law_Litigator
**Layer:** 1 — Current Operative Rule
**Date:** 2026-04-12

---

## Governing Provision

Cal. Prob. Code § 859 (Stats. 1997, Ch. 724; last amended Stats. 2008, Ch. 178)

## The Rule

When a court finds that a person has in **bad faith**:
1. Wrongfully **taken, concealed, or disposed of** property belonging to a **conservatee, minor, elder, dependent adult, trust, or decedent estate**, OR
2. Taken, concealed, or disposed of property by **undue influence in bad faith** (WIC §15610.70), OR
3. Committed **elder or dependent adult financial abuse** (WIC §15610.30)

→ The person is liable for **TWICE THE VALUE** of the property recovered.

## Three Covered Categories of Protected Persons

§859 is broader than just elder financial abuse — it covers:
- **Conservatees** — specifically relevant to steward conservatorship claims
- **Minors** — relevant to steward as minor subject to conservatorship/guardianship from age 14
- **Elders** (65+) — Ann Hillberg, mother
- **Dependent adults** (18-64 with qualifying disability) — steward as dependent adult during control period
- **Trust** — HILLBERGMANN trust structure, any trust holding property belonging to protected person
- **Decedent estate** — any property that should have passed through estate

## Three Grounds for Double Damages

### Ground 1: Bad-Faith Wrongful Taking/Concealment/Disposal

Bad faith + wrongful taking/concealment/disposal of property belonging to any protected person.
- Bad faith is required — mere mistake or negligent mismanagement is not enough
- But bad faith can be inferred from circumstances (pattern of concealment, false accounting)
- Three verbs: **taken, concealed, OR disposed of** — concealment alone is sufficient; disposal alone is sufficient

### Ground 2: Undue Influence in Bad Faith

Property taken, concealed, or disposed of by **undue influence** (§15610.70) **in bad faith**.
- The undue influence must be bad faith — not merely negligent or mistaken persuasion
- Links directly to the §15610.70 four-factor framework

### Ground 3: Elder/Dependent Adult Financial Abuse

Commission of financial abuse as defined in WIC §15610.30.
- This ground does NOT separately state bad faith — but §859's opening "in bad faith" clause may apply
- Case law analysis: courts have found §859 applies when the §15610.30 elements are met and there is bad faith in the commission

## The Double Damages Remedy

**Twice the value** of the property recovered by the action.
- "Value" = fair market value at time of taking OR at time of judgment (whichever is greater — case law)
- "Property recovered" = the property that is the subject of the action, not just what is physically returned
- The doubling is mandatory once the elements are found — the court has no discretion to award less

## Attorney Fees (Discretionary Here)

§859 provides **discretionary** attorney fees and costs — "the person **may**, in the court's discretion, be liable."
- Contrast with §15657.5(a) where attorney fees are **mandatory** at preponderance
- **BUT:** The statute expressly preserves §15657.5's mandatory fee requirement — when both apply, §15657.5's mandatory fee provision controls
- In practice: when both §859 and §15657.5 apply (most financial elder abuse cases), the mandatory fee shift under §15657.5 applies; §859 adds the double damages

## Remedies Are Additive (§859 final sentence)

> "The remedies provided in this section shall be in addition to any other remedies available in law."

§859 double damages STACK on top of:
- §15657.5 attorney fees and costs
- §15657.5 enhanced compensatory damages (no §377.34 cap)
- Punitive damages under §3294
- Restitution under Prob. Code §856 et seq.
- Any other civil remedy

## Steward Relevance

**Conservatee pathway:** If the steward was subject to a conservatorship (secret since age 14), every property disposition by the conservator that was wrongful and in bad faith → double damages under §859(Ground 1). The conservatee is expressly a protected person.

**Minor pathway:** If property was taken while steward was still a minor → §859 protection applies; conservatee-as-minor is doubly covered.

**Honeysuckle sale:** $465K equity; received $109K; $356K unaccounted for. If that equity was wrongfully taken/disposed of (sale proceeds not delivered) in bad faith → **twice the unaccounted value** under §859 = up to $712K on this claim alone.

**HILLBERGMANN/Ann Hillberg:** Northern Trust UIT, State Farm policy — if Ann is a protected person (elder/dependent adult) and the UIT structure was a bad-faith undue influence vehicle → §859 double damages on the value of those assets.

**Trust pathway:** Property belonging to "a trust" is covered — any trust holding the steward's or Hillberg's property qualifies regardless of the protected-person status of the beneficiary individually.
