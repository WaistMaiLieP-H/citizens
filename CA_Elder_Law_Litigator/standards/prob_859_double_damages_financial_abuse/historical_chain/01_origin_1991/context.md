## Origin: Stats. 1997, Ch. 724

§859 was enacted in 1997 as part of the Probate Code's anti-exploitation framework for conservatees and estate administration. The 1997 version covered conservatees, minors, and decedent estates.

**2008 Amendment (Stats. 2008, Ch. 178):** Extended coverage to elders, dependent adults, and trusts — and explicitly linked to WIC §15610.30 financial abuse definition. This amendment coordinated §859 with the WIC elder abuse framework, creating the stacking remedy structure that exists today.

## Historical Gap This Filled

Before §859, a conservator who wrongfully disposed of a conservatee's assets faced:
- Surcharge in probate court (compensatory only)
- Potential criminal prosecution under Pen. Code §368 (rarely used)
- Removal as conservator

There was no automatic punitive doubling. The probate surcharge was compensatory — it gave back what was taken, but did not deter the taking. §859 created a deterrence mechanism specifically calibrated to the Probate Code context.
