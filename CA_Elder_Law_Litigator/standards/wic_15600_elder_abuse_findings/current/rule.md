# Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 15600 — Rule

## What This Section Does

§15600 is the legislative findings section — it states what problem the Elder and
Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act exists to solve. It is:

1. **Interpretive authority:** Courts read the entire chapter through §15600's lens.
2. **Brief authority:** The Legislature's own statement of why these enhanced remedies exist.
3. **§15657.5 fee mandate context:** §15600(j) expressly states the intent is to "enable
   interested persons to engage attorneys to take up the cause of abused elderly persons
   and dependent adults." The mandatory attorney's fee provision in §15657.5 directly
   implements §15600(j).

## The Findings That Matter Most

**§15600(h):** "Cases of abuse of these persons are seldom prosecuted as criminal matters,
and few civil cases are brought in connection with this abuse due to problems of proof,
court delays, and the lack of incentives to prosecute these suits."

This finding is why:
- The evidentiary threshold for mandatory fees is only preponderance (§15657.5(a))
- The damages cap is lifted at clear and convincing evidence (§15657.5(b))
- Double damages are available for bad faith (Prob. Code §859)

**§15600(j):** "It is the further intent of the Legislature in adding Article 8.5
(commencing with Section 15657) to this chapter to enable interested persons to engage
attorneys to take up the cause of abused elderly persons and dependent adults."

This is the explicit attorney's fee enablement — the Legislature chose this language
deliberately because without economic incentive, complex elder financial abuse cases
(like UIT/trust/insurance compound identity schemes) would never be litigated.

## Application to Ann Hillberg/Mother Cases

Both §15600(h) (proof problems, court delays, lack of incentives) and §15600(j)
(attorney enablement) are the predicate for why the mandatory fee and double damage
provisions exist — and why those provisions are the lead remedies in the Hillberg case.
