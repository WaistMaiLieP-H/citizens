# § 15600 Origin: The 1982 Wound

California's Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act was first enacted in
1982 after legislative hearings documented a systematic gap: elders were being financially
exploited by family members, trustees, and care providers, but neither the criminal system
(proving intent beyond reasonable doubt) nor the civil system (individual small-dollar
claims not worth attorney time) provided practical remedies.

The 1991 addition of Article 8.5 (§§15657 et seq.) added the enhanced civil remedies
specifically to close the civil enforcement gap. The Legislature's explicit finding in
§15600(h) — few cases brought due to proof problems and lack of incentives — was the
predicate for the mandatory fees provision in §15657.5. You cannot get attorneys to take
complex elder financial abuse cases without a fee mechanism. §15600(j) acknowledges this.

The EADACPA has been significantly expanded over the decades:
- 1982: Original protective services framework
- 1991: Enhanced civil remedies added (§§15657, 15657.5)
- 1998: Mandated reporter duties expanded to include financial abuse
- 2013: Undue influence definition codified in §15610.70
- Ongoing: Dependent adult coverage expanded; financial institution duties added
