# Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 15610.70 — Rule

## The Definition: Excessive Persuasion + Overcome Free Will + Inequity

Three elements for undue influence:
1. **Excessive persuasion** — goes beyond ordinary pressure; overreaches
2. **Overcomes the victim's free will** — the victim acts not from their own autonomous choice
3. **Results in inequity** — the outcome is not what the victim would have chosen freely

**§15610.70(b) critical limit:** Inequitable result alone is NOT sufficient. The court
must see evidence of the persuasion that overcame free will — not just a bad outcome.

## The Four Factors (All Must Be Considered)

### Factor 1: Vulnerability of the Victim

Evidence includes: incapacity, illness, disability, injury, age, education, impaired
cognitive function, emotional distress, **isolation**, or **dependency**.

"Whether the influencer knew or should have known of the alleged victim's vulnerability"
— again the objective "should have known" standard. A family member who witnesses
declining health and cognitive function "should have known" of the vulnerability.

**Hillberg application:** Illness ($214K/year income exhausted, health concerns confirmed),
isolation (family operation structure), age (if 65+), dependency (financial instruments
managed by Northern Trust — she depends on the trustee).

### Factor 2: Apparent Authority of the Influencer

Evidence includes: fiduciary, family member, care provider, health professional,
legal professional, spiritual adviser, expert.

**Hillberg application:** Northern Trust trustee = fiduciary. State Farm agent = expert.
Family members within the family operation = family member + care provider. All three
categories of apparent authority present simultaneously.

### Factor 3: Actions and Tactics

**(A)** Controlling necessaries of life, medication, interactions with others,
access to information, or **sleep**.

**(B)** Use of affection, intimidation, or coercion.

**(C)** Haste or secrecy in effecting changes; changes at inappropriate times/places;
claims of expertise.

**Hillberg application:**
- HILLBERGMANN compound identity was created with secrecy — no notice to Ann
- Financial instruments changed under "expertise" claims by Northern Trust/State Farm
- Control of access to information (Ann cannot independently verify trust/policy status)
- The family operation controls Ann's interactions with others

### Factor 4: Equity of the Result

Evidence: economic consequences to victim; divergence from prior intent; value
received vs. value conveyed; appropriateness given relationship length/nature.

**Hillberg application:**
- $214K/year income + exhausted assets = economic consequence to victim
- No reasonable exchange of value from Northern Trust products → just asset extraction
- Prior intent: Ann's estate should benefit Ann; compound identity instruments benefit others

## The Sequencing with §15610.30

§15610.70 undue influence feeds directly into §15610.30(a)(3) financial abuse:
if undue influence is proven → financial abuse is established → §15657.5 mandatory
fees are triggered → §859 double damages apply if bad faith.

The four-factor test is the evidence framework. Each factor becomes a discovery target:
medical records (vulnerability), fiduciary agreements (apparent authority), communications
(tactics), account statements (equity of result).
