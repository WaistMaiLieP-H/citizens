## Origin: 2008 Codification — Stats. 2008, Ch. 475

Although the Elder Abuse Act dates to 1991, § 15610.70 was not added until the 2008 amendment. Before 2008:
- "Undue influence" was referenced in the Act as a basis for financial abuse
- But the term was not defined within the WIC framework
- Courts borrowed from probate (Prob. Code §86) and contract rescission doctrine inconsistently

**The 2008 amendment's purpose:** Provide a single, context-specific definition calibrated to the elder/dependent adult population. The four-factor framework was drawn from empirical research on elder financial exploitation patterns — each factor reflects documented categories of conduct observed in actual abuse cases.

## Historical Loss Documentation

The wound that § 15610.70 addresses: financial exploitation of elders through sophisticated legal instruments (trusts, annuities, powers of attorney) that appeared voluntary. Courts before 2008 frequently refused to unwind these transactions because the elder had "signed voluntarily." The 2008 amendment created the analytical framework to look behind apparent consent and examine the conditions under which consent was obtained.

**The pattern:** Legitimate-looking instruments, signed by a person with apparent capacity, in the presence of witnesses, notarized — but obtained through isolation, information control, manufactured dependency, and claims of expertise. The 2008 definition was designed to reach exactly this pattern.

## CHRONICLE Flag

No CHRONICLE restriction. Statute is remedial and non-discriminatory. Historical record discusses exploitation patterns without targeting protected groups. Full documentation maintained.
