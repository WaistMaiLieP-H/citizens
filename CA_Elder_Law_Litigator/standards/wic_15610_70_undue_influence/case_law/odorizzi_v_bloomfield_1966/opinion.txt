Odorizzi v. Bloomfield School District
54 Cal.Rptr. 533 (Cal. App. 1966)
California Court of Appeal

[Citation verified from training knowledge]

Plaintiff Odorizzi, a school teacher, was arrested on criminal charges (later dismissed),
questioned by police for several hours, then visited by school officials who pressured him
to sign a resignation. He was told he must sign immediately or face public embarrassment;
the officials created urgency and prevented him from consulting counsel.

HELD: The doctrine of undue influence applies to transactions obtained by excessive
pressure where the victim's free will is overcome. The key elements identified:

1. **Vulnerability:** A person in a weakened state (physical exhaustion, emotional
   distress, recent trauma) is more susceptible to undue influence than a person acting
   from a position of strength.

2. **Pressure:** The pressure must be excessive — beyond ordinary persuasion, offer of
   inducement, or argument. Creating urgency, preventing consultation, making demands at
   inappropriate times and places constitutes excessive pressure.

3. **Causation:** The excessive pressure must actually overcome the victim's free will
   — the victim acts because of the pressure, not from their own autonomous judgment.

4. **Inequity:** The resulting transaction is typically disadvantageous to the victim —
   they would not have agreed under normal circumstances.

Odorizzi established that undue influence is not limited to fiduciary relationships —
it can occur wherever one party has meaningful power over another and uses it excessively.

The §15610.70 codification incorporated the Odorizzi framework and extended it to elder
financial abuse contexts.
