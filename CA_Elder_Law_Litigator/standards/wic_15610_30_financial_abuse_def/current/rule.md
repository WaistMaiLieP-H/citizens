## Governing Provision

Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 15610.30 (Stats. 1991, Ch. 774; amended Stats. 1998, Ch. 946; Stats. 2008, Ch. 475)

## The Rule

"Financial abuse" of an elder or dependent adult has three paths:

### Path 1: Wrongful Use or Fraud (§15610.30(a)(1))
Takes, secretes, appropriates, obtains, or retains real or personal property **for wrongful use or with intent to defraud**.
- Five verbs: take, secret, appropriate, obtain, OR retain — any one is sufficient
- Covers real property (land, buildings), personal property (cash, securities, vehicles, trust interests)
- "Wrongful use" is defined in §15610.30(b): knew or should have known the conduct is likely to be harmful to the elder

### Path 2: Assisting (§15610.30(a)(2))
**Assists** another in taking/secreting/appropriating/obtaining/retaining for wrongful use or to defraud.
- Third-party facilitator liability — reaches banks, attorneys, financial institutions, family members who assist the primary actor
- Das v. Bank of America (2010): bank that assists wrongful transfer may be directly liable under this section

### Path 3: Undue Influence (§15610.30(a)(3))
Takes/secretes/appropriates/obtains/retains real or personal property **by undue influence** as defined in § 15610.70.
- No intent-to-defraud required — undue influence alone is sufficient
- Cross-reference: wic_15610_70_undue_influence for the 4-factor test

## "Wrongful Use" Standard

§15610.30(b): Wrongful use is a **knew or should have known** standard (objective foreseeability), not a specific intent standard. Person knew or should have known the conduct is **likely to be harmful** to the elder/dependent adult.
This is significantly lower than fraud — negligence-equivalent conduct can constitute financial abuse.

## "Property" — Broad Coverage (§15610.30(c))

An elder is "deprived of a property right" when the abuse occurs through:
- **Agreement** — contract, loan, annuity agreement
- **Donative transfer** — gift, inter vivos transfer
- **Testamentary bequest** — change to will/trust
- Property held **directly or by a representative** — the elder does not need to hold the property personally

## "Representative" Coverage (§15610.30(d))

The statute expressly covers financial abuse committed by:
- Conservators and trustees
- Estate representatives
- Attorneys-in-fact (agents under power of attorney)

This makes conservatorship itself a covered mechanism of financial abuse if the representative takes property for wrongful use or by undue influence.
