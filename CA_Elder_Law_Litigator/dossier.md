### Ann Hillberg — UIT/State Farm/Northern Trust Investigation

**The pattern:**
- HILLBERGMANN compound identity — Ann Hillberg's identity merged with another
- State Farm insurance policy in her name (or partially in her name)
- Northern Trust variable product trusts — sophisticated financial instruments
- $214K/year income but asset-exhausted — classic financial abuse pattern
- Documents at NonFamilyLaw/Hillberg_Ann_StateFarm/

**The elder financial abuse theory:**
Ann Hillberg, if 65+, is an "elder" under WIC §15610.27. If she is a "dependent adult"
(18-64 with functional limitations, WIC §15610.23), the same protections apply.

The Northern Trust trustee and the State Farm agent are "representatives" under
WIC §15610.30(d). Any act by them that took/secreted/appropriated property for wrongful
use, or through undue influence, is financial abuse by definition.

The HILLBERGMANN compound identity suggests identity merger — if Ann's identity was
merged with another person's identity without her consent, ALL financial instruments
opened or modified in that compound identity may constitute financial abuse (obtaining
property by deception and through undue influence over a vulnerable elder).

**Prob. Code §859 double damages:** If the financial abuse was in bad faith — and a
compound identity scheme is inherently bad faith — TWICE the value of property taken
is recoverable.

**Mandated reporter failure (§15630):** The Northern Trust officer who administered
the trusts, and the State Farm agent who issued the policy, are financial institutions
and licensed professionals who had obligations under §15630. If they knew or reasonably
suspected financial abuse and failed to report to APS, they committed a misdemeanor
and are civilly liable.
