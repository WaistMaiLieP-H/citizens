### CAL. WIC CODE § 15610.30 — Financial abuse definition — FETCHED
**Text:** FETCHED (full text — 2026-04-12)
**Key holdings:**
- Financial abuse: **(1)** Takes/secretes/appropriates/obtains/retains real or personal property for wrongful use or with intent to defraud; **(2)** Assists in same; **(3)** Acts by undue influence (WIC §15610.70)
- **"Wrongful use"** = knew or should have known conduct likely to be harmful to elder/dependent adult
- Covers any property right — agreements, donative transfers, testamentary bequests, property held by representative
- **"Representative"** = conservator, trustee, estate representative, OR attorney-in-fact
**Standard ID:** `wic_15610_30_financial_abuse_def`

### CAL. WIC CODE § 15610.70 — Undue influence definition — FETCHED
**Text:** FETCHED (full text — 2026-04-12)
**Key holdings:**
- "Excessive persuasion that causes another person to act or refrain from acting by overcoming that person's free will and results in inequity"
- 4 factors: **(1) Vulnerability** — incapacity, illness, disability, age, impaired cognition, isolation, dependency; **(2) Apparent authority** — fiduciary, family member, care provider, health professional, legal/spiritual adviser; **(3) Actions/tactics** — controlling necessaries of life, medication, interactions, information, sleep; use of affection, intimidation, coercion; haste or secrecy in changes; **(4) Equity of result** — economic consequences, divergence from prior intent, value received vs. conveyed
- Evidence of inequitable result ALONE is NOT sufficient — must establish the influence
**Standard ID:** `wic_15610_70_undue_influence`

### CAL. WIC CODE § 15657.5 — Financial abuse enhanced remedies — FETCHED
**Text:** FETCHED (full text — 2026-04-12)
**Key holdings:**
- **§15657.5(a):** Preponderance standard — if financial abuse proven, court SHALL award attorney fees and costs (including conservator's litigation fees)
- **§15657.5(b):** Clear and convincing standard — if financial abuse + recklessness/oppression/fraud/malice proven, §377.34 cap on damages does NOT apply
- **§15657.5(c):** For punitive damages against employer based on employee acts: must satisfy §3294(b) standard (advance knowledge, ratification, or managing agent) — same employer standard as §15657
- **§15657.5(d):** Does not affect separate punitive damages under §3294
- **§15657.5(e):** Money judgment must expressly state it is based on financial elder/dependent adult abuse claim
**NOTE vs. §15657:** §15657 (physical abuse/neglect) requires recklessness/malice for enhanced remedies; §15657.5 (financial abuse) awards attorney fees at preponderance — lower bar for financial abuse victims
**Standard ID:** `wic_15657_5_financial_abuse_remedies`

**Text:** FETCHED (full text — 2026-04-12)

## CASE LAW SEEDS

1. **Welf. & Inst. Code § 15657 — Delaney v. Baker**, 20 Cal.4th 23 (1999) — Landmark: enhanced remedies apply to professional caretakers (nursing homes, professional fiduciaries); recklessness sufficient; sets standard still used today
2. **Conservatorship of Davidson**, 113 Cal.App.4th 1035 (2003) — Undue influence; professional trustee exerted excessive persuasion over elder; breach of fiduciary duty + financial elder abuse
3. **Bernard v. Foley**, 39 Cal.4th 794 (2006) — Care custodian prohibition on donative transfers from dependent adult; even if elder "wants" to give asset to caretaker, transfer is presumptively invalid
4. **Stebley v. Litton Loan Servicing, LLP**, 202 Cal.App.4th 522 (2011) — Elder financial abuse applied to mortgage/property context; loan modification fraud targeting elder = financial abuse
5. **Das v. Bank of America, N.A.**, 186 Cal.App.4th 727 (2010) — Financial institution liability for elder financial abuse; bank that assists wrongful transfer may be liable; § 15610.30 reaches third-party facilitators

## HISTORICAL CHAIN SEED

**The wound:** California passed the Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act in 1991 (Stats. 1991, c. 774). Before it, elders who were financially exploited had only fraud and breach of fiduciary duty — civil claims that required them to finance litigation against wealthy adversaries while terminally ill. The Act's key innovation: if you prove recklessness, oppression, fraud, or malice, you get attorney fees paid by the defendant. This turned otherwise non-economic cases into viable ones. The Delaney v. Baker (1999) Supreme Court decision then extended this to professional caretakers. The wound that remained: financial abuse by professionals hiding behind trust documents, variable annuities, and insurance products structured to appear voluntary. The Northern Trust UIT is exactly that structure — a product designed to look like a voluntary financial instrument that actually captures an elder's assets under professional management.
