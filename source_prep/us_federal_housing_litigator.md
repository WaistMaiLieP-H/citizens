### GOV CODE § 12955 — FEHA housing discrimination
**Text:** FETCHED (full text — 2026-04-12)
**Key holdings:**
- **§12955(a):** Unlawful for owner of housing accommodation to discriminate or harass any person because of: race, color, religion, sex, gender identity, gender expression, sexual orientation, marital status, national origin, ancestry, familial status, **source of income, disability**, veteran/military status, or genetic information
- **§12955(b):** Unlawful to inquire into any protected characteristic of person seeking to purchase, rent, or lease housing
- **§12955(e):** Financial institutions providing assistance for purchase/refinance/construction CANNOT discriminate on protected basis in terms, conditions, or privileges of that assistance
- **§12955(f):** Unlawful for owner to harass, evict, or discriminate in RETALIATION against person who opposed unlawful practices or participated in proceedings — nothing herein delays unlawful detainer
- **§12955(h):** For-profit blockbusting: inducing sales by representations about entry of protected-class persons into neighborhood = unlawful
- **§12955(i)(1):** Real estate transaction participants (including appraisers) cannot discriminate in availability or terms of transaction on protected basis
- **§12955(k):** Unlawful to otherwise make unavailable or deny a dwelling based on protected class discrimination
- **§12955(l):** Discriminatory land use practices, restrictive covenants, zoning denials based on protected class = housing discrimination
- **§12955(m):** Includes PERCEPTION that person has protected characteristic, or ASSOCIATION with person who has characteristic
- **§12955(p)(1):** "Source of income" includes federal, state, local public assistance AND housing subsidies including Section 8 vouchers
**Application — Honeysuckle:** If the forced sale involved any element of disability-based control (conservatorship targeting a disabled person) or financial institution discrimination in the refinancing/sale terms, §12955(a) and (e) apply. If the $465K equity gap was achieved through discriminatory terms by the financial institutions involved, §12955(e) is the hook.
**Standard ID:** `gov_12955_feha_housing_discrimination`

### GOV CODE § 12956.1 — Restrictive covenant disclosure obligations
**Text:** FETCHED (full text — 2026-04-12)
**Key holdings:**
- **§12956.1(b)(1):** County recorder, title company, escrow company, real estate broker, real estate agent, or association that provides copy of declaration, governing document, or deed MUST place cover page/stamp on first page stating that any restriction based on race, color, religion, disability, or other protected class VIOLATES state and federal fair housing laws and is VOID
- **§12956.1(b)(3):** Title companies and escrow companies delivering deed to owner must also provide Restrictive Covenant Modification form
- **§12956.1(c):** Anyone who records a document FOR THE EXPRESS PURPOSE of adding a racially restrictive covenant = MISDEMEANOR; 3-year SOL from discovery
**Application — HILLBERGMANN title:** If HILLBERGMANN compound identity documents were recorded that contained any discriminatory covenant or restriction, §12956.1(c) creates criminal exposure. If title company or escrow failed to disclose existing restrictive covenants in the Honeysuckle closing documents, §12956.1(b)(1) was violated.
**Standard ID:** `gov_12956_1_restrictive_covenant_disclosure`

### GOV CODE § 12989.1 — FEHA housing civil action
**Text:** FETCHED (full text — 2026-04-12)

### CIV CODE § 1102.3 — TDS delivery and cancellation rights
**Text:** FETCHED (full text — 2026-04-12)
**Key holdings:**
- Seller MUST deliver completed written TDS as soon as practicable BEFORE TRANSFER OF TITLE
- If TDS delivered AFTER execution of offer to purchase: buyer has:
  - 3 days after in-person delivery
  - 5 days after mailing
  - 5 days after electronic delivery

## CASE LAW SEEDS

1. **Fair Housing Act cases — general:** Fair Housing Council of San Fernando Valley v. Roommates.com, LLC, 521 F.3d 1157 (9th Cir. 2008) — FHA applies to website facilitating housing preferences based on protected class; intermediaries not immune
2. **Meyer v. Holley**, 537 U.S. 280 (2003) — Vicarious liability under FHA; corporate employer liable for agent's FHA violations; conventional respondeat superior applies
3. **Havens Realty Corp. v. Coleman**, 455 U.S. 363 (1982) — Tester standing under FHA; anyone whose ability to exercise FHA rights is injured has standing; organizational plaintiffs have standing for diversion of resources
4. **Huntington Branch, NAACP v. Town of Huntington**, 844 F.2d 926 (2d Cir. 1988) — Disparate impact under FHA; neutral policies with discriminatory effect = FHA violation; burden shifts to defendant to show business necessity
5. **California cases — TDS:** Jue v. Smiser, 23 Cal.App.4th 312 (1994) — Seller's obligation to disclose material facts not on TDS form; TDS is floor, not ceiling; fraudulent concealment claim survives TDS compliance
6. **Burnett v. Chimney Sweep**, 123 Cal.App.4th 1057 (2004) — FEHA housing §12955 discrimination; marital status discrimination in rental housing; damages available under §12989.2

## HONEYSUCKLE SALE — SPECIFIC FINDINGS

**The document problem:** 19 unsigned documents in a $605K single-family residential sale. Under CIV §1102, the TDS is mandatory and non-waivable. Under §1102.3, the TDS must be delivered before transfer of title. If the TDS was among the 19 unsigned documents:

1. **Delivery failure** — TDS not delivered before transfer = §1102 violation
2. **Cancellation right never triggered** — if TDS delivered after offer acceptance but before closing, the 3/5-day cancellation clock started; if the party was under conservatorship control, that clock ran without a meaningful opportunity to cancel
3. **Waiver is void** — any provision in the 19 unsigned documents purporting to waive disclosure rights = CIV §1102(c) void as against public policy
4. **Agent liability** — if a real estate broker or agent participated in the sale knowing the TDS was unsigned, BPC §10176 (broker misconduct, owned by CA_Real_Estate_Attorney) and DRE license liability attach

**The equity gap:** $605K sale price → $465K equity → only $109K reached Michael. The $356K gap requires accounting at every step: payoff of liens, closing costs, broker commissions, escrow charges. Each disbursement must be documented and justified. The 4-jurisdiction complexity (CA sale, possible other-state entities receiving proceeds) suggests a structured dispersal designed to obscure the destination of equity.

## HISTORICAL CHAIN SEED

**The wound:** The Fair Housing Act of 1968 was passed one week after Martin Luther King Jr. was assassinated. It was the third major civil rights statute in five years — after the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and Voting Rights Act of 1965. The wound the FHA was meant to close: neighborhoods locked by redlining (banks refusing mortgages in "hazardous" — meaning Black — neighborhoods) and restrictive covenants (deeds that literally prohibited sale to non-white buyers). The FHA made both illegal. The wound did not close. The forms changed. Redlining became "steering" — real estate agents directing Black buyers away from white neighborhoods. Restrictive covenants became HOA rules and informal understanding. The Transfer Disclosure Statement requirement (CIV §1102) came from a different wound: California sellers who concealed material defects, collected the proceeds, and left buyers with uninhabitable property. The TDS made disclosure mandatory and waiver void. Both wounds converge in a case where a vulnerable party — disabled, possibly conserved, surveilled — signs or doesn't sign documents that transfer the largest asset of their life, and the equity disappears.
