Harris v. Capital Growth Investors XIV
805 P.2d 873 (Cal. 1991)

SUMMARY (from training knowledge — see verification status)

The California Supreme Court held that the Unruh Civil Rights Act applies to arbitrary discrimination by business establishments — not only discrimination based on the listed protected characteristics. The case clarified that the enumerated characteristics in CIV §51 are illustrative, not exhaustive.

Key holdings:
- "Business establishments" under Unruh Act are to be interpreted broadly
- The enumerated categories (race, sex, religion, etc.) are not a closed list; the Act prohibits arbitrary, invidious discrimination even on non-listed bases
- However, not all differential treatment triggers Unruh — the discrimination must be "arbitrary" in the sense of lacking a reasonable basis in fact or law

Note for this corpus: Harris is included as a case showing the breadth of "business establishment" construction under CIV §51 and the Act's expansive anti-discrimination mandate.
