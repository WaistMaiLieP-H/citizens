# CIV § 51 Unruh Civil Rights Act — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** civ_51_unruh_civil_rights_act
**Citizen:** CA_Disability_Rights_Litigator
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

All business establishments in California must provide full and equal access to their accommodations, facilities, privileges, and services to persons with disabilities. An ADA violation is automatically a CIV §51 violation — no separate showing required. Direct civil action; no administrative exhaustion required. Minimum $4,000 statutory damages per offense under CIV §52.

The companion statute, CIV §54 (Disabled Persons Act), specifically covers public buildings, medical facilities (hospitals, clinics, physician offices), and public facilities — with $1,000 minimum per offense (CIV §54.3) and a parallel per se ADA rule.

---

## Three Critical Features

### Feature 1 — "Business Establishment" Is Extremely Broad
California courts have interpreted "business establishment" to include virtually any entity that holds itself open to the public in a commercial context:
- Hospitals and medical facilities (explicitly covered by CIV §54 — hospitals, clinics, physicians' offices)
- Courts (state courts are "business establishments" for Unruh purposes under certain circumstances)
- Professional offices (attorney offices, physician offices)
- Financial institutions, insurance companies
- Transportation providers
- Hotels, restaurants, retail establishments

**Key limitation:** Private clubs (genuinely private) and purely private associations may be excluded.

### Feature 2 — ADA Per Se Rule (§51(f) and §54(c))
The ADA per se rule eliminates the need to analyze the Unruh standards separately when ADA is violated:
- **§51(f):** ADA violation = CIV §51 violation (automatically)
- **§54(c):** ADA violation = CIV §54 violation (automatically)

This is the tactical entry point: if ADA Title II or Title III is violated (access to public entity or public accommodation), the California statutory damages ($4,000 per offense for §51/§52; $1,000 per offense for §54/§54.3) attach automatically without proving California-specific elements.

### Feature 3 — No Administrative Exhaustion Required
Unlike FEHA, Unruh/Disabled Persons Act claims do NOT require CRD complaint or right-to-sue letter before filing in civil court. Direct civil action filed in Superior Court. This makes them strategically valuable:
- File immediately without waiting for administrative process
- SOL: 2 years under CCP §335.1 (personal injury SOL applies to Unruh claims)
- Can be combined with FEHA claims (exhaustion required only for FEHA, not Unruh)

## Damages Framework

### CIV §52 — Damages for Unruh (§51) Violations
| Damage Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Actual damages | Full compensatory damages |
| Statutory minimum | $4,000 per offense |
| Punitive damages | Up to 3x actual damages |
| Attorney's fees | Mandatory for prevailing plaintiff |

### CIV §54.3 — Damages for Disabled Persons Act (§54) Violations
| Damage Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Actual damages | Full compensatory damages |
| Additional (treble) | Up to 3x actual damages |
| Statutory minimum | $1,000 per offense |
| Attorney's fees | Mandatory for prevailing plaintiff |

**One-recovery rule (§54.3(c)):** Cannot collect under both §52 (Unruh) and §54.3 (Disabled Persons) for the same act. Choose the larger: §52's $4,000 minimum generally exceeds §54.3's $1,000 minimum.

**But:** §54.3 remedies are NONEXCLUSIVE — can also file CRD complaint and pursue other remedies simultaneously.

---

## The "Per Incident" Calculation

Each separate act of denial or interference is a separate offense with its own minimum statutory damages. In a pattern-of-denial case:

**Example pattern:** If the employer/insurer denied access to medical care on 12 separate occasions:
- 12 × $4,000 = $48,000 minimum statutory damages under Unruh/§52
- Plus actual damages (additional medical costs, delay damages, progression of injury)
- Plus attorney's fees

In a 16-year pattern of denial affecting multiple contexts (medical facilities, courts, employment), the "per offense" count can be substantial independent of actual damages.

## Application to Active Cases

### SIRVA — Denial of Medical Facility Access
The SIRVA injury and subsequent denial of appropriate treatment at medical facilities (hospitals, clinics, physician offices) falls directly within CIV §54(a): "medical facilities, including hospitals, clinics, and physicians' offices." Each denial of access to a medical facility to obtain care for a disability-caused condition = one offense under §54; $1,000 minimum per offense.

If the denial also violates ADA Title III (public accommodation — medical office) or ADA Title II (public entity program) → §54(c) per se violation → no separate California analysis needed.

### Dr. Wiita — Court Proceedings
State court proceedings are covered entities under ADA Title II (public entity programs). If the court failed to accommodate a disability in the criminal/competency proceedings — and ADA Title II was violated — §51(f) makes it an Unruh violation. Direct civil action against the state entity possible under ADA Title II + Unruh overlay.

### Medical Care Denial Pattern — Per-Offense Count
Each denial of required medical treatment to a person with a disability at a covered facility is a separate §54 offense. Over a multi-year denial pattern (bilateral ankles, SIRVA, spine), the per-offense statutory minimum accumulates independently of actual damages proof.

## §51 Unruh vs. §54 Disabled Persons Act — Tactical Choice

| Scenario | §51 (Unruh) | §54 (Disabled Persons Act) |
|---|---|---|
| Business establishment denial | Primary vehicle | Available if business = public facility |
| Medical facility denial | Available (business establishment) | Direct coverage (hospitals/clinics explicitly named) |
| Court/public building access | Available if "business establishment" | Direct coverage (public buildings) |
| Minimum damages per offense | $4,000 (§52) | $1,000 (§54.3) |
| ADA per se rule | Yes (§51(f)) | Yes (§54(c)) |
| Administrative exhaustion | Not required | Not required |
| Same-act recovery cap | Cannot combine §52 + §54.3 for same act | Cannot combine §54.3 + §52 for same act |
