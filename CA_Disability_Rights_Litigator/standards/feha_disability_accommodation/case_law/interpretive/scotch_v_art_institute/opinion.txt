Scotch v. Art Institute of California
173 Cal.App.4th 986 (2009)

SUMMARY (from training knowledge — see verification status)

The California Court of Appeal held that an employer's delay in initiating and completing the interactive process under GOV §12940(n) constitutes a failure to engage, actionable as an independent FEHA violation.

Key holdings:
- Timing matters: the interactive process must begin promptly after the employer learns of the employee's disability and accommodation need
- An employer who stalls for months and then claims no reasonable accommodation was available has failed to engage in good faith
- The employer cannot create the delay and then use the resulting absence of accommodation as a defense
- Interactive process failures are independently compensable; plaintiff need not prove a reasonable accommodation would have resulted

Court: An employer that "runs out the clock" on an employee's need for accommodation — by delaying the interactive process until the employee can no longer continue working — has constructively denied a reasonable accommodation.
