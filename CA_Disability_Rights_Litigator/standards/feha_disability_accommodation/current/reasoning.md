# GOV § 12940 FEHA Disability Accommodation — Reasoning and Historical Loss

## Why This Standard Exists — The Information and Power Gap

When an employee becomes disabled — whether from an industrial injury, illness, or acquired condition — the employer controls the workplace. The employer decides which functions are essential and which are marginal. The employer decides which assignments exist and which schedules are available. The employer decides what "reasonable" means in practice. Without a mandatory process that requires the employer to demonstrate good faith engagement with the disabled employee's needs, the information and power asymmetry almost always produces the same result: the disabled employee is managed out, without any genuine exploration of whether they could have remained employed with support.

GOV §12940(m) and (n) address this structural problem. The reasonable accommodation duty says: you must actually try. The interactive process duty says: you must try with the employee, not around them. Together, they convert what would otherwise be an employer's unilateral judgment call into a documented process with legal accountability on both sides.

## The 1992 ADA Alignment and FEHA Expansion

California's FEHA disability protections significantly expanded in 1992 to align with and exceed the federal Americans with Disabilities Act (1990). The interactive process requirement was codified and clarified over subsequent amendments to make explicit what courts had already been requiring under earlier FEHA interpretations. California's version is deliberately broader than the ADA in several ways:

- **More employees covered:** ADA covers employers of 15+; FEHA covers employers of 5+
- **Disability defined without mitigating measures:** California explicitly includes the "without regard to mitigating measures" standard — medication-controlled conditions still count
- **Broader "regarded as" coverage:** California's "regarded as" disabled definition sweeps broadly
- **Retaliation for accommodation request is independently actionable** under §12940(m)(2) — explicitly stated, not just implied

## The Historical Loss — Paper Compliance vs. Genuine Engagement

The interactive process requirement is theoretically robust. In practice, the historical loss is paper compliance — employers go through the motions of the interactive process without genuine engagement:
- A single HR meeting is documented as the "interactive process"
- The employer's determination that no accommodation is possible is made before the process begins
- Treating physicians are never consulted
- Alternative positions are never explored

Courts distinguish genuine interactive process from paper compliance. But detecting the distinction requires discovery: internal HR documents, communications between HR and the employee, medical documentation requests, and the timeline of the process. Injured workers who cannot access discovery — or who settle before it runs — never know whether a genuine interactive process occurred.

The deeper historical loss: workers compensation and FEHA run on parallel tracks that should reinforce each other. In practice, they are often compartmentalized by the employer and insurer: the WC system processes the injury claim, and the FEHA accommodation obligation is quietly ignored. The worker doesn't know to assert both claims simultaneously, and by the time the limitation becomes clear, SOL issues arise.
