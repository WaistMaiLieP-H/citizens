# GOV § 12940(m)/(n) FEHA Disability Accommodation — Operative Rule

## The Rule

An employer covered by FEHA must: (1) provide reasonable accommodation for the known physical or mental disability of an employee or applicant unless the accommodation would cause undue hardship; and (2) engage in a timely, good faith, interactive process with the employee or applicant to identify effective reasonable accommodations. Failure to do either is an independent violation of FEHA — separately actionable from discrimination.

---

## Element Analysis

### Element 1 — Covered Employer [§12926(d)]
FEHA applies to employers with 5 or more employees. The state, political subdivisions, and cities are covered employers. The scope is extremely broad — virtually all employers in California except very small businesses.

### Element 2 — Known Disability [§12940(m), §12940(n)]
The duty to accommodate and to engage in the interactive process is triggered by the employer's **knowledge** of the employee's physical or mental disability or medical condition. The knowledge need not come from a formal disclosure — a supervisor observing symptoms, a note from a doctor, or even a conversation about limitations can constitute "known" disability.

**Disability defined broadly (§12926(m)/(j)):**
- Physical disability: physiological condition affecting any enumerated body system that limits a major life activity (including musculoskeletal — bilateral ankle injuries; shoulder; spine)
- Mental disability: psychological condition limiting a major life activity (including REGARDED AS having such condition)
- Medical condition: cancer, or any health impairment related to a diagnosis or record of cancer or from a genetic characteristic
- **Without regard to mitigating measures:** glasses, medication, prosthetics — if the underlying condition limits without those measures, it qualifies

### Element 3 — Reasonable Accommodation Duty [§12940(m)(1)]
The employer must identify and provide an accommodation that enables the employee to perform the essential functions of the job. Reasonable accommodation examples:
- Making existing facilities accessible
- Job restructuring (reallocating marginal functions)
- Modified schedules
- Reassignment to a vacant position
- Acquisition/modification of equipment
- Adjusting examinations, training materials, policies
- Providing interpreters or readers

The employer bears the burden of proving **undue hardship** if it refuses accommodation. Undue hardship requires significant difficulty or expense — not mere inconvenience or cost.

### Element 4 — Interactive Process Duty [§12940(n)]
The interactive process is **mandatory** — not optional. When an employee or applicant requests accommodation (or the employer knows of the need), the employer must:
1. Initiate the process promptly
2. Engage in good faith
3. Explore all available options
4. Document the dialogue

**Refusing to engage is an independent violation.** It does not matter whether a reasonable accommodation ultimately exists — if the employer refuses to engage in the process, §12940(n) is violated independently.

**Employer cannot unilaterally decide** that no accommodation exists without completing the interactive process.

### Element 5 — Retaliation Prohibition [§12940(m)(2), (h)]
Retaliating against an employee for requesting accommodation — regardless of whether the request was granted — is an independent violation. The request alone is protected activity.

## Causation and Damages Framework

**Causation:** Mixed-motive applies — disability need not be the ONLY reason for the adverse action; sufficient if it was a "substantial motivating reason."

**Damages available (CRD/court track):**
- Actual damages (lost wages, lost benefits, future earning capacity)
- Emotional distress damages
- Punitive damages (§3294 malice/oppression standard)
- Attorney's fees (mandatory for prevailing plaintiff)
- Injunctive relief (reinstatement, policy change)

**Administrative prerequisite:**
- Must file complaint with Civil Rights Department (CRD — formerly DFEH) within 3 years of violation
- CRD issues right-to-sue letter (immediate upon request)
- 1 year from right-to-sue to file civil action

## Interactive Process — Attack Framework

When the employer failed to accommodate or engage in interactive process:

| Failure Mode | Evidence to Obtain |
|---|---|
| Employer never responded to accommodation request | Request letter/email; employer's silence or denial; no acknowledgment |
| Employer acknowledged but never engaged | Internal communications; HR files; calendar of meetings — there were none |
| Employer engaged once, then stopped | Documentation of breakdown; who stopped the process; final communication |
| Employer unilaterally determined no accommodation possible | Written denial without process; failure to consult treating physician; failure to explore alternatives |
| Employer imposed unreasonable burden on employee | Demanded multiple medical certifications; imposed deadlines inconsistent with medical timeline |

## Application to Active Cases

### Bilateral Ankle Injuries — Employment Accommodation
After the industrial injury, the employer's obligation to accommodate the known physical disability (bilateral ankle impairment) is governed by §12940(m)/(n). If the employer:
- Was aware of the functional limitations from treating physician's reports
- Failed to explore accommodation options (modified duty; different assignment; schedule change)
- Terminated, demoted, or otherwise adversely acted

...then §12940(m) (failure to accommodate) and/or §12940(n) (failure to engage interactive process) are independently violated, separate from the workers compensation claim.

## §12940(m) vs §12940(n) — Distinct Claims

| Claim | What It Requires | What the Employer Did Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| §12940(m) — Failure to accommodate | Employer failed to provide a reasonable accommodation that existed | Ignored available options; refused without exploring |
| §12940(n) — Failure to engage interactive process | Employer did not engage in good faith process | Never responded; engaged superficially; abandoned process |
| §12940(a) — Discrimination | Adverse action because of disability | Termination, demotion, pay cut because of impairment |
| §12940(h) — Retaliation | Adverse action because of protected activity | Fired for complaining about discrimination/requesting accommodation |

All four can be pled simultaneously. All four are independently actionable. Attorney's fees follow the prevailing plaintiff under GOV §12965.
