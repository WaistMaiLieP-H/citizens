## The 1974 Extension to Disability

California's Fair Employment Practice Act (FEPA) — originally enacted in 1959 — addressed race, religion, color, national origin, and ancestry. Disability was added in subsequent amendments, with comprehensive disability employment protections developing through the 1970s. The Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) as it exists today was enacted in 1980, consolidating and expanding the prior framework.

The disability provisions reflect a legislative judgment that physical and mental disability are characteristics irrelevant to an employee's ability to perform their job — a judgment that employment decisions should be based on demonstrated inability to perform essential functions, not on assumption, stigma, or inconvenience.

## Pre-1992: The Missing Interactive Process

Before 1992 and the ADA's enactment, reasonable accommodation was required under California law, but the specific mechanism — the interactive process — was less formally structured. The employer's obligation to engage in good faith dialogue was implied from the reasonable accommodation duty but not expressly codified as a separate violation.
