# Historical Chain — Origin: ADA Title I Employment (1990)

**Standard:** ada_12112_title_i_employment
**Era:** 1990 — Americans with Disabilities Act

---

## The 1990 Enactment

The Americans with Disabilities Act was signed into law on July 26, 1990. ADA Title I became effective July 26, 1992 for employers with 25+ employees and July 26, 1994 for employers with 15+ employees.

The ADA was the product of a decade-long disability rights movement. Congressional findings: 43 million Americans with disabilities; discrimination pervasive and persistent; existing law (principally §504 of the Rehabilitation Act) applied only to programs receiving federal funding — leaving private employers largely uncovered.

Title I filled the private employer gap. It required covered employers to provide reasonable accommodation, prohibited discrimination in all employment conditions, and created the interactive process framework that California would later codify explicitly in FEHA §12940(n).

## The 2008 ADAAA Correction

The Supreme Court interpreted ADA's "substantially limits a major life activity" standard narrowly — requiring severe restriction. This excluded many people with significant impairments (corrected vision; controlled conditions) from ADA coverage. The ADAAA (2008) explicitly rejected those interpretations, restored broad coverage, and required that "substantially limits" not demand significant restriction.
