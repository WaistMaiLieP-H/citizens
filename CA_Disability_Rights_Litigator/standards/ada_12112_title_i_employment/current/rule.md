# 42 USC § 12112 ADA Title I — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** ada_12112_title_i_employment
**Citizen:** CA_Disability_Rights_Litigator
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

A covered employer with 15 or more employees may not discriminate against a qualified individual with a disability in any aspect of employment. Discrimination includes: failure to provide reasonable accommodation; using qualification standards that screen out disabled individuals; and any limitation, segregation, or classification based on disability. ADA Title I violation is a per se CIV §51 (Unruh) violation in California under §51(f).

---

## Four-Element Framework

### Element 1 — Covered Entity [§12111(2), §12111(5)]
ADA Title I applies to employers with 15+ employees. Labor organizations and employment agencies are also covered entities. The 15-employee threshold is the primary distinction between ADA (federal) and FEHA (California, 5+ employees). For employers with 5-14 employees: FEHA covers, ADA does not.

### Element 2 — Qualified Individual with a Disability [§12111(8)]
The plaintiff must be:
- (a) An individual with a disability (physical or mental impairment substantially limiting a major life activity; or record of such impairment; or being REGARDED AS having such impairment)
- (b) Qualified — able to perform the essential functions of the job with or without reasonable accommodation

**Substantially limits:** Post-ADAAA (2008 amendments), "substantially limits" is broadly construed — it does not require severe restriction. The ADAAA explicitly rejected the Supreme Court's narrow interpretation in *Sutton v. United Air Lines* (1999) and *Toyota Motor Mfg. v. Williams* (2002).

**Without regard to mitigating measures:** Per the ADAAA, the substantially limits analysis disregards mitigating measures (medication, prosthetics, hearing aids, etc.) — except for ordinary eyeglasses/contact lenses.

### Element 3 — Adverse Action [§12112(a)]
Any adverse employment action: refusal to hire; discharge; compensation reduction; denial of promotion; demotion; denial of training; or any other change in terms, conditions, or privileges of employment.

### Element 4 — Because of Disability [§12112(b)]
Causation: the disability was the "but-for" cause of the adverse action (federal standard — more restrictive than California's "substantial motivating reason" standard under FEHA).

---

## Reasonable Accommodation Duty [§12112(b)(5)]

Failure to provide reasonable accommodation is an independent form of discrimination under Title I. The framework mirrors FEHA §12940(m):
- Employer must identify and provide an accommodation that enables the individual to perform essential job functions
- Employer bears burden of demonstrating undue hardship if it refuses
- Interactive process required (implied from Title I; explicit in California FEHA §12940(n))

**Essential functions:** Functions that are fundamental to the position; employer's written job description is evidence but not determinative.

## Federal vs. California Comparison

| Issue | ADA Title I (42 USC §12112) | FEHA (GOV §12940) |
|---|---|---|
| Employer size | 15+ employees | 5+ employees |
| Causation standard | But-for | Substantial motivating reason |
| Mitigating measures | Excluded (ADAAA) | Excluded |
| Interactive process | Implied | Explicit (§12940(n)) |
| Administrative exhaustion | EEOC charge (300 days) | CRD complaint (3 years) |
| Punitive damages | Limited (intentional discrimination + malice/reckless) | Available under §3294 |
| CA statutory damages trigger | ADA violation = §51 Unruh violation | N/A |

**Strategic principle:** Always plead both ADA Title I and FEHA §12940 in disability employment cases. FEHA's broader coverage (5-employee threshold; substantial motivating reason causation; longer SOL) makes it the primary California vehicle. ADA Title I triggers the §51(f) Unruh per se rule ($4,000 minimum per offense) and opens the federal court track.

## Application to Active Cases

### Bilateral Ankle Injuries — Employment Accommodation
After industrial injury resulting in bilateral ankle impairment: if the employer has 15+ employees, ADA Title I §12112(b)(5) requires reasonable accommodation. This runs parallel to the FEHA §12940(m) track. The ADA violation also triggers §51(f) Unruh per se — $4,000 minimum per offense in California civil court.

### UA342 Identity Replacement — "Retired at 44"
If the employer falsely coded Michael's employment status as "retired" — functioning as if he were disabled and separating him from employment without accommodation or interactive process — ADA §12112(b)(1) (limiting/segregating based on disability or perceived disability) and §12112(b)(5) (no reasonable accommodation) are implicated.
