# Historical Chain — Origin: ADA Title II (1990)

**Standard:** ada_12131_title_ii_public_services
**Era:** 1990 — ADA Title II enacted; 1999/2004 — Supreme Court landmark decisions

---

## 1990 — The Government Gap

Before 1990, §504 of the Rehabilitation Act (1973) applied to programs receiving federal financial assistance. Most state and local government programs received some federal funding — but not all, and enforcement was uneven. ADA Title II closed the gap: all state and local government entities, regardless of federal funding status, must ensure equal access for disabled individuals.

## 1999 — Olmstead: Institutionalization Is Discrimination

*Olmstead v. L.C.* (1999) 527 U.S. 581: the Supreme Court held (6-3) that unjustified institutionalization of disabled persons is discrimination under Title II. The ADA requires states to provide services in the most integrated setting appropriate to the individual's needs. States must have plans for moving disabled individuals out of institutions and into community-based settings when appropriate. The decision transformed Title II from an access statute into an integration mandate.

## 2004 — Tennessee v. Lane: Courts Must Be Accessible

*Tennessee v. Lane* (2004) 541 U.S. 509: the Supreme Court held that Congress validly abrogated state sovereign immunity for Title II suits involving fundamental rights — including access to the courts. George Lane, a paraplegic, had been arrested for refusing to crawl up courthouse stairs. The Court held this was cognizable under Title II and that sovereign immunity did not bar the suit.
