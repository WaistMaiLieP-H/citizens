# 42 USC §§ 12131–12132 ADA Title II — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** ada_12131_title_ii_public_services
**Citizen:** CA_Disability_Rights_Litigator
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

No qualified individual with a disability may be excluded from participation in, denied benefits of, or subjected to discrimination by any public entity — which includes all state and local governments, agencies, departments, and instrumentalities. This covers courts, courthouses, administrative agencies, police departments, public hospitals, and every other state or local government program and service. ADA Title II violation is a per se CIV §51 (Unruh) violation under §51(f).

---

## Scope — What Is a "Public Entity"

Every state and local government entity is a "public entity" under Title II. No revenue threshold; no employee count threshold. All of them:
- State and federal courts (Superior Court, Court of Appeal, Supreme Court)
- State agencies (DDS, SSA by cross-reference, CalVCB, CDI, CSLB, OAH)
- County and city governments and their departments
- Police departments (including Antioch PD, Oakland PD, Concord PD)
- Public hospitals and county health departments
- Probate courts (conservatorship proceedings)
- WCAB (Workers Compensation Appeals Board)
- CRD (Civil Rights Department)

### The Court Access Right — Tennessee v. Lane

*Tennessee v. Lane* (2004) 541 U.S. 509 is the controlling authority for court access claims under Title II: Congress validly abrogated state sovereign immunity as applied to fundamental rights — and the right of access to the courts is a fundamental right. States cannot claim immunity from Title II suits in federal court when the claim involves access to judicial services.

**What court access requires:**
- Physical access to courthouses (ramps, elevators, accessible restrooms)
- Effective communication accommodations (interpreters, captioning, accessible formats)
- Reasonable modifications to court procedures and policies
- Equal access to court programs and services for disabled litigants

### The Olmstead Integration Mandate

*Olmstead v. L.C.* (1999) 527 U.S. 581: unnecessary institutionalization of disabled persons is discrimination. Title II requires states to provide services in the most integrated setting appropriate to the individual's needs. Applied to conservatorship: unnecessary placement of a person under conservatorship when community-based services would suffice is an Olmstead violation if the placement is driven by disability.

## Three-Element Framework

### Element 1 — Qualified Individual with a Disability
Same definition as ADA Title I, but applied to program participation: an individual who, with or without reasonable modifications, meets the essential eligibility requirements for the service or program. Courts cannot add disability-based requirements that are not essential eligibility criteria.

### Element 2 — Public Entity
State or local government entity or instrumentality. No threshold — all government entities are covered.

### Element 3 — Exclusion, Denial, or Discrimination
The individual was:
- Excluded from participation in a public entity's service or program
- Denied the benefits of a public entity's service or program
- Subjected to discrimination by a public entity

Discrimination includes failure to make reasonable modifications; failure to provide effective communication; imposing eligibility criteria that screen out disabled persons without necessity; and Olmstead-prohibited unnecessary institutionalization.

---

## Reasonable Modifications [28 CFR § 35.130(b)(7)]

A public entity must make reasonable modifications to its policies, practices, and procedures when necessary to avoid discriminating against disabled persons — unless the modification would fundamentally alter the nature of the service or program. This is the Title II parallel to the Title I "reasonable accommodation" duty — applied to government services rather than employment.

**Court-specific examples:**
- Providing ASL interpreter for deaf litigant (effective communication)
- Scheduling hearings at accessible courtrooms
- Allowing additional time for a litigant whose disability affects processing speed
- Providing printed materials in accessible format
- Modifying filing procedures for litigants with mobility impairments

## Application to Active Cases

### Dr. Wiita Competency Proceedings — Court Access
If the competency proceedings under PC §1368 were conducted without reasonable modifications for any disability — or if the criminal proceedings themselves denied equal access to a disabled defendant — ADA Title II §12132 is violated. *Tennessee v. Lane* confirms state courts are covered public entities with no sovereign immunity for court access claims.

### Conservatorship Proceedings — Olmstead
If secret conservatorship proceedings were conducted without providing appropriate participation opportunities to the conservatee — and if the conservatorship placement was unnecessary when community-based services were available — the Olmstead integration mandate under Title II is violated.

### CalVCB Administrative Proceedings — Agency Discrimination
CalVCB is a state agency — a "public entity" under Title II. If CalVCB's application and appeal process denied equal access to a disabled applicant (unreasonable deadlines; inaccessible process; failure to communicate effectively), Title II is violated. This runs parallel to the CalVCB APA challenge under GOV §§11340/11513/§1094.5.

### Police Interactions — Antioch PD
Police departments are public entities under Title II. Failure to accommodate a disability during an arrest or detention — including failure to recognize a disability-related behavior as disability rather than non-compliance — is a Title II violation.

## Damages in Federal Court vs. California State Court

| Court | Title II Damages Available |
|---|---|
| Federal court | Compensatory damages (intentional discrimination required for money damages — *Daveri v. Hanover Ins. Co.* line); injunctive relief available without proof of intentional discrimination |
| California Superior Court | ADA Title II violation = per se Unruh violation → $4,000 minimum statutory damages per offense (no intentional discrimination requirement for the California statutory minimum) |

**Strategic principle:** Federal court for injunctive relief and systemic change. California Superior Court for the per-offense statutory damages using the §51(f) Unruh per se rule. Often file both.
