# Historical Chain — Origin: Rehabilitation Act Section 504 (1973)

**Standard:** rehab_act_504_no_exclusion
**Era:** 1973 — Rehabilitation Act enacted; 1977 — Regulations signed; 1987 — CRRA correction

---

## The Single Sentence (1973)

Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (PL 93-112) was enacted as a brief addition to a vocational rehabilitation statute. Its core prohibition — no program receiving federal financial assistance may exclude disabled individuals solely by reason of disability — was modeled on Title VI (race) and Title IX (sex) civil rights provisions. It passed without the deliberation that typically accompanies major civil rights legislation.

## The Four-Year Wait (1973–1977)

The statute required HEW to issue implementing regulations. Four years passed without regulations. Three Secretaries of HEW (Weinberger, Mathews, Califano) refused to sign. The delay was about cost: accessible buildings, accessible programs, employment accommodations — all required federal funding recipients to spend money they had not budgeted.

## The 1977 Sit-In

On April 5, 1977, disability rights organizations coordinated simultaneous occupations of HEW offices in eight cities. The San Francisco occupation, at 50 Fulton Street, lasted 25 days. The occupiers — including many wheelchair users, blind individuals, and others with significant disabilities — refused to leave. Solidarity came from the Black Panther Party (who brought food), United Farm Workers, and other civil rights organizations.

On April 28, 1977, HEW Secretary Joseph Califano signed the Section 504 regulations. The sit-in had worked. The 1977 regulations became the foundation of modern disability rights law.

## 1984 — Grove City Narrowing; 1987 — CRRA Restoration

*Grove City College v. Bell* (1984) narrowed §504 to program-specific coverage. Congress responded with the Civil Rights Restoration Act of 1987, extending §504 (and parallel civil rights statutes) to institution-wide coverage when the entity receives any federal funding.
