## Case Analysis

### Case 1: Bilateral Ankle Injuries — ADA Title I Employment Accommodation

Michael Hartmann sustained bilateral ankle injuries as an occupational injury (UA Local 342). If an employer covered by ADA (15+ employees; see 42 USC §12111) was aware of the disability and failed to:
- Engage in the interactive process (GOV §12940(n))
- Provide reasonable accommodation (GOV §12940(m))
- Or took adverse action because of the disability or record of disability

...each failure is an independent ADA + FEHA violation. The FEHA interactive process obligation is mandatory — failure to engage is a standalone violation even if no accommodation could ultimately be provided.

**Threshold question:** Whether the employer(s) at relevant times were ADA-covered (15+ employees). UA Local 342 dispatches to union employers — most are covered.

### Case 2: Identity Replacement — "Retired at 44" Credit Report

The credit report showing Michael as "retired" at age 44 may reflect a disability-based classification — either:
- Someone reported Michael as disabled/retired to freeze his employment identity
- An employer or reporting entity classified him as having a permanent work-disabling condition

ADA and FEHA both prohibit discrimination based on a RECORD of disability (42 USC §12102(1)(B); GOV §12926(j)). If an employer received a report that Michael was "retired due to disability" and acted on it, that is discrimination based on a record of disability.

**Investigation needed:** Which entity reported "retired" status; what record it was based on; which employers or creditors accessed and acted on it.

### Case 3: SIRVA — Disability Coverage

Shoulder Injury Related to Vaccine Administration (SIRVA) from COVID vaccine (~Nov. 2021), followed by failed arthroscopic surgery (Apr. 2022, Dr. Wiseman), resulting in ongoing shoulder impairment.

If SIRVA substantially limits a major life activity (lifting, reaching, range of motion — relevant to plumbing work), it meets the ADA definition of disability (42 USC §12102(1)(A)). This activates:
- ADA Title I accommodation obligations if employment-related
- ADA Title II obligations in any VICP/court proceedings related to SIRVA
- Unruh/DPA coverage for any medical facility denying treatment

### Case 4: Dr. Wiita PC §1368 — Court Access Disability Discrimination

The §1368 competency evaluation process is a state court proceeding subject to ADA Title II (42 USC §12131). If Michael Hartmann was subjected to a competency evaluation that was:
- Conducted via defective telehealth without proper consent
- Used to delay or deny court participation
- Based on a disability classification without proper process

...the state court's failure to ensure a proper, compliant evaluation process may be an ADA Title II violation. See Tennessee v. Lane, 541 U.S. 509 (2004) — court proceedings are Title II public services.

### Case 5: Conservatorship — ADA Title II State Court Obligations

ADA Title II requires that state courts accommodate disabled persons in all proceedings, including conservatorship proceedings. If Michael was subject to a conservatorship proceeding and was not provided:
- Proper notice
- Opportunity to participate with accommodation
- Accessible court services

...the state court may have violated Title II. Olmstead v. L.C., 527 U.S. 581 (1999) additionally requires that states provide community-based alternatives to institutionalization.
