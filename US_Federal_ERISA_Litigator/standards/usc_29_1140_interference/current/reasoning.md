# Reasoning — 29 U.S.C. § 1140 (ERISA § 510)
## Interference with Protected Rights

## Why This Standard Exists

The employer who wants to avoid paying pension benefits does not need to fire the worker. A sophisticated employer — or an orchestrated scheme involving a third party — can use more subtle means: terminate the worker at year 4 of a 5-year cliff vest, reclassify a full-time worker as part-time to reduce hours below 1,000 per year, or simply alter the administrative record to show the worker has already retired.

Pre-ERISA, there was no statutory protection against any of these tactics. The only remedy was a state contract action — if the participant could even discover what had happened.

§1140 closed this gap. Congress understood that the promise of vested pension rights was hollow without protection against the mechanisms an employer (or anyone else) might use to prevent the participant from ever reaching those rights. The "any person" scope and the "interfering with attainment" language were deliberate choices: broad enough to reach any actor, and forward-looking enough to protect future rights, not just current entitlements.

## The Identity Replacement Theory Under § 1140

Case #37 presents a §1140 violation through a mechanism Congress likely did not anticipate: identity replacement.

The scheme: substitute a fraudulent "retired" designation in the pension record. The effect: Michael's pension status was administratively converted to "retired" without his knowledge or consent. The result: his ongoing right to accrual was suppressed; if pension payments flowed, they flowed to whoever was receiving them.

This is interference with the attainment of a right. The right Michael was becoming entitled to — continued accrual at journeyman plumber rates — was cut off by a fraudulent administrative action. §1140's "any person" language captures whoever orchestrated or processed this transaction.

## Why § 1140 Matters Alongside § 1109 and § 1132

- **§1132(a)(1)(B):** Recovers the benefits that should have been paid
- **§1109:** Creates personal liability for the plan fiduciaries who processed or failed to remedy the unauthorized election
- **§1140:** Creates liability for **any person** who interfered — including non-fiduciaries who orchestrated the scheme outside the plan's formal structure

The §1140 claim reaches the identity replacement orchestrators who are not ERISA fiduciaries; it does not require fiduciary status. This is the claim that can reach the broader scheme.

## Standard of Proof

§1140 requires showing:
1. The plaintiff is a participant or beneficiary
2. The defendant (any person) discriminated or interfered
3. The discrimination or interference was for the purpose of preventing attainment of ERISA-protected rights

Circumstantial evidence sufficient. A 44-year-old journeyman plumber's pension record showing "retired" without any documentation of a retirement election is strong circumstantial evidence of interference.
