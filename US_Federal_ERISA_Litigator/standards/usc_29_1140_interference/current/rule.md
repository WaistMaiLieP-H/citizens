# Rule — 29 U.S.C. § 1140 (ERISA § 510)
## Interference with Protected Rights

**Citation:** 29 U.S.C. § 1140; ERISA § 510
**Authority:** Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974, Pub. L. 93-406

---

## The Rule

It is unlawful for **any person** to:
1. Discharge, fine, suspend, expel, discipline, or **discriminate** against a participant for exercising a right under an ERISA plan or this subchapter; or
2. **Interfere with the attainment** of any right to which a participant **may become entitled** under the plan or this subchapter; or
3. Retaliate against a person who provides information or testifies in a proceeding relating to ERISA.

Enforcement: §1132 applies — same civil enforcement mechanism as all other ERISA claims.

## The "Any Person" Breadth

Unlike §1140's employer-focused anti-retaliation prong, the "interfering with attainment" prong applies to **any person** — not just the employer. This means:
- Plan administrators who process unauthorized elections are persons within §1140's scope
- Third parties who orchestrate identity replacement schemes are persons within §1140's scope
- Identity thieves who use stolen credentials to alter pension records are persons within §1140's scope

## What "Interference with Attainment" Covers

The statute protects benefits the participant **may become entitled to** — not just currently vested benefits. This forward-looking protection covers:
- Suppression of pension access before full accrual
- Unauthorized retirement elections that trigger early pension calculations at reduced benefit levels
- Identity substitutions that reroute pension payments to unauthorized recipients
- Administrative freezes imposed without the participant's knowledge or consent

## The Discriminatory Purpose Test

Courts evaluate circumstantial evidence of discriminatory intent. In Case #37, the unauthorized "retired at 44" designation:
1. Directly suppressed Michael's ability to attain ongoing pension accrual
2. Was not authorized by Michael
3. Coincides with a documented pattern of identity replacement affecting all financial and administrative records

## Case #37 Application

The unauthorized retirement election (or designation) placed in Michael's UA342 pension record is a §1140 violation: it interfered with his right to attain ongoing pension benefit accrual by terminating his participant status without his consent. The "any person" scope reaches whoever submitted or processed the unauthorized designation — including identity thieves and plan administrators who failed to verify authenticity.

**Primary claim:** §1132(a)(1)(B) benefit recovery + §1132(a)(3) equitable relief (restoration of participant status, injunction against continued interference)
**Companion claim:** §1109 fiduciary breach personal liability for plan administrators who processed or failed to remedy the unauthorized designation
