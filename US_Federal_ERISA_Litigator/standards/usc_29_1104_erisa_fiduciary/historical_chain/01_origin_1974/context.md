# §1104 Origin: The Common Law Trust Translated to Federal Statute

## The Wound

Before ERISA, pension fund trustees owed duties under state trust law — where it applied.
In many cases, the pension fund was not a trust at all — it was a contractual fund with
no fiduciary obligations. Employers could direct pension assets for their own benefit.
Multi-employer fund trustees sometimes invested in related-party transactions that harmed
the fund. Workers had no federal standard for what trustees owed them.

## Congress's Choice: The Prudent Expert

ERISA §404 codified common law trust duties and extended them to all plan fiduciaries.
The "prudent man" standard was the traditional trust law test — a trustee should exercise
the judgment of a prudent man. ERISA added "familiar with such matters" — creating the
"prudent expert" standard. Pension management requires expertise; the standard reflects that.

The key innovation: **"solely in the interest of the participants and beneficiaries."**
This exclusivity requirement had no parallel in the prior law. It prohibited exactly
what Congress had documented in hearings: trustees acting in the interest of the sponsoring
employer, related parties, or their own financial interests.

## Multi-Employer Taft-Hartley Funds

Taft-Hartley multi-employer plans have joint labor-management trustees. §404 applies
to all of them — labor trustees and management trustees equally. The duty of loyalty
runs to the participants and beneficiaries, not to the union or to the contributing
employers. This is a structural tension the law resolved in favor of the worker.
