# Donovan v. Bierwirth — Holding

**Citation:** 680 F.2d 263 (2d Cir. 1982)
**Court:** Second Circuit (Judge Friendly)
**Standard:** ERISA §404 conflict-of-interest analysis

## The Rule

When an ERISA fiduciary acts under a conflict of interest:

1. The duty of loyalty is absolute — "solely in the interest of participants and
   beneficiaries" means 100%, not primarily or substantially.

2. The conflicted fiduciary bears the burden of proving the decision was untainted.

3. Courts apply heightened scrutiny to conflicted decisions.

4. The test is not whether the fiduciary believed they were acting in participants'
   interests — it is whether the decision actually was in participants' interests,
   free from any taint of self-dealing.

## Application to UA342

The UA342 trustees who are also contributing employers or who have relationships
with parties who benefit from Michael's pension freeze face a Donovan conflict.

If any trustee voted to implement (or ignore) the fraudulent "retired" status while
having a financial or personal stake in the outcome, the Donovan burden applies:
**they must prove the decision was untainted — and the default is that it was not.**

The practical challenge: without discovery into the plan's administrative records,
Michael cannot know who made the alteration decision, when, or why. The §502(a)
complaint must get to discovery. Donovan helps establish why the trustees, once
named, bear the burden rather than Michael.
