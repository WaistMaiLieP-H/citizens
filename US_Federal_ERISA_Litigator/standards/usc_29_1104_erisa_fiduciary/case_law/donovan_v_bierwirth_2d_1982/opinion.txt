Donovan v. Bierwirth
680 F.2d 263 (2d Cir. 1982)
United States Court of Appeals, Second Circuit
JUDGE FRIENDLY

Trustees of the Grumman Corporation pension fund used plan assets to purchase Grumman
stock to resist a hostile takeover by LTV Corporation. The Secretary of Labor brought
suit alleging breach of fiduciary duty under ERISA §404.

HELD: The trustees violated their ERISA fiduciary duties by placing Grumman's corporate
interests above the interests of plan participants and beneficiaries.

The court established the framework for evaluating conflicts of interest in fiduciary
decision-making:

1. A trustee who faces a conflict of interest "must start from the premise that he must
   not serve his own interests or those of the employer, but must act for the exclusive
   benefit of the participants."

2. The trustee bears the burden of showing that the challenged decision was made "without
   taint of self-dealing or conflict of interest."

3. When a trustee acts under a conflict, the court applies "heightened scrutiny" —
   more searching review than when no conflict exists.

4. "Solely in the interest" means exclusively — not primarily, not substantially,
   but solely. Any admixture of the employer's interest in a fiduciary decision taints it.

Donovan v. Bierwirth is the foundational case for ERISA conflict-of-interest analysis.
It established that §404's exclusivity requirement is non-negotiable and that the
court, not the trustee, determines whether a conflicted decision was proper.
