# 29 U.S.C. §1104 — ERISA §404: Fiduciary Duties

## Statutory Rule (Training Knowledge — verified=false)

§1104(a)(1): A fiduciary shall discharge his duties with respect to a plan solely in
the interest of the participants and beneficiaries and:

**(A) for the exclusive purpose of:**
   (i) providing benefits to participants and their beneficiaries; and
   (ii) defraying reasonable expenses of administering the plan;

**(B) with the care, skill, prudence, and diligence under the circumstances then
prevailing that a prudent man acting in a like capacity and familiar with such matters
would use in the conduct of an enterprise of a like character and with like aims**
(the "prudent man" or "prudent expert" standard);

**(C)** by diversifying the investments of the plan so as to minimize the risk of
large losses (investment duty — less relevant to UA342);

**(D)** in accordance with the documents and instruments governing the plan insofar
as such documents and instruments are consistent with the provisions of this subchapter.

**[Verified=false — verify at law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1104]**

## The Four Fiduciary Duties

1. **Duty of loyalty:** Act solely in the interest of participants and beneficiaries
   — not in the interest of the employer, the trustees' employers, or any third party.
   A trustee who alters a participant's record to benefit a third party has violated
   the duty of loyalty.

2. **Prudent expert standard:** Act with the care and skill of a prudent expert
   familiar with employee benefit plan administration. Negligent record-keeping
   violates this duty; fraudulent record alteration is a categorical breach.

3. **Duty to diversify:** (Investment context — less relevant to record-keeping claims)

4. **Duty to follow plan documents:** Administer the plan according to its governing
   documents. A trustee who creates a fraudulent "retired" status that contradicts
   the actual plan document provisions violates §1104(a)(1)(D).

## Application to UA342 Fiduciary Breach Theory

The pension record alteration theory under §404:

1. The UA342 trustees are fiduciaries under §1002(21)(iii) — they exercise
   discretionary authority in plan administration

2. Altering Michael's record to show "retired at 44" without his consent:
   - Violates the duty of loyalty (not in Michael's interest — destroys his pension)
   - Violates the prudent expert standard (no legitimate reason to show false retirement status)
   - Violates the duty to follow plan documents (if the plan requires accurate records
     of actual retirement, a false "retired" entry contradicts plan terms)

3. Remedy via §502(a)(2): Fiduciary breach relief to the plan — restoration of assets,
   correction of records, removal of breaching trustee
   Remedy via §502(a)(3): Individual equitable relief — reformation of the plan record,
   restoration of benefit eligibility

## The Honest Litigation Risk

§1104 fiduciary breach claims against pension fund trustees are difficult:
- Multi-employer trustees are jointly managed by labor and management
- Courts give some deference to trustee decisions
- Proving specific intent to harm a beneficiary requires substantial evidence
- The plan's own counsel will argue administrative error, not fraud

The §502(a)(3) + Varity reformation theory is the cleaner vehicle than §1104 alone.
