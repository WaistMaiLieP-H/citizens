# Historical Origin — 29 U.S.C. § 1053 (ERISA § 203) — 1974

## The wound

Before ERISA, a pension was a promise — and nothing more. Workers spent 20 or 30 years earning a pension only to see it disappear. The mechanism was simple: pension plans had no federal vesting requirements. An employer could promise retirement benefits and then change the vesting rules, terminate the plan, or simply close the plant before vesting conditions were met.

The Studebaker plant closure (December 1963) became the public symbol. Workers in the union pension plan received a few cents on the dollar — or nothing. A Senate labor subcommittee investigation found the problem was national: pension promises were being made and broken with no legal recourse.

The wound § 1053 addresses: a worker who devoted their working life to a trade could reach retirement age and discover that their employer contributions had not "vested" and could be forfeited. There was no federal minimum standard for when those contributions became the worker's property.

## The 1974 enactment

ERISA (Pub. L. 93-406), signed on Labor Day 1974, established the first federal minimum vesting standards in § 203 (29 U.S.C. § 1053). The original Act provided two options: 10-year cliff vest or 5-to-15-year graded schedule.

**TRA 1986:** Replaced original schedules with the current 5-year cliff or 3-to-7-year graded schedule — workers who left at year 9 receiving nothing was still too slow.

**EGTRRA 2001:** Added accelerated vesting for employer matching contributions (3-year cliff or 2-6-year graded).

## Case #37 anchor

A UA Local 342 journeyman plumber with 16+ years of covered employment is fully vested under any ERISA vesting schedule. The § 1053(a) "nonforfeitable" language is absolute — an unauthorized retirement election, a fraudulent identity substitution, or an administrative freeze cannot extinguish vested rights. The word "nonforfeitable" means not capable of being taken. The legal claim in Case #37 is not whether the benefits are vested (they are, unambiguously) but why authorized access to them has been suppressed.
