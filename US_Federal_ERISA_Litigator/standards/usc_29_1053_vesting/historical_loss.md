# Historical Loss — 29 U.S.C. § 1053 Failures

## Failure Mode 1 — Hours Undercounted; Vesting Delayed or Denied

**The Loss:** A plan administrator credits fewer hours than the worker actually worked — through administrative error, record destruction, or intentional manipulation. The worker's years-of-service count is short. The worker does not satisfy the vesting schedule on the administrator's records even though they satisfied it in reality. The worker's accrued benefit is treated as forfeitable.

**The Fix:** § 1053(c)(1) requires the plan to credit hours based on records. Under ERISA § 209 (29 U.S.C. § 1059), plans must maintain records sufficient to determine benefits. If the plan cannot produce records, the burden may shift to the plan — courts have found that a plan cannot deny benefits based on missing records where the plan was responsible for keeping them.

---

## Failure Mode 2 — Plan Amendment Retroactively Reduces Accrued Benefit (Anti-Cutback Violation)

**The Loss:** A plan is amended after a worker has accrued a benefit. The amendment purports to reduce the previously accrued benefit or eliminate an early retirement option. The worker loses a benefit they earned under the prior plan terms.

**The Fix:** § 1054(g) (anti-cutback rule) prohibits plan amendments that reduce already-accrued benefits. This works in tandem with § 1053 — the nonforfeiture guarantee means the plan cannot take away what has already vested. A post-accrual amendment that reduces vested benefits violates both § 1053 and § 1054(g).

---

## Failure Mode 3 — Suspension Without Required Notice

**The Loss:** A plan suspends benefit payments without providing the notice required under 29 C.F.R. § 2530.203-3. The participant does not know why payments stopped or how to challenge the suspension. Payments are missed for months or years.

**The Fix:** ERISA regulations require that when a plan suspends benefits, it must notify the participant, explain the reason, and provide a claims procedure. Failure to provide notice is a separate ERISA violation independent of whether the suspension itself was lawful.

---

## Failure Mode 4 — Expulsion/Termination Triggers Wrongful Forfeiture Attempt

**The Loss:** A plan participant is expelled from the union or terminated from covered employment. The plan administrator treats the expulsion as triggering forfeiture of pension benefits. This is unlawful — once vested, the right to accrued benefits is nonforfeitable regardless of the reason for separation from employment or union membership status.

**Significance for this case:** Michael Hartmann's suspension (2/3/2025) and expulsion (3/3/2025) from UA Local 342 cannot be used to forfeit his vested pension benefits. § 1053 makes those benefits nonforfeitable upon satisfaction of the vesting schedule. The expulsion may affect future accrual or eligibility for current distributions, but it cannot eliminate already-vested accrued benefits.

---

## Failure Mode 5 — Identity Substitution in Pension Records

**The Loss:** A person with a similar name, similar SSN, or related household connection causes the plan to attribute their credits or debts to another participant. The affected participant's accrued benefit is reduced, denied, or credited to a different account. This can arise from clerical error, fraud, or intentional identity substitution.

**Significance for this case:** The Treasury Department's response that bonds held "under Michael's name" belong to "another individual" corroborates a possible identity substitution pattern. If the same pattern has affected the UA342 pension trust records — where another person's hours or credits have been attributed to or from Michael Hartmann's account — the actual accrued benefit may differ from what the fund's records show. A plan audit and independent hours verification are appropriate remedies under ERISA § 502(a)(3).
