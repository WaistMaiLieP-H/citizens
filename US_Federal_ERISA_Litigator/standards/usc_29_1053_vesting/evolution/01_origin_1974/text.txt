29 U.S.C. § 1053 — Minimum Vesting Standards
As enacted: Pub. L. 93-406, 88 Stat. 855 (September 2, 1974)

Original 1974 vesting schedules (LESS favorable than current):

Option 1 — 10-YEAR CLIFF VESTING:
Employee is fully vested after 10 years of service; 0% before that.

Option 2 — "RULE OF 45" (AGE + SERVICE COMBINED):
If employee's age and years of service total at least 45, employee must be at least 50% vested.
Each additional year of service adds 10% until 100% vested.

Option 3 — 5-15 YEAR GRADED:
  5 years  = 25%
  6 years  = 30%
  7 years  = 35%
  8 years  = 40%
  9 years  = 45%
  10 years = 50%
  11 years = 60%
  12 years = 70%
  13 years = 80%
  14 years = 90%
  15+ years = 100%

Year of service: 1,000 hours in 12-month period — same definition as current.

Note: The 1974 schedules allowed 10-year cliff vesting, meaning workers who left before 10 years
(or were terminated before 10 years) could receive nothing for all employer contributions. This is
exactly the Studebaker scenario — workers terminated or laid off before reaching the vesting threshold.
The 10-year cliff was a major target of subsequent reform.
