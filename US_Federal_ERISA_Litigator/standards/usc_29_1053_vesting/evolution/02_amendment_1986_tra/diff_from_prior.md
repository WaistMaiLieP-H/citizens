# Diff: 1974 Origin → 1986 Tax Reform Act

## The core change: halving the vesting period

1974 allowed 10-year cliff vesting. After 1986, the maximum cliff is 5 years.
The 1986 reform cut the maximum cliff vesting period in half.

## Why 1986?

By the mid-1980s, two data points:
1. The 1974 schedules hadn't solved the problem. Workers were still losing pension benefits after
   8-9 years of service because of the 10-year cliff.
2. The tax reform act was the vehicle — Congress bundled the vesting improvement with the massive
   TRA 1986 overhaul to get it passed.

The result was the 5-year cliff / 3-7 year graded standard that still applies today as the
MINIMUM for standard pension accruals (employer matching has been further accelerated by EGTRRA 2001).

## Logical delta

1974 logic: "Some vesting protection, however long, is better than none."
1986 logic: "The window in which a worker can lose all employer contributions is too long.
Cut it from 10 years to 5 years maximum."

## Application to Case #37

Under the 1986 standards (which applied to Michael's UA 342 work starting ~2009), after 5 years
of plan participation Michael was fully vested under the cliff schedule — or 20-100% under the
graded schedule starting at year 3. By year 7 (2016 at the latest), Michael was 100% vested
regardless of which schedule UA 342's plan uses. Vesting is not an issue in Case #37.
