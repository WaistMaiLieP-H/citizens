# Reasoning — How Courts Apply 29 U.S.C. § 1053

## Core Function

§ 1053 sets the minimum vesting schedules for defined benefit pension plans. Once an employee has worked enough years to satisfy the applicable vesting schedule, their accrued benefit is "nonforfeitable" — the plan cannot take it away. The statute creates the fundamental protection: years of work create property rights in pension benefits.

## The Two Vesting Schedules

**Five-Year Cliff (§ 1053(a)(2)(A)):** Zero vesting until year 5, then 100%. An employee who leaves after 4.9 years gets nothing from employer contributions.

**Three-to-Seven Graded (§ 1053(a)(2)(B)):** Graduated vesting starting at year 3 (20%) through year 7 (100%). An employee who leaves after 3 years has a vested right to 20% of accrued employer contributions.

Most collectively bargained plans (union plans including UA Local 342) use the cliff vesting schedule because the collective bargaining agreement defines the vesting terms. The specific schedule must be verified in the plan document.

## "Year of Service" Definition

§ 1053(c)(1) defines "year of service" as a 12-month period during which an employee has at least 1,000 hours of service. This definition is critical for workers with variable hours — if a worker completes fewer than 1,000 hours in any 12-month period, that year does not count toward vesting.

For UA Local 342 members: hours are tracked through the Joint Apprenticeship and Training Program and the pension trust fund. A member's hours of service record from the union is evidence of years of service toward vesting.

## Break-in-Service Rules

§ 1053(c)(3) establishes "break in service" rules — a period when an employee stops accruing service credit. However, a "break in service" does not forfeit ALREADY VESTED benefits. If a worker is fully vested and then has a break, the vested benefit survives. Non-vested benefits can be forfeited after a sufficient break, but vested benefits are "nonforfeitable" under § 1053(a).

## The Nonforfeiture Guarantee

"Nonforfeitable" means the plan cannot condition, reduce, or eliminate the accrued benefit based on any subsequent event — including:
- The participant's death (survives to beneficiary)
- The participant's disability
- The participant's termination of employment (once vested)
- Any plan amendment made after the benefit accrued (anti-cutback rule, § 1054(g))

## Application to UA342 Pension

UA Local 342 is a multi-employer defined benefit pension plan governed by ERISA. A member who has worked sufficient hours to satisfy the applicable vesting schedule has a nonforfeitable right to the accrued benefit. Suspension of pension benefits before the member is eligible to receive them — or failure to credit properly documented hours — implicates § 1053.

The documented 17,327.75 hours in Michael Hartmann's UA342 record, spanning from apprenticeship through journeyman status, represents the primary evidence for: (1) years of service calculation; (2) vesting completion; (3) accrued benefit amount. Any discrepancy between the union's hours record and the pension trust's records is a § 1053 compliance issue.

## Suspension and Termination Distinction

A pension benefit can be SUSPENDED (payments temporarily stopped) for certain reasons (e.g., return to covered employment over earnings limits). But suspension is different from forfeiture. A vested benefit cannot be forfeited — only deferred. If a plan claims a benefit has been "reduced" or "denied" rather than suspended pending eligibility, the plan must demonstrate that the specific ERISA exception applies.
