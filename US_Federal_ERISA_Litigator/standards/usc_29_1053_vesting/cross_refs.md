# Cross-References — 29 U.S.C. § 1053

## Within US_Federal_ERISA_Litigator

| Standard | Relationship |
|---|---|
| `usc_29_1132_civil_enforcement` | § 1132(a)(1)(B) is the primary enforcement mechanism for vesting rights — a participant brings suit under § 502(a)(1)(B) to recover benefits denied in violation of § 1053 |
| `usc_29_1109_fiduciary_breach` | A plan administrator who improperly calculates vesting service or treats vested benefits as forfeitable may be liable for fiduciary breach under § 1109 |
| `usc_29_1104_fiduciary_duties` | The prudent man standard (§ 1104) requires accurate maintenance of service records; records failures that impair vesting rights violate the fiduciary duty |
| `usc_29_1113_erisa_sol` | Statute of limitations for § 1053 claims; concealment of vesting miscalculation triggers the fraud-or-concealment extension |
| `usc_29_1001_erisa_purpose` | § 1001's Congressional findings about pension security inform the broad remedial construction of § 1053's vesting protections |

## Federal Statutory Cross-References

- **29 U.S.C. § 1054** (ERISA § 204) — benefit accrual requirements and the anti-cutback rule (§ 1054(g)); works with § 1053 to protect both the right to vest and the benefit amount once vested
- **29 U.S.C. § 1059** (ERISA § 209) — employer recordkeeping obligations; plans must maintain records to determine vesting; supports shifting burden to plan when records are missing
- **29 U.S.C. § 1025** (ERISA § 105) — benefit statement rights; a participant may request a statement of accrued and vested benefits
- **29 C.F.R. § 2530.200b-2** — DOL regulations defining "hour of service" for vesting calculations
- **29 C.F.R. § 2530.203-3** — suspension of benefits rules; notice requirements when benefits are suspended

## Multi-Employer Plan Specific

For multi-employer plans (like UA Local 342 pension trust), additional rules apply under:
- **29 U.S.C. § 1381 et seq.** (ERISA § 4201 et seq.) — withdrawal liability provisions
- **PBGC regulations** — Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation insures certain benefits in multi-employer plans up to statutory limits

The plan document and collective bargaining agreement govern the specific vesting schedule — ERISA sets the floor (minimum vesting) but the CBA/plan may be more generous.
