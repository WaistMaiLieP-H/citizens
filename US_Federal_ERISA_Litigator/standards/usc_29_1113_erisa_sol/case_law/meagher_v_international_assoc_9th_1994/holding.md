## The Rules

**Three-year trigger:** Only actual knowledge (not inquiry notice, not constructive knowledge)
starts the three-year period. You must know the identity of the fiduciary, the specific breach,
and that it was a breach — not just that something seems wrong.

**Fraud/concealment exception:** Any active misrepresentation, deliberate omission, or
affirmative act of concealment by the fiduciary triggers the six-year-from-discovery period.

## Application to UA342

The identity replacement scheme is fraud or concealment by definition:
- The pension record was altered without Michael's knowledge or consent
- The alteration was not disclosed to Michael
- The scheme actively prevented Michael from accessing information that would have
  revealed the breach (SIM swap fraud, isolation, communications interception)

Michael had no "actual knowledge" of the breach until the credit report revealed
"retired at 44." He had no reason before that to examine his pension record or
believe it had been altered.

**The filing window:**
Six years from the date Michael discovered the "retired at 44" credit report entry.
This is the operative SOL under the fraud/concealment exception.

**Tolling for communications fraud:**
If the SIM swap / communications interception can be proven to have prevented Michael
from receiving plan communications (annual funding notices, benefit statements) that
would have revealed the fraudulent "retired" status, additional equitable tolling
arguments apply — the fraudulent concealment was multi-layered and systematic.

## The Three-Year Warning

If any court finds that Michael had "actual knowledge" of the pension status issue
before the 3-year cutoff, the claim is barred unless the fraud/concealment exception
applies. The litigation must be prepared to argue:
1. No actual knowledge until specific discovery date
2. Even if inquiry notice existed, Ninth Circuit requires actual knowledge
3. Fraud/concealment exception independently extends the period to six years from discovery
