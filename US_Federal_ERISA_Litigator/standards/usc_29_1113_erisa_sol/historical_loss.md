# Historical Loss — 29 U.S.C. § 1113 Failures

## Failure Mode 1 — Three-Year Clock Misidentified

**The Loss:** A participant receives a denial of benefits and waits more than three years before filing suit, believing the six-year period applied. The defendant moves to dismiss because the participant had "actual knowledge" of the breach when they received the denial. The court agrees — the three-year period ran from the denial date. The case is time-barred.

**Prevention:** When a benefit denial occurs, assume the three-year clock has started. File suit (after exhausting administrative appeals) within three years of the denial — do not rely on the six-year period unless there is a specific reason actual knowledge was not acquired at the time.

---

## Failure Mode 2 — Concealment Not Argued; Fraud Extension Lost

**The Loss:** A plan's fiduciaries manipulated records for years before the participant discovered the breach. The breach occurred 8 years before filing. The participant does not specifically plead fraud or concealment. The court applies the standard six-year period from the breach date and dismisses the claim as time-barred.

**Prevention:** Where any breach involves misrepresentation, omission of material information, or concealment of the fiduciary's acts, explicitly plead fraud or concealment under § 1113. The extension runs six years from discovery — but only if the pleading establishes concealment.

---

## Failure Mode 3 — Wrong SOL Applied to § 1132(a)(1)(B) Claim

**The Loss:** A participant suing for benefits under § 1132(a)(1)(B) argues that § 1113's six-year period applies. The court holds § 1113 only applies to fiduciary breach claims — for § 1132(a)(1)(B) benefit recovery, the applicable SOL is borrowed from state law. The state law SOL (e.g., 4-year California contract SOL) is shorter than expected.

**Prevention:** For benefit recovery claims under § 1132(a)(1)(B), calculate both: (1) the borrowed state SOL from the final denial; (2) whether § 1113's period might also apply if the claim is reframed as a fiduciary breach.

---

## Failure Mode 4 — Discovery Rule Not Applied to Concealed Breach

**The Loss:** A plan administrator concealed a fiduciary breach by providing inaccurate plan statements for years. The participant eventually discovered the manipulation. The court applies the six-year period from the date of the breach (not discovery) because the plaintiff did not specifically establish "fraud or concealment" under § 1113's extension language. The claim is time-barred even though the participant could not have known earlier.

**Prevention:** § 1113's "fraud or concealment" language requires affirmative showing of concealment. Document: (1) what the fiduciary did to hide the breach; (2) what the participant received that was inaccurate; (3) when and how the participant actually discovered the true facts.
