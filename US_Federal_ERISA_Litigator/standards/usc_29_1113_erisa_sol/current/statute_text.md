## Full Text

No action may be commenced under this subchapter with respect to a fiduciary's breach of any responsibility, duty, or obligation under this part, or with respect to a violation of this part, after the earlier of—

**(1)** six years after **(A)** the date of the last action which constituted a part of the breach or violation, or **(B)** in the case of an omission, the latest date on which the fiduciary could have cured the breach or violation, or

**(2)** three years after the earliest date on which the plaintiff had actual knowledge of the breach or violation;

except that in the case of fraud or concealment, such action may be commenced not later than six years after the date of discovery of such breach or violation.

---

**Structural Table:**

| Track | Clock starts | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Long-stop (§1113(1)) | Date of last act or omission | 6 years |
| Knowledge-based (§1113(2)) | Date of actual knowledge of breach | 3 years |
| Fraud/concealment exception | Date of discovery | 6 years from discovery |

**"Actual knowledge" (Intel v. Sulyma, 140 S.Ct. 768 (2020)):** Requires that plaintiff actually knew of the breach — not merely had access to disclosing documents. Constructive notice insufficient.

**Ongoing breach (Tibble v. Edison, 575 U.S. 523 (2015)):** Each failure to monitor or correct a continuing breach is a new act; the 6-year long-stop runs from the last act, not the first.

**Equitable tolling:** Identity theft, incapacity, and systematic interference with a participant's ability to pursue claims may toll § 1113's periods beyond the fraud-or-concealment extension.
