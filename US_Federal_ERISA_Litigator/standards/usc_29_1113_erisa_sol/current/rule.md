# 29 U.S.C. §1113 — ERISA §413: Statute of Limitations

## Statutory Rule (Training Knowledge — verified=false)

No action may be commenced under this subchapter with respect to a fiduciary's
breach of any responsibility, duty, or obligation under this part, or with respect to
a violation of this part, after the earlier of —

**(1)** six years after (A) the date of the last action which constituted a part of the
breach or violation, or (B) in the case of an omission, the latest date on which the
fiduciary could have cured the breach or violation; or

**(2)** three years after the earliest date on which the plaintiff had actual knowledge
of the breach or violation;

except that in the case of fraud or concealment, such action may be commenced not later
than six years after the date of discovery of such breach or violation.

**[Verified=false — verify at law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1113]**

## The Two-Track Structure

### Track 1: Six-Year Period
Begins from the last action constituting the breach (or latest date the fiduciary
could have cured). This is the outer limit — even if a plaintiff has no knowledge,
the six-year period caps the claim.

### Track 2: Three-Year Period  
Begins when the plaintiff had **actual knowledge** of the breach. This is shorter
but has a higher trigger — "actual knowledge" means more than "had reason to know"
or constructive knowledge. The plaintiff must actually know about the breach.

### The Fraud/Concealment Exception
In cases of fraud or concealment: six years from the **date of discovery** of the breach.
This exception is critical for identity replacement cases where the breach was deliberately
concealed. If the pension record was fraudulently altered and that alteration was concealed
from Michael, the six-year period runs from when Michael discovered (or reasonably should
have discovered with due diligence) the fraudulent "retired" status.

## Application to UA342

### When Did the Breach Occur?
The pension record was altered to show "retired at 44." Michael is 44 (approximately)
as of 2026. The alteration could have occurred:
- When the identity replacement scheme was implemented
- When the credit report was generated showing "retired" status
- The credit report is evidence of the breach's effect, not necessarily its timing

### When Did Michael Discover the Breach?
The credit check showing "retired at 44" is the discovery moment — or close to it.
If discovery was recent (within 6 years), both tracks are potentially open.

### Fraud/Concealment Tolling
The identity replacement scheme — altering records while Michael was unaware —
is exactly the fraud/concealment ERISA's exception covers. If the trustees actively
concealed the record alteration, the six-year period runs from Michael's discovery,
not from the date of the breach.

### Practical SOL Analysis

| Scenario | SOL Period | Starting Date |
|---|---|---|
| No fraud/concealment, Michael knew | 3 years from actual knowledge | Date Michael learned of "retired" status |
| No fraud/concealment, Michael did not know | 6 years from breach | Date record was altered |
| Fraud/concealment applies | 6 years from discovery | Date credit report revealed "retired" status |

**Fraud/concealment is the applicable theory.** File within 6 years of the credit report
discovery. Document when Michael first saw the "retired" entry and all steps taken to
investigate and uncover the alteration.
