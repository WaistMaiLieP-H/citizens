## Federal Case Law on § 1113

- **Intel Corp. Investment Policy Committee v. Sulyma, 140 S.Ct. 768 (2020)** — "actual knowledge" for 3-year period requires plaintiff to have actually known, not merely had access to disclosing documents; critical for concealed breaches
- **Tibble v. Edison International, 575 U.S. 523 (2015)** — an ongoing breach (e.g., maintaining imprudent investments) gives rise to a continuing duty; new six-year period runs from each new act of breach; important for cases with continuing fiduciary failures

## Tolling Doctrines

Beyond § 1113's fraud-or-concealment extension, courts have applied equitable tolling to ERISA claims where:
- The plan administrator's misrepresentations prevented the participant from knowing they had a claim
- The participant was under a disability preventing them from pursuing the claim
- Extraordinary circumstances beyond the participant's control prevented timely filing

For cases involving incapacity, identity theft, or systematic interference with a participant's ability to pursue claims, equitable tolling may extend § 1113's periods beyond the fraud-or-concealment extension.
