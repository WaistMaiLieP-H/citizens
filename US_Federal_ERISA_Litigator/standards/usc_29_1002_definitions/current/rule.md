## §1002(1) — "Employee Welfare Benefit Plan" / "Welfare Plan"

Any plan, fund, or program established or maintained by an employer or by an employee
organization, or by both, for the purpose of providing for its participants or their
beneficiaries, through the purchase of insurance or otherwise, medical, surgical, or
hospital care or benefits, or benefits in the event of sickness, accident, disability,
death or unemployment, or vacation benefits, apprenticeship or other training programs,
or day care centers, scholarship funds, or prepaid legal services.

**Case #37 anchor:** UA342's health and welfare fund is a §1002(1) welfare plan. Cal. CIV
§56.10(c)(21) cross-references "employee welfare benefit plan, as defined under Section 3(1)
of ERISA (29 U.S.C. Sec. 1002(1))" — confirming the statutory address and that California
courts treat ERISA §1002(1) as determinative for plan-type classification.

---

## §1002(2) — "Employee Pension Benefit Plan" / "Pension Plan"

Any plan, fund, or program established or maintained by an employer or by an employee
organization, or by both, to the extent that by its express terms or as a result of
surrounding circumstances such plan, fund, or program:

(i) provides retirement income to employees, or
(ii) results in a deferral of income by employees for periods extending to the termination
of covered employment or beyond,

regardless of the method of calculating the contributions made to the plan, the method
of calculating the benefits under the plan, or the method of distributing benefits from
the plan.

**Case #37 anchor:** The UA Local 342 multi-employer defined benefit pension fund is a
§1002(2) pension benefit plan — it provides retirement income, is established under a
collective bargaining agreement, and is a Taft-Hartley §302(c)(5) joint labor-management
trust. This classification is the gateway to §§1053, 1104, 1109, 1132, and 1140.

---

## §1002(3) — "Employee Benefit Plan" / "Plan"

The term "employee benefit plan" or "plan" means an employee welfare benefit plan or
an employee pension benefit plan or a plan which is both an employee welfare benefit
plan and an employee pension benefit plan.

**Function:** Umbrella term used wherever the statute addresses both welfare and pension
plans together. When only "pension plan" or "welfare plan" is referenced, the narrower
§1002(2) or §1002(1) definition controls.

## §1002(7) — "Participant"

Any employee or former employee of an employer, or any member or former member of an
employee organization, who is or may become eligible to receive a benefit of any type
from an employee benefit plan which covers employees of such employer or members of
such organization, or whose beneficiaries may be eligible to receive any such benefit.

**Case #37 anchor — CRITICAL:** The definition turns on whether Michael "is or may become
eligible to receive a benefit" — not on what the plan's corrupted administrative record
currently reflects. If the pension record fraudulently shows "retired at 44," the underlying
factual employment history still establishes participant status. A fraudulently altered record
cannot retroactively strip statutory participant standing.

---

## §1002(8) — "Beneficiary"

A person designated by a participant, or by the terms of an employee benefit plan, who
is or may become entitled to a benefit thereunder.

---

## §1002(21)(A) — "Fiduciary"

A person is a fiduciary with respect to a plan to the extent:

(i) he exercises any discretionary authority or discretionary control respecting management
of such plan or exercises any authority or control respecting management or disposition
of its assets;

(ii) he renders investment advice for a fee or other compensation, direct or indirect,
with respect to any moneys or other property of such plan, or has any authority or
responsibility to do so; or

(iii) he has any discretionary authority or discretionary responsibility in the
administration of such plan.

**Case #37 anchor:** Any plan trustee or administrator who processed, approved, or failed
to correct the unauthorized "retired at 44" designation is acting under §1002(21)(A)(iii) —
exercising discretionary authority or responsibility in plan administration. That makes the
record alteration or failure-to-correct a fiduciary act subject to §1104 and personal
liability under §1109. Fiduciary status is functional, not formal: what matters is what
the person actually did, not what their title says.

---

## §1002(23) — "Normal Retirement Benefit"

The greater of (A) the early retirement benefit under the plan, or (B) the benefit under
the plan commencing at normal retirement age.

**Vesting schedule function:** §1002(23) defines the benefit that §1053 vesting schedules
protect. A participant's accrued benefit is measured against normal retirement benefit
to determine whether partial vesting percentages have been satisfied.

---

## §1002(24) — "Accrued Benefit"

(A) In the case of a defined benefit plan, the individual's accrued benefit determined
under the plan and expressed in the form of an annual benefit commencing at normal
retirement age.

(B) In the case of a plan which is an individual account plan, the balance of the
individual's account.

**Case #37 anchor:** The UA342 plan is a defined benefit plan. Michael's accrued benefit
is his annual benefit earned through the date his participation was effectively suppressed
by the unauthorized retirement designation. The fraudulent record alteration did not
eliminate the accrued benefit — it prevented further accrual and suppressed access to
what had already accrued.

---

## §1002(37)(A) — "Multiemployer Plan"

A plan maintained pursuant to one or more collective bargaining agreements between one
or more employee organizations and more than one employer.

**Case #37 anchor:** UA Local 342 operates a multi-employer plan under a CBA. This
classification governs which ERISA provisions apply and confirms that both the Union
and the participating employers are parties with obligations under the plan. The
Multiemployer Pension Plan Amendments Act of 1980 (MPPAA) applies; withdrawal liability
rules may be relevant if an employer claims Michael's employment terminated.
