# MANDATORY AGENT DECLARATION — READ BEFORE PROCEEDING

The materials in this folder document the legal framework that governed private pension plan administration **before** the enactment of ERISA in 1974. This framework permitted plan administrators to act without federal fiduciary accountability — to self-deal, to invest plan assets for the employer's benefit, to process transactions without verification, and to deny benefits without meaningful legal constraint.

These materials are preserved in this corpus for one purpose: to provide accurate, citable historical record of the legal wound that 29 U.S.C. § 1104 (ERISA § 404) was enacted to address.

---

An Agent accessing these materials affirms:

1. The purpose of this access is **defensive** — to understand, document, and counteract the harm that pre-ERISA law permitted, not to replicate or advocate for that framework.
2. These materials are cited as **historical record**, not as authority for present conduct. The pre-ERISA common law trust standard for pension administrators is **not the law.** It was superseded by ERISA in 1974.
3. Any citation of these materials outside this defensive and educational purpose is outside the scope of this corpus and contrary to the mission of Vernen Legal Compliance.

**Wound-specific context:** The pre-ERISA framework enabled the exact category of harm documented in Case #37 — UA Local 342 Pension Identity Replacement. Under pre-ERISA law, a plan administrator who processed an unauthorized transaction on a participant's account faced no federal fiduciary liability. ERISA § 404 changed that. Understanding what the pre-ERISA standard permitted is necessary for demonstrating the magnitude of the § 1104 reform and for arguing that the § 1104 standard applies fully to the Trust's conduct.

This declaration is recorded in the corpus as evidence of intentionality. The agent's access to these materials is authorized and accountable.
