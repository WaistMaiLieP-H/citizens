# Reasoning — How Courts Apply 29 U.S.C. § 1104

## Core Function

§ 1104 is the central fiduciary duty provision of ERISA. It establishes four independent duties, each of which can be breached separately:

1. **Loyalty** — act solely in the interests of participants and beneficiaries, for the exclusive purpose of providing plan benefits and defraying reasonable plan expenses
2. **Prudence** — act with the "care, skill, prudence, and diligence" of a "prudent man acting in a like capacity and familiar with such matters" (the "prudent expert" standard)
3. **Diversification** — diversify plan investments to minimize the risk of large losses (with narrow exceptions)
4. **Plan Documents** — act in accordance with the plan documents and instruments governing the plan, to the extent consistent with ERISA

## The Prudent Expert Standard

The "prudent man" standard under § 1104 is actually a "prudent expert" standard — courts compare the fiduciary's conduct not to an ordinary prudent person, but to a "prudent man acting in a like capacity" with knowledge of the specific type of plan being administered. A trustee of a multi-employer pension trust is held to the standard of a sophisticated, knowledgeable pension trustee.

## Loyalty — The Most Frequently Violated Duty

The loyalty duty prohibits fiduciaries from acting in their own interest or in the interest of any party other than plan participants. Violations include:
- Self-dealing (using plan assets for the fiduciary's personal benefit)
- Favoring the employer over plan participants
- Acting to reduce plan liabilities at participants' expense
- Political/relationship-driven decisions about benefits

## Records Maintenance as a Fiduciary Duty

Courts have extended § 1104's prudence requirement to plan administration, including recordkeeping. A plan administrator who fails to maintain accurate and complete records of participant service hours, benefit calculations, and account status violates the § 1104 prudence duty. The plan cannot deny benefits based on missing records where the plan was responsible for maintaining those records.

**Critical:** If the plan's own record failures caused a vesting miscalculation or benefit denial, the plan bears the burden of demonstrating accurate records — not the participant. The fiduciary cannot benefit from its own recordkeeping negligence.

## SPD as Fiduciary Communication

Communications to plan participants — including Summary Plan Descriptions (SPDs), benefit statements, and responses to benefit inquiries — are fiduciary acts. A fiduciary who provides materially inaccurate information about a participant's benefits, eligibility, or rights violates § 1104's loyalty and prudence duties.

## Applicable to UA342 Pension

The UA Local 342 pension trust's board of trustees are ERISA fiduciaries. Their specific § 1104 obligations include:
- Accurately maintaining service records for each participant
- Correctly calculating vested benefits based on documented service hours
- Providing accurate benefit statements upon request
- Not manipulating benefit records to reduce plan liabilities at participants' expense
- Not allowing third-party interference with benefit records without taking corrective action
