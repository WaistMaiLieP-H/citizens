# Rule — 29 U.S.C. § 1109 (ERISA § 409)
## Liability for Breach of Fiduciary Duty

**Source:** Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974, Pub. L. 93-406, § 409, 88 Stat. 886 (Sept. 2, 1974)  
**Current through:** Pub. L. 117-2 (American Rescue Plan Act, 2021)

---

### (a) Personal liability of fiduciary

Any person who is a fiduciary with respect to a plan who breaches any of the responsibilities, obligations, or duties imposed upon fiduciaries by this subchapter shall be personally liable to make good to such plan any losses to the plan resulting from each such breach, and to restore to such plan any profits of such fiduciary which have been made through use of assets of the plan by the fiduciary, and shall be subject to such other equitable or remedial relief as the court may deem appropriate, including removal of such fiduciary. The fiduciary may also be removed from office.

### (b) No liability for acts before or after fiduciary status; co-fiduciary acts

No fiduciary shall be liable with respect to a breach of fiduciary duty under this subchapter if such breach was committed before he became a fiduciary or after he ceased to be a fiduciary with respect to any particular transaction, and no fiduciary shall be liable for a breach of fiduciary duty with respect to any particular transaction if he was not a fiduciary at the time such transaction occurred.

---

**Note — Co-Fiduciary Liability (§ 1105):**  
Section 1105 extends § 1109 liability to fiduciaries who knowingly participate in another fiduciary's breach, knowingly conceal a breach, or have knowledge of a breach and fail to make reasonable efforts to remedy it. Co-fiduciary liability means all trustees on a joint board (union and employer) may be jointly and severally liable for a breach by any one trustee where the others knew or should have known.
