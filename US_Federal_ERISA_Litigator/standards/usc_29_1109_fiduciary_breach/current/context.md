# Context — 29 U.S.C. § 1109 ERISA Fiduciary Breach

## The wound and the promise

**The wound:** Before ERISA, pension plan administrators owed no enforceable federal duty to plan participants. They could self-deal, invest in risky employer ventures, accept kickbacks from service providers, and make benefit decisions based on their own financial interest — all without legal consequence under federal law.

**The promise:** Section 1109 makes every fiduciary personally liable for losses to the plan caused by their breach of duty. Combined with § 1104's "prudent expert" standard, this creates the highest standard of conduct in federal law — stricter than corporate law's business judgment rule, modeled on trust law's strictest fiduciary obligations.

## Who is a fiduciary under ERISA?

### Functional definition (§ 1002(21)(A))
A person is a fiduciary to the extent they:
- Exercise any discretionary authority or control over plan management
- Exercise any authority or control respecting plan assets
- Render investment advice for compensation with authority to make investment decisions
- Have any discretionary authority or responsibility in plan administration

The definition is functional, not formal. A person who exercises discretionary authority IS a fiduciary even if not named as one in the plan document.

### Named vs. functional fiduciaries
- **Named fiduciary:** Identified in the plan document; has overall responsibility
- **Functional fiduciary:** Exercises discretionary authority over specific functions; liable for those functions

**Application to Case #37:** The UA Local 342 pension plan administrator who processed the fraudulent retirement election exercised discretionary authority over plan administration. That discretion included the decision to verify the identity of the participant submitting the retirement election. Failure to verify — processing an election submitted by an unauthorized person — is an exercise of that discretionary authority. The administrator is a functional fiduciary with respect to that transaction.

## The § 1104 duty standard — "prudent expert"

Section 1104 requires the skill "that a prudent man acting in a like capacity and familiar with such matters would use." This is the prudent *expert* standard, not the prudent layperson standard of trust law. For a pension plan administrator:

- A prudent expert in plan administration would implement identity verification protocols for retirement elections
- A prudent expert would not process a retirement election based solely on a paper submission without confirming the participant's identity
- A prudent expert receiving a retirement election for a 44-year-old union plumber — well below normal retirement age — would treat the submission as heightened-risk and require verification

## Elements of a § 1109 fiduciary breach claim

1. **The defendant is a plan fiduciary** — named or functional
2. **The defendant breached a fiduciary duty** under § 1104 (prudence, loyalty, following plan terms, diversification)
3. **The plan suffered a loss** — or the fiduciary made a profit through use of plan assets
4. **Causation** — the breach caused the loss

### Loss calculation for Case #37

| Loss Category | Calculation Method |
|---|---|
| Benefit payments to fraudulent claimant | Actual payments made under the unauthorized election |
| Accrual loss | Benefit accrual foregone during period of fraudulent election (UA Local 342 journeyman-scale benefit accrual) |
| Restoration cost | Administrative cost of correcting the fraudulent election |
| Investigation cost | Plan's cost to investigate and prosecute the fraud |

The plan is made whole under § 1109; the steward's individual losses are remedied under § 1132(a)(1)(B) and (a)(3).

## The verification failure theory

**The core argument for Case #37:**

A pension plan administrator processing a retirement election exercises discretionary authority over plan administration. The duty of prudence under § 1104(a)(1)(B) requires implementing identity verification procedures adequate to prevent unauthorized benefit elections — particularly:
- For sub-normal-retirement-age elections (age 44)
- In an era of documented identity fraud against benefit systems
- Where the plan has any record of the participant's contact information and could verify

**The breach:** Processing the election without adequate identity verification, allowing an unauthorized third party to trigger retirement status and pension freeze for an active participant.

**The DOL guidance anchor:** The Department of Labor has issued guidance on cybersecurity and identity verification obligations for pension plan fiduciaries (DOL Cybersecurity Program Best Practices, April 2021). While not binding, DOL guidance defines the standard of care a prudent expert fiduciary would apply.

## Co-fiduciary liability (§ 1105)

Under § 1105, a fiduciary may be liable for another fiduciary's breach if the fiduciary:
- Knowingly participated in the breach
- Enabled the breach through concealment
- Failed to remedy a known breach when the duty required remediation

If the fraudulent election was facilitated by an insider — someone within the plan administration — co-fiduciary liability under § 1105 extends to any fiduciary who had knowledge and failed to act.

## The 6-year limitation on § 1109 claims

Under § 1113:
- 6 years from the last act that constituted the breach, OR
- 3 years from actual knowledge of the breach by the complainant

Where fraud is involved, the 6-year period applies (the longer limitation), and fraudulent concealment tolls even that period.

**Application:** If the fraudulent retirement election was filed ~2019-2020 (the credit check showing "retired at 44" suggests it has been on record for some period), the 6-year limitations period runs from the date of the filing. Investigation is needed to establish when the unauthorized election was submitted.

## Bilateral analysis

**As complainant:** The steward pursues § 1109 breach via § 1132(a)(2) against the plan administrator for processing the unauthorized election without adequate verification. Relief: plan restored to position it would occupy absent the breach; steward's accrual status restored; benefits paid to the fraudulent claimant recovered.

**As respondent:** No scenario in which the steward is a plan fiduciary or faces § 1109 liability.
