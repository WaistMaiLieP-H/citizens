# Reasoning — How Courts Apply 29 U.S.C. § 1109

## Core Function

§ 1109 establishes personal liability for ERISA fiduciaries who breach their duties. A fiduciary who breaches any of the § 1104 duties is personally liable for: (1) any losses to the plan resulting from the breach; (2) any profits made through use of plan assets; AND (3) "such other equitable or remedial relief as the court may deem appropriate, including removal of such fiduciary."

## "Personal Liability" — Against the Individual

The liability attaches to the fiduciary personally — not just to the plan or the employer. A trustee or plan administrator who breaches their duty can be personally liable for the amount of the plan's losses.

## Elements of a § 1109 Claim

1. **Fiduciary status** — the defendant was a fiduciary as defined by § 1002(21)(A): a person who exercises discretionary authority or control over plan management, plan assets, or plan administration
2. **Breach of duty** — the defendant breached one of the § 1104 duties (prudence, loyalty, diversification, compliance with plan documents)
3. **Causation** — the breach caused a loss to the plan
4. **Loss** — the plan suffered an identifiable loss

The breach must cause the loss — correlation alone is insufficient. Courts examine whether the plan would have suffered the loss if the fiduciary had acted properly.

## Equitable Relief Including Removal

§ 1109's "other equitable or remedial relief" includes the court ordering the removal of a fiduciary. A trustee or plan administrator who has committed fiduciary breach can be removed from their position. This remedy is distinct from monetary recovery — it protects the plan and its participants from future harm.

## Co-Fiduciary Liability

§ 1105 (not separately briefed here) extends liability to a fiduciary who: (1) participates knowingly in another fiduciary's breach; (2) knowingly undertakes to conceal a breach; or (3) fails to cure a breach upon learning of it. Co-fiduciary liability means that a trustee who learned of record manipulation by a co-trustee and did nothing can be jointly liable.

## Distinction From § 1132(a)(2)

§ 1109 defines the liability; § 1132(a)(2) is the enforcement mechanism. A participant sues under § 1132(a)(2) to obtain "appropriate relief" as defined by § 1109. Any recovery under § 1132(a)(2)/§ 1109 goes to the PLAN — not directly to the individual participant. The participant benefits indirectly as a plan beneficiary.

## Application to UA342 Pension Claim

If the pension trust's trustees or administrators: (1) failed to maintain accurate service hour records; (2) allowed identity substitution to corrupt a participant's benefit account; (3) suspended benefits without basis; OR (4) failed to investigate and correct known errors — they have breached § 1104 duties and are personally liable under § 1109 for losses to the plan (and by extension, the participant's benefit). The claim is filed under § 1132(a)(2), recovery goes to the plan, and the plan is then obligated to pay the corrected benefit.
