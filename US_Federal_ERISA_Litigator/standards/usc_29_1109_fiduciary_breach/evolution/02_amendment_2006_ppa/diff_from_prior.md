# Diff: 1974 Origin → 2006 PPA (§ 1109)

## What stayed the same

§ 1109(a): Personal liability for fiduciary breach — no change. The core rule — you breach, you pay —
has been stable for 52 years.

§ 1109(b): No liability for breaches before/after fiduciary period — no change.

## What PPA 2006 added

**§ 1109(c):** Clarified that ERISA exemptions and prohibited-transaction class exemptions do not
shield a fiduciary from § 1109 liability. This was a response to defendants arguing that if their
conduct was covered by a prohibited-transaction exemption, § 1109 liability couldn't attach.
Congress rejected that reading explicitly.

## Logical delta

The § 1109 amendment confirms the one-way ratchet: new exemptions don't create new escape hatches
from fiduciary liability. If anything, the amendment expanded the reach of § 1109 by foreclosing the
exemption-as-defense argument.

## Application to Case #37

PPA 2006 changes are favorable: if the plan administrator claims that processing the unauthorized
retirement election was somehow covered by a plan provision or administrative exemption, § 1109(c)
forecloses that defense. The fiduciary who processed the fraudulent election without adequate
verification cannot hide behind plan document provisions authorizing expedited processing.
