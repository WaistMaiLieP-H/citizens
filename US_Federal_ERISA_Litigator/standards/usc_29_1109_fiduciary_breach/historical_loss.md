# Historical Loss — 29 U.S.C. § 1109 Failures

## Failure Mode 1 — Breach Proven but No Causation; No Recovery

**The Loss:** A participant proves that a fiduciary breached the § 1104 prudence duty (e.g., by making an imprudent investment). However, the plan's investment recovered and there was no net loss. Because § 1109 requires a "loss to the plan," the participant cannot recover despite the proven breach.

**Prevention:** Frame the loss carefully — § 1109 allows recovery of all losses traceable to the breach, including opportunity cost (what the plan would have earned if proper investments had been maintained). Lost earnings on mismanaged assets can be the measure of loss even without a dollar-for-dollar capital loss.

---

## Failure Mode 2 — Fiduciary Status Not Established

**The Loss:** A participant sues a plan administrator for breach of fiduciary duty. The defendant demonstrates that the administrator was exercising "purely ministerial functions" — applying plan terms mechanically without discretionary authority. Courts hold that ministerial administrators are not fiduciaries under ERISA § 1002(21)(A). The § 1109 claim fails.

**Prevention:** Identify the specific actions that constitute the exercise of discretionary authority — approval/denial of claims, interpretation of plan terms, selection of service providers, investment decisions. Document the discretionary nature of the conduct before filing.

---

## Failure Mode 3 — Recovery Goes to Plan, Not Individual

**The Loss:** A participant prevails on a § 1109/§ 1132(a)(2) fiduciary breach claim. The court orders the fiduciary to restore losses to the PLAN. The plan administrator then applies normal benefit calculation rules to the restored funds — and the individual participant's benefit increases only modestly compared to what the breach cost them personally.

**Structural Note:** This is the design of ERISA's fiduciary breach scheme. For direct individual recovery, the participant must also plead § 1132(a)(1)(B) (benefits recovery) or § 1132(a)(3) (equitable relief including surcharge). A complete remedy typically requires both claims.

---

## Failure Mode 4 — Co-Fiduciary Liability Not Pursued

**The Loss:** One trustee commits a fiduciary breach; other trustees knew but did nothing. The participant only sues the individual trustee who committed the breach. That trustee lacks assets to satisfy the judgment. The co-trustees who enabled the breach through inaction are not defendants.

**Prevention:** Under § 1105, co-fiduciaries who know of a breach and fail to cure it are also liable. In any fiduciary breach case involving a board of trustees, name all trustees who knew or should have known of the breach as co-defendants.
