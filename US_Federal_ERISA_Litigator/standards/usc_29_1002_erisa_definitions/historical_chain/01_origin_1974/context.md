# §1002 Origin: Why Definitions Matter in ERISA

ERISA §3 (29 U.S.C. §1002) contains 48 definitions. The definitions are not technical
housekeeping — they determine who ERISA protects and who owes duties.

The critical definitional battles in ERISA litigation are:

1. **"Participant" standing:** Courts have held that whether someone has standing under
   §502(a) turns entirely on §1002(7) participant status. A plan administrator who
   claims a worker is "not a participant" is making a §1002(7) argument.
   In the UA342 case, if the pension record shows "retired," the plan may argue Michael
   lacks participant standing — because he is treated as a former employee who has
   already exercised his pension right. The counter-argument: participant status flows
   from actual employment history, not from a potentially fraudulent plan record.

2. **"Fiduciary" definition:** §1002(21) is broad — anyone who exercises discretionary
   authority in plan administration is a fiduciary. This is not limited to named
   fiduciaries. A plan administrator who processes record changes is a §1002(21)(iii)
   fiduciary. Anyone who altered Michael's record exercised that discretionary authority.

3. **"Employee benefit plan" scope:** §1002(3) + (1) define what ERISA governs.
   Multi-employer Taft-Hartley funds are explicitly within scope — confirmed in the
   statutory text and in NLRB v. Amax Coal Co. (1981) line of cases.

Congress defined all these terms in §3 because the prior law (the Welfare and Pension
Plans Disclosure Act) had no uniform definitions. State courts applied state contract
law. Federal courts had no clear standard. ERISA §3 created a single federal dictionary.
