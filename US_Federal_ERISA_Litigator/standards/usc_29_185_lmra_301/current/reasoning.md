# Reasoning — 29 U.S.C. § 185 (LMRA § 301)
## Why This Standard Is In the ERISA Citizen

## The Taft-Hartley Structure of UA342's Pension Fund

UA Local 342's pension fund is not a creature of ERISA alone. It is a joint labor-management trust created by collective bargaining under the Labor Management Relations Act of 1947 (Taft-Hartley Act). The LMRA §301 collective bargaining agreement between UA Local 342 and the contributing employers obligates those employers to contribute to the pension fund. The pension fund exists because of that CBA; ERISA then governs how the fund administers benefits.

This dual foundation — §301/LMRA for the CBA structure, ERISA for benefit administration — creates concurrent jurisdiction and concurrent claims that must be precisely distinguished.

## The Critical Distinction for Case #37

**§301/LMRA claim (CBA violation):**
- Employer stops contributing to the fund as required by the CBA
- Dispute about CBA terms (contribution rates, covered work, eligible employees)
- Union's duty of fair representation in grievance procedures
- Route: §301 federal court action, grievance/arbitration may be required first

**ERISA §502(a) claim (plan administration violation):**
- Plan shows Michael as "retired" without his authorization
- Plan fails to provide required disclosures
- Plan administrator processes an unauthorized retirement election
- Route: §502(a) federal court action, plan's internal claims procedure first, no CBA arbitration required

**Case #37 is an ERISA §502(a) case.** The wrongful act is plan administration (unauthorized retirement designation), not CBA violation (employer failing to contribute). Correctly framing the claim as ERISA rather than §301 is essential to avoiding CBA grievance exhaustion requirements.

## When §301 Matters Alongside ERISA

If, as part of the broader identity replacement scheme, the contributing employer was manipulated into ceasing contributions (e.g., reporting Michael as "no longer employed" or "retired" to the fund), a §301 claim against that employer is available alongside the ERISA claim against the plan.

Additionally: if UA Local 342 failed to represent Michael's interests in the pension dispute — either by ignoring his complaints or actively participating in the misrepresentation — a hybrid §301/duty of fair representation claim is available against the union.

## Central States v. Central Transport (1985)

The Supreme Court in Central States Pension Fund v. Central Transport, Inc. (472 U.S. 559 (1985)) established that multi-employer pension fund trustees have broad audit rights over employer records — they can audit employer payroll and contribution records to ensure accurate reporting. This audit power is §301-compatible and supports discovery of whether employer records were manipulated to show Michael as retired.
