# 29 U.S.C. §185 — LMRA §301: Suits for Violation of Contracts

## Statutory Rule (Training Knowledge — verified=false)

§185(a): Suits for violation of contracts between an employer and a labor organization
representing employees in an industry affecting commerce as defined in this chapter, or
between any such labor organizations, may be brought in any district court of the United
States having jurisdiction of the parties, without respect to the amount in controversy
or without regard to the citizenship of the parties.

## Relationship to ERISA in Multi-Employer Plans

For Taft-Hartley multi-employer pension funds (UA342), two bodies of law apply
concurrently and require careful sequencing:

### LMRA §301 Governs:
- The collective bargaining agreement (CBA) between UA Local 342 and contributing employers
- Disputes about what the CBA requires contributing employers to contribute to the fund
- Grievance and arbitration requirements under the CBA
- Employer obligation to participate in the fund

### ERISA Governs:
- The pension fund's administration of benefits to participants
- Fiduciary duties of the plan trustees
- Vesting, accrual, and benefit calculation
- Participant rights against the plan (§502(a) enforcement)

### The Exhaustion Intersection:
Before bringing an ERISA §502(a) claim, a participant with a CBA-based claim must
exhaust CBA grievance procedures if the claim is for a contractual right under the CBA.

**Critical distinction for UA342:**
- If Michael's claim is "the plan wrongfully shows me as retired" (plan administration
  error) → ERISA §502(a) route, no LMRA §301 exhaustion required
- If Michael's claim is "the contributing employer stopped making contributions required
  by the CBA" → LMRA §301 route, grievance exhaustion may be required first

The identity replacement case is an ERISA §502(a) case (plan administration), not a
LMRA §301 case (CBA violation). LMRA §301 appears here because the plan structure
is Taft-Hartley — the fund exists because of §301 collective bargaining.

## Why §301 Is In This Citizen's Scope

UA Local 342 is a building trades union. The pension fund is a Taft-Hartley joint
labor-management trust — its existence is a creature of LMRA §301 collective bargaining.
Understanding §301's concurrent scope prevents the litigator from accidentally framing
an ERISA claim in CBA terms, triggering exhaustion requirements that do not apply.
