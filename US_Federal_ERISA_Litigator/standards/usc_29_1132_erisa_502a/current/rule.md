# ERISA Civil Enforcement — §502(a) — Rule

**Authority:** 29 U.S.C. § 1132 (ERISA § 502(a))
**Jurisdiction:** Federal — exclusive
**Effective:** 1974 (ERISA); substantially interpreted through Supreme Court decisions

## The Rule

ERISA §502(a) is the exclusive civil enforcement mechanism for employee benefit plan claims.
There is no state law remedy for plan benefit disputes — ERISA preempts all state causes of action
that relate to employee benefit plans (§514). A plan participant who is denied benefits has one
path: §502(a) in federal district court.

**The operative subsections:**

**§502(a)(1)(B):** A participant or beneficiary may bring a civil action to recover benefits due
under the terms of the plan, to enforce rights under the plan, or to clarify rights to future
benefits. This is the primary benefit-recovery claim.

**§502(a)(2):** A participant, beneficiary, or fiduciary may bring a civil action for appropriate
relief under §409 (personal liability of breaching fiduciaries) — remedies flow to the plan, not
the individual.

**§502(a)(3):** A participant, beneficiary, or fiduciary may bring a civil action to enjoin any
act or practice violating ERISA or the plan terms, or to obtain other appropriate equitable relief.
After *Varity Corp. v. Howe* (1996), this includes individual equitable relief (surcharge,
reformation, estoppel) when §502(a)(1)(B) provides no adequate remedy.

**§502(f):** Federal district courts have exclusive jurisdiction over §502(a) actions. State courts
have no jurisdiction. State remedies are preempted.

## Key Elements

1. **Exclusive federal remedy** — no state court, no state tort, no emotional distress damages
2. **§502(a)(1)(B)** — recover benefits or enforce rights under plan terms
3. **§502(a)(3)** — equitable relief including plan record correction and surcharge
4. **Administrative exhaustion** — must exhaust plan's internal claims and appeals before suit
   (required by §503 claims procedures; failure to exhaust is procedural bar)
5. **Defendant** — the plan, the plan administrator, or breaching fiduciaries
6. **Remedies under (a)(1)(B)** — contractual benefits only; no consequential damages, no
   emotional distress (held in *Massachusetts Mut. Life Ins. Co. v. Russell*, 473 U.S. 134 (1985))

## What This Means in Practice

If UA342 pension fund records were corrupted to show retirement status and benefits were frozen
as a result, the §502(a)(1)(B) claim is: the plan owes the participant benefits due under the
plan terms because the freeze was based on a fraudulent record, not a legitimate plan determination.
The §502(a)(3) claim runs alongside: reform the plan records to remove the fraudulent retirement
entry and restore the participant's active status. Equitable reformation is available under
*Amara* (2011) and *Varity*.

The remedial limitation is real: ERISA §502 will not pay for the emotional harm of discovering
that someone stole your retirement identity and froze your pension. It will recover the pension.
