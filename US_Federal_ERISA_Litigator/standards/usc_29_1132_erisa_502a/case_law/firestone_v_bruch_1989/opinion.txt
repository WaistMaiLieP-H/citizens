Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch
489 U.S. 101 (1989)
United States Supreme Court

JUSTICE O'CONNOR delivered the opinion of the Court.

This case requires us to determine the appropriate standard of judicial review of benefit
determinations by fiduciaries or plan administrators under the Employee Retirement Income
Security Act of 1974 (ERISA).

Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. maintained a termination pay plan for its employees.
The plan provided benefits to employees who were "laid off" but not to those who
"resign, retire, or are discharged for cause."

Respondents were employees at Firestone's Plastics Division who were terminated when
the division was sold. Firestone denied them termination pay on the ground they were
not "laid off." Respondents brought suit under §502(a)(1)(B).

The question: What standard of review applies when a plan fiduciary (or administrator
granted discretion by the plan) denies benefits?

HELD: A denial of benefits challenged under §502(a)(1)(B) is to be reviewed under
a de novo standard unless the benefit plan gives the administrator or fiduciary
discretionary authority to determine eligibility for benefits or to construe the terms
of the plan.

Where discretion is granted, the court reviews for abuse of discretion (arbitrary
and capricious standard). Where no discretion is granted, review is de novo.

The de novo default is appropriate because ERISA's legislative history reveals that
Congress intended courts to determine, independently, whether plan administrators
properly applied plan terms. Where the employer also serves as the plan administrator,
a structural conflict of interest exists that courts must consider when determining
whether the fiduciary's decision was reasonable.

The structural conflict does not automatically trigger de novo review but is a factor
in assessing whether the administrator abused its discretion.
