# Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch — Holding

**Citation:** 489 U.S. 101 (1989)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Standard:** §502(a)(1)(B) benefit denial review

## The Rule

When a plan administrator denies benefits under §502(a)(1)(B):

1. **Default standard = de novo.** The court reviews the denial independently,
   without deference to the administrator's interpretation of the plan.

2. **Exception = abuse of discretion.** If the plan document expressly grants
   the administrator "discretionary authority" to interpret plan terms or
   determine eligibility, the court reviews only for abuse of discretion
   (arbitrary and capricious).

3. **Structural conflict of interest** (employer = administrator) is a factor
   in the abuse-of-discretion analysis — it can reduce the deference given
   to the administrator's decision.

## Why This Matters for UA342

The UA Local 342 pension plan is a Taft-Hartley multi-employer fund.
The board of trustees (joint labor-management) administers the fund — this is
a structural conflict if management trustees tilt decisions against beneficiaries.

The critical question on filing: Does the plan document grant the trustees
discretionary authority? If yes → abuse of discretion standard (harder for claimant).
If the plan is silent or ambiguous → Firestone default = de novo (easier for claimant).

In identity replacement / pension freeze cases where the beneficiary contends
the person shown as "retired" is not them, the standard of review question may be
bypassed entirely — the factual dispute is about identity, not plan interpretation.

ERISA §502(a)(1)(B), 29 U.S.C. §1132(a)(1)(B) as enacted 1974, unchanged
