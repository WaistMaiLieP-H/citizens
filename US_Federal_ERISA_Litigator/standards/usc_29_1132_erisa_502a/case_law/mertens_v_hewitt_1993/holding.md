# Mertens v. Hewitt Associates — Holding

**Citation:** 508 U.S. 248 (1993)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Standard:** §502(a)(3) — what "equitable relief" excludes

## The Rule

ERISA §502(a)(3) "other appropriate equitable relief" does NOT include:
- Compensatory damages
- Consequential damages
- Punitive damages
- Any money damages that were traditionally legal (not equitable) remedies

The phrase means relief that was historically available in courts of equity
(injunction, reformation, constructive trust, accounting, surcharge, restitution
of unjust enrichment) — not money paid to compensate a loss.

## The ERISA Remedial Gap

This is the landmark case establishing the ERISA remedial gap:

A plan participant who is wrongfully denied a pension can recover:
- The benefit itself (§502(a)(1)(B))
- Equitable reformation of plan records (§502(a)(3) via Varity)
- Injunctive relief (§502(a)(3))

A plan participant CANNOT recover:
- Lost wages and earnings during the benefit denial period
- Medical expenses incurred because of the denial
- Financial hardship damages
- Emotional distress
- Punitive damages

This gap has been extensively criticized. The Ninth Circuit has called it "troubling."
But Congress has not amended ERISA to close it. The gap is the law.

## Litigation Strategy Note

In the UA342 case, the remedial gap is significant:
- **Recoverable:** The pension benefits Michael should have received since the
  freezing of his account (the accumulated missed payments), plus reformation of
  the fraudulent "retired" record
- **NOT recoverable under ERISA:** The ~$2.4M in consequential damages from the
  identity replacement (lost financial opportunities, inability to obtain credit,
  costs of rebuilding identity)

The consequential damages must be pursued through parallel non-ERISA claims:
RICO, fraud, §1983 (state actor involvement if proven), state tort claims
(preemption analysis required for each). ERISA preemption (§514) bars state
claims that "relate to" the ERISA plan itself, but does not preempt claims
based on independent fraud/identity theft conduct.
