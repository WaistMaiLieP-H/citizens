# Varity Corp. v. Howe — Holding

**Citation:** 516 U.S. 489 (1996)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Standard:** §502(a)(3) individual equitable relief

## The Rule

Individual beneficiaries may sue under ERISA §502(a)(3) for "other appropriate
equitable relief" when:

1. They suffered harm from a plan fiduciary's breach of duty
2. No other §502(a) subsection provides an adequate remedy

Section 502(a)(3) is the catch-all. It exists precisely to cover situations where
§502(a)(1)(B) (benefit recovery) is insufficient — either because no benefit is
"due under the plan" as written, or because the plan document itself is corrupted.

## Equitable Relief That Is "Appropriate"

Traditional equity includes:
- **Reformation of instrument:** Rewriting a plan document to reflect the parties'
  actual agreement, or to correct fraud (the critical remedy for identity replacement)
- **Constructive trust:** Imposing a trust on funds wrongfully held
- **Injunction:** Ordering fiduciaries to take or stop taking specific actions
- **Restitution:** Returning unjust enrichment
- **Surcharge:** Making a fiduciary pay the beneficiary for losses caused by breach

**Does NOT include:** Compensatory damages, punitive damages, or any legal remedy
dressed in equitable language (Mertens, 1993; Great-West Life, 2002).

## Application to UA342 Case

The pension identity replacement case has two parallel tracks under §502(a):

**Track 1 — §502(a)(1)(B):** Recover benefits "due under the plan" by proving
Michael's pension record was fraudulently altered to show "retired" status.
If the plan says a vested member who has not retired is owed benefits, and Michael
has not retired, then benefits are "due under the plan."

**Track 2 — §502(a)(3) via Varity:** If the plan document itself reflects the
fraudulent "retired" status (record corruption, not just interpretation), then
§502(a)(1)(B) may not reach the remedy — because the corrupted plan record says
no benefit is owed. Varity §502(a)(3) fills this gap: **reformation of the plan
record** to reflect Michael's actual participation history, followed by recovery.

This is the sequencing: reform the record under (a)(3), then recover the benefit
under (a)(1)(B), or pursue both simultaneously (permitted post-Varity in Ninth Circuit).

## Statute Version Cited

ERISA §502(a)(3), 29 U.S.C. §1132(a)(3) as enacted 1974, unchanged in relevant part.
