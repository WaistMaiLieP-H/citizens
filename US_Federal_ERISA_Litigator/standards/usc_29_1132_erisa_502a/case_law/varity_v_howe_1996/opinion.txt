Varity Corp. v. Howe
516 U.S. 489 (1996)
United States Supreme Court

JUSTICE BREYER delivered the opinion of the Court.

Varity Corporation was the successor to Massey-Ferguson, Inc. Varity created a
subsidiary, Massey Combines Corporation (MCC), and transferred employees and their
benefit plans to MCC. Varity told the employees that their benefits were secure
under MCC. This was false. MCC failed. The employees lost their benefits.

Plaintiffs brought suit under §502(a)(3) seeking "other appropriate equitable relief"
for breach of fiduciary duty under ERISA §404.

The question: Can individual plan beneficiaries sue under §502(a)(3) for individual
equitable relief when they have suffered individual losses from a fiduciary's breach?

HELD: Yes. Section 502(a)(3) authorizes individual beneficiaries to sue for
equitable relief when other §502(a) subsections do not provide an adequate remedy.

Section 502(a)(3) is a "catch-all" that provides relief for injuries not covered by
other subsections. Where a plaintiff cannot recover under §502(a)(1)(B) (because,
e.g., no benefits are "due under the plan"), §502(a)(3) fills the gap.

The Court emphasized that §502(a)(3)'s reference to "appropriate equitable relief"
must be read in light of traditional equity — the relief must be equitable in nature.
This means injunctions, restitution, account of profits, reformation of instruments,
constructive trusts — but not compensatory or punitive damages (see Mertens).

Varity established that individual plan beneficiaries are proper plaintiffs under
§502(a)(3) — not just the plan itself — where fiduciary breach caused individual harm.
