# Historical Loss — 29 U.S.C. § 1132 Failures

## Failure Mode 1 — State Court Filing Dismissed as ERISA-Preempted

**The Loss:** A participant whose pension was wrongfully denied files a state court breach of contract or fraud claim. The defendant removes to federal court and moves to dismiss on ERISA preemption grounds. The state claims are dismissed. The participant may refile under § 1132, but state law damages (emotional distress, punitive) are unavailable. The participant is left with only the benefit amount plus possible attorney's fees.

**Prevention:** ERISA preemption analysis is the threshold question for any benefit-denial case. If the plan is an ERISA plan, all claims must be framed under § 1132 from the outset.

---

## Failure Mode 2 — Failure to Exhaust Administrative Remedies

**The Loss:** A participant receives a denial letter from the plan and immediately files suit in federal court without appealing through the plan's internal procedures. The defendant moves to dismiss for failure to exhaust. The court dismisses the case. The participant must go back through the claims process, losing months and possibly running into SOL issues.

**Prevention:** When a claim is denied, immediately invoke the plan's internal appeals process. Document every step. Only file suit after the internal process is exhausted or after the plan fails to respond within required timeframes.

---

## Failure Mode 3 — Seeking Compensatory Damages Not Available Under ERISA

**The Loss:** A participant's pension benefits were denied due to a fiduciary's fraudulent manipulation of records. The participant files a § 1132(a)(3) claim seeking compensatory damages for financial harm beyond the withheld benefits. The court dismisses the damages claim, following Mertens — § 1132(a)(3) allows only traditional equitable relief, not compensatory damages.

**The Gap:** ERISA does not provide compensatory damages for individual victims of fiduciary fraud beyond the benefit itself. This is a significant limitation that Congress has been criticized for not addressing.

---

## Failure Mode 4 — Wrong Defendant Named

**The Loss:** A participant sues the employer instead of the plan administrator or the pension trust. The court holds the employer is not a proper defendant for the specific § 1132 claim. The suit against the employer is dismissed. Potential SOL issues arise while plaintiff amends to name the correct defendant.

**For multi-employer plans:** The plan administrator is the trustee board of the pension trust — not the individual employer and not the union itself. The pension trust is a separate legal entity. The complaint must name the trust.

---

## Failure Mode 5 — Pension Fraud + Identity Substitution Not Recoverable as ERISA Claim

**The Loss:** A plan participant's pension records were manipulated by a third party who is not a plan fiduciary. The participant files a § 1132 claim against the plan for the manipulation. The plan defends that the plan itself did nothing wrong — a third-party fraudster manipulated records without plan complicity. The plan's breach-of-fiduciary-duty claim is defeated because the fiduciaries didn't know.

**Prevention:** In identity substitution / records manipulation cases, consider parallel claims: (1) § 1132(a)(1)(B) against the plan for benefits due; (2) § 1132(a)(2) against individual fiduciaries for breach of duty to maintain accurate records; (3) § 1132(a)(3) equitable relief including plan reformation to correct the records; (4) separate federal fraud claims (18 U.S.C. § 1028) against the manipulating party.
