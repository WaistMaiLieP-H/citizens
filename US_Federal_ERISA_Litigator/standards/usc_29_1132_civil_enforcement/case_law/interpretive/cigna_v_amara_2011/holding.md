# CIGNA Corp. v. Amara (2011) 563 U.S. 421

## Citation
563 U.S. 421, 131 S.Ct. 1866, 179 L.Ed.2d 843 (2011)

## Court
United States Supreme Court

## Date
May 16, 2011

## Author
Justice Breyer (unanimous as to § 1132(a)(3) holding)

## Procedural History
District court found CIGNA violated ERISA disclosure requirements when converting pension plan from defined benefit to cash balance; ordered make-whole relief. Second Circuit affirmed. Supreme Court reversed on the remedy source but upheld broad equitable relief under § 1132(a)(3).

## Central Holding

Section 1132(a)(3) authorizes "appropriate equitable relief" that includes reformation of plan terms, surcharge against the fiduciary, and estoppel — not merely injunctions and restitution in the narrow sense. District courts have the full traditional equity toolkit under § 1132(a)(3).

## Key Doctrinal Points

### 1. § 1132(a)(1)(B) does not authorize reforming plan terms
Plan terms govern what is "due" under § 1132(a)(1)(B). A court cannot rewrite the plan itself under (a)(1)(B) — the plan document controls. This limits (a)(1)(B) to enforcing existing plan terms as written.

### 2. § 1132(a)(3) reaches traditional equitable remedies
The remedies historically available in courts of equity include:
- **Surcharge:** monetary compensation for a loss caused by a trustee's breach of duty (equitable damages in the fiduciary context)
- **Reformation:** rewriting the plan document to conform to what participants were promised
- **Estoppel:** holding the plan administrator to representations made to participants even if contrary to plan terms
- **Constructive trust / disgorgement:** disgorging benefits obtained through breach

### 3. § 1132(a)(3) fills the gap where (a)(1)(B) is inadequate
Where plan terms themselves are the wrong — where the administrator promised one plan but delivered another — § 1132(a)(3) is the appropriate enforcement vehicle. The two subsections work together: (a)(1)(B) enforces the plan as written; (a)(3) remedies when the written plan itself is the product of a violation.

### 4. Individual relief is available under § 1132(a)(3)
Amara rejected the argument that § 1132(a)(3) authorizes only plan-wide injunctive relief. Individual make-whole relief — money damages functioning as equitable surcharge — is available to individual participants.

## Application to Case #37 — UA Local 342 Pension Freeze

The Amara framework is directly applicable to the fraudulent retirement election scenario:

| Amara Remedy | Case #37 Application |
|---|---|
| Surcharge | Monetary compensation for pension benefits lost during the period the fraudulent election was in effect; loss of accrual credit; any benefits paid to the fraudulent claimant that reduced the plan |
| Reformation | Restoring the steward's benefit accrual status to pre-unauthorized-election state; correcting the plan records to reflect actual service and participation |
| Estoppel | If the plan administrator made representations about benefit status to the steward that conflict with current frozen status |
| Disgorgement | Recovery of any benefits actually paid out to the fraudulent claimant under the unauthorized retirement election |

**The structural argument:** The unauthorized retirement election altered the steward's plan status without authorization. Section 1132(a)(1)(B) enforces "benefits due under the terms of the plan" — the plan never authorized an election filed by an unauthorized third party. Section 1132(a)(3), under Amara, authorizes reformation and surcharge to restore the status quo ante.

## Relationship to Firestone Tire v. Bruch (1989)

Firestone governs the standard of review for (a)(1)(B) claims. Amara governs the remedy scope for (a)(3) claims. Together:
- (a)(1)(B) + Firestone: can the court override the administrator's denial? Answer depends on discretionary authority grant in plan document.
- (a)(3) + Amara: even if the plan document authorized the denial, equitable relief is available where the administrator obtained the plan terms through misrepresentation or breach.

## Significance for ERISA Enforcement

Amara is the leading post-1974 expansion of ERISA enforcement scope. Before Amara, defendants argued that ERISA's remedies were limited to benefits due under the plan as written. After Amara, the full equity toolkit is available where the plan document itself reflects a breach or misrepresentation.
