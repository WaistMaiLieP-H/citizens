# Reasoning — How Courts Apply 29 U.S.C. § 1132

## Core Function

§ 1132 (ERISA § 502) is the exclusive civil enforcement mechanism for ERISA claims. Federal courts have exclusive jurisdiction over most ERISA claims. The statute provides specific remedies tied to specific types of violations.

## The Primary Claims

**§ 1132(a)(1)(B) — Benefits Recovery:** A participant or beneficiary may sue to recover benefits due under the plan, to enforce rights under the plan, or to clarify rights to future benefits. This is the bread-and-butter claim for denied pension benefits, denied disability benefits, and improperly withheld plan distributions.

**§ 1132(a)(2) — Fiduciary Breach (plan-wide):** The Secretary of Labor, a participant, or a beneficiary may sue for appropriate relief under § 1109 (fiduciary liability). This claim is for recovery on behalf of the PLAN, not individual recovery. Any recovery goes back to the plan; the participant benefits indirectly.

**§ 1132(a)(3) — Equitable Relief:** A participant, beneficiary, or fiduciary may sue to enjoin ERISA violations or to obtain "other appropriate equitable relief." This is the catch-all provision. Courts have narrowly construed "equitable relief" to mean remedies that were traditionally equitable — injunction, surcharge, reformation, estoppel — not compensatory damages.

## ERISA Preemption

§ 1144 (ERISA § 514) preempts all state laws that "relate to" employee benefit plans. This is one of the most aggressive preemption provisions in federal law. State tort claims, state contract claims, and state statutory claims that "relate to" ERISA plans are preempted and must be recast (if at all) as § 1132 claims. The sole exception is for state law that "regulates insurance."

**Practical effect:** A participant whose pension was wrongfully denied cannot sue in state court for breach of contract, fraud, or emotional distress damages. The only remedy is under § 1132, which does not provide compensatory or punitive damages for individual benefit denials (only equitable relief and benefits themselves).

## The Equitable Relief Limitation (Mertens Problem)

In Mertens v. Hewitt Associates (1993), the Supreme Court held that "other appropriate equitable relief" in § 1132(a)(3) means only equitable remedies as traditionally understood in equity courts — not money damages. This created a significant gap: a participant harmed by a fiduciary breach who cannot recover under § 1132(a)(1)(B) (because the benefit isn't "due under the plan") may have no adequate remedy under § 1132(a)(3) either.

Subsequent cases (CIGNA Corp. v. Amara, 2011; Varity Corp. v. Howe, 1996) have expanded the equitable relief available, including surcharge (monetary relief through equity), plan reformation, and equitable estoppel.

## Attorney's Fees

§ 1132(g) gives courts discretion to award attorney's fees and costs to either party in an ERISA action. Courts apply a five-factor test (Hummell factors in the Ninth Circuit) to determine whether fees are appropriate: (1) degree of the opposing party's culpability; (2) ability of the opposing party to pay; (3) whether fee award would deter similar conduct; (4) benefit conferred on the plan or plan members; (5) relative merits of the parties' positions.

## Administrative Exhaustion Requirement

Before filing suit under § 1132, a participant must generally exhaust the plan's internal claims and appeals procedures. Courts have imposed this exhaustion requirement through case law; it is not expressly in the statute. Failure to exhaust typically results in dismissal without prejudice. Exception: exhaustion is excused when it would be futile (e.g., the plan administrator has definitively decided the issue) or when the plan fails to provide a claims procedure.

## Application to UA342 Pension Claim

A § 1132(a)(1)(B) claim is the appropriate vehicle to recover any pension benefits wrongfully withheld, miscalculated, or denied due to erroneous vesting calculations or improper suspension. The claim must be filed in federal district court (exclusive jurisdiction for most § 1132 claims). The UAL342 pension trust is the defendant plan administrator.
