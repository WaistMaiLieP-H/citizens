# Historical Chain — 29 U.S.C. § 1132 ERISA Civil Enforcement

## The wound

In the decades before ERISA, private pension plans existed largely outside federal regulation. The catastrophic wound that triggered ERISA: in 1963, Studebaker Corporation closed its South Bend, Indiana plant. The pension plan was underfunded — thousands of workers who had spent careers earning pensions received nothing or a fraction of what they were owed. The Studebaker collapse exposed the fundamental problem: pension promises without legal enforceability are just promises.

Systematic abuses included:
- Plans with vesting requirements so long that most workers never qualified
- Benefits that could be reduced or eliminated before retirement
- No disclosure to participants about plan status or their benefit rights
- No fiduciary standards for plan administrators
- No federal enforcement mechanism

## Legislative origin — ERISA (1974)

The Employee Retirement Income Security Act was enacted on Labor Day 1974 (Pub. L. 93-406). ERISA § 502 (29 U.S.C. § 1132) was the enforcement centerpiece — Congress's mechanism for making pension rights enforceable in federal court.

Key innovations of the 1974 enactment:
- **Federal preemption:** ERISA preempts most state law claims related to pension plans, creating a uniform federal regime
- **Minimum vesting standards:** Workers must vest in accrued benefits within defined periods
- **Funding standards:** Plans must maintain minimum funding levels
- **PBGC:** Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation established to insure defined-benefit plans
- **Federal civil enforcement:** § 1132 created the participant's right to sue in federal court with exclusive federal jurisdiction

## Key amendments

**1984 — Retirement Equity Act:** Extended spousal rights; survivor annuity requirements
**1986 — Tax Reform Act:** Accelerated vesting schedules
**1996 — Small Business Job Protection Act:** SIMPLE plans authorized
**2006 — Pension Protection Act:** Funding reform; transparency requirements
**2019 — SECURE Act:** RMD age changes; portability improvements

## The promise

ERISA was enacted on the promise that a worker who earns a pension has a legally enforceable right to receive it. Section 1132 is the enforcement of that promise — federal courts, federal jurisdiction, no amount-in-controversy threshold, attorney fees available. The worker who was wronged by their pension plan has access to the federal courthouse.
