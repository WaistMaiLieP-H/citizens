Ingersoll-Rand Co. v. McClendon
498 U.S. 133 (1990)
United States Supreme Court

JUSTICE O'CONNOR delivered the opinion of the Court.

Respondent McClendon was employed by Ingersoll-Rand. He was terminated. He brought
suit in Texas state court under a Texas common law tort theory — wrongful discharge
to prevent vesting of pension benefits. The Texas Supreme Court affirmed a judgment
for McClendon under state tort law.

Ingersoll-Rand appealed, arguing ERISA §514 preempted the Texas tort claim because
it "related to" an employee benefit plan.

HELD: ERISA preempts the Texas common law wrongful discharge cause of action because:

1. ERISA §514(a) preempts "any and all State laws insofar as they may now or hereafter
   relate to any employee benefit plan"
2. Texas's wrongful discharge tort theory specifically concerned the relationship
   between the employer's conduct and the employee benefit plan
3. ERISA §502(a) provides a comprehensive federal enforcement scheme that displaces
   the state cause of action

The Court further held that ERISA §502(a) itself creates a cause of action covering
McClendon's claim — an employee may bring a federal claim under §502(a)(1)(B)
or §502(a)(3) for interference with pension rights, even where state law is preempted.

The preemption ruling is the primary ERISA §514 precedent: if a state law claim
"relates to" an ERISA plan — even if the state created the claim specifically to fill
a gap ERISA left — it is preempted. ERISA's federal scheme is exclusive.
