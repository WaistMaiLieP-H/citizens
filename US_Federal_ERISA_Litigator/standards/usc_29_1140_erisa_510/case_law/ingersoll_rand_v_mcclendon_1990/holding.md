# Ingersoll-Rand Co. v. McClendon — Holding

**Citation:** 498 U.S. 133 (1990)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Standard:** ERISA §514 preemption + §502(a) as the exclusive remedy

## The Rule

1. **ERISA §514 preempts state law claims that "relate to" an employee benefit plan.**
   "Relate to" is construed broadly — any state cause of action with a connection to
   an ERISA plan is preempted, even where Congress did not specifically contemplate
   the state law theory.

2. **ERISA §502(a) is the exclusive enforcement mechanism.** Where a state claim is
   preempted, the plaintiff's remedy, if any, must be found in §502(a).

3. **This creates the ERISA preemption trap:** State law claims with broader remedies
   (punitive damages, emotional distress, consequential damages) are preempted and
   replaced by the narrower §502(a) remedial scheme (Mertens).

## Application to UA342

The pension identity replacement case must be analyzed for §514 preemption:

**Claims that likely survive preemption (independent of the plan):**
- Identity theft (18 U.S.C. §1028) — not a plan-related claim
- Wire fraud (18 U.S.C. §1343) — predicate acts based on communications
- RICO (18 U.S.C. §1962) — if pattern of racketeering, preemption does not bar
- §1983 (42 U.S.C. §1983) — if state actors participated; not a plan claim

**Claims likely preempted (replace with §502(a)):**
- State fraud claim based on plan record manipulation — "relates to" the plan
- State breach of fiduciary duty claim against plan trustees — replaced by §502(a)(2)/(3)
- State contract claim for pension benefits — replaced by §502(a)(1)(B)

The litigation strategy: lead with federal ERISA claims + independent federal claims
(RICO, §1983, identity theft statutes). State claims survive only where they rest on
conduct wholly independent of the plan itself.
