The Ninth Circuit applies a McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting framework to §510 claims:

**Step 1 — Plaintiff's prima facie case:**
Plaintiff must show:
(a) Plaintiff was a participant in an ERISA plan
(b) Plaintiff was subjected to an adverse action
(c) The adverse action was taken to interfere with ERISA rights or to retaliate
    for exercising ERISA rights
(d) Causal connection between the adverse action and protected status/activity

Temporal proximity between adverse action and approaching vesting date is strong
circumstantial evidence of §510 intent.

**Step 2 — Employer's burden:**
Employer articulates a legitimate, non-discriminatory reason for the adverse action.

**Step 3 — Plaintiff's rebuttal:**
Plaintiff must show the employer's stated reason is pretextual — that the actual
motivation was interference with ERISA rights.

The burden of persuasion remains with the plaintiff throughout.

Courts in the Ninth Circuit have held that §510 claims are not limited to pre-vesting
termination — the statute covers any discriminatory conduct intended to interfere with
a participant's ERISA rights, including post-vesting benefit manipulation.
