## The Rule

In the Ninth Circuit, §510 interference claims proceed under McDonnell Douglas
burden-shifting:

1. Plaintiff makes a prima facie showing of: (a) plan participation, (b) adverse action,
   (c) causal connection to ERISA rights, (d) temporal proximity or other circumstantial
   evidence of discriminatory intent

2. Employer articulates a legitimate reason

3. Plaintiff shows pretext — that the real motivation was ERISA interference

## Application to UA342 §510 Analysis

The UA342 case does not fit the standard employment termination §510 pattern.
The question is whether "discriminate against a participant" covers plan record
manipulation — altering the record to show "retired" status without the participant's
knowledge or consent.

**Argument that §510 applies:**
- The alteration is an act of "discrimination" against Michael as a participant
- The specific intent to interfere with his pension rights is evidenced by:
  (1) the fraudulent "retired" status in the credit report
  (2) the pension freeze coinciding with other elements of the identity replacement scheme
  (3) Michael's actual employment status (UA342 plumber, not retired)
- §510's "discriminate against" language is broad — no circuit has held it exclusive to employment termination

**Argument §510 may not reach:**
- §510 was written for employment adverse actions, not plan administration acts
- Plan trustees who alter records may be acting as fiduciaries under §404, not as employers under §510
- Fiduciary breach theory (§502(a)(2)/(a)(3)) may be the better vehicle for plan-record corruption

**Litigation note:** Plead both §510 and §502(a)(3) fiduciary breach as alternative theories.
