# 29 U.S.C. §1140 — ERISA §510: Interference with Protected Rights

## Statutory Rule

It shall be unlawful for any person to discharge, fine, suspend, expel, discipline,
or discriminate against a participant or beneficiary for exercising any right to which
he is entitled under the provisions of an employee benefit plan, this subchapter, or
section 185 of this title [LMRA §301], or for the purpose of interfering with the
attainment of any right to which such participant may become entitled under the plan
or this subchapter.

It shall also be unlawful for any person to discharge, fine, suspend, expel, or
discriminate against any person because he has given information or has testified or
is about to testify in any inquiry or proceeding relating to this chapter or section
185 of this title.

The provisions of section 1132 of this title [§502] shall be applicable in the
enforcement of this section.

## Elements of a §510 Claim

1. **Protected status:** Plaintiff is a "participant or beneficiary" of an ERISA plan
2. **Adverse action:** Discharge, fine, suspension, expulsion, discipline, or discrimination
3. **Causal nexus:** The adverse action was taken:
   - (a) **to retaliate** for exercising plan rights, OR
   - (b) **to interfere** with the attainment of rights the participant would become entitled to
4. **Specific intent:** Plaintiff must show the employer/plan was specifically motivated
   by the intent to interfere — temporal proximity plus circumstantial evidence of motivation

## Critical Limitation: Specific Intent

§510 requires proof of the employer's specific intent to interfere with ERISA rights.
This is NOT a negligence standard. It is not enough to show that the adverse action
had the effect of depriving plaintiff of benefits. The plaintiff must show the adverse
action was motivated by the intent to interfere.

In practice: circumstantial evidence of motivation is sufficient — timing (action
taken shortly before vesting), pattern evidence (history of terminating employees
before vesting), and pretextual justification are the typical proof elements.

## Application to UA342 Pension Identity Replacement

§510 is the direct claim where the interference theory is:

1. Michael is a participant in the UA Local 342 Taft-Hartley pension plan
2. His pension record was altered to show "retired" status (the discriminatory act)
3. The alteration was done specifically to interfere with his right to receive pension
   benefits that he was entitled to (or would become entitled to) under the plan
4. The credit report showing "retired at 44" is circumstantial evidence of the alteration

The traditional §510 case involves an employer firing a worker before vesting.
The UA342 case involves identity replacement — altering the plan record itself.
§510's "discriminate against a participant" language is broad enough to cover
record manipulation as discrimination, particularly where the effect (and apparent
intent) is to deny pension rights.

## Enforcement

§510 is enforced through §502(a) — the same civil enforcement mechanism as all
ERISA claims. Remedies: reinstatement, back benefits, injunctive relief, attorney's fees.
Consequential damages remain barred (Mertens).
