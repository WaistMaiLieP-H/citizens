# US_Federal_ERISA_Litigator — Case Methodology

## Phase 1: Plan identification and document demand

**Step 1.1:** Identify the full legal name of the plan: "UA Local 342 Pension Plan" or its formal ERISA title. Obtain the EIN from any plan document, W-2, or correspondence received.

**Step 1.2:** Determine plan type: defined benefit vs. defined contribution (UA Local 342 plumber pension is expected to be a defined benefit multiemployer plan under a collective bargaining agreement).

**Step 1.3:** Send written document demand via certified mail to the plan administrator (the UA Local 342 pension fund office). Request:
- Summary Plan Description (SPD)
- Plan document and trust agreement
- Most recent Annual Report (Form 5500)
- Statement of benefit accrual and vesting as of the date of the fraudulent election
- Statement of current benefit status
- All correspondence related to the steward's account (participant name + SSN)
- All records of any retirement election or benefit commencement submitted under the steward's account

**Step 1.4:** Track the 30-day response deadline. If the administrator fails to produce documents within 30 days, the $110/day penalty under § 1132(c)(1) begins accruing on day 31.

**Output:** Certified mail records; demand letter; penalty accrual tracking.

## Phase 2: Status investigation — the fraudulent election

**Step 2.1:** From the credit check discovery (showing "retired at 44"), establish:
- The date the retirement status first appeared
- What external records reflect this status (credit reporting agencies, government records)
- Whether any government agencies (SSA, IRS) have records consistent with the fraudulent election

**Step 2.2:** From plan documents received in Phase 1, establish:
- The plan's authentication requirements for retirement elections
- The form of submission required (paper, online, in-person)
- Whether the election could have been submitted by mail without in-person verification

**Step 2.3:** Request from the plan the specific retirement election document — the paper or electronic submission that triggered the "retired" status. This is the primary evidence of the fraudulent transaction.

**Step 2.4:** Analyze the election document for:
- Signature (compare to genuine signature)
- Submission method (mail, online portal, in-person)
- Date submitted vs. date processed
- Any authentication mechanism applied by the plan

**Output:** Timeline of the fraudulent election; chain of evidence for the unauthorized transaction; authentication failure analysis.

## Phase 3: Claim construction

**Step 3.1:** Construct the § 1132(a)(1)(B) claim — benefits due:
- Identify the benefit formula from the SPD
- Calculate total credited service through the date of the fraudulent election
- Calculate the benefit that should have continued accruing from the fraudulent election date to present
- Quantify the frozen vs. earned benefit differential

**Step 3.2:** Construct the § 1132(a)(2) + § 1109 claim — fiduciary breach:
- Document the plan's failure to verify identity before processing the election
- Apply DOL Cybersecurity Guidance (April 2021) as the prudent expert standard
- Identify the plan losses: any payments made under the fraudulent election + accrual loss

**Step 3.3:** Construct the § 1132(a)(3) + Amara equitable relief claim:
- Surcharge: monetary losses from the fraudulent election period
- Reformation: court order correcting the plan records to restore active participant status
- Disgorgement: recovery of any benefits paid to the fraudulent claimant

**Step 3.4:** Assess § 1132(c)(1) penalty:
- If plan fails to produce documents within 30 days: $110/day from day 31
- If plan produces documents but withholds the retirement election records specifically: additional basis for penalty and adverse inference in litigation

**Step 3.5:** Draft parallel criminal referral:
- To FBI: § 1028(a)(7) aggravated identity theft (use of SSN/identifying information to file retirement election)
- To DOL Office of Inspector General: employee benefit fraud
- To SSA OIG: if SSA records were used or affected

**Output:** Claim structure document; damages calculation; criminal referral letters.

## Phase 4: Exhaustion analysis and pre-suit demand

**Step 4.1:** Determine whether the plan's internal appeal process applies. Arguments for excusing exhaustion:
- The claim is not a routine benefit denial — it is an unauthorized transaction
- The plan's own procedure has not been triggered (no "denial" was issued; the election was processed as valid)
- Futility: the plan's position (if known) makes internal appeal futile
- Violation of claims procedure regulations: if the plan failed to issue any notice to the steward of the retirement election, § 2560.503-1 was violated

**Step 4.2:** If exhaustion is required, file the internal appeal:
- Written demand to the plan administrator explaining that the retirement election was unauthorized
- Request for immediate reversal of the election and restoration of active participant status
- Request for identification of who submitted the election and by what means
- Reserve right to amend claims based on documents produced

**Step 4.3:** Send pre-suit demand letter:
- Identify the violation (unauthorized retirement election)
- Identify the remedy sought (restore status, pay accrued benefits)
- Set a response deadline (30 days)
- State intent to file federal court action under § 1132 if not resolved

**Output:** Exhaustion record; pre-suit demand letter; response tracking.

## Phase 5: Federal court filing

**Step 5.1:** File in U.S. District Court — ERISA cases are filed in the district where the plan is administered, where the breach occurred, or where the participant resides. For UA Local 342 (Concord, CA area): Northern District of California is the appropriate venue.

**Step 5.2:** Complaint counts:
- Count 1: Recovery of Benefits Due — 29 U.S.C. § 1132(a)(1)(B)
- Count 2: Fiduciary Breach — 29 U.S.C. § 1132(a)(2) + § 1109
- Count 3: Equitable Relief — 29 U.S.C. § 1132(a)(3) (reformation + surcharge + disgorgement)
- Count 4: Document Penalty — 29 U.S.C. § 1132(c)(1) (if applicable)
- Count 5: Attorney Fees — 29 U.S.C. § 1132(g)

**Step 5.3:** Discovery targets in ERISA litigation:
- The retirement election document (the primary evidence)
- Plan administrator's authentication procedures (written policies)
- Any log of how the election was received and processed
- Identity of anyone who had access to submit elections on behalf of participants
- Communications within the plan regarding this election

**Output:** Federal complaint; service on plan administrator; discovery plan.
