## Skill 1 — §502(a) Benefit Recovery Action
**Authority:** 29 USC §1132(a)(1)(B)
Brings civil action by a plan participant to recover benefits due under the plan or to enforce
rights under the plan. Identifies the correct defendant (plan administrator), the correct
forum (federal district court), and the administrative exhaustion requirement.

## Skill 2 — §510 Interference Identification
**Authority:** 29 USC §1140
Identifies conduct that discriminates against a participant to prevent attainment of any right
to which the participant may become entitled under the plan. Maps the specific discriminatory
act (record substitution, status change, employer action) to the §1140 elements.

## Skill 3 — Fiduciary Duty Analysis
**Authority:** 29 USC §1104
Analyzes whether a plan administrator breached the duty of prudence or the exclusive benefit
rule when freezing or denying benefits based on records that may have been corrupted. A
fiduciary who accepts a fraudulent retirement record without independent investigation may
be breaching the duty to act prudently.

## Skill 4 — Multi-Employer Plan Structure Mapping
**Authority:** 29 USC §186(c)(5); 29 USC §1002(37)
Maps the Taft-Hartley multi-employer plan structure: joint trustee board, employer contribution
obligations under §1145, PBGC insurance, vesting schedules, and the CBA as the instrument
of plan participation.

## Skill 5 — LMRA §301 Concurrent Jurisdiction Analysis
**Authority:** 29 USC §185
Identifies whether a claim arising from violation of the CBA pension provisions may be brought
under LMRA §301 concurrently with ERISA. Analyzes preemption interplay.

## Skill 6 — Statute of Limitations and Tolling Analysis
**Authority:** 29 USC §1113
Applies the 3-year (from earliest date of knowledge) and 6-year (from date of breach) limitations
periods under ERISA §413. Identifies tolling arguments for identity-based fraud where the
plaintiff could not have discovered the breach until the fraudulent record was exposed.

## Skill 7 — Administrative Exhaustion Mapping
**Authority:** 29 USC §1133; plan documents
Maps the plan's internal claim and appeal procedures required before federal suit. Identifies
whether exhaustion was completed, excused, or tolled. Exhaustion failure is the most common
procedural bar in ERISA benefit cases.

## Skill 8 — Standard of Review Determination
**Authority:** Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch, 489 U.S. 101 (1989)
Determines whether the plan document grants the administrator discretion. If yes: abuse of
discretion review (deferential). If no: de novo review (favorable to plaintiff). The standard
of review is outcome-determinative in most ERISA benefit denial cases.

## Skill 9 — ERISA Preemption Analysis
**Authority:** 29 USC §1144; Alessi v. Raybestos-Manhattan, Inc.
Identifies which state law claims are preempted by ERISA §514 and which survive. All state
law claims that "relate to" an employee benefit plan are preempted. This means the only
remedy for pension fraud is federal ERISA — no state tort, no emotional distress.

## Skill 10 — §502(a)(3) Equitable Relief
**Authority:** 29 USC §1132(a)(3); Varity Corp. v. Howe
Where §502(a)(1)(B) cannot provide complete relief, §502(a)(3) allows appropriate equitable
relief — including surcharge (make-whole monetary relief framed as equitable) and reformation
of plan records to correct fraudulent entries.
