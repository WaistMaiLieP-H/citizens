# Umbrella 08 — Environmental / External Impact

**Definition.** What must not be damaged. Emissions and discharge limits, hazardous materials handling, waste management, conservation of shared natural resources. The rules that protect the environment from compounding harm by individual actors who each made small good-faith decisions.

**Scope.** Air, water, land, hazardous materials, hazardous waste, drinking water, groundwater, oceans, wildlife, endangered species, environmental review of projects, climate change response.

**Examples.**
- Clean Air Act (CAA, 42 USC § 7401 et seq.)
- Clean Water Act (CWA, 33 USC § 1251 et seq.)
- Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA, hazardous waste)
- Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA / Superfund)
- Toxic Substances Control Act (TSCA)
- Endangered Species Act (ESA)
- National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA, federal review)
- California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA, state review)
- Porter-Cologne Water Quality Control Act (California water quality)
- Proposition 65 (California Safe Drinking Water and Toxic Enforcement Act of 1986)

**Canonical Citizen owners.**
- EPA Compliance Specialist
- California Air Resources Board (CARB) Compliance Specialist
- California Water Boards (SWRCB / RWQCB) Compliance Specialist
- CalRecycle / DTSC (Department of Toxic Substances Control) Specialist
- CEQA Consultant
- Environmental Engineer
- Wildlife Biologist (ESA-qualified)

**Candidate seed standard for first build.**

**California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA), Public Resources Code § 21000 et seq.** Adopted in 1970 (modeled on the federal NEPA from 1969), CEQA requires public agencies to identify the environmental impacts of their actions and to avoid or mitigate those impacts when feasible. It is one of the most consequential and most-litigated California statutes and the foundation of modern California environmental review.

**Primary-source URL:**
- https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayexpandedbranch.xhtml?lawCode=PRC&division=13.&title=&part=&chapter=&article=
- https://opr.ca.gov/ceqa/ (Governor's Office of Planning and Research, the state CEQA clearinghouse)

**Why this seed:** CEQA is the doctrinal embodiment of "good intentions outrunning standards at scale" — without CEQA, individual development decisions accumulate environmental harm that no single actor intended. Building CEQA as a witnessed standard establishes the umbrella's foundational precedent for review-and-mitigate doctrine.

**Backup candidate.** Proposition 65 (Cal. H&S Code § 25249.5 et seq.) — California's right-to-know toxic substances law, with the famous warning labels.

**Status.** No standards built yet under this umbrella.

**Cross-cutting notes.**
- Environmental overlaps Authority (01) and Procedure (02) heavily — CEQA itself is a procedural overlay (the EIR process) on substantive environmental requirements.
- Environmental overlaps Property (Umbrella 12) — environmental restrictions on land use are also property-rights restrictions; CEQA litigation often pivots on this overlap.
