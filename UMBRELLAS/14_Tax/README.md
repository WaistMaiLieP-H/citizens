# Umbrella 14 — Tax

**Definition.** Tax statutes, tax procedure, tax accounting standards, tax exemption rules. The rules that determine what is taxed, who pays, when they pay, how they file, and what happens when they don't.

**Scope.** Federal income tax, federal estate and gift tax, federal payroll tax, state income tax, sales and use tax, property tax, excise tax, payroll tax, tax-exempt organization rules, tax procedure (audit, appeals, collection, criminal tax enforcement).

**Examples.**
- Internal Revenue Code (IRC, 26 USC) — particularly Subtitle A (Income Taxes), Subtitle B (Estate and Gift Taxes), Subtitle C (Employment Taxes)
- Treasury Regulations (26 CFR)
- IRS Revenue Rulings, Revenue Procedures, Notices, Announcements
- Internal Revenue Manual (IRM) — IRS internal procedure
- Tax Court Rules of Practice and Procedure
- California Revenue and Taxation Code (CRTC)
- California Franchise Tax Board (FTB) regulations
- California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA) regulations (sales/use tax)
- California Board of Equalization (BOE) regulations (property tax)
- Proposition 13 (Cal. Const. Art. XIII A) — California property tax limitation
- IRC § 501(c)(3) and related sections (tax-exempt organizations)
- Circular 230 (also Ethics Umbrella 06 — practice before the IRS)

**Canonical Citizen owners.**

**Candidate seed standard for first build.**

**Internal Revenue Code § 61 — Gross income defined.** Section 61 is the foundation of the entire federal income tax: "Except as otherwise provided in this subtitle, gross income means all income from whatever source derived, including (but not limited to) the following items..." Every federal income tax controversy starts here, because the threshold question in any tax case is: was this income? If yes (under § 61), then proceed; if no, the analysis ends.

**Primary-source URL:**
- https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/61
- https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title26-section61&edition=prelim

**Why this seed:** § 61 is the doctrinal entry point to the entire Internal Revenue Code. Every other tax provision is either an exclusion from § 61, a deduction against § 61 income, a credit against § 61-derived tax, or a procedural rule applied to § 61 calculations. Building § 61 first establishes the umbrella's foundational definition.

**Secondary seed candidate.** California Constitution Article XIII A (Proposition 13) — California's property tax limitation, passed by voter initiative in 1978. The most-litigated and most-politically-contested provision in modern California tax law. Directly relevant to any California real property analysis.

**Tertiary seed candidate.** IRC § 501(c)(3) — tax-exempt organizations under the federal code. Foundational for any nonprofit work, and relevant to charitable trust and fiduciary contexts.

**Status.** No standards built yet under this umbrella.

**Cross-cutting notes.**
- Tax overlaps Authority (01) heavily — every Tax standard is itself a statute or regulation.
- Tax overlaps Procedure (02) — tax court has its own procedural rules separate from the FRCP, and IRS administrative procedure is its own discipline (Internal Revenue Manual, Office of Appeals, etc.).
- Tax overlaps Finance (13) — many tax issues turn on financial classification questions (is this a debt or equity? a sale or a license?).
- This catalog separates Tax from Finance because the agencies, the procedure, the courts, and the specialty bar are all different — but a future architectural decision could merge them.
