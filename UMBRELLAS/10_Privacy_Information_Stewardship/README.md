# Umbrella 10 — Privacy / Information Stewardship

**Definition.** Personal data, confidentiality, privileged communication, the duty of care owed to information about people. The rules that govern what may be collected, who may access it, how long it may be kept, and what must happen when it is breached.

**Scope.** Health information, financial information, education records, attorney-client privilege, work-product doctrine, consumer privacy, biometric data, children's online privacy, electronic surveillance limits.

**Examples.**
- HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Part 164 Subpart E) and HIPAA Security Rule (45 CFR Part 164 Subpart C)
- Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA, Cal. Civ. Code § 56 et seq.)
- California Consumer Privacy Act / California Privacy Rights Act (CCPA / CPRA, Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq.)
- General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, EU)
- Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA)
- Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA)
- Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA)
- Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA)
- California Information Practices Act (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798)
- Attorney-client privilege doctrine and work-product doctrine
- HITECH Act (2009) — modified HIPAA, added breach notification

**Canonical Citizen owners.**
- HIPAA Privacy Officer
- HIPAA Security Officer
- CCPA / CPRA Compliance Specialist
- Data Protection Officer (DPO, GDPR-required role)
- Records Manager (with privacy specialty)
- PRIVAXIS (existing Vernen catalog persona for privacy)

**Candidate seed standard for first build.**

**HIPAA Privacy Rule, 45 CFR § 164.502 (Uses and Disclosures of Protected Health Information — General Rules).** This section is the core operational rule of the entire HIPAA Privacy Rule — it states the general prohibition on uses and disclosures of PHI except as permitted by the rule. Every other HIPAA Privacy section is either an exception to § 164.502, a procedural overlay on it, or a definitional foundation for it.

**Primary-source URL:**
- https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.502
- https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CFR-2024-title45-vol2/CFR-2024-title45-vol2-sec164-502

**Why this seed:** Direct relevance to the steward's medical fraud documentation. Every breach of his medical records, every disclosure made without authorization, every record altered after the fact — these are all § 164.502 violations. Building this standard gives the steward an immediate analytical tool for his medical fraud audit.

**Backup candidate.** Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA, Cal. Civ. Code § 56.10) — the California state-level analog, often more protective than HIPAA, and binding in California-specific litigation.

**Status.** No standards built yet under this umbrella.

**Cross-cutting notes.**
- Privacy overlaps Integrity (Umbrella 09) — the chain of custody for medical records is both a Privacy and an Integrity concern.
- Privacy overlaps Authority (Umbrella 01) — HIPAA itself is a federal statute that creates the Privacy obligations.
- HIPAA preempts state law only to the extent that state law is LESS protective than HIPAA (45 CFR § 160.203). When state law is MORE protective, state law applies. CMIA is more protective than HIPAA in several respects, so California medical providers must apply CMIA, not HIPAA, for those provisions. Documenting the CMIA-vs-HIPAA more-protective-standard analysis is itself outstanding work.
