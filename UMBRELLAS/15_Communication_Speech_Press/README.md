# Umbrella 15 — Communication / Speech / Press

**Definition.** First Amendment doctrine, telecommunications regulation, broadcast standards, libel and defamation, journalism ethics, the rules that govern what may be said, broadcast, published, or transmitted, and what consequences attach to false or harmful speech.

**Scope.** Free speech doctrine; defamation (libel and slander); commercial speech; obscenity; incitement; fighting words; true threats; political speech; campaign finance; broadcast regulation (FCC); telecommunications regulation; common carrier doctrine; cable and satellite regulation; internet intermediary immunity; press shield laws; FOIA and state public records acts.

**Examples.**
- First Amendment to the U.S. Constitution
- California Constitution Article I § 2 (free speech)
- Communications Act of 1934 (47 USC § 151 et seq.)
- Communications Decency Act § 230 (47 USC § 230) — internet intermediary immunity
- Federal Election Campaign Act (FECA) and Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act (BCRA)
- California Anti-SLAPP statute (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16)
- California Shield Law (Cal. Const. Art. I § 2(b); Cal. Evid. Code § 1070)
- Defamation case law (New York Times v. Sullivan 1964, Gertz v. Robert Welch 1974, Curtis Publishing v. Butts 1967)
- FCC regulations (47 CFR)
- Children's Television Act
- Telecommunications Act of 1996
- FOIA (5 USC § 552) and California Public Records Act (Cal. Gov. Code § 7920.000 et seq.)

**Candidate seed standard for first build.**

**California Code of Civil Procedure § 425.16 — Anti-SLAPP statute (Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation).** California's statute that allows defendants in lawsuits arising from acts in furtherance of free speech or petition rights to file a special motion to strike at the outset of the case. If granted, the defendant is entitled to attorney's fees. One of the strongest free-speech protection mechanisms in any U.S. state.

**Primary-source URL:**
- https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=425.16&lawCode=CCP

**Why this seed:** The California anti-SLAPP statute is unusually consequential for pro se litigants because it can dispose of a meritless retaliatory lawsuit at the pleading stage and award fees. Directly relevant to any litigant who is being sued for things they said, wrote, or filed. Builds in the umbrella's most operationally useful protective mechanism.

**Secondary seed candidate.** California Public Records Act (Cal. Gov. Code § 7920.000 et seq.) — the state-level transparency law that gives any person the right to inspect or obtain copies of public records held by California state and local agencies.

**Tertiary seed candidate.** New York Times v. Sullivan (376 US 254, 1964) — the actual-malice standard for public-official defamation. Foundational case-law standard rather than a statutory standard, but central to all modern defamation analysis.

**Cross-cutting notes.**
- Communication overlaps Authority (01) — most Communication standards are themselves statutes.
- Communication overlaps Privacy (10) — speech that exposes private facts implicates both First Amendment doctrine and privacy tort doctrine.
- Communication overlaps Procedure (02) — FOIA and CPRA are procedural mechanisms for getting information out of the government.
