# Umbrella 11 — Family / Personal Status

> **The umbrella the steward's case lives in.** Omitted from the first draft of the catalog and corrected here.

**Definition.** Marriage, divorce, child custody, adoption, conservatorship, guardianship, paternity, name change, civil unions, domestic partnerships. Anything that defines the legal status of a person in relation to other persons, or that subordinates one person's autonomy to another's authority by court order.

**Scope.** Dissolution of marriage; legal separation; nullity; child custody and visitation; child support; spousal support; domestic violence protective orders (DVRO); juvenile dependency (state removal of children); juvenile delinquency status; conservatorship of the person and estate (LPS and probate); guardianship of minors; adoption (private, agency, intercountry, stepparent); ICWA (Indian Child Welfare Act) status; CLETS records; restraining orders generally; name change petitions; gender marker changes; emancipation of minors.

**Examples.**
- California Family Code (the entire code, particularly Divisions 6 (Nullity, Dissolution, and Legal Separation), 8 (Custody of Children), 10 (Domestic Violence Prevention Act))
- California Probate Code Divisions 4 (Guardianship and Conservatorship) and 5 (Other Protective Proceedings)
- Welfare and Institutions Code §§ 300 et seq. (Juvenile Dependency)
- Welfare and Institutions Code §§ 5000 et seq. (LPS Conservatorship)
- Indian Child Welfare Act (ICWA, 25 USC § 1901 et seq.)
- Adoption and Safe Families Act (ASFA)
- Domestic Violence Prevention Act (DVPA, Cal. Family Code § 6200 et seq.)
- Uniform Child Custody Jurisdiction and Enforcement Act (UCCJEA, Cal. Family Code § 3400 et seq.)
- CLETS (California Law Enforcement Telecommunications System) records governing protective orders

**Candidate seed standard for first build.**

**California Family Code § 3011 — Best Interest of the Child (factors).** This is the core statutory test for custody decisions in California. It enumerates the factors a court must consider when determining the best interest of the child: the health, safety, and welfare of the child; any history of abuse; the nature and amount of contact with both parents; and the habitual or continual illegal use of controlled substances or alcohol. Every contested custody decision in California is decided under this rubric.

**Primary-source URL:**
- https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=3011&lawCode=FAM
- https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayexpandedbranch.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&division=8.&title=&part=2.&chapter=&article= (full Custody of Children part)

**Why this seed:** This is the statutory test under which the steward's sixteen years of custody outcomes were nominally decided. Building this as a witnessed standard creates a working tool for analyzing whether the procedural and substantive applications of § 3011 in the steward's case (and in California family courts generally) actually satisfied the rule's requirements. It is also an extremely high-leverage standard for any future Vernen client navigating custody.

**Secondary seed candidate.** California Probate Code § 1801 (Conservatorship — appointment standards) and § 2101 (Conservator's standard of care) — directly relevant to the steward's conservatorship discovery (per `project_conservatorship_discovery.md` and `project_conservatorship_breakthrough.md`).

**Tertiary seed candidate.** Welfare and Institutions Code § 300 (Juvenile Dependency — grounds for jurisdiction) — defines when the state may take jurisdiction over a child. Critical for understanding the boundary between family-court matters and dependency-court matters.
