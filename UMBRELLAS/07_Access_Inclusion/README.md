# Umbrella 07 — Access / Inclusion

**Definition.** Who must be served, what barriers must be removed, what accommodations must be provided. The rules that prevent some people from being structurally locked out of services, employment, public spaces, or civic participation.

**Scope.** Disability access, civil rights, language access, age discrimination, sex/gender discrimination, religious accommodation in employment and education, accessible technology, voting access.

**Examples.**
- Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) — Titles I, II, III, IV, V
- Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973
- California Unruh Civil Rights Act (Cal. Civ. Code § 51)
- California Disabled Persons Act (Cal. Civ. Code § 54 et seq.)
- Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (federal funding non-discrimination)
- Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (employment)
- Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972 (sex discrimination in education)
- Voting Rights Act of 1965
- WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, incorporated by reference into many regulations)
- DFEH / FEHA (California Fair Employment and Housing Act)

**Canonical Citizen owners.**
- ADA Compliance Specialist
- Civil Rights Investigator (federal HHS Office for Civil Rights, DOJ, EEOC)
- DFEH Investigator (California)
- Accessibility Architect (web/digital)
- Voting Access Specialist

**Candidate seed standard for first build.**

**Americans with Disabilities Act, Title II (Nondiscrimination on the Basis of Disability in State and Local Government Services), 42 U.S.C. § 12131 et seq., and 28 CFR Part 35.** Title II is the section that applies to courts, public schools, government services, and any public entity. Directly relevant to multiple parts of the steward's case file (court accessibility for pro se litigants, accommodations for disabled litigants).

**Primary-source URL:**
- https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-126/subchapter-II
- https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/title-ii-2010-regulations/

**Why this seed:** Title II is the public-entity title — the one that binds the courts, the schools, the police departments, the agencies. When the public apparatus discriminates against disabled persons, Title II is the mechanism. The steward's federal § 1983 work touches Title II adjacent issues.

**Backup candidate.** Cal. Gov. Code § 11135 (state-funded program non-discrimination) — California's equivalent of Title VI for state-funded programs, including all California public schools and universities.

**Status.** No standards built yet under this umbrella.

**Cross-cutting notes.**
- Access overlaps Authority (Umbrella 01) when the Access standard is itself a statute.
- Access overlaps Procedure (Umbrella 02) when an accommodation is procedural (e.g., extra time for a hearing, sign language interpretation, large-print filings).
- The Field Act (Umbrella 05) and the ADA Title II both apply to public schools — the Field Act protects them physically from earthquakes, the ADA protects them from excluding students with disabilities. Standards in different umbrellas often co-apply to the same artifact.
