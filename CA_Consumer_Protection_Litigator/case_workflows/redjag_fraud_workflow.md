## Phase 1 — Violation Matrix

Based on the scanned 63-page file. The following violations need to be verified against
the actual documents.

### Yo-Yo Financing Violations

**Rees-Levering (CC §§2981-2984):**
- If dealer delivered the vehicle before financing was final and later changed terms,
  that is a classic yo-yo under Rees-Levering §2982.3: dealer must notify buyer within
  10 days if financing is not completed; buyer may rescind. Failure = statutory violation.
- Civil remedy: rescission + all payments made + §3294 punitive damages if willful.
- Verify: Was there a "spot delivery" with a conditional sales contract? Did dealer call
  back with different terms?

**TILA (15 USC §1638):**
- Direct TILA civil claim: 1-year SOL from consummation of credit = TIME-BARRED for 2018
- Affirmative defense / recoupment: NO SOL. Any collection action on the vehicle debt
  can be met with a TILA recoupment defense under 15 USC §1640(e): "amount of such
  recoupment shall not exceed the finance charge in connection with which such failure
  occurred."
- Yo-yo violation: if APR, finance charge, or payment schedule disclosed at delivery
  differed from the final note terms, §1638(a)(3), (4), (6) were violated.
- Reg Z violation: verify against any "spot delivery" conditional sales contract vs. final
  financing documents (should both be in the 63-page file).

**Vehicle Code §11711 (dealer misrepresentation):**
- 3-year SOL from violation.
- 2018 purchase: time-barred for §11711 civil claim UNLESS fraudulent concealment tolled.
- If dealer concealed the CARFAX switch and plaintiff didn't discover it until later,
  fraudulent concealment tolling may extend. Document when steward first learned of the
  wrong CARFAX.

### Wrong CARFAX / Stolen Vehicle Violations

**CLRA (CC §1750 et seq.):**
- 3-year SOL from discovery of violation.
- If steward discovered the wrong CARFAX after purchase, SOL runs from discovery.
- Violation: §1770(a)(5) misrepresentation that goods have characteristics they don't;
  §1770(a)(7) advertisement of goods without intent to sell them as advertised.
- Stolen/stripped vehicle: if the VIN on the CARFAX didn't match the actual vehicle
  history, that is a §1770(a)(5) affirmative misrepresentation — potentially CLRA.
- CLRA requires a 30-day cure notice before filing suit for damages.

**UCL §17200 (unlawful, unfair, or fraudulent business acts):**
- 4-year SOL from discovery (Bus. & Prof. Code §17208).
- "Unlawful" prong: Rees-Levering violations + TILA violations + §11711 violations are
  all predicate unlawful acts under UCL.
- "Fraudulent" prong: the wrong CARFAX delivery and concealment of the vehicle's actual
  history is a "likely to deceive" business practice.
- Remedy: restitution (return of monies paid) + injunction. UCL does not allow damages
  or attorneys' fees without Civ §3294 claim.

**Civil Deceit (CC §1709):**
- 3-year SOL (CCP §338(d)).
- Elements: representation known to be false + intent to deceive + justifiable reliance +
  resulting damage.
- The wrong CARFAX = false representation. Was it intentional? A dealer substituting a
  clean CARFAX for a stolen vehicle's history is almost certainly intentional.
- Damages: out-of-pocket (difference between represented value and actual value of a
  stolen/stripped vehicle — could be the full purchase price).

## Phase 4 — Action Plan by Claim Track

### Track A: Collection Defense (IMMEDIATE — if collection action is active)

1. File answer in collection action asserting:
   - Rees-Levering rescission defense
   - TILA recoupment defense (finance charge offset)
   - CLRA affirmative defense
   - UCL unlawful prong as affirmative defense
2. File cross-complaint in the collection action asserting:
   - CC §1709 civil deceit (damages + punitive)
   - UCL §17200 (restitution)
   - CLRA §1750 (actual + punitive + fees)
   - Rees-Levering civil remedy

### Track B: Independent Action (if no active collection)

1. Send CLRA §1782 30-day cure notice to dealer (or successor dealer/owner)
2. File California superior court complaint after 30 days
3. Plead: Rees-Levering, UCL §17200, CLRA, CC §1709 deceit, Veh §11711 (if SOL not barred)
4. Seek preliminary injunction on collection activity while suit is pending
5. Serve dealer's bonding company (Vehicle Code §11711.1: $50K dealer bond)

### Track C: Dealer Bond Claim (parallel to Track B)
California auto dealers are required to maintain a $50,000 license bond under Veh §11711.1.
CLRA and §11711 violations are covered. Bond claim does not require litigation.
File directly with the bonding company after establishing the violation.
