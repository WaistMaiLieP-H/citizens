# Colgan v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc. (2006)

**Cite:** 135 Cal.App.4th 663
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second District
**Date:** January 25, 2006

## The rule the case stands for

**"Made in U.S.A." representations are actionable under the CLRA when the product incorporates significant foreign-sourced components or labor, even if final assembly occurs in the United States.** The representation is material to a reasonable consumer, and restitution is an available remedy calculated on the "price premium" attributable to the misrepresentation.

The case also established an early methodology for measuring CLRA restitution damages — the difference between what the consumer paid and the value they actually received.

## Plain-language restatement

Leatherman labeled its multi-tools "Made in U.S.A." but used foreign-sourced components in significant parts of the product. A California appellate court held this violated the CLRA (and the UCL and FAL). The court allowed restitution based on the premium consumers paid because they believed they were buying an American-made product.

## Doctrinal contribution

Colgan did three things:

1. **Materiality of origin claims.** The court held that "Made in U.S.A." is a label consumers care about as a matter of fact — materiality was supported by survey evidence, but the court signaled that materiality could often be presumed.

2. **Restitution methodology.** Restitution under CLRA §1780(a)(3) and UCL §17203 is measured by the difference between the price paid and the actual value received, not the plaintiff's subjective disappointment or the defendant's profit. This anchored later "price premium" cases.

3. **Preemption rejection.** The defendant argued federal origin-labeling rules (FTC "all or virtually all" standard) preempted California's stricter interpretation. The court held federal rules did not preempt.

## Subsequent history

- **Informed Kwikset:** The California Supreme Court's 2011 Kwikset decision expressly built on Colgan's framework.
- **Continues to control:** restitution methodology in CLRA/UCL label-misrepresentation cases.

## Why this matters for the steward's cases

Colgan's restitution methodology supplies the template for calculating CLRA damages in the RedJag case. The measure is not "what did the steward expect" but rather "what was the difference between what the steward paid and what the vehicle was actually worth given the true facts (wrong CARFAX, stripped mechanics)." That difference is the restitution number. Combined with CLRA punitive damages (§1780(a)(4)) and attorney fees (§1780(e)), it provides the full damages model for the claim.

Colgan also confirms that a representation about a car's history or condition is material as a matter of California consumer protection law — consumers care about origin, history, and condition facts, and the CLRA treats that care as presumptively reasonable.
