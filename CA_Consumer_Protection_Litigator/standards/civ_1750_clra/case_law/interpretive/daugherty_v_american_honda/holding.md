# Daugherty v. American Honda Motor Co. (2006)

**Cite:** 144 Cal.App.4th 824
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second District
**Date:** November 8, 2006

## The rule the case stands for

**The CLRA does not require a manufacturer to disclose a defect that manifests only after the warranty period, absent an unreasonable safety hazard or an affirmative misrepresentation.**

A mere failure to disclose a product defect that does not threaten safety and does not manifest during the warranty period is not actionable under §1770 as a "representation" or omission.

## Plain-language restatement

Honda knew that a particular engine component could eventually fail, but the failures happened outside the warranty period and did not pose a safety risk. The court held that Honda's silence about the defect was not a violation of the CLRA. The statute requires something affirmative — either a false statement or a concealment of a safety hazard — not just awareness of a durability shortcoming.

## Why this is a LIMITING case

Daugherty is the primary defense authority cited by manufacturers to limit CLRA "omission" claims. Plaintiffs must respond to Daugherty in every omission case by showing either:
1. An affirmative misrepresentation (not mere silence), OR
2. A safety-related defect (which triggers a duty to disclose under the Daugherty exception itself), OR
3. A warranty-period failure (which Daugherty does not reach).

## Subsequent history and status

- **Narrowed by:** Wilson v. Hewlett-Packard Co., 668 F.3d 1136 (9th Cir. 2012) — affirmed Daugherty's general rule but clarified that safety hazards require disclosure.
- **Refined by:** Collins v. eMachines, Inc. (2011) 202 Cal.App.4th 249 — held that eMachines' knowledge of a motherboard defect that could damage data triggered disclosure duty because of economic harm risk.
- **Current status:** Daugherty remains authoritative for the "no duty to disclose mere durability defects outside warranty" proposition. The safety exception is broadly construed.

## Why this matters for the steward's cases

The RedJag (wrong CARFAX, stripped vehicle) and KiaSoul matters are NOT Daugherty-barred because:

1. **Affirmative misrepresentation.** CARFAX representation is an affirmative statement, not silence. Daugherty's "mere omission" limit doesn't apply.
2. **Stripped vehicle safety implications.** A vehicle sold as complete that is mechanically stripped has potential safety implications, bringing the case within the Daugherty safety exception even if the §1770 omission theory were used.
3. **Representations made during the sale.** Daugherty concerns post-sale manifestation of hidden defects. The steward's cases involve representations made as part of the sale transaction, which are §1770 affirmative-representation cases, not §1770 omission cases.

The Citizen must plead the cases in the affirmative-representation box from the start to avoid triggering Daugherty defensively.
