ORIGIN TEXT — 1970 ENACTMENT
Source: Stats. 1970, Ch. 1550, §1
Effective: January 1, 1971

The Consumers Legal Remedies Act was enacted in 1970 by Chapter 1550 of the 1970 Statutes. The core structure as originally enacted included:

§1750 — Short title ("Consumers Legal Remedies Act")
§1751 — Waiver void (anti-waiver provision)
§1752 — Non-exclusivity of remedies
§1760 — Liberal construction mandate
§1761 — Definitions (goods, services, person, consumer, transaction)
§1770 — The original enumerated list of deceptive practices (originally subdivisions (a)(1)-(18)). The core prohibitions on:
  - passing off, misrepresenting source/sponsorship/approval,
  - misrepresenting affiliation/certification,
  - deceptive geographic origin representations,
  - misrepresenting characteristics/uses/benefits,
  - representing deteriorated goods as new,
  - misrepresenting standard/quality/grade,
  - disparaging another's goods,
  - bait-and-switch advertising,
  - advertising without intent to supply reasonable demand,
  - false "reasons for" price-reduction claims,
  - representing rights/remedies/obligations that do not exist,
  - representing unnecessary parts/repair as needed,
  - misrepresenting prior supply in accordance with representation,
  - rebates contingent on post-consummation events,
  - misrepresenting salesperson authority,
  - inserting unconscionable contract provisions.

§1780 — Private right of action: actual damages (with $100 minimum per individual action — later increased), injunctive relief, restitution of property, punitive damages, "any other relief deemed proper."
§1781 — Class action provision.
§1782 — 30-day pre-suit notice-and-cure requirement for damages claims (no notice required for injunctive relief).
§1783 — Three-year statute of limitations.
§1784 — Innocent affirmative defense (bona fide error + correction within 30 days of notice).

KEY FEATURES OF THE 1970 ENACTMENT:
1. Liberal construction mandate was written into the text (§1760) — unusual for a California statute and a deliberate instruction to courts that the statute was to be read favorably to consumers.
2. Anti-waiver clause (§1751) — consumers cannot contractually waive the CLRA. This was in direct response to form-contract waivers that had become standard in the 1960s.
3. The enumerated list (§1770) was modeled on the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act (UDTPA, 1966 revision) but expanded — California added practices not in the uniform model.
4. Individual private right of action + class action right in the same statute. This is the structural innovation.
5. Attorney fee provision (§1780(d) in original numbering) — prevailing plaintiff fees mandatory. This is what makes the statute economically viable in individual low-dollar consumer cases.
