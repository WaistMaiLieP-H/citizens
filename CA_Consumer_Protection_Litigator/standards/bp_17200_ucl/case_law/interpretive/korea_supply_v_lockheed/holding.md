# Korea Supply Co. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.
## 29 Cal.4th 1134 (2003)

**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** July 7, 2003

---

## Issue decided

Does the UCL allow recovery of non-restitutionary disgorgement — i.e., profits the defendant
made from its unfair competition that were not taken directly from the plaintiff?

---

## Holding

**No.** The UCL's equitable remedies (§17203) are limited to:
1. **Injunctive relief** (prohibiting future violations), and
2. **Restitution** — returning to the plaintiff money or property that was wrongfully taken
   FROM the plaintiff (or obtained from the plaintiff by an unfair practice).

**Non-restitutionary disgorgement is NOT available.** A plaintiff cannot recover, under the
UCL, profits the defendant earned from third parties as a result of its unfair conduct —
only profits that were taken FROM this specific plaintiff.

**The distinction:**
- Restitution: "Give back to plaintiff what defendant took from plaintiff." ✓ Available.
- Non-restitutionary disgorgement: "Give up profits from all victims, not just plaintiff."
  ✗ Not available under the UCL directly (though available to the AG/DA in government enforcement).

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**UCL monetary recovery is bounded.** In auto fraud cases, the UCL restitution is the amount
Michael paid on the fraudulent contracts — down payments, monthly payments, fees paid to the
dealer or assignee. It does not extend to profits the dealer made from other defrauded consumers
who are not parties to Michael's case.

**This is why companion claims matter:** CLRA provides actual damages beyond restitution (§1780);
Rees-Levering provides forfeiture of the finance charge (§2983); Civ §1709 deceit provides
out-of-pocket damages; Civ §3294 punitive damages are available for fraud. The UCL's restitution
is one piece of the damages mosaic, not the whole picture.

**Restitution + injunction as the UCL's strategic value:** Even though UCL restitution is
bounded by what was taken from the plaintiff, the injunctive relief component has independent
strategic value. An injunction against the dealer/collector's practices protects against
future harm and adds leverage in settlement.
