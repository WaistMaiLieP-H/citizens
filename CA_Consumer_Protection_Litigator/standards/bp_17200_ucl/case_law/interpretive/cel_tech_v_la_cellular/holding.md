# Cel-Tech Communications, Inc. v. Los Angeles Cellular Telephone Co.
## 20 Cal.4th 163 (1999)

**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** May 3, 1999

---

## Issue decided

What is the standard for "unfair" business practices under the UCL's second prong in the
context of competitor-injury cases? And does the UCL preempt other statutory remedies or
operate independently?

---

## Holding — Part 1: The "unfair" standard for competitor cases

In competitor (non-consumer) UCL cases, "unfair" means: conduct that threatens an incipient
violation of an antitrust law, OR violates the policy or spirit of one of those laws because
its effects are comparable to or the same as a violation of the law, OR otherwise significantly
threatens or harms competition.

**The tethering requirement for competitor cases:** Courts cannot invoke the "unfair" prong
to prohibit competition that is vigorous but not otherwise unlawful. The "unfair" standard must
be tethered to some legislatively declared policy or the court is making competition policy
that belongs to the Legislature.

**Note on consumer vs. competitor context:** Cel-Tech's "unfair" standard applies squarely to
competitor-injury UCL cases. For consumer cases (like Michael's), courts use a somewhat different
"unfair" test — often the FTC three-part balancing test (substantial harm / not reasonably
avoidable / not outweighed by benefits). California courts have not uniformly applied Cel-Tech
to consumer cases, and there is an ongoing split. Cel-Tech is the leading case on the "unfair"
prong generally.

---

## Holding — Part 2: UCL operates independently of other statutes

The UCL is not preempted by other statutes that address the same conduct. A defendant who
violates the CLRA, Rees-Levering, FDCPA, or any other statute simultaneously violates the
UCL unlawful prong — these are independent claims with independent remedies. The UCL claim
survives even if the predicate statute's claim fails on some element (as long as the UCL's
own elements are met).

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Multi-statute pleading strategy:** Cel-Tech confirms that Michael can plead:
- CLRA + UCL unlawful (borrowing CLRA violation) — independent claims, additive remedies
- Rees-Levering + UCL unlawful (borrowing Rees-Levering violation) — independent claims
- FDCPA/Rosenthal + UCL unlawful (borrowing collection violation) — independent claims

Each "predicate violation" independently establishes the UCL unlawful prong. Failure to prove
one predicate does not defeat the UCL claim if another predicate succeeds.

**Auto dealer practices as "unfair":** Yo-yo financing and multi-document contract strategies
that systematically extract more money from consumers after the initial sale are "unfair"
under either the consumer or the Cel-Tech standard — they cause substantial consumer harm,
are not reasonably avoidable (consumer already surrendered their trade-in), and provide no
legitimate business benefit beyond extraction.
