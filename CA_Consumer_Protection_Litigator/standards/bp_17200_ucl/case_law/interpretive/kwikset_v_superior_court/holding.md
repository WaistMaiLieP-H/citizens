# Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court
## 51 Cal.4th 310 (2011)

**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** January 27, 2011

---

## Issue decided

What does "lost money or property as a result of unfair competition" require under the
Prop 64 standing amendment to §17204? Specifically, does a consumer who paid a premium
price for a product falsely labeled "Made in USA" have standing if the product still functioned
as expected?

---

## Holding

**Yes, standing is satisfied.** A consumer who purchased a product based on a false "Made in USA"
label and would not have purchased it (or would have paid less) had the representation been true
has "lost money or property as a result of unfair competition" under §17204.

**The standing test established:**
1. **Lost money or property:** Paying money for something that would not have been purchased
   but for the false representation = lost money. "The plaintiffs paid more for the products
   than they otherwise would have or bought products they would not have otherwise bought."
2. **"As a result of":** Requires that the economic injury flow from the unfair competition —
   causal connection required. But "the test is met when a plaintiff can show he or she... was
   induced to act or forbear from acting."
3. **The fraud/reliance nexus:** For the fraudulent prong, the "as a result of" element
   incorporates a reliance requirement: consumer must have actually relied on the
   misrepresentation (not merely been exposed to it).

**Scope of "money or property":**
- Paid too much because of fraud = lost money (excess purchase price)
- Paid for something worthless because of fraud = lost money (full purchase price)
- Paid the "right" price for something that turned out to be defective = depends on facts
- Lost the benefit of the bargain (did not get what was promised) = lost money

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Auto fraud cases:** Michael paid money (down payments, contract payments, fees) for vehicles
that were fraudulently represented — wrong vehicle history (CARFAX), yo-yo financing, stripped
or misrepresented vehicle condition. Under Kwikset, every dollar paid on those contracts is
"lost money as a result of" the UCL unlawful + fraudulent violations. Prop 64 standing satisfied.

**UCL restitution measure:** Under Kwikset, restitution in auto fraud cases is the full amount
paid to the extent the defendant received unjust benefit. In Rees-Levering forfeiture cases,
the finance charge is forfeited; the UCL restitution claim allows recovery of additional amounts
wrongfully extracted (down payment on a void contract, excess payments made under illegal
collection pressure).

**The reliance point (fraudulent prong):** For the UCL fraudulent prong, Kwikset requires
actual reliance — Michael must show he saw the fraudulent representation and it caused him
to act. In the CARFAX/vehicle history misrepresentation case (RedJag), reliance is established
by the transaction itself: he would not have purchased a car with a false history report had
he known the truth.
