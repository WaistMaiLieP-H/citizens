# UCL Historical Chain — California B&P §§17200–17210

## The wound (pre-1933, pre-1977)

California business in the 1920s-1930s suffered from predatory competitor practices —
companies deliberately sold below cost to destroy competitors, engaged in bait-and-switch
advertising, and used deceptive trade names. Individual consumers had no access to courts
without specific fraud claims; regulatory enforcement was slow and underfunded. The Legislature
wanted a broad, flexible tool that could reach novel competitive abuses before they became
entrenched practices.

---

## 1933: Unfair Practices Act

California enacted the Unfair Practices Act to target specific competitor-injury practices.
A limited tool — it listed specific prohibited practices (below-cost selling, secret rebates)
but lacked the breadth to reach novel forms of deception. Consumer protection was an afterthought.

---

## 1977: The modern UCL — three-prong reform

The Legislature replaced the 1933 framework with a three-prong structure of unlimited breadth:
unlawful, unfair, or fraudulent. "Any person" had standing. The "borrowed violation" unlawful
prong was immediately recognized by courts. UCL became the capstone claim in California consumer
and competitor fraud cases.

---

## 1999: Cel-Tech defines "unfair"

The California Supreme Court provided the first clear standard for the "unfair" prong in
competitor cases — tethered to antitrust policy. Consumer-case "unfair" standards continued
developing through lower court decisions.

---

## 2003: Korea Supply limits restitution

The California Supreme Court held that UCL monetary relief is limited to restitution from
the plaintiff — non-restitutionary disgorgement is not available. Clarified that UCL is
primarily an injunctive statute, with restitution as a limited monetary remedy.

---

## 2004: Proposition 64 — standing reform

California voters amended §17204: "any person" standing replaced by "injury in fact + lost
money or property." Professional plaintiffs eliminated. Consumers with actual harm retained
full access. Government enforcement retained "any person" standing.

---

## 2011: Kwikset defines Prop 64 standing

The California Supreme Court interpreted Prop 64 standing broadly: paying money for a
fraudulently represented product = lost money even if the product functioned. Causation
(reliance) required for the fraudulent prong. The Court signaled that UCL's consumer
protection mission was not cut down by Prop 64 — only professional plaintiff abuse was targeted.

---

## Current framework (2026)

The three-prong structure (unlawful/unfair/fraudulent) + the borrowed-violation unlawful prong
mechanism are stable after 45 years. Prop 64 standing (§17204) requires injury and causation
but is satisfied by any concrete economic loss. Korea Supply limits monetary remedy to
restitution. The UCL is the indispensable overlay claim in every California consumer fraud case.

**The arc:** 1933 narrow competitor-protection → 1977 broad three-prong consumer/competitor
tool → 1999/2003 judicial definition of "unfair" and restitution limit → 2004 standing reform
→ 2011 standing interpreted broadly → current stable framework.
