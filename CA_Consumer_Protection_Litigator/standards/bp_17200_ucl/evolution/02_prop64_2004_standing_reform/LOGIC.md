# UCL — Evolution Stage 02: Proposition 64 (2004) — Standing Reform

**Enacted:** Proposition 64, November 2, 2004 ballot initiative
**Effective:** November 3, 2004

---

## The wound this stage answered (from the defendant's perspective)

By 2003-2004, UCL plaintiff's bar had weaponized the "any person" standing rule. Plaintiffs who
had never interacted with the defendant, never purchased the product, and never been harmed were
filing UCL class actions based on technical regulatory violations. The initiative backers argued
that professional plaintiffs and their attorneys were filing extortionate strike suits, forcing
settlements in cases where no actual consumer had been harmed. This was characterized as a crisis
for California business.

**Proposition 64's amendment to §17204:** Changed "any person" to require that a private plaintiff
"suffered injury in fact and lost money or property as a result of the unfair competition."

---

## The new standing framework (post-Prop 64)

**Standing test (two elements):**
1. Plaintiff suffered injury in fact (Article III-like concrete injury)
2. Plaintiff lost money or property as a result of the defendant's UCL violation

**"Lost money or property" — Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310:**
The California Supreme Court interpreted the Prop 64 standing requirement broadly:
- "Lost money or property" does not require a large loss — paying money for a product that
  would not have been purchased but for the fraudulent representation is sufficient
- "As a result of" requires causation — the UCL violation must have caused the economic injury
- But the standard is liberal: "The loss of money that plaintiffs allege... is the precise type
  of injury in fact required to satisfy Proposition 64."

**What Prop 64 did NOT change:**
- The three-prong substantive structure (unlawful/unfair/fraudulent) — unchanged
- The remedies (restitution + injunctive relief) — unchanged
- The ability to "borrow" violations from other statutes for the unlawful prong — unchanged
- Government enforcement (AG, DA, city attorney) — no standing requirement for government
  enforcement; any person standard still applies to government plaintiffs

---

## Application: Prop 64 standing for Michael's claims

**Auto fraud cases (all four):** Michael paid money for vehicles that were fraudulently
represented and are subject to Rees-Levering forfeiture. Paying money based on false
representations = "lost money as a result of" the fraudulent/unlawful practices. Prop 64
standing clearly satisfied.

**Debt collection cases:** Making payments on void contracts or under illegal collection
pressure = "lost money" through the coercive/fraudulent collection conduct. Prop 64 standing.

**ChexSystems:** Being denied banking access and the economic costs of operating without
a bank account (check-cashing fees, inability to set up direct deposit, etc.) = economic
injury from the fraudulent/unfair reporting. Prop 64 standing if documentable.
