# Logical Diff: Stage 01 (1977) → Stage 02 (Prop 64, 2004)

## What the 1977 statute allowed

"Any person" had standing to sue for UCL violations — no requirement of personal injury.
A competitor, a professional plaintiff, or a consumer who never interacted with the defendant
could sue if they could identify a UCL violation.

## What Prop 64 changed (§17204)

"Any person" → "any person who has suffered injury in fact and has lost money or property
as a result of the unfair competition."

Two new required elements for PRIVATE plaintiffs:
1. Injury in fact (concrete, not hypothetical)
2. Lost money or property caused by the violation

**Note:** Government plaintiffs (AG, DA, city attorney) retained the "any person" standing.

## What Prop 64 did NOT change

The three-prong substantive structure is identical — unlawful, unfair, fraudulent.
Remedies are identical — restitution and injunctive relief (no damages).
The "borrowed violation" unlawful prong mechanism is unchanged.
Class action procedure is unchanged (though certification is harder post-Prop 64).

## Net practical effect

Professional-plaintiff standing is eliminated. Consumers who have actually been harmed retain
full access. Government enforcement is untouched. For Michael's cases — where real money was
lost and real harm occurred — Prop 64 is not a meaningful barrier. His standing is stronger
than the typical pre-2004 case because he has specific, documented economic losses.
