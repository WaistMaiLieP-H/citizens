# Logical Diff: Stage 01 (1977) → Stage 02 (1999 + 2015)

## What the 1977 Rosenthal Act left uncertain

Medical debt collection was technically within Rosenthal's broad "debt collector" definition
(§1788.2(c)) but practitioners sometimes argued medical billing departments were not "regularly
engaged in debt collection" in the statutory sense. Rosenthal's 1977 text also contained no
special provisions for medically vulnerable debtors.

## What 1999 added

- Expressly covered medical debt collectors — ended the argument that medical billing was
  outside the statute
- No substantive additional requirements; just confirmation of existing coverage

## What 2015 added (§1788.100)

Affirmative obligations on medical debt collectors:
1. **150-day credit reporting moratorium:** May not report medical debt to CRA for 150 days
   after first billing statement — gives patient time to exhaust insurance and assistance options
2. **Charity care disclosure:** Must inform patient of charitable care and financial assistance
   programs before commencing collection
3. **Reasonable payment plan:** Must offer reasonable payment plan before referring to collection
4. **Rights disclosure:** Collection communications must include specific consumer rights language

## Net operational change for Michael

The 1977 structure (incorporating federal FDCPA + original creditor coverage + punitive
damages) is unchanged. The 2015 additions give additional tools specific to medical debt:
the 150-day moratorium and charity care disclosure create additional compliance checkpoints
that, if violated, are standalone Rosenthal violations independent of the core FDCPA-incorporated
prohibitions.
