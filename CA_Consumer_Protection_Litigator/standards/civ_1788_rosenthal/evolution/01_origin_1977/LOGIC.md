# Rosenthal Act — Evolution Stage 01: Origin 1977

**Enacted:** Stats. 1977, Ch. 907 (AB 2109, Assemblyman Barry Rosenthal)
**Effective:** January 1, 1978
**Codified:** California Civil Code §§1788–1788.33

---

## The wound this enactment answered (California-specific)

When Congress enacted the federal FDCPA in September 1977, it deliberately excluded original
creditors. California debt collectors in 1977 included not only professional collection agencies
but original creditors (banks, finance companies, retailers) that collected their own debts
aggressively. California consumers suffered the same abuses documented in the Congressional
record — but when the creditor was the bank itself rather than a collection agency, the federal
FDCPA provided no protection.

Assemblyman Rosenthal's bill filled this gap with a California-specific statute that:
1. Incorporated the federal FDCPA's substantive prohibitions (via §1788.17) — no need to
   draft parallel California prohibitions; simply reference and apply the federal standards
2. Extended "debt collector" to cover original creditors — the key California expansion
3. Added California-specific prohibitions on criminal prosecution threats and harassment
4. Provided the same private right of action as FDCPA but added punitive damage potential

---

## The 1977 logical structure

**Core logical move:** California used the "piggyback" technique — instead of building a
parallel statutory scheme, it incorporated the federal FDCPA's substantive rules by reference
(§1788.17) and then extended their application to original creditors. This was legislatively
efficient and ensured California consumers always received at least the current federal standard.

**The §1788.17 incorporation mechanism:** Whatever the federal §§1692b–1692j say is the rule,
that rule automatically applies in California. When Congress strengthened the FDCPA (as with
the 1986 attorney amendment), California consumers automatically got the benefit without
separate California legislation.

**What the 1977 Rosenthal Act added beyond FDCPA:**
1. Original creditor coverage (the primary addition)
2. California-specific harassment prohibitions (§§1788.10–1788.16)
3. Punitive damages availability (§1788.30(b)) — not in federal FDCPA

**What the 1977 Rosenthal Act still did not address:**
- Medical debt collectors (addressed in 1999 and 2015 amendments)
- Electronic communications (addressed in 2004 amendment)
- Specific medical debt protections for low-income consumers (various post-2010 amendments)
