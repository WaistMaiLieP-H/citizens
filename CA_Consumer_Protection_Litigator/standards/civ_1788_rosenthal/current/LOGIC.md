# California Civil Code §§1788–1788.33 — Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act
## Current Framework — Logical Structure

**Standard:** Cal. Civ. Code §§1788–1788.33 (Rosenthal FDCPA)
**Build method:** Logical Delta
**Key distinction from federal FDCPA:** Covers ORIGINAL CREDITORS — the most important gap
the federal FDCPA does not fill.

---

## Why this statute exists (the wound it answers)

The federal FDCPA (1977) covered only third-party debt collectors — not original creditors
collecting their own debts. California enacted its own debt collection statute the same year
(1977) with a critical difference: the Rosenthal Act covers any person who, "in the ordinary
course of business, regularly, on behalf of himself or herself or others, engages in debt
collection." This includes banks, credit card companies, auto dealers, and other original
creditors that are expressly excluded from the federal FDCPA's "debt collector" definition.

The additional wound Rosenthal answered: California consumers were harassed by the original
creditors themselves — not just by third-party agencies. A bank that called a consumer 20 times
a week about a credit card debt, threatened criminal prosecution, or used profane language was
beyond the reach of the federal FDCPA. Rosenthal reached them.

---

## The Critical Difference: Original Creditor Coverage

**Federal FDCPA §1692a(6):** "Debt collector" = person who regularly collects debts owed
to ANOTHER. Original creditors collecting their own debts = NOT covered.

**Rosenthal §1788.2(c):** "Debt collector" = "any person who, in the ordinary course of
business, regularly, on behalf of himself or herself or others, engages in debt collection."
Original creditors = COVERED.

**Practical impact:** When a bank, auto dealer (KiaSoul, Jaguar dealers), or other original
creditor engages in abusive collection conduct:
- Federal FDCPA: no claim (original creditor exemption)
- California Rosenthal Act: valid claim with identical substantive rights

---

## Substantive Prohibitions (Incorporates Federal FDCPA)

**§1788.17:** "Every debt collector collecting or attempting to collect a consumer debt shall
comply with the provisions of Sections 1692b to 1692j, inclusive, of, and shall be subject to
the remedies in Section 1692k of, Title 15 of the United States Code."

**Logical effect of §1788.17:** The Rosenthal Act incorporates the federal FDCPA's substantive
prohibitions (§§1692b–1692j) by reference. Every federal FDCPA prohibition applies under the
Rosenthal Act — and then some. A collector who violates federal §1692e also violates Rosenthal
§1788.17. This means California consumers get federal FDCPA protections PLUS California's
broader "debt collector" definition (covering original creditors).

**California-specific prohibitions (§§1788.10–1788.16) that add to federal framework:**
- §1788.10: Prohibits threats of criminal prosecution for civil debts in any form
- §1788.11: Prohibits calling repeatedly or continuously with intent to annoy
- §1788.11(c): Prohibits making calls before 8am or after 9pm
- §1788.12: Prohibits communicating false information to any person
- §1788.13: Prohibits false representation that debt collector is an attorney when not
- §1788.14: Prohibits collecting amounts not authorized by law or agreement
- §1788.15: Prohibits threatening to take any action that cannot legally be taken
- §1788.16: Prohibits threatening criminal prosecution when civil debt only

---

## Damages — Rosenthal exceeds federal FDCPA

**§1788.30(a):** Actual damages.

**§1788.30(b):** Statutory damages in the amount the court deems appropriate — between
$100 and $1,000 per individual action. (Same ceiling as FDCPA but structurally identical.)

**§1788.30(b) — PUNITIVE DAMAGES:** "In the case of any action by an individual, such
additional damages as the court may allow." California courts have held this includes
punitive damages under Civil Code §3294 when the collector acted with malice, oppression,
or fraud. **The federal FDCPA does not allow punitive damages. This is Rosenthal's most
significant damages advantage.**

**§1788.30(c):** Attorney fees and costs — mandatory if consumer prevails.

**SOL:** 1 year from date of violation. Civ. Code §1788.30(f).

## Auto fraud application (all four Jaguar / KiaSoul cases)

The auto dealers and their assignee finance companies that collected on the fraudulent
installment sales contracts are "debt collectors" under both federal FDCPA (if third-party
assignee) and California Rosenthal (whether original dealer or assignee). Any abusive
collection conduct on the fraudulent contracts is:
- §1788.17 / §1692e(2)(A): false representation of the legal status of the debt (the debt
  is voidable under Rees-Levering §2981.9 — collecting on a void contract is a false
  representation of enforceability)
- §1788.15: threatening legal action (filing collection lawsuit) on a debt that cannot
  legally be enforced due to Rees-Levering violations
- §3294 punitive damages if the dealer/collector knew the contract was fraudulent and
  collected anyway (Rosenthal §1788.30(b))

## Debt collectors in Michael's portfolio covered by Rosenthal

- Auto dealers (original creditors on installment contracts) — Rosenthal only (not FDCPA)
- Finance companies that took assignment of installment contracts — both FDCPA and Rosenthal
- Collection agencies that purchased or were assigned debt — both FDCPA and Rosenthal
- Collection attorneys who sent demand letters — both FDCPA (Heintz) and Rosenthal
- Banks collecting on ChexSystems-flagged account (if pursuing collection) — Rosenthal only
- Any creditor collecting a medical debt — both FDCPA and Rosenthal (Rosenthal §1788.100 covers
  medical debt explicitly as of 2015 amendment)
