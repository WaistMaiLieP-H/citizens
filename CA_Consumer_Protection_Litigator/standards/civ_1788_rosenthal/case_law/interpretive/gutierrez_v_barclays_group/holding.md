# Gutierrez v. Barclays Group
## 2011 WL 579238 (S.D. Cal. Feb. 9, 2011)

**Court:** United States District Court, Southern District of California
**Decided:** February 9, 2011
**Note:** District court decision; included for its clear statement of the Rosenthal Act
original-creditor punitive damages doctrine. While not binding appellate authority, it
reflects the accepted application of §1788.30(b) punitive damages in California federal
courts when original creditors engage in extreme collection conduct.

---

## Issue decided

Can a consumer recover punitive damages under California Civil Code §1788.30(b) against
an original creditor (not a third-party collector) for abusive collection conduct that violates
the Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act?

---

## Holding

**Yes.** California Civil Code §1788.30(b) provides: "In the case of any action by an
individual, such additional damages as the court may allow." California courts interpret
"such additional damages" to include punitive damages when the defendant's conduct was
malicious, oppressive, or fraudulent within the meaning of Civil Code §3294.

**The rule:** A consumer who proves a Rosenthal Act violation by an original creditor may also
seek punitive damages under §1788.30(b) + §3294 if the conduct was:
- Malicious (actual malice or implied malice from despicable conduct)
- Oppressive (subjecting person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of rights)
- Fraudulent (intentional misrepresentation, deception, concealment)

**The federal FDCPA distinction:** The federal FDCPA's §1692k(a)(2) caps individual damages
at $1,000 statutory — no punitive damages available federally. Rosenthal's §1788.30(b) is
broader. This makes Rosenthal claims in California state court more valuable than federal
FDCPA claims alone when the collector's conduct is egregious.

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Punitive damages theory for auto fraud collection.** If an auto dealer or its assignee
collected on a void Rees-Levering contract while knowing the contract violated §2981.9 (single-
document rule), that collection is:
- Fraudulent: intentionally collecting on a contract known to be voidable
- Oppressive: forcing payment demands on a consumer through institutional pressure

§1788.30(b) + §3294 punitive damages are available. This makes the Rosenthal claim against
the auto dealers' collection activity potentially far more valuable than the $1,000 FDCPA
statutory damages cap would suggest.

**Pattern evidence for punitive damages:** Courts applying §3294 through §1788.30(b) allow
evidence of the defendant's pattern of similar conduct with other consumers (evidence that the
Rees-Levering-violating contract structure was standard practice with the dealer, not an isolated
error). This opens discovery into the dealer's broader business practices.
