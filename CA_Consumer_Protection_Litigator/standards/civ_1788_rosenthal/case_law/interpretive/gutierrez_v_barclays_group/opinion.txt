GUTIERREZ v. BARCLAYS GROUP
NO. 10-CV-1012-DMS (BGS) (S.D. Cal. 2011)
United States District Court, Southern District of California
Decided: 2011

---

FACTS:

Plaintiffs brought claims under the Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (Cal. Civ. Code § 1788 et seq.) and the federal FDCPA (15 U.S.C. § 1692 et seq.) against Barclays Group and its debt collection affiliate for allegedly harassing debt collection phone calls. The calls were made at times and with a frequency that plaintiffs alleged constituted harassment under both statutes.

The case addressed the Rosenthal Act's prohibition on harassment and abuse, particularly the provisions governing communication frequency and timing of collection calls.

---

HOLDING — ROSENTHAL HARASSMENT PROVISIONS:

ROSENTHAL § 1788.11 PROHIBITION:
No debt collector shall "cause a telephone to ring repeatedly or continuously to annoy the person called" or "place telephone calls without disclosure of the caller's identity." Cal. Civ. Code § 1788.11(d)-(e).

The court applied the "least sophisticated debtor" standard in analyzing whether collection practices would be harassing or abusive — a standard lower than the "reasonable person" to provide extra protection to unsophisticated consumers.

MULTIPLE CALLS IN A SHORT PERIOD:
Evidence of repeated calls in a short time period — particularly when the debtor has asked for them to stop or has communicated that the calls are inconvenient — can support a Rosenthal harassment claim. The frequency and pattern of calls is a fact question.

PRIVATE RIGHT OF ACTION:
The Rosenthal Act provides a private right of action for violations. Civil Code § 1788.30:
- Actual damages (including emotional distress damages);
- Statutory damages up to $1,000 per case;
- Attorney's fees to prevailing plaintiff.

CLASS ACTIONS:
The Rosenthal Act supports class actions. In a class action, the statutory damages cap is the lesser of $500,000 or 1% of the collector's net worth (per the incorporated FDCPA class cap, 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(2)(B)).

---

THE RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN ROSENTHAL AND FDCPA:

The Rosenthal Act incorporates by reference many FDCPA prohibitions (§§ 1788.11-1788.14), and California courts apply FDCPA case law to interpret parallel Rosenthal provisions. Federal FDCPA cases are therefore persuasive authority for Rosenthal claims.

Key differences remain:
- Rosenthal covers first-party collectors (original creditors); FDCPA does not;
- Rosenthal has no bona fide error defense as robust as the federal version;
- Rosenthal's "debt collector" definition is broader.

---

APPLICATION TO AUTO FRAUD COLLECTION:

For a consumer subjected to debt collection activity following a void or rescinded auto financing transaction (such as a yo-yo financing contract returned to the dealer):
1. The finance company or debt buyer collecting the deficiency is a "debt collector" under Rosenthal;
2. Repeated calls about a debt the consumer disputes as not owed may constitute harassment under § 1788.11;
3. Misrepresenting the amount or character of the deficiency (where the underlying contract was rescinded) is a false representation under § 1788.13;
4. The private right of action includes actual damages, $1,000 statutory damages per case, and mandatory attorney's fees.
