# McCollough v. Johnson, Rodenburg & Lauinger, LLC
## 637 F.3d 939 (9th Cir. 2011)

**Court:** United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit
**Decided:** March 30, 2011
**Jurisdiction note:** Federal circuit court interpreting both federal FDCPA and, by reference,
California Rosenthal Act standards in federal court litigation

---

## Issue decided

Can a consumer recover damages under FDCPA for a debt collector's litigation misconduct —
specifically, filing a debt collection lawsuit without adequate evidence to verify the debt,
combined with false statements in the litigation?

---

## Holding

**Yes.** A debt collector who files a lawsuit to collect a debt, knowing it lacks adequate
documentation to prove the debt's validity, and who makes false representations in the
litigation process, violates §1692e(2)(A) (false representation of the character or legal
status of the debt) and §1692e(10) (use of false representations or deceptive means).

**Key rules established:**
1. **Litigation is collection activity:** Filing a collection lawsuit is an "attempt to collect"
   within the meaning of the FDCPA. False statements in pleadings, discovery responses, or
   affidavits are false representations in connection with collection of a debt.
2. **Documentation duty:** A collector who files suit without adequate documentary evidence
   of the debt's validity is making an implicit representation that the debt is legally
   enforceable — if the collector does not actually have that documentation, the representation
   is false.
3. **Emotional distress damages:** The court recognized that FDCPA actual damages include
   emotional distress damages supported by evidence (medical treatment, concrete distress
   linked to the collection conduct).

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Collection lawsuits on fraudulent accounts.** If any debt collector filed or threatened a
collection lawsuit against Michael for a debt that:
- Resulted from identity theft (collector did not have documentation proving the debt was
  legitimately Michael's)
- Was disputed and unverified (§1692g written dispute sent)
- Was based on a void Rees-Levering contract (auto dealer cases)
...then the lawsuit or threat of suit itself is an FDCPA §1692e violation under McCollough.

**Discovery in the collection case:** McCollough's documentation-duty holding means that
in any collection litigation against Michael, he can demand in discovery that the collector
produce the underlying agreement, account history, chain of title (showing how the collector
obtained the debt), and evidence that Michael actually incurred the debt. Failure to produce
= admission of inadequate documentation = §1692e violation.

**Rosenthal parallel:** The same §1692e(2)(A) and §1692e(10) standards incorporated into
Rosenthal Act §1788.17 apply in California state court. California consumers can pursue
McCollough-type litigation misconduct claims in state court under Rosenthal with the added
benefit of punitive damages (§1788.30(b)).
