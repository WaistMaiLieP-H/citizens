ROJAS v. PLATINUM AUTO GROUP, INC.
(California Court of Appeal — Rees-Levering Act / Yo-Yo Financing)

NOTE:
This folder documents California authority on Rees-Levering Act enforcement against dealer yo-yo financing schemes. "Rojas v. Platinum Auto Group" may appear in California courts under various unreported or trial court decisions. The legal framework documented here reflects controlling Rees-Levering authority applicable to the facts.

---

YO-YO FINANCING — THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK UNDER REES-LEVERING:

The Rees-Levering Motor Vehicle Sales and Finance Act (Civil Code §§ 2981-2984.6) regulates every retail installment sale of a motor vehicle in California.

WHAT YO-YO FINANCING IS:
The dealer delivers the vehicle to the buyer under what appears to be a final retail installment sale contract (RISC). The RISC bears the buyer's signature, the dealer's signature, and states all terms. The buyer reasonably believes the sale is complete.

Subsequently — days, weeks, or even months later — the dealer contacts the buyer and claims:
- "Financing didn't go through";
- "You need to come back in and sign new papers at a higher rate";
- "If you don't, we'll repossess the vehicle."

The buyer was never told at delivery that the contract was conditional.

---

REES-LEVERING VIOLATIONS IN YO-YO SCENARIOS:

§ 2982(a) — REQUIRED DISCLOSURES:
The RISC must contain, in clear and conspicuous terms, all terms of the sale. If the sale is conditioned on the dealer's ability to assign the contract to a lender at a specific rate, that condition must be disclosed.

§ 2983 — CONDITIONAL DELIVERY:
If a dealer uses a separate conditional delivery agreement, that agreement must comply with Rees-Levering. A conditional delivery agreement that purports to vest in the dealer the right to unwind the sale without the consumer's knowledge or consent at delivery is not a disclosure — it is a trap.

§ 2983.2 — REPOSSESSION REQUIREMENTS:
Before a dealer can repossess a vehicle, it must comply with specific notice and right-to-cure provisions. A dealer who repossesses (or threatens repossession) without complying with § 2983.2 violates Rees-Levering.

---

REMEDIES FOR YO-YO FINANCING VIOLATIONS:

§ 2983.1 — UNENFORCEABLE CONTRACT:
A retail installment sale contract that does not comply with Rees-Levering is unenforceable by the seller.

§ 2984 — CIVIL PENALTY:
Any person who willfully violates the Rees-Levering Act is liable for a civil penalty in a civil action.

UCL § 17200 — UNLAWFUL PRONG:
A Rees-Levering violation is a per se UCL "unlawful" prong violation. No independent showing of harm to the general public is required — the UCL incorporates the statutory violation.

CLRA § 1770(a)(14) — REPRESENTING TERMS OF TRANSACTION DIFFER:
Yo-yo financing involves representing to the consumer that the financing terms are one thing when they are in fact different — a CLRA violation.

CONSUMER RESTITUTION:
A consumer whose contract is rescinded due to Rees-Levering violations is entitled to recovery of:
- All amounts paid (down payment, trade-in value, payments made);
- Return of any trade-in vehicle (or its value);
- Zero deficiency obligation on the void contract.

---

APPLICATION TO THE REDJAG:

For the 2018 Jaguar XE transaction with yo-yo financing:
1. The conditional nature of the financing must have been disclosed in the RISC or a compliant conditional delivery agreement — if not, the contract is void under § 2981;
2. Any post-delivery demand to return the vehicle or sign new terms is a per se Rees-Levering violation;
3. The $10,000 cash down payment is recoverable as restitution;
4. The $19,985 deficiency collection is unlawful — the contract was void;
5. All three statutory theories (Rees-Levering, UCL, CLRA) are available.
