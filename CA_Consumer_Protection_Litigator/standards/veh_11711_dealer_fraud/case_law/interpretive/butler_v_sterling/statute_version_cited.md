# Statute at Time of Butler v. Sterling (2000)

**Federal statute applied:** Truth in Lending Act, 15 U.S.C. §1638 (disclosure requirements)
and §1640 (civil liability). TILA was in its current form — the 2000 Sixth Circuit decision
interpreted the then-current TILA provisions.

**California §11711 relevance:** Butler v. Sterling does not interpret California Vehicle
Code §11711. It is cited for the factual pattern (yo-yo financing) and the legal analysis
of conditional delivery fraud, which California courts have also recognized as actionable.

**TILA civil remedy (15 U.S.C. §1640) — for potential cross-claim:**
- Actual damages + twice the finance charge (minimum $200, maximum $2,000) for individual actions
- Actual damages + minimum $500,000 or 1% of net worth for class actions
- Attorneys' fees to prevailing plaintiff
- 1-year SOL from date of violation

**SOL note for Jaguar XE TILA claim:** 2018 transaction + 1-year TILA SOL = time-barred
unless tolled. TILA equitable tolling may apply if the violation was fraudulently
concealed. Analyze tolling before pleading TILA independently.
