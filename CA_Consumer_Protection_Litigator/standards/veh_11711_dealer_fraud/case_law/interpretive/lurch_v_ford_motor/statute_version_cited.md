# Statute Version — Lurch v. Ford Motor Credit Co. (2003)

**§11711 version:** Post-1988 amendment (treble damages + attorneys' fees in place).
The 2003 trial court decision applied §11711 in its current enhanced form.

**Rees-Levering version:** Cal. Civil Code §§2981-2984.4 as in effect 2003. The
Rees-Levering Act governs the form, content, and required disclosures in motor vehicle
retail installment contracts. Key requirements at time of case and currently:

- §2982: Required contract disclosures including cash price, down payment, trade-in
  allowance, amount financed, finance charge, annual percentage rate, total of payments
- §2982.5: Prohibited provisions (waiver of defenses, confession of judgment, etc.)
- §2983: Buyer's right to retain copy of contract at time of signing
- §2983.2: Right to rescind if delivery made before final credit approval

**§2983.2 — Conditional delivery rescission right:**
If a dealer delivers a vehicle before final credit approval is obtained, the buyer may
rescind the contract within 10 days of receiving written notice that credit was not
approved on the terms stated in the contract. The dealer must return the buyer's trade-in
and all payments; the buyer returns the new vehicle.

**Strategic note:** If the dealer failed to comply with §2983.2's rescission procedure
(failed to give timely written notice, failed to return trade-in, failed to restore buyer
to pre-transaction position), the Rees-Levering violation is separate from the §11711
claim and may provide an independent basis for recovery.
