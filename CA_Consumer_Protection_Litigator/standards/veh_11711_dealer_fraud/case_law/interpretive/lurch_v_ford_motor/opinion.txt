California Yo-Yo Financing — Established Principles Document

This opinion.txt documents the California conditional delivery / yo-yo financing pattern as established across multiple California court decisions and secondary authorities. The specific Lurch v. Ford Motor Credit citation requires primary-source verification. This document anchors the legal framework for applying § 11711, Rees-Levering (§ 2981 et seq.), and Civil Code § 1709/1710 to yo-yo financing transactions in California.

---

I. CONDITIONAL DELIVERY — THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK

Under California Vehicle Code § 11713.2(b), a licensed dealer who makes a conditional sale must either:
(a) obtain written acknowledgment from the buyer that the sale is conditional and subject to financing approval; OR
(b) disclose in writing the conditions under which the transaction may be unwound.

A conditional delivery becomes a misrepresentation under § 11711 when:
1. The written disclosures fail to clearly convey the conditional nature;
2. The circumstances of the transaction (delivery + trade-in acceptance) imply finality;
3. The buyer is not given a reasonable opportunity to review and understand any conditional terms; OR
4. The dealer fails to disclose its practice of presenting conditional deals as final.

---

II. THE TRADE-IN ELEMENT

California courts have consistently treated the immediate resale or disposition of the buyer's trade-in vehicle as aggravating evidence in yo-yo financing cases. When a dealer:
- Takes the buyer's trade-in at delivery;
- Immediately wholesales or retails the trade-in before confirming financing on the new vehicle;
- Later tells the buyer financing "fell through" after the trade-in is unreturnable;

...the dealer has created a situation where the buyer has no ability to unwind the transaction on equal terms. This conduct supports:

(a) Actual fraud under Civil Code § 1709 (intentional misrepresentation of the deal as final);
(b) Concealment under Civil Code § 1710(3) (failure to disclose the conditional nature of the delivery + the already-resold trade-in);
(c) § 11711 misrepresentation in the dealer's licensed activity.

---

III. DAMAGES METHODOLOGY FOR CONDITIONAL DELIVERY CASES

A. If buyer accepted renegotiated terms:
- Difference between original contract rate/terms and renegotiated rate/terms, calculated over the full loan term (present value)
- Down payment paid on initial contract (restitution if contract was void/voidable)
- Any fees paid in connection with the initial transaction

B. If buyer returned vehicle after losing trade-in:
- Value of trade-in at time of transaction (not resale price — FMV at the time buyer surrendered it)
- Down payment surrendered
- Any consequential costs (rental vehicle during the period, consequential damages from loss of transportation)

C. If dealer proceeds to collection after buyer disputes:
- All collection costs, credit damage, and consequential economic losses from collection activity become part of consequential damages from the original misrepresentation
- FDCPA/Rosenthal Act violations in the collection process provide an independent damages stream

---

IV. REES-LEVERING FORFEITURE

Under Civil Code § 2983.4 (the Rees-Levering penalty provision), a conditional sale contract that fails to comply with the Rees-Levering Act's disclosure requirements is void as to the seller. A void contract cannot form the basis of any collection action. If the original RedJag contract failed Rees-Levering requirements (single-document rule, mandatory disclosures), the debt the collection agency is pursuing is uncollectible.

---

V. SIGNIFICANCE FOR REDJAG

The RedJag transaction (2018 Jaguar XE, $10,000 cash down payment, current $19,985 collection action):

1. If the original contract failed to clearly disclose conditional delivery terms → § 11711 misrepresentation;
2. If the CARFAX provided was wrong → § 11711 misrepresentation in the vehicle sale;
3. If the Rees-Levering single-document rule was violated → contract void → $19,985 collection action is uncollectible;
4. If the vehicle was subsequently stripped or had undisclosed damage → § 1709 deceit / concealment;
5. If the collection attorney makes legally incorrect representations in pursuing the void contract → FDCPA § 1692e (Heintz) + Rosenthal Act.
