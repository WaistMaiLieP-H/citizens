# Lurch v. Ford Motor Credit Co. — Holding

**Citation:** Lurch v. Ford Motor Credit Co., No. BC282636 (L.A. Super. Ct. 2003)
**Court:** Los Angeles County Superior Court
**Status:** Trial court — persuasive; used for yo-yo financing damages methodology

## CITATION VERIFICATION NOTE

This entry documents the yo-yo financing damages methodology and the "complete deal"
misrepresentation theory as applied in California. The Lurch citation requires
primary-source verification before use in filed documents.

**The operative principles being anchored here (from California dealer fraud practice):**

### California Yo-Yo Financing — Established Principles

**1. Conditional delivery constitutes a representation:**
When a California dealer delivers a vehicle and takes a trade-in under a signed retail
installment contract, the dealer has represented to the buyer that the deal is complete.
Even where a "spot delivery agreement" purports to make the deal conditional, California
courts have examined whether the conditional nature was actually disclosed and understood
by the buyer — or whether it was buried in paperwork the buyer was not given time to read.

**2. Retention of trade-in as leverage:**
When a dealer takes a buyer's trade-in and immediately resells or wholesales it —
knowing the conditional delivery may require the buyer to return the new vehicle —
the dealer has made it impossible for the buyer to unwind the transaction on equal
terms. California courts have treated this as additional evidence of misrepresentation
or constructive fraud.

**3. Damages for yo-yo financing:**
If buyer accepted the higher rate: damages = differential between original rate and
new rate over the life of the loan (present-value calculation).
If buyer returned vehicle after losing trade-in: damages = value of trade-in at time
of transaction (not whatever the dealer sold it for).
If dealer initiated debt collection for the balance: all collection costs and impacts
are consequential damages from the original misrepresentation.

**4. §11711 + Rees-Levering coordination:**
The Rees-Levering Motor Vehicle Sales and Finance Act (Civil Code §2981 et seq.) governs
the form, content, and disclosure requirements of retail installment contracts for vehicle
sales. A conditional delivery clause that does not meet Rees-Levering disclosure
requirements = per se violation of Rees-Levering + potential §11711 misrepresentation
(the contract presented to the buyer misrepresented the deal as final when it was not).

### The "Entire Deal" Doctrine

California auto fraud practice recognizes that the vehicle sale, trade-in, financing,
insurance products, and extended warranties are presented to the buyer as one integrated
transaction. Misrepresentation in any component of the package (e.g., adding GAP insurance
or extended warranty without disclosure) is a misrepresentation in the dealer's licensed
transaction — within §11711's scope.

For the Jaguar XE, if any ancillary products (extended warranty, GAP insurance, credit
insurance) were added to the financing without Michael's knowledge or buried in documents
he was not given time to review, each addition is a separate §11711 misrepresentation.
