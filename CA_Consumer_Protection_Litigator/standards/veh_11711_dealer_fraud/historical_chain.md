# Historical Chain — Cal. Vehicle Code §11711 (Dealer Fraud Civil Remedy)

## Chain Summary

§11711's evolution moves from a redundant-with-common-law remedy to a functional
deterrence statute through the addition of treble damages and mandatory fee-shifting.
The statute's power comes from the combination of: dealer's licensed status as anchor,
treble damages as deterrent, and mandatory fees as access enabler.

## Stage 1 — Origin (Vehicle Code Framework, ~1959-1961)

**Wound:** No statutory civil remedy tied to dealer's licensed status; common law fraud's
scienter and reliance requirements routinely defeated buyer claims; dealer information
advantages were structural and unaddressed.

**Response:** §11711 — civil action for fraud, misrepresentation, false promise, or deceit
in licensed transactions; actual damages. Licensed status as anchor instead of common law
fraud elements.

**Gap:** Actual damages only; no fee-shifting; small claims uneconomical to pursue;
no deterrence premium over common law recovery.

## Stage 2 — Treble Damages + Mandatory Fees (~1988)

**Wound:** Actual-damages-only framework provided no meaningful deterrence; systematic
yo-yo financing, vehicle history misrepresentation, and title fraud were rational business
strategies; small-value claims were economically unviable without fee-shifting.

**Response:** §11711(b) treble damages (court's discretion, up to 3×) + §11711(c)
mandatory attorneys' fees (prevailing plaintiff). Changed the dealer cost-benefit
calculation and made small-value fraud cases viable.

**Key asymmetry:** Treble damages are discretionary; fees are mandatory. Pattern of
conduct → stronger case for treble discretion.

## Current State

§11711 as amended through 1988:
- Elements: licensed dealer + fraud/misrepresentation/false promise/deceit in licensed
  transaction + loss or damage
- Damages: actual + treble (discretionary) + attorneys' fees (mandatory)
- Preserves §3294 punitive avenue
- SOL: 3 years (CCP §338, fraud/misrepresentation); discovery rule
