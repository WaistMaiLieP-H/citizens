# Evolution Stage 02 — Treble Damages and Mandatory Attorneys' Fees (1988)
## The Deterrence Enhancement

### THE WOUND

Through the 1970s and early 1980s, studies of California auto dealer fraud revealed that
actual-damages-only recovery was insufficient to deter systematic dealer fraud.

The economic calculus for dealers: a misrepresented vehicle transaction generating
$3,000–$5,000 in extra profit per deal, with only a small fraction of buyers pursuing
civil claims (cost, time, complexity) and recovering only actual damages when they did,
made fraud a rational business strategy. Actual damages recovery without fee-shifting
also meant that small-dollar fraud claims ($2,000–$5,000) were economically unviable
for plaintiffs to pursue — attorney fees exceeded the likely recovery.

The pattern was systemic:
- Yo-yo financing affected a high percentage of conditional delivery transactions
- Wrong vehicle history disclosure (pre-CARFAX) was undetectable by buyers
- Title defects were difficult to discover until title transfer failed
- "As is" clauses and integration clauses in form contracts attempted to waive all claims

Common law fraud recovery was already available — the question was whether §11711 as
originally enacted provided any practical improvement. Without fee-shifting and enhanced
damages, it did not.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1988 AMENDMENT

The Legislature added two provisions that transformed §11711 from a redundant fraud
remedy into a functional consumer protection statute:

**§11711(b) — Treble damages:**
Court may, in its discretion, award up to three times actual damages. The treble
damages provision serves two functions:
1. Deterrence: changes the dealer's cost-benefit calculation — expected cost of fraud
   = (probability of suit) × (3× actual damages + attorneys' fees)
2. Access: larger potential recovery justifies plaintiff investment in the case

**§11711(c) — Mandatory attorneys' fees:**
Court shall award reasonable attorneys' fees to the prevailing plaintiff. This provision:
1. Makes small-value fraud cases economically viable (fees are compensated separately)
2. Shifts the cost of litigation to the wrongdoer
3. Creates a fee-risk deterrent for dealers (facing both damages and fee exposure)

### LOGICAL DELTA

| Element | Pre-1988 | Post-1988 |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Damages | Actual damages only | Actual damages + up to 3× treble at court's discretion |
| Attorneys' fees | Not expressly available | Mandatory to prevailing plaintiff |
| §3294 punitive | Preserved | Explicitly preserved ("any other civil remedy available") |
| Deterrence calculus | Rational fraud for dealers | Changed: expected cost = 3× damages + fees |

### DISCRETIONARY VS. MANDATORY

The treble damages are discretionary ("may ... award") while fees are mandatory ("shall
award"). This distinction matters:

- Fees are automatic upon prevailing — no showing of egregious conduct required
- Treble damages require the court to exercise discretion — factors: intentionality,
  pattern of conduct, whether dealer corrected after complaint, harm severity

For the Jaguar XE case: three separate fraud violations (wrong CARFAX, yo-yo financing,
title defect) establish a pattern that would support the court's discretion to award
treble damages rather than a single isolated misrepresentation.
