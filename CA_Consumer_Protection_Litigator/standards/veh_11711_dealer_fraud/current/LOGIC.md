# Cal. Vehicle Code §11711 — New Motor Vehicle Dealer Fraud Civil Remedy
## Current Operative Architecture

### RULE

§11711 provides a direct civil action against a licensed California motor vehicle dealer
for fraud, misrepresentation, false promise, or false statement in the sale or lease of
a motor vehicle. The statute provides enhanced damages — up to three times actual damages
— plus attorneys' fees and costs.

**Operative text (§11711(a)):**
"Any person who suffers any loss or damage by reason of any fraud practiced on that person,
or any fraudulent representation, false promise, misrepresentation, or deceit made by a
licensee in the performance of the acts for which a license is required, may bring an
action for recovery of damages, or both compensatory and punitive damages, in a court of
competent jurisdiction against such licensee and against any person who assisted the
licensee or participated in such fraud or misrepresentation."

**Treble damages provision (§11711(b)):**
"In addition to any other civil remedy available, the court may, in its discretion, award
punitive damages not in excess of three times the actual damages."

**Attorneys' fees (§11711(c)):**
The court shall award reasonable attorneys' fees to the prevailing plaintiff.

### THE WOUND THIS LAW ANSWERED

Before §11711 a defrauded car buyer had to pursue common law fraud — which required:
proving specific false representation + knowledge of falsity + intent to induce reliance
+ justifiable reliance + actual damage. Each element required independent proof; scienter
was difficult to establish against a dealership; and even winning a fraud case produced
only actual damages with no enhancement mechanism (punitive damages under §3294 required
the separate malice/oppression/fraud showing at a high evidentiary bar).

Car dealers held significant information advantages over buyers:
- Access to auction records, prior damage reports, and vehicle history unavailable to buyers
- Control over the finance process with conditional delivery terms buyers didn't understand
- Physical control of the sale environment — time pressure, multiple parties, complex documents

Dealers exploited these advantages systematically: yo-yo financing (delivering on one rate,
calling to demand higher rate), misrepresenting vehicle history, bait-and-switch on advertised
prices, and concealing title defects. Common law fraud's scienter and reliance requirements
were difficult to satisfy on these fact patterns.

§11711 was designed to close this gap by: (1) making the dealer's licensed status the trigger
rather than proving common law fraud elements; (2) providing treble damages as a deterrence
mechanism; and (3) requiring mandatory attorneys' fees to make small-value fraud cases viable.

### ELEMENTS

1. **Defendant is a licensed motor vehicle dealer** under California Vehicle Code Division 5
   (specifically a "dealer" as defined in §285 — new/used motor vehicles sold at retail for
   commission or profit)

2. **Fraud, misrepresentation, false promise, or deceit** in the performance of acts requiring
   a license — i.e., in the sale, lease, or negotiation of a vehicle transaction

3. **Plaintiff suffered loss or damage** — economic harm causally connected to the fraud
   or misrepresentation (includes money paid, value of vehicle received vs. represented)

**Note on scienter:** §11711 uses the phrase "fraud practiced on that person, or any
fraudulent representation, false promise, misrepresentation, or deceit" — courts have
construed this to include both intentional fraud and negligent misrepresentation made
in a dealer's licensed capacity. The dealer's licensed status creates a duty that lowers
the scienter bar compared to common law fraud.

### ENHANCED DAMAGES ARCHITECTURE

**Actual damages:**
The difference between what the buyer paid and what they received — i.e., the out-of-pocket
loss from the misrepresentation. For yo-yo financing: additional finance charges paid above
the original contract rate. For misrepresented vehicle condition: cost of repair, or
fair market value of vehicle as represented minus fair market value as actually received.
For wrong CARFAX: if prior damage reduced value, difference in value with vs. without
disclosed damage.

**Treble damages (§11711(b)):**
Court may award up to 3× actual damages. This is discretionary (unlike Song-Beverly's
2× civil penalty which is mandatory once willfulness is established). Factors courts
consider: nature and severity of the misrepresentation, whether it was intentional or
systematic, prior complaints against the same dealer, whether the dealer corrected the
misrepresentation after discovery.

**Attorneys' fees (§11711(c)):**
Mandatory award to prevailing plaintiff — unlike common law fraud where fees are not
available without a contract or statutory fee-shifting provision. This makes §11711 one
of the few California statutes with both treble damages AND mandatory fee-shifting for
individual consumer auto fraud cases.

**Punitive damages under §3294:**
§11711(a) explicitly preserves "any other civil remedy available" including common law
punitive damages under §3294. If the dealer's conduct constitutes malice, oppression, or
fraud under §3294, punitive damages are available on top of treble damages — though
courts generally will not stack both treble and §3294 punitive recovery for the same
conduct (one or the other, based on which is larger).

### SCOPE — "ACTS FOR WHICH A LICENSE IS REQUIRED"

§11711 applies only to acts performed in the dealer's licensed capacity — the actual
sale, lease, negotiation, and financing of a vehicle. It does not apply to collateral
dealer conduct (e.g., a dealer's employee committing an unrelated tort on dealership
property).

**Within scope:**
- Misrepresentation about vehicle history, condition, title, or prior use
- False statements about financing terms (interest rate, total finance charge, monthly payment)
- Yo-yo financing — the conditional delivery/financing contingency that was not disclosed
- Wrong vehicle history report presented as the subject vehicle's history
- Misrepresentation that the vehicle was new, certified, or otherwise designated when it was not
- Concealment of title defect (stolen, branded, salvage) — the Vehicle Code requires disclosure

**Potentially outside scope:**
- Post-sale conduct by the dealer unrelated to the vehicle transaction
- Manufacturer defects (Song-Beverly track is separate)
- Debt collection after the sale (Rosenthal Act track)

### JAGUAR XE 2018 APPLICATION

**Three independent §11711 violations on the Jaguar XE transaction:**

**Violation 1 — Wrong CARFAX:**
The dealer presented a CARFAX report for a different vehicle (different VIN) as the
history for the Jaguar XE being sold. This is a direct misrepresentation of the
vehicle's history in the sale transaction — within the dealer's licensed capacity.
Actual damages: reduction in fair market value from undisclosed prior damage/history.
Evidence: both VINs, the CARFAX provided, the actual VIN on the vehicle.

**Violation 2 — Yo-yo financing / conditional delivery:**
Dealer represented financing as complete at signing. Contract signed, vehicle delivered.
Dealer later demanded return of vehicle or acceptance of higher financing rate. The
initial representation that financing was approved and complete = misrepresentation in
the performance of acts requiring a license (negotiating and completing a vehicle
finance transaction).
Actual damages: financial loss from the rate differential if Michael accepted the higher
rate; or if vehicle was repossessed/returned — loss of down payment, transportation costs,
opportunity costs.
Evidence: original contract terms, dealer's subsequent demand, communications showing
demand for rate change or return.

**Violation 3 — Stolen/stripped vehicle:**
If the vehicle was stolen or title-compromised at the time of sale, the dealer sold a
vehicle it either knew or should have known had a title defect. Vehicle Code §5900 et seq.
require dealers to provide clear title. Selling a vehicle with a title defect = per se
misrepresentation in the dealer's licensed capacity.
Actual damages: full purchase price ($19,985 balance) + $10,000 down payment = purchase
price of a vehicle that could not legally be transferred.
Evidence: theft reports, auction records, subsequent title investigation.

**Combined damages calculation:**
- Actual damages: down payment ($10,000) + vehicle payments made + debt collection costs
  ($19,985 claimed against Michael) = approximately $30,000+ in actual damages
- Treble: up to $90,000+ at court's discretion
- Attorneys' fees: mandatory
- UCL/FAL stack: §17200 and §17500 violations run separately; restitution of all money paid

### DEALER LICENSE AS ANCHOR

The dealer's California DMV dealer license number appears on the purchase contract.
This anchors §11711 standing: plaintiff need only show (1) the defendant was a licensed
dealer at the time of sale and (2) the misrepresentation occurred in a transaction
requiring that license. Obtain the dealer's license number from the DMV Business Partner
Automation lookup (bpa.dmv.ca.gov) and confirm the license was active at the date of sale.

### SOL

§11711 does not specify its own SOL. The 3-year tort SOL (CCP §338) applies for fraud
and misrepresentation claims. Discovery rule: 3 years from when plaintiff discovered or
should have discovered the fraudulent nature of the transaction.

For the Jaguar XE (2018 transaction): if the fraud was not discovered until the vehicle
was revealed as stripped/title-defective, SOL runs from discovery — not the transaction
date. This may toll the SOL significantly past the 2018 transaction date.
