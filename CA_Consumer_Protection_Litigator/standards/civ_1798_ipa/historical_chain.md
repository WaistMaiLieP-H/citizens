# Historical Chain — Cal. Civil Code §§1798-1798.78 (Information Practices Act of 1977)

## Chain Summary

The IPA was modeled on the federal Privacy Act with California-specific enhancements —
a lower damages floor without willfulness requirement, and individual employee liability.
Its evolution is primarily about boundary clarification: what records it covers, which
agencies it reaches, and how it interacts with the later CCPA framework.

## Stage 1 — Origin 1977 (Stats. 1977, Ch. 709, SB 1983)

**Wound:** No California statute governing state agency collection, use, or disclosure
of personal information about individuals; no access or amendment rights; no civil remedy
for harm from erroneous state agency records.

**Response:** IPA — collection limitation, notice, disclosure limits, access, amendment,
civil remedy with $200 floor (no willfulness required), individual employee liability
($2,500 per willful disclosure).

**Key departure from federal model:** $200 floor without willfulness; §1798.53 individual
employee liability; broader agency definition than federal Act.

**Gap:** Digital record systems, automated transfers, and electronic databases not
addressed.

## Stage 2 — Technology Amendments + CCPA Carve-Out (1994-2020)

**Wound:** IPA's paper-record architecture did not clearly reach electronic transfers
and digital databases; CCPA (2018) created private-sector framework requiring
confirmation that IPA remains exclusive for state agencies.

**Response:** Incremental amendments clarifying electronic transfers are "disclosures";
CCPA explicitly governs private businesses — IPA remains the exclusive framework for
California state agency personal records.

**Key boundary:** IPA ≠ CCPA. State agency records = IPA. Private business data = CCPA.

## Current State

§§1798-1798.78 as amended through 2002:
- State agency collection limitation (§1798.14)
- Notice at collection (§1798.17)
- Disclosure limitation (§1798.24)
- Access right — inspect records about yourself (§1798.34)
- Amendment right — challenge inaccurate records (§1798.35)
- Accounting of disclosures (§1798.40)
- Civil remedy: §1798.45 — $200 floor + actual damages + fees (no willfulness required)
- Injunction: §1798.47
- Individual employee liability: §1798.53 — $2,500 per willful disclosure + actual damages + fees
- SOL: 3 years (CCP §338) from violation or discovery

## Cases in Chain

2. Shaffer v. Superior Court, 33 Cal.App.4th 993 (1995) — §1798.53 individual employee
