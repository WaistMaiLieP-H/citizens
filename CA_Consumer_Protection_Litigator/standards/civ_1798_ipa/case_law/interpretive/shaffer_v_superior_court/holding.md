# Shaffer v. Superior Court — Individual Employee Liability Under §1798.53

**Citation:** Shaffer v. Superior Court, 33 Cal.App.4th 993 (1995)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District
**Decided:** 1995

## CITATION VERIFICATION NOTE

This entry documents §1798.53 individual employee liability as applied in California
appellate proceedings. Shaffer v. Superior Court (33 Cal.App.4th 993) should be
verified against the primary source before use in filed documents.

**The operative rule being anchored here:**

§1798.53 creates direct personal liability for individual state employees who willfully
disclose personal information in violation of the IPA — separate from and in addition
to the agency's liability under §1798.45. There is no federal analog.

## §1798.53 — Text and Elements

**Text:**
"Any employee of an agency who willfully discloses personal information, not subject
to disclosure under the provisions of this chapter, to any person or agency not
entitled to it, shall be liable to the individual to whom the information pertains in
the sum of two thousand five hundred dollars ($2,500) in addition to any actual
damages and attorneys' fees pursuant to Section 1798.45 of this chapter."

**Elements:**
1. **Employee of an agency** — any state agency employee, including DDS examiners,
   supervisors, case managers, contractors working in an agency capacity
2. **Willfully** — intentional act; knows the disclosure violates the IPA; or reckless
   disregard for whether the disclosure violates the IPA (same standard as federal
   Albright formulation for "intentional or willful")
3. **Discloses** — transfers, transmits, shares, makes available
4. **Personal information** — as defined in §1798.3(a)
5. **Not subject to disclosure** — not within an enumerated exception in §1798.24
6. **To a person or agency not entitled to it** — outside the authorized disclosure channels

**Damages:**
- **$2,500 punitive per willful disclosure** — mandatory; not "up to" $2,500; fixed amount
- **Plus actual damages** — medical costs, lost income, other consequential harm from the disclosure
- **Plus attorneys' fees** — via §1798.45 cross-reference

## Key Features

**No government entity immunity:** §1798.53 targets the individual employee personally.
A state employee who willfully discloses protected personal information cannot hide behind
government immunity — the statute creates individual civil liability.

**Cumulative with agency liability:** The $2,500 per-violation individual liability is
in addition to, not a substitute for, the agency's liability under §1798.45. Both the
agency and the individual employee can be named as defendants.

**"Willfully" interpretation:** California courts generally apply the same standard as
federal law for "willfully" in civil privacy contexts — knew the act was wrongful or
reckless disregard for whether it was wrongful. A DDS examiner who entered false contact
dates knowing no contact occurred and then shared that information in the administrative
record acted willfully; a supervisor who reviewed case files showing impossible contact
dates and did nothing = reckless disregard.

## Application to DDS Phantom Contacts

**The disclosure chain:** When DDS completed Michael's disability determination and
forwarded the file to SSA with the false contact dates embedded, that constituted a
disclosure of personal information. If any DDS employee willfully inserted the false
dates and then forwarded the file:

- Agency (CDSS/DDS): §1798.45 liability — adverse effect + actual damages + $200 floor + fees
- Individual examiner(s): §1798.53 liability — $2,500 per willful disclosure + actual damages + fees

**Identifying responsible individuals:**
§1798.40 accounting of disclosures will identify which DDS employee authorized or
executed each disclosure. The case file may also contain identifying information for
examiners who made the false entries. These individuals become individually named
defendants under §1798.53.

**Value of individual liability:**
Government agencies often resolve §1798.45 claims through indemnification without
individual employees facing consequences. §1798.53 creates a parallel path that names
the individual — creating personal stake in the litigation for the employee and their
supervisory chain.
