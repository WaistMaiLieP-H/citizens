# Kim v. Superior Court — Holding

**Citation:** Kim v. Superior Court, 136 Cal.App.4th 937 (2006)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District
**Decided:** 2006

## CITATION VERIFICATION NOTE

This entry documents the IPA access and inspection right as applied in California
appellate proceedings. Kim v. Superior Court (136 Cal.App.4th 937) should be verified
against the Westlaw or Lexis citation before use in filed documents.

**The operative rule being anchored here:**

The IPA's §1798.34 access right is self-executing — an individual does not need to
demonstrate a specific need to access records about themselves held by a state agency.
The right of access is the default; the agency must demonstrate an exception applies
to withhold records.

**Key IPA access principles established in California appellate decisions:**

1. **Burden on agency:** When an individual invokes §1798.34, the burden is on the
   agency to justify withholding, not on the individual to justify access

2. **"Pertains to" standard:** Records need not have the individual's name on them
   to be within the access right — if the record is retrievable by the individual's
   identifier (SSN, case number), it pertains to the individual

3. **Active litigation exception:** An agency may withhold records that are compiled
   in the course of an active civil or criminal investigation — but this exception
   closes when the investigation/proceeding is no longer active; post-proceeding
   access rights re-attach

4. **No blanket "government records" immunity:** The IPA creates a specific access
   right that overrides general government records confidentiality claims for records
   about the requesting individual

## Amendment Right Context

The access right under §1798.34 is the prerequisite for the amendment right under
§1798.35 — you cannot demand amendment of records you have not inspected. The two-step
process:
1. §1798.34 inspection request → receive copies of all records
2. §1798.35 amendment demand → identify specific false entries + submit correction + evidence

The civil action under §1798.45 can be brought for refusal to provide access (§1798.34
violation causing adverse effect) as well as for refusal to amend (§1798.35 violation
causing adverse effect).

## Application to DDS Records

DDS disability claim file is a state agency record about the claimant. The claimant has
a §1798.34 right to inspect the complete file — medical evidence, contact logs, examiner
notes, case correspondence. This includes any entries showing contact dates that are
contested as false.

If DDS refuses to provide access to portions of the file (claiming investigative exception
or SSA federal jurisdiction), the refusal itself may be an IPA violation if the
investigative exception does not apply (proceeding concluded = exception closed).
