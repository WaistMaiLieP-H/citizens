# Historical Chain — 15 U.S.C. §1638 (Truth in Lending Act)

## The Three-Stage Evolution

### Stage 01 — 1968 Origin: TILA Enacted

Congress enacted the Truth in Lending Act in 1968 to create standardized consumer
credit disclosures. The core innovation: the Annual Percentage Rate (APR) as a
standardized cost-of-credit metric that enables comparison shopping. Civil liability
under §1640 provided a private enforcement mechanism.

The limitation: no specific form requirements; no APR tolerance; wave of technical
violation lawsuits for trivial errors.

---

### Stage 02 — 1980 Simplification: Tolerances and Streamlining

The 1980 Truth in Lending Simplification and Reform Act reduced paperwork, established
APR tolerances (1/8 of 1% for regular transactions), created the good-faith reliance
defense for official Board interpretations, and established the "segregated disclosure"
requirement (APR and finance charge in a prominent box).

---

### Stage 03 — 2010 Dodd-Frank: CFPB Takes Over

TILA enforcement authority transferred to the new Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.
Regulation Z re-adopted at 12 C.F.R. Part 1026. CFPB actively targets auto lending
TILA violations including yo-yo financing and spot delivery schemes.

---

## What the History Tells Us

TILA §1638 has been stable in its core requirements since 1968: APR, finance charge,
payment schedule must be accurately disclosed before credit is consummated. Yo-yo
financing violates this stable core by presenting inaccurate terms in the original
contract and then changing them after delivery.
