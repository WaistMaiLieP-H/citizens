# Evolution Stage 03 — 2010 Dodd-Frank: CFPB Jurisdiction

### THE WOUND

The 2008 financial crisis revealed systematic failures in consumer credit disclosure
across mortgage, auto, and other lending markets. The Federal Reserve Board — which had
implemented TILA through Regulation Z — had been criticized for lax enforcement and
for prioritizing bank interests over consumer protection.

The gap: TILA enforcement was split across multiple agencies (Fed, OCC, FTC) with
overlapping and sometimes contradictory jurisdiction. Auto lending in particular had
received less rigorous scrutiny than mortgage lending.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 2010

Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (Pub. L. 111-203, July 21, 2010):

**Transfer to CFPB:** Transferred most TILA rulemaking and enforcement authority
from the Federal Reserve Board to the new Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB).
Regulation Z was re-adopted by the CFPB at 12 C.F.R. Part 1026.

**Enhanced auto lending scrutiny:** Dodd-Frank's requirements for auto lending
disclosure enforcement were strengthened. The CFPB has actively targeted yo-yo financing
and spot delivery schemes as TILA violations.

**CFPB enforcement track:** The CFPB has authority to enforce TILA §1638 through
administrative enforcement actions, civil money penalties, and restitution orders.
For individual consumers, §1640 civil claims remain the primary remedy.

**Current framework:** 12 C.F.R. Part 1026 (CFPB Regulation Z) is the operative
implementing regulation; the CFPB's Official Staff Commentary provides interpretive
guidance on specific disclosure issues including yo-yo financing.

### PROVENANCE

Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act, Pub. L. 111-203
Title X (Consumer Financial Protection Act of 2010)
Effective: CFPB operational July 21, 2011
12 C.F.R. Part 1026 (CFPB Regulation Z) — re-adopted from Federal Reserve Board Regulation Z
