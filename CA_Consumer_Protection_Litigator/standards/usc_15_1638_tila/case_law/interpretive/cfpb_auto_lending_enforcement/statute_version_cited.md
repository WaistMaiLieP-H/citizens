# Statute Version Cited — CFPB Auto Lending Enforcement

**Enforcement period:** 2011-present (post-Dodd-Frank, CFPB operational)
**Rules cited:** 15 U.S.C. §§1638(a), 1640 + 12 C.F.R. Part 1026 (CFPB Regulation Z)

## The Provisions at Issue in Yo-Yo Financing Enforcement

**15 U.S.C. §1638(a)(4) — APR:** The finance charge expressed as an annual percentage rate.
Yo-yo financing violation: original contract disclosed one APR; final financing had a
higher APR; original disclosure was therefore inaccurate.

**15 U.S.C. §1638(a)(3) — Finance charge:** Dollar amount of credit cost. If the
total finance charge changed from original to final contract, the original disclosure
was inaccurate.

**15 U.S.C. §1638(a)(6) — Payment schedule:** Number and amount of scheduled payments.
If monthly payments changed from original to final, the original schedule was inaccurate.

## Current Regulatory Framework

12 C.F.R. §1026.18 (CFPB Regulation Z) specifies the exact form and content requirements
for closed-end credit disclosures (including auto financing). The CFPB Official Staff
Commentary to §1026.18 addresses specific issues including APR accuracy tolerances.

## SOL Context for 2018 Jaguar XE

For the 2018 vehicle purchase, direct TILA civil claims under §1640(e) are time-barred.
The TILA analysis and the CFPB enforcement pattern remain relevant as:
1. Background for the California state law claims (CLRA, Rees-Levering, UCL, Veh §11711)
2. Affirmative defense / recoupment in any collection proceedings on the vehicle debt
3. Supporting evidence that yo-yo financing is a recognized fraud pattern
