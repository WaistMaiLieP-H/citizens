# CFPB Auto Lending Enforcement — Yo-Yo Financing / Spot Delivery Pattern

**Type:** CFPB enforcement history + regulatory guidance (pattern documentation)
**Agency:** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau
**Relevance:** Establishes CFPB's interpretation that spot delivery / yo-yo financing violates TILA

## Summary

The CFPB has specifically addressed spot delivery and yo-yo financing as TILA violations
through supervisory guidance, examination procedures, and enforcement actions.

### CFPB Auto Finance Examination Procedures

The CFPB's Auto Finance Examination Procedures (published in the CFPB Supervision and
Examination Manual) specifically identify spot delivery practices as areas requiring
examination scrutiny. The CFPB examiners are instructed to look for:

1. Whether consumers received required TILA disclosures before taking delivery
2. Whether the terms disclosed in the original contract matched the final finalized
   terms of the credit
3. Whether consumers were pressured to accept different terms after delivery (yo-yo)
4. Whether the APR disclosed in the original contract was accurate or whether it was
   a "placeholder" rate that changed after the dealer arranged financing

### The TILA Violation in Yo-Yo Financing — CFPB Position

The CFPB's official position: when a dealer uses spot delivery and the consumer is
later required to return the vehicle or sign a new contract with different terms, there
is a TILA violation because:

1. The original contract disclosed terms that were never the real terms of the credit
2. The APR in the original contract was inaccurate (the actual APR was higher)
3. The consumer did not receive accurate TILA disclosures before consummating the
   actual credit transaction

### CFPB Supervisory Actions

The CFPB has taken supervisory actions against auto dealers and finance companies that
used spot delivery practices with TILA disclosure failures. Many of these result in
confidential supervisory findings rather than public enforcement orders, but the
CFPB's public supervisory highlights have referenced auto lending TILA violations.

### FTC Enforcement — Parallel Track

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has enforcement authority over TILA compliance for
certain auto dealers. The FTC has brought enforcement actions against dealers for:
- Yo-yo financing (advertising financing terms and then changing them after delivery)
- "Packed" payment schemes (including hidden charges in payment amounts)
- Inaccurate APR disclosures in advertising and contracts

## What This Establishes for Jaguar XE TILA Analysis

**CFPB has confirmed:** Yo-yo financing with inaccurate APR disclosure = TILA violation.
The claim that financing "fell through" and consumer must return vehicle or sign new
contract is a recognized deceptive practice that violates TILA.

**One-year SOL note:** The TILA §1640(e) one-year SOL runs from the date of the
violation. For the 2018 Jaguar XE purchase, direct TILA civil claims are likely time-
barred. However:

1. **Affirmative defense:** TILA violations can be raised as affirmative defenses or
   recoupment in any collection action for the debt, without regard to the SOL (§1640(e))
2. **State law claims:** California's CLRA (3-year SOL), Rees-Levering (4-year), and
   UCL (4-year) claims based on the same facts have longer limitations periods
3. **CFPB complaint:** A consumer complaint to the CFPB (consumerfinance.gov) triggers
   CFPB examination of the dealer/lender, regardless of TILA SOL for civil claims

## Verification Note

CFPB enforcement history and examination procedures are public documents available at
consumerfinance.gov. Individual enforcement orders and supervisory findings should be
verified at the CFPB website before citing in filed documents.
