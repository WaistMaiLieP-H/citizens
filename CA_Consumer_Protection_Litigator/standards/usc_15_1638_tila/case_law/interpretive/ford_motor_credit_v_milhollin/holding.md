# Ford Motor Credit Co. v. Milhollin — Holding

**Citation:** Ford Motor Credit Co. v. Milhollin, 444 U.S. 555 (1980)
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Decided:** January 15, 1980
**Author:** Marshall, J.

## Facts

Milhollin borrowed money from Ford Motor Credit under an installment sales contract.
The contract disclosed certain TILA-required information, but the borrowers claimed
the disclosures were inadequate or inaccurate in certain respects. The dispute involved
the interpretation of what specific disclosures were required under TILA §1638 and
the Federal Reserve Board's Regulation Z.

## Holding

**Courts should defer to the Federal Reserve Board's (now CFPB's) interpretation of
Regulation Z in implementing the Truth in Lending Act — unless the interpretation is
demonstrably irrational or plainly inconsistent with the Act.**

The Court held:
1. Congress delegated to the Federal Reserve Board substantial authority to implement
   TILA through rulemaking and interpretation
2. The Board's staff opinions and official commentary on Regulation Z carry significant
   weight — they are entitled to judicial deference
3. When a creditor relies in good faith on a Board interpretation (official commentary),
   that good faith reliance is relevant to TILA liability analysis
4. TILA's purpose — enabling comparison shopping by providing meaningful disclosure —
   should inform the interpretation of disclosure requirements

## Rules Distilled

1. **Regulation Z deference:** Courts defer to CFPB (formerly Fed) Regulation Z
   interpretations unless plainly inconsistent with TILA
2. **Official commentary governs:** CFPB Official Staff Commentary to Regulation Z
   (12 C.F.R. Part 1026) is an authoritative source for specific disclosure requirements
3. **Good faith reliance:** A creditor who relied on an official Board interpretation
   may have a defense to civil liability even if the interpretation later changes
4. **Purpose guides interpretation:** TILA disclosure requirements must be interpreted
   in light of the purpose — enabling consumers to understand and compare credit costs
5. **APR centrality:** The APR is the most important TILA disclosure — it must
   accurately reflect the cost of credit; inaccuracies beyond the tolerance = violation

## Significance for Jaguar XE TILA Claims

Milhollin establishes that CFPB Regulation Z and Official Staff Commentary govern
the specific requirements for auto financing disclosures. For the Jaguar XE:

- The question of whether the yo-yo financing scheme produced an inaccurate APR is
  governed by 12 C.F.R. §1026.22's APR accuracy standards
- The CFPB's tolerance framework (1/8 of 1%) determines whether the disclosed APR
  was materially inaccurate
- Any official CFPB guidance on spot delivery / yo-yo financing would be entitled to
  deference in the civil TILA claim analysis

**Post-1980:** The deference principle established in Milhollin continued under Dodd-Frank;
the CFPB replaced the Fed but retained the Official Staff Commentary structure.
Milhollin's deference holding was weakened by Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo (2024),
which overruled Chevron deference for agency regulations — but the Official Staff
Commentary to Regulation Z may still be persuasive authority for auto financing disclosures.
