# Song-Beverly Historical Chain — California Civil Code §§1790-1795.8

## The wound (pre-1970)

California auto consumers in the 1960s were subject to manufacturer warranty disclaimers that
gutted the implied warranty of merchantability. The fine print in standard purchase agreements
disclaimed all implied warranties and limited the buyer to the express warranty — which the
manufacturer controlled, interpreted, and repaired through its dealer network. A consumer with a
"lemon" — a vehicle with systematic defects that multiple repairs could not cure — had no remedy
short of litigation under common-law warranty theories that were costly and difficult to prosecute.

---

## 1970: Song-Beverly enacted — no-disclaimer, repair-or-replace, mandatory fees

California Assembly Member Song and State Senator Beverly sponsored the Act that bears their names.
Three core innovations: (1) prohibition on implied warranty disclaimer for consumer goods;
(2) repair-or-replace obligation after a reasonable number of attempts; (3) mandatory attorney
fees for the prevailing buyer (the enforcement mechanism).

---

## 1994: Tanner Consumer Protection Act — presumption added

The §1793.22 Tanner presumption resolved a recurring litigation dispute: how many repair attempts
constitute a "reasonable number"? The Tanner Act answered: 4+ attempts for the same problem, or
30+ cumulative days out of service within 18 months/18,000 miles, raises a rebuttable presumption.
This converted the test from an unguided jury question to a structured evidentiary framework.

---

## 2002: Used vehicle expansion (AB 2331)

Extended Song-Beverly to used vehicles sold with express warranties — covering the certified
pre-owned market. Coverage is limited to the express warranty period, but replacement/refund
rights, the Tanner presumption, and mandatory fees all apply.

---

## 2006-present: Case law develops cumulative defect and willfulness standards

Robertson v. Fleetwood (2006): cumulative defects can constitute a nonconformity.
Brand v. Hyundai (2014): willfulness = knowing non-compliance, evidenced by demand + ignored response.
Jensen v. BMW (2021): civil penalty standards; formal demand before litigation = evidence.

---

## Current framework (2026)

Song-Beverly is the primary California lemon law: no implied warranty disclaimer, 4-attempt/30-day
Tanner presumption, replacement/refund right, up to 2× civil penalty for willful non-compliance,
mandatory attorney fees. New and used (with express warranty) vehicles covered.

**The arc:** UCC disclaimer gap (pre-1970) → no-disclaimer + repair-or-replace (1970) → Tanner
presumption (1994) → used vehicle expansion (2002) → cumulative defect + willfulness case law
(2006-2021) → current stable framework.

**For Michael's cases:** Any auto purchase with a written warranty (new or certified pre-owned)
that had defects repaired 4+ times, or any vehicle that was out of service 30+ days for warranty
repair, carries a Song-Beverly claim with replacement/refund right + potential 2× civil penalty
+ mandatory attorney fees.
