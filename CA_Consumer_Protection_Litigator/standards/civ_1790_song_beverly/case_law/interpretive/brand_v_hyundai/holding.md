# Brand v. Hyundai Motor America — Holding (Civil Penalty Willfulness)

**Citation:** 226 Cal.App.4th 1538 (2014)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District
**Decided:** 2014

## Facts

Buyer brought Song-Beverly claims for repeated repair failures on a new Hyundai. After multiple
repair attempts that failed to cure the defect, the buyer demanded replacement or refund. Hyundai
refused. At trial, the buyer sought the §1794(c) civil penalty of up to 2× actual damages based
on Hyundai's willful failure to comply.

## Holding

**Willfulness standard under §1794(c):**
"Willful" under Song-Beverly means the manufacturer knew of its obligation to replace or refund
under §1793.2(d) and deliberately failed to fulfill it. It does not require bad faith or malice —
only knowing non-compliance.

**Evidence of willfulness:**
1. The manufacturer had knowledge that the repair attempts had not conformed the vehicle to warranty
2. The manufacturer was aware of its replacement/refund obligation under §1793.2(d)
3. The manufacturer chose not to replace or refund despite the failed repair history

A manufacturer that ignores a buyer's formal demand for replacement or refund after the Tanner
presumption has been triggered has the evidence stacked against it on willfulness.

**The civil penalty is up to 2× — not automatic:**
The court has discretion to award up to 2× actual damages, but the award is not mandatory even
if willfulness is shown. Courts consider the degree of willfulness, the manufacturer's response
to the buyer's demands, and the nature of the defects.

## Rule distilled

Willfulness = knowing non-compliance with the replacement/refund obligation. Evidence: number
of repair attempts, formal demands ignored, Tanner presumption triggered, manufacturer's
documented awareness of its statutory obligations.

**Practical implication:** Send a formal Song-Beverly replacement/refund demand after 4 repair
attempts (or 30 days out of service). Keep a paper trail. If the manufacturer ignores it, the
demand letter + repair history is direct evidence of willfulness for the 2× civil penalty.

## Significance for Michael's cases

For the auto fraud cases where warranty repair was attempted and failed:
1. Document the repair attempt history (ROs — repair orders)
2. Send written demand for replacement or refund citing §1793.2(d) and §1793.22
3. Manufacturer's failure to respond = willfulness evidence for the civil penalty

Note: For the RedJag, the fraud (wrong CARFAX, stripped vehicle, yo-yo financing) may mean the
warranty was procured by fraud and the contract is void/voidable under Rees-Levering — in which
case Song-Beverly operates parallel to the Rees-Levering void contract claim.
