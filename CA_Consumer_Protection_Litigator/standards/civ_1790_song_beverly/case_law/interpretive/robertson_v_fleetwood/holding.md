# Robertson v. Fleetwood Travel Trailers — Holding

**Citation:** 144 Cal.App.4th 785 (2006)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, First Appellate District
**Decided:** 2006

## Facts

Purchasers of a new recreational vehicle brought Song-Beverly claims after the manufacturer failed
to repair numerous defects after multiple attempts. The manufacturer argued that various defects
were minor or cosmetic and did not substantially impair the vehicle's use or value.

## Holding

**1. "Nonconformity" standard:** Under §1793.2(d), a nonconformity is a defect or condition that
substantially impairs the use, value, or safety of the vehicle to the buyer. The substantial
impairment test is measured against the buyer's **reasonable expectations** — not a purely
objective test.

**2. Individual defects vs. cumulative defects:** A series of individual defects that each might
not independently constitute a substantial impairment can together constitute a nonconformity.
The buyer does not need to prove that one single defect substantially impaired the vehicle —
the cumulative pattern of defects can satisfy the standard.

**3. Manufacturer's obligation on multiple repair attempts:** Once the Tanner presumption or
the equivalent "reasonable number of attempts" standard is met, the burden shifts — the
manufacturer must offer replacement or refund or face willfulness exposure under §1794(c).

## Rule distilled

Cumulative defects can satisfy Song-Beverly's nonconformity standard even if no single defect
would be sufficient. Substantial impairment is measured against the buyer's reasonable expectations
for the type of product purchased.

## Significance for Michael's auto fraud cases

The RedJag was sold with a wrong CARFAX (undisclosed prior damage), stripped components, and yo-yo
financing — these are not warranty defects per se, but the cumulative character of the fraud means
that the vehicle as delivered did not conform to the representations made about it. To the extent
there were also mechanical/electrical defects that were the subject of repair attempts, Robertson
supports aggregating all defects into a Song-Beverly claim.
