# Song-Beverly — 2002 Used Vehicle Expansion

## The wound this amendment answered

The original 1970 Song-Beverly Act covered only **new** consumer goods. A used vehicle sold with
a remaining factory warranty or a dealer's express warranty was not covered. The gap was significant:
certified pre-owned vehicles with manufacturer warranties were economically treated like new vehicles
by buyers (they paid near-new prices for near-new vehicles with factory backing), but had no Song-Beverly
replacement/refund rights when those vehicles turned out to have systematic defects.

The certified pre-owned market grew dramatically through the 1990s. Buyers were purchasing vehicles
with written warranties and treating those warranties as equivalent to new-vehicle coverage, but had
none of Song-Beverly's repair-or-replace protections if the warranty failed to cover defects properly.

## The logical shift

AB 2331 (2002) extended Song-Beverly to used vehicles sold with express warranties. The coverage is
narrower than new-vehicle coverage:
- **Coverage period:** The duration of the applicable express warranty, not the statutory Tanner period
- **Defect scope:** Only defects covered by the express warranty
- **§1793.22 Tanner presumption:** Applies if within the express warranty period, the same problem
  has been subject to 4+ repair attempts or the vehicle has been out of service for 30+ days
- **Replacement/refund right:** Same as new vehicles — but the "actual price paid" calculation uses
  the used vehicle purchase price

## Operational significance for Michael's auto fraud cases

The Jaguar XE (RedJag) and KiaSoul cases involve vehicles that were sold with written warranties
(express warranties from manufacturer and/or dealer). If either vehicle was sold as a certified
pre-owned vehicle with an express warranty, or if the dealer provided a written warranty at sale,
Song-Beverly coverage applies.

**The key question for each auto fraud case:** Was there a written express warranty at the time of
sale? If yes, Song-Beverly's repair-or-replace right is available regardless of whether the vehicle
was new or used.

Note: The RedJag fraud (yo-yo financing, wrong CARFAX, stripped vehicle) suggests the vehicle was
sold under false pretenses that may independently void the contract. Song-Beverly's warranty claims
operate parallel to and independently of the Rees-Levering contract void/voidability claim.

## Diff from prior stage

| Dimension | Pre-2002 | Post-2002 |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | New consumer goods only | New + used with express warranty |
| Used vehicles without warranty | Not covered | Still not covered (implied warranty only: 30-60 days or "as is") |
| Replacement/refund right | New vehicles | New + used with express warranty |
| Tanner presumption | New vehicles only | Applies to used vehicles within the express warranty period |
