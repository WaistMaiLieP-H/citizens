# Historical Chain — Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §17500 (False Advertising Law)

## Chain Summary

§17500's evolution moves through three shifts: creation of the substantive standard
(1941), judicial expansion and then legislative retraction of standing (1970s-2004),
and case law defining what "misleading" means in practice (2002-2008 and ongoing).

## Stage 1 — Origin 1941 (Stats. 1941, Ch. 807)

**Wound:** No efficient statutory remedy for mass consumer advertising deceptions;
common law fraud's scienter and reliance requirements defeated recovery against
broadcast advertising.

**Response:** §17500 — "untrue or misleading" + "knows or should know" standard;
broad coverage of all commercial speech to the public; primarily an administrative/
criminal enforcement tool at enactment.

**Gap:** Private right of action unclear; internet advertising unaddressed.

## Stage 2 — Private Right Expansion + Prop 64 Reform (1970s-2004)

**Wound (1970s-2004):** Judicial interpretation created unlimited private standing;
"private attorney general" theory allowed suits by parties with no actual harm;
shakedown suit industry emerged; businesses faced extortionate litigation.

**Response:** Prop 64 (Nov. 2004) — §17535 amended to require injury-in-fact + lost
money or property as a result of the violation. Internet amendment (1998) confirmed
online advertising in statutory scope.

**Intermediate:** In re Tobacco II Cases, 46 Cal.4th 298 (2009) clarified reliance
requirements for class representatives post-Prop 64.

**Gap:** "As a result of" causation standard remains contested; reliance requirement
scope unclear for non-class individual actions.

## Stage 3 — "Misleading" Standard Defined (2002-2008)

**Wound:** What does "misleading" mean in practice? Can a technically-true statement
be misleading? Does a back-label disclosure cure a front-label deception? Is corporate
speech about business practices "advertising" subject to §17500?

**Response (Kasky 2002):** Three-part commercial speech test — speaker + audience +
commercial content = §17500 applies; First Amendment not a defense for false commercial speech.

**Response (Williams v. Gerber 2008):** Reasonable consumer test defined — significant
portion of public acting reasonably could be misled; back-label disclosure does not
automatically cure front-label deception; deception by implication actionable.

## Current State

§17500 as amended through 1998 + Prop 64 (2004), interpreted through Kasky (2002) and
Williams v. Gerber (2008):
- Substantive prohibition: untrue or misleading statement + knows or should know
- Scope: all commercial speech to consumers (Kasky three-part test)
- Misleading standard: reasonable consumer; deception by implication; back-label cure doctrine rejected
- Standing: injury in fact + lost money or property as a result of violation
- Remedies: injunction + restitution (private); civil penalties up to $2,500/violation (public enforcement)
- SOL: 4 years (§17208)
- UCL coordination: §17500 violation = per se §17200 unlawful prong violation

## Cases in Chain

1. Kasky v. Nike, Inc., 27 Cal.4th 939 (2002) — commercial speech test
2. Williams v. Gerber Products Co., 552 F.3d 934 (9th Cir. 2008) — reasonable consumer test
3. In re Tobacco II Cases, 46 Cal.4th 298 (2009) — Prop 64 reliance requirement
