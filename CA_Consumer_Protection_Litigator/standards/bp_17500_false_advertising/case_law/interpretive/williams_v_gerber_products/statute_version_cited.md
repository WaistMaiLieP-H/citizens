# Statute Version at Time of Williams v. Gerber Products (2008)

**§17500 version:** Post-1998 internet amendment (Stats. 1998, Ch. 581) and post-Prop 64
(2004). The 2008 Ninth Circuit decision interpreted §17500 and §17200 in their current
amended form (with Prop 64 standing requirements in place). The court was applying the
post-Prop 64 "reasonable consumer" and "likely to deceive" standards.

**§17200 post-Prop 64 text (§17204 standing):**
Plaintiff must have "suffered injury in fact and have lost money or property as a result
of the unfair competition." Williams was a class action; the named plaintiffs had
purchased the Gerber products — the standing element was not contested in this appeal.

**Current operative text:** §17500 and §17200 are unchanged from 2008. Williams v. Gerber
Products' reasonable consumer formulation remains the governing standard in the Ninth Circuit
for California false advertising and UCL claims.
