# In re Tobacco II Cases — Holding

**Citation:** In re Tobacco II Cases, 46 Cal.4th 298 (2009)
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** 2009
**Author:** Moreno, J.

## Facts

Consolidated class action against tobacco manufacturers alleging violations of California
Business and Professions Code §17200 (UCL) arising from decades of advertising and public
statements about the safety and addictiveness of cigarettes. The trial court certified a
class. The question before the California Supreme Court was the effect of Proposition 64
(2004) — which added standing requirements — on the ability of class members who had not
personally been exposed to the specific misrepresentations to maintain the class action.

## Holding

**1. Standing for class representatives post-Prop 64:**
Under Prop 64, the class representative must personally satisfy the standing requirement —
"suffered injury in fact and lost money or property as a result of" the unfair competition.
For §17200 and §17500 claims based on misrepresentation, the class representative must
show actual reliance on the misrepresentations at issue.

**2. Class members — reliance not required individually:**
Once a class representative establishes standing through personal reliance and injury,
absent class members do not each need to show individual reliance. The representative's
standing satisfies the class-wide standing requirement. Individual class members' claims
are governed by the evidence of class-wide deception.

**3. "As a result of" causation — reliance test for misrepresentation claims:**
For UCL/FAL claims grounded in misrepresentation (not all three prongs — the reliance
requirement applies to the fraudulent prong and §17500 claims based on deceptive
statements), the "as a result of" causation element requires the class representative
to show reliance — that they actually read, saw, or were exposed to the specific
misrepresentations and that those misrepresentations were a substantial factor in the
decision to purchase.

**4. Exposure is sufficient; purchase price as harm:**
The class representative does not need to show they would have acted differently in
every detail — it is sufficient to show they were exposed to the misrepresentations and
purchased the product (paying money = "lost money" under Prop 64).

## Rules Distilled

1. Class representative: must personally show reliance on the specific misrepresentations
2. Absent class members: no individual reliance required once representative establishes standing
3. Reliance = exposure to + substantial factor in decision; not "but for" causation
4. Purchase price constitutes "lost money" — economic injury element satisfied by the payment
5. "As a result of" for non-misrepresentation UCL claims (unlawful prong): economic injury
   must be causally connected but reliance not required

## Significance for Individual Auto Fraud Claims

In re Tobacco II's reliance framework applies to individual (non-class) actions:
the named plaintiff must show personal exposure to the misrepresentation + economic loss.

**For Jaguar XE claim:**
- Misrepresentation exposure: dealer presented the wrong CARFAX, stated financing was
  complete, and represented the vehicle as available and saleable
- Reliance: Michael proceeded with the purchase based on those representations
- Lost money: down payment ($10,000 cash) + monthly payments + resulting debt collection

The reliance showing is built into the facts — the dealer's misrepresentations were
presented directly to Michael at the point of sale; he did not merely see an advertisement
but was the direct recipient of the false representations. Direct-recipient reliance is
stronger than the class-wide "general exposure" theory Tobacco II addressed.
