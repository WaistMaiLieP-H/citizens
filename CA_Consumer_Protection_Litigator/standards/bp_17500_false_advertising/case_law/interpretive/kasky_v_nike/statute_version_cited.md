# Statute Version at Time of Kasky v. Nike (2002)

**§17500 version:** Pre-Prop 64 (2002 decision, pre-November 2004 ballot initiative).
At time of Kasky, private standing under §17535 was available to "any person" without
the injury-in-fact and "lost money or property" requirement that Prop 64 added.

**Post-Prop 64 impact on Kasky's standing analysis:**
Kasky (a private plaintiff who purchased Nike products) would likely satisfy post-Prop 64
standing as well — he was a consumer who had "lost money" in the sense that he purchased
Nike products partly based on the labor practice representations. However, the pre-Prop 64
standing analysis in the California Supreme Court's opinion should not be relied upon for
current standing requirements; use the post-Prop 64 §17204/§17535 standing standard.

**Commercial speech holding:** The three-part commercial speech test from Kasky is not
affected by Prop 64 — it interprets the substantive scope of the statute, not standing.
Kasky's commercial speech analysis remains controlling law.

**Current operative text:** §17500's substantive prohibition is unchanged. Kasky's
three-part commercial speech test remains good law for identifying what speech is within
the statute's reach.
