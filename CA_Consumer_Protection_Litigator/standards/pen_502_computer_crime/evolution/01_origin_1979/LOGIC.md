# Evolution Stage 01 — Origin 1979: First California Computer Crime Law

### THE WOUND

In 1979, California was home to the emerging personal computer industry. Unauthorized
access to business mainframes and corporate computer systems was occurring. The law
had no adequate response: criminal trespass and theft statutes were designed for
physical property and did not map cleanly onto unauthorized access to computer systems.

California acted five years before the federal government — the CFAA would not be
enacted until 1984. California's 1979 statute was narrow in scope: it targeted specific
forms of computer fraud and access alteration.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1979

California enacted a narrow computer crime statute focused primarily on:
- Unauthorized access to computer systems
- Alteration or destruction of data in computer systems
- Using computer services without authorization

The criminal penalties were established, but the civil remedy framework was not yet
developed. The 1979 statute served as a placeholder establishing the principle that
unauthorized computer access was illegal.

### PROVENANCE

Stats. 1979, Ch. 858
Cal. Penal Code §502 (original enactment)
