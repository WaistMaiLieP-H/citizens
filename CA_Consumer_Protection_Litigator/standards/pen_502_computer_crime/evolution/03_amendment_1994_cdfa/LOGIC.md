# Evolution Stage 03 — 1994+ Incremental Amendments: Expanding Definitions and Modern Applications

### THE WOUND

The 1988 comprehensive rewrite established a solid framework, but the technology
continued to evolve rapidly through the 1990s and 2000s:
- The internet became the primary medium for computer communication
- Wireless networks (WiFi) created new attack vectors
- Identity theft emerged as a major computer crime category
- Social engineering and phishing attacks circumvented technical access controls

California made incremental amendments through the 1990s and 2000s to keep §502
current with technological developments.

### KEY INCREMENTAL AMENDMENTS

**1994 (Stats. 1994, Ch. 1266):** Expanded §502(c) to add specific provisions targeting
unauthorized access to government computer systems and computer fraud schemes.

**2000s amendments:** Expanded definitions to include internet service providers and
web-based systems; clarified that "computer network" includes internet connections.

**Modern applications documented in case law (not statutory amendment):**
Courts have applied §502 to:
- Unauthorized scraping of website data (hiQ v. LinkedIn; Facebook v. Power Ventures)
- Identity theft via computer account takeover (People v. Childs and related cases)
- SIM swap-enabled unauthorized account access (applying §502(c)(1) and (c)(7))

### CURRENT OPERATIVE VERSION

The current §502 (as amended through 2021) retains the 1988 framework with expanded
definitions and enumerated prohibited acts. The civil remedy provisions have been
clarified to confirm that individuals (not just business owners) can sue under §502(e).

### PROVENANCE

Multiple incremental amendments 1994 through 2021
See Cal. Penal Code §502 (current version) via California Legislative Counsel
