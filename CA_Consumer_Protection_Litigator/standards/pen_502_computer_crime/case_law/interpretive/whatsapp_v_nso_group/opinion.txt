WHATSAPP INC. v. NSO GROUP TECHNOLOGIES LTD.
17 F.4th 930 (9th Cir. 2021)
United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit
Decided: November 8, 2021

---

FACTS:

WhatsApp (owned by Meta) sued NSO Group — the Israeli surveillance technology company that makes Pegasus spyware — for using WhatsApp's infrastructure to install spyware on approximately 1,400 mobile devices of journalists, lawyers, human rights workers, and government officials. NSO Group exploited a zero-day vulnerability in WhatsApp to send malicious code to target devices without the targets' knowledge or consent.

WhatsApp brought claims under the CFAA (18 U.S.C. § 1030), California Penal Code § 502, and for breach of contract (violation of WhatsApp's terms of service).

NSO Group asserted foreign sovereign immunity (arguing it was acting as an agent of foreign governments) and challenged personal jurisdiction.

---

HOLDING — FOREIGN SOVEREIGN IMMUNITY REJECTED:

The Ninth Circuit held that NSO Group — a private corporation — does not qualify for foreign sovereign immunity simply because its product is used by foreign governments. NSO Group was not a foreign state or an instrumentality of a foreign state for purposes of the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA).

A private company that sells surveillance technology to governments is not itself entitled to sovereign immunity. The immunity belongs to the sovereign, not its contractors or suppliers.

"NSO Group's status as a contractor for foreign governments does not transform NSO Group into a foreign sovereign for FSIA purposes." 17 F.4th at 940 (paraphrase).

---

HOLDING — CFAA AND § 502 STANDING:

WhatsApp, as the operator of the computer infrastructure through which the attacks were routed, had standing to bring CFAA and § 502 claims for damage to its systems, even though the ultimate victims of the spyware were WhatsApp users on their own devices.

The court confirmed that § 502's civil remedy provision (§ 502(e)) is available to any person or entity injured by a violation — including a system operator whose infrastructure is used as a conduit for unauthorized access attacks against its users.

---

IMPORTANCE FOR SURVEILLANCE AND SPYWARE CASES:

WhatsApp v. NSO Group establishes critical principles for cases involving commercial spyware and surveillance technology:

1. COMMERCIAL SPYWARE IS NOT IMMUNE: Companies that develop and sell surveillance tools are not shielded from civil liability by the fact that their customers are governments or law enforcement.

2. ZERO-DAY EXPLOITATION = § 502 VIOLATION: Using software vulnerabilities to access devices without authorization is "without permission" under § 502, regardless of technical sophistication.

3. INFRASTRUCTURE VICTIMS HAVE STANDING: The operator of a communication platform through which attacks are routed has § 502 standing even if the targeted data resided on user devices.

4. TERMS OF SERVICE ENFORCEMENT: NSO Group violated WhatsApp's terms of service by creating accounts and using WhatsApp's servers in violation of those terms — independently supporting the § 502 claim.

---

APPLICATION TO SIM SWAP / PHONE SURVEILLANCE:

For cases involving commercial-grade surveillance tools used against private individuals:
1. If spyware (commercial or custom) was installed on a victim's device without consent, each installation and each data extraction is a § 502 violation;
2. The person or entity who deployed the spyware — whether a state actor, private investigator, or individual — is liable under § 502 if they were not authorized to access the device;
3. NSO Group's loss of sovereign immunity argument illustrates that private parties cannot hide behind government contracts when committing unauthorized computer access.

FOR SIM SWAP ATTACKS: The phone carrier and the attacker both potentially violated § 502 — the carrier by facilitating unauthorized access through the SIM swap, the attacker by using that access to enter the victim's accounts.
