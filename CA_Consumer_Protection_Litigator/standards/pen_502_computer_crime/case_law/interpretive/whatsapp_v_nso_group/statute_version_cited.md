# Statute Version Cited — WhatsApp v. NSO Group

**Case filed:** 2019; 9th Circuit jurisdictional ruling: 2021
**Statute version applicable:** Cal. Penal Code §502(c)(1), §502(c)(7), §502(b)(12) (current version)

## §502 Provisions Asserted

The complaint asserted violation of §502(c)(1) (fraud scheme via unauthorized access)
and §502(c)(7) (simple unauthorized access). Both operate in parallel with the CFAA claims.

## §502(b)(12) — "Without Permission"

The operative definition: access that has not been "explicitly authorized by the owner,
lessee, or authorized user." WhatsApp never authorized NSO Group's access to its servers
for the purpose of delivering malware. The permission that existed (normal operation of
WhatsApp's servers) did not include NSO's exploitation access.

## Parallel with CFAA

The complaint pleads §502 and CFAA simultaneously. This is standard practice in
California federal court civil computer crime litigation:
- §502 provides state law coverage without the CFAA's $5,000 loss threshold
- CFAA provides federal jurisdiction and the $5,000+ civil remedy path
- Both claims proceed on the same facts
