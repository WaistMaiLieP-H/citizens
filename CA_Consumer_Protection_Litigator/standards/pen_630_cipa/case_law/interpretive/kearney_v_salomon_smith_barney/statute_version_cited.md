# Statute Version Cited — Kearney v. Salomon Smith Barney

**Case decided:** 2006
**Statute version applicable:** Cal. Penal Code §632 (post-1992; §632.7 was available but §632 was the provision applied to the brokerage call recordings)

## The Core Provision Applied

§632's all-party consent requirement for confidential communications was the operative
standard. The brokerage recorded telephone calls (which are confidential communications
under Flanagan) without the California plaintiffs' consent.

## §632.7 Availability But Not Applied

The 1992 amendment adding §632.7 (cellular/cordless, no confidentiality required) was
in effect at the time of Kearney but was not the provision analyzed — the calls at
issue were likely landline or mixed calls, so §632 governed.

## Current Application

For SIM swap cellular claims (§632.7), the confidentiality requirement is not needed.
Kearney's extraterritoriality holding applies equally to §632.7 claims — if the victim
is a California resident, California's CIPA protections apply regardless of where the
interceptor was located.
