# Evolution Stage 01 — Origin 1967: California Invasion of Privacy Act

### THE WOUND

By 1967, wiretapping and surveillance technology had advanced well beyond the
regulatory framework that existed. Private detectives recorded spouse's phone calls
in divorce proceedings. Businesses wiretapped competitor communications. Law
enforcement conducted surveillance without the restrictions that would later come
from federal legislation (the federal Wiretap Act / Title III was not enacted until 1968).

California's Legislature found the existing law inadequate. The state had a
constitutional right of privacy (Cal. Const. Art. I §1 had been interpreted to include
privacy interests), but no criminal statute clearly prohibited private eavesdropping
with modern technology.

The wound was consent asymmetry: one party to a conversation could record or enable
recording of the conversation without the other party's knowledge, and this was
effectively legal. California's Legislature viewed this as a fundamental violation of
personal liberty.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1967

California enacted Chapter 1.5 of the Penal Code (§§630 et seq.) — the Invasion of
Privacy Act. Key design choices:

**All-party consent:** Unlike what would become the federal standard (one-party consent),
California required consent of ALL parties to a confidential communication. This
reflected a stronger privacy-protective stance than federal law would take.

**Criminal penalties:** CIPA violations were criminal from the beginning — not merely
civil. This reflected the Legislature's view that wiretapping was a serious offense,
not merely a civil wrong.

**Civil remedy:** §637.2 created a private right of action with a minimum damages floor
($5,000 per violation in current form; the original amount was lower). This allowed
individuals to sue without proving actual damages.

**Confidential communication requirement for §632:** The statute's main recording
prohibition required the communication to be "confidential" — defined by reasonable
expectation of privacy, not the actual content. This test is fact-specific and favors
plaintiffs in most telephonic communication contexts.

### LOGICAL DELTA — PRE-CIPA vs. POST-CIPA (1967)

| Element | Pre-1967 | Post-1967 (CIPA) |
|---------|----------|------------------|
| Wiretapping by private actors | Not specifically prohibited | §631 criminal prohibition |
| Recording conversations | Common law tort only (uncertain) | §632 criminal prohibition + civil remedy |
| Consent requirement | None (beyond common law) | All-party consent for confidential communications |
| Civil remedy | Common law damages (uncertain) | §637.2 statutory minimum damages |
| Federal vs. state | Federal law not yet enacted | California more protective than federal |

### PROVENANCE

Stats. 1967, Ch. 1509
Cal. Penal Code §§630 et seq. (original enactment)
