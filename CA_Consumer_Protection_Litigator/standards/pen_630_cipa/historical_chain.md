# Historical Chain — Cal. Penal Code §§630-638 (CIPA)

## The Four-Stage Evolution

### Stage 01 — 1967 Origin: California Invasion of Privacy Act

California enacted the Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA) in response to widespread
wiretapping by private actors — investigators, spouses in divorce proceedings, and
businesses. The Legislature made a critical choice that distinguished California from
what would become federal law: all-party consent was required, not merely one-party
consent.

§631 (wire interception), §632 (recording of confidential communications), and §637.2
(civil remedy) formed the original framework. The "confidential communication" definition
(§632(c)) used an objective reasonable-expectation-of-circumstances test.

**Key departure from federal law:** Federal Wiretap Act (Title III, 1968) adopted
one-party consent. California was more protective from the beginning.

---

### Stage 02 — 1985 Amendment: Wireless Begins

Cordless phones and early cellular phones required statutory extension. §§632.5 and
632.6 were added to cover cordless and cellular interception, respectively. Both
retained the "confidential communication" requirement.

The gap identified: the confidentiality requirement created a potential defense for
cellular interception (radio waves traveling through open airspace vs. protected wire).

---

### Stage 03 — 1992 Amendment: §632.7 and the Confidentiality Gap Closed

§632.7 eliminated the confidentiality requirement for cellular/cordless interceptions.
Any interception of a cellular or cordless call without all-party consent is prohibited.
This is the single most important amendment for modern SIM swap claims.

**The significance:** A defendant cannot argue that cellular communications intercepted
through a SIM swap lacked a reasonable expectation of privacy because they traveled
over radio waves. §632.7 prohibits the interception categorically, without any
confidentiality analysis.

---

### Stage 04 — 2016 Amendment: Modern Cellular Architecture

§632.7 was updated to reflect modern cellular network architecture — ensuring coverage
of communications transmitted to or from cellular devices in hybrid network environments.
This is the current operative version.

---

## What the History Tells Us

CIPA has been progressively expanded at each technological inflection point:
1. Wired telephony (1967)
2. Early wireless (1985)
3. Cellular standardization (1992) — the most important amendment for modern claims
4. Modern cellular architecture (2016)

Each amendment maintained California's core position: stronger privacy protection than
federal law; all-party consent; private right of action with minimum damages floor.

## The Federal-State Stack

CIPA and the federal Wiretap Act (18 U.S.C. §§2510-2522) run simultaneously. They
are not mutually exclusive. A single SIM swap interception event triggers:
- Federal §2511 liability ($10,000 minimum per violation) + attorneys' fees (mandatory)
- California §632.7/§631 liability ($5,000 minimum per violation)

The California claim is governed by California's stricter all-party consent rule.
Kearney (2006) established that California's rule applies to protect California
residents even when the interceptor is outside California.

## Key Cases in the Evolution

| Case | Year | Contribution |
