# §§1709-1711 Origin — 1872 Civil Code Codification

## The wound this statute answered

Pre-1872 California courts applied English common-law deceit doctrine, which required formal proof
of scienter (knowledge of falsity) and left uncertain the exact definition of actionable fraud.
The 1872 Civil Code codification project (led by David Dudley Field, who had also drafted the
New York Civil Code) sought to reduce common-law uncertainty by stating the fraud doctrine
in statutory terms.

The 1872 codification did not create new law — it organized and codified existing common-law
principles into a definitive statutory framework that California courts could apply consistently.

## The logical structure the 1872 codifiers chose

**§1709:** The basic rule — willful deceit with intent to induce detrimental reliance = damages.
Three requirements locked in: (1) willful deceit, (2) intent to induce action, (3) actual damages.

**§1710:** The definition of deceit — four modes:
1. Knowing false statement (classic fraud)
2. Reckless assertion without knowledge
3. Suppression of material fact (the nondisclosure prong, which required later case law to define the "duty to disclose")
4. Promissory fraud (promise without intent to perform)

**§1711:** The specific application to contract-inducing fraud — extending liability to anyone
who "suggests a falsehood or suppresses a truth" to induce consent to a property transaction.

## What the 1872 codification did NOT resolve

The 1872 text left the "duty to disclose" under §1710(3) undefined. What facts is a party "bound
to disclose"? The common law answer depended on the relationship and circumstances. California
courts developed the duty-to-disclose doctrine through case law over the next 150 years:
- Fiduciary relationships: always a duty
- Arm's-length transactions: duty when one party has superior knowledge of latent defects
- Partial disclosure: duty to correct misleading half-truths
- Active concealment: always a duty not to affirmatively hide material facts

The 1872 text created the framework; the courts filled in the duty-to-disclose content.
