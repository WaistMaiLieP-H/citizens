# Roberts v. Ball, Hunt, Hart, Brown & Baerwitz — Holding

**Citation:** 57 Cal.App.3d 104 (1976)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District
**Decided:** 1976

## Facts

Plaintiffs sued lawyers (Ball, Hunt, Hart, Brown & Baerwitz) for negligent misrepresentation in
connection with a real estate transaction. The plaintiffs alleged they relied on the defendants'
representations about the financial condition of a property investment, which turned out to be false.
The question was whether §1710(2) (positive assertion without knowledge of truth) required the
same level of scienter as §1710(1) (knowing false statement).

## Holding

**§1710(2) — negligent misrepresentation requires:**
1. A positive assertion of a material fact
2. Without reasonable grounds for believing it to be true
3. With intent that the plaintiff rely on it
4. Plaintiff's justifiable reliance
5. Resulting damage

**Scienter distinction between §1710(1) and §1710(2):**
- §1710(1): Defendant knows the statement is false
- §1710(2): Defendant makes a positive assertion without reasonable grounds — knowledge of falsity
  is not required, only objective unreasonableness of making the assertion

**This is important for cases where the dealer/seller may not have "known" a specific fact was
false but made representations without any factual basis:** A dealer who asserts a vehicle has
"no prior damage" without having checked the vehicle's history, or without reasonable grounds for
the assertion, is liable under §1710(2) even if it did not know the representation was false.

## Rule distilled

§1710(2) creates liability for negligent misrepresentation — asserting facts without reasonable
grounds. It fills the gap between intentional fraud (§1710(1)) and mere opinion (not actionable).
Applies to professional and commercial contexts where representations are made with the expectation
of reliance.

## Significance for Michael's auto cases

For dealers who provided false CARFAX reports, made representations about vehicle condition, or
stated financing terms without reasonable grounds:
- If the dealer knew the representation was false: §1710(1) (intentional)
- If the dealer had no reasonable basis for the representation: §1710(2) (negligent)
- Either way: §1709 liability for resulting damages

The §1710(2) theory is available even if the dealer can claim it "didn't know" the CARFAX
was wrong — if it had no reasonable basis for asserting the vehicle's clean history, §1710(2)
covers the negligent assertion.
