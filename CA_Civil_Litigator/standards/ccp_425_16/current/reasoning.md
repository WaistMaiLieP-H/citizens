# Reasoning — Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 425.16 (Anti-SLAPP)

**Standard ID:** CCP_425_16_ANTI_SLAPP
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why This Standard Exists

The California Legislature enacted § 425.16 in 1992 in direct response to a documented pattern: powerful defendants (developers, corporations, government officials) weaponizing litigation to silence critics. A citizen would speak at a planning hearing, testify against a permit, or write a letter of complaint — and would receive a lawsuit demanding damages. The lawsuit was never meant to win; it was meant to drain the speaker dry. This tactic — the Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation — worked because most individuals cannot afford to defend a civil lawsuit to verdict.

The Legislature's stated finding: "there has been a disturbing increase in lawsuits brought primarily to chill the valid exercise of the constitutional rights of freedom of speech and petition for the redress of grievances." The word "disturbing" is in the statute (§ 425.16(a)) — strong language for a code provision.

The core design choice was fee-shifting combined with early dismissal. Neither alone was sufficient. Early dismissal without fee-shifting would merely accelerate the case, leaving defendants still exposed to litigation costs. Fee-shifting without early dismissal would require defendants to survive the full case before recovering — too late for many.

---

## The Two-Step Architecture

**Step One: Defendant's burden.** The defendant must show the plaintiff's claim arises from protected activity — an act in furtherance of the right of petition or free speech in connection with a public issue. This is deliberately broad. "Protected activity" under § 425.16(e) includes:
- Written or oral statements before a legislative, executive, judicial, or other official proceeding;
- Written or oral statements made in connection with an issue under consideration by a government body;
- Any written or oral statement in a place open to the public or a public forum on an issue of public interest;
- Any other conduct in furtherance of the exercise of these rights.

The broadness is intentional: the statute is to be "construed broadly." (§ 425.16(a).)

**Step Two: Plaintiff's burden.** If defendant meets Step One, the burden shifts to plaintiff to demonstrate a probability of prevailing on the claim — i.e., that the claim is legally sufficient and supported by a sufficient prima facie showing of facts to sustain a favorable judgment. This is a summary-judgment-like showing made at the pleading stage, before any discovery.

The asymmetry is critical: defendant must show the claim *arises from* protected activity; plaintiff must show *probability of prevailing*. Showing the claim arises from protected activity does not mean the defendant wins — it means plaintiff must now show a real case exists.

---

## Discovery Stay

Once an anti-SLAPP motion is filed, all discovery is stayed unless the court orders otherwise upon a noticed motion showing good cause. This stay is a core feature, not a procedural accident. Without it, the plaintiff could use discovery to impose the very cost burden the statute was designed to prevent. The stay makes the motion function as an early gatekeeping mechanism.

---

## Attorney Fees — The Enforcement Mechanism

Mandatory fees upon prevailing on the motion are the statute's teeth. Under § 425.16(c)(1), a prevailing defendant "shall be entitled to recover" attorney fees and costs. The word "shall" is mandatory — a court has no discretion to deny fees to a prevailing defendant.

The fee award serves two purposes: (1) compensating defendants who were forced to defend protected activity; (2) deterring future SLAPP suits by making them economically irrational for plaintiffs who lose.

Conversely, a plaintiff who prevails on an anti-SLAPP motion brought frivolously or solely to delay may recover fees against the moving defendant. (§ 425.16(c)(1), second sentence.)

---

## Why the 60-Day Window Matters

The motion must be filed within 60 days of service of the complaint (or later at court discretion). This creates urgency — defendants must identify the anti-SLAPP issue early and move promptly. The timing reflects the legislative intent: anti-SLAPP protection should operate as an early exit from litigation, not a late-stage defense.

---

## Historical Loss Without This Standard

Before § 425.16, speakers had no mechanism to exit meritless litigation early. A defendant forced to litigate a SLAPP to verdict — even if they won — had already suffered the intended consequence: years of legal costs, distraction, and chilling effect. The statute was a direct legislative acknowledgment that the litigation process itself was being weaponized, independent of outcome.

---

## Application in Practice

Anti-SLAPP motions are among the most heavily litigated in California civil practice. Courts apply the standard in:
- Defamation claims arising from statements in news coverage or public meetings;
- Malicious prosecution claims following prior litigation;
- Interference claims arising from petitioning activity (filing complaints, testifying);
- Claims against public interest advocates or nonprofit watchdogs;
- Claims against individuals who reported misconduct to government agencies.

The standard interacts directly with the Government Claims Act (§ 810 et seq.) when the protected activity involves government proceedings.
