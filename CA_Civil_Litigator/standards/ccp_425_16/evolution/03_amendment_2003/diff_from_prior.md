# CCP 425.16 -- Diff from 02_amendment_1997

## Prior State (Post-1997)

The anti-SLAPP statute applied broadly to any claim arising from protected activity, defined as speech or conduct in connection with a public issue or issue of public interest. There were no exemptions for commercial speech, public interest litigation, or class actions. There was no penalty for filing a frivolous anti-SLAPP motion beyond the existing fee-shifting provision in 425.16(c).

## What Changed

**New statute: CCP 425.17 -- Exemptions from anti-SLAPP.**

Three exemptions carved out:

1. **(b) Public interest exemption.** Anti-SLAPP does not apply to actions brought solely in the public interest or on behalf of the general public, where the plaintiff does not seek greater relief than defendant's wrongful gains, and the action enforces an important right affecting the public interest. This stopped polluters and civil rights violators from using anti-SLAPP against the people suing them.

2. **(c) Commercial speech exemption.** Anti-SLAPP does not apply to claims against businesses arising from their commercial statements or conduct (selling goods/services to buyers/customers), as long as the plaintiff is not a competitor and the statements were made to actual or prospective customers. This stopped companies from claiming their advertising was protected speech.

3. **(d) Government enforcement exemption.** Anti-SLAPP does not apply to enforcement actions by the Attorney General, district attorney, city attorney, or similar officers. This clarified and expanded existing protections for government enforcement.

**New statute: CCP 425.18 -- SLAPPback.**

Created a freestanding cause of action: if you were the target of a frivolous anti-SLAPP motion (one filed without probable cause and for an improper purpose), you could bring a SLAPPback action for damages, including attorney's fees. The SLAPPback action itself was exempt from anti-SLAPP motions.

## What Did Not Change

- The core anti-SLAPP statute (425.16) was not substantively amended
- The two-step test remained identical
- The definition of protected activity under (e)(1)-(4) remained identical
- The discovery stay, fee-shifting, and hearing timeline remained identical
- The "shall be construed broadly" directive remained in place

## Net Effect

The statute's reach was preserved but its abuse potential was reduced. Legitimate claims against commercial actors, public interest enforcement actions, and class actions could proceed without being derailed by anti-SLAPP motions. Meanwhile, the anti-SLAPP protection for citizens speaking on public issues remained fully intact.
