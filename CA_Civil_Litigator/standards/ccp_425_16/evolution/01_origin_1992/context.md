# CCP 425.16 -- Origin Context

## Enactment

Stats. 1992, Ch. 726 (S.B. 1264). Added to the Code of Civil Procedure as Section 425.16. Effective January 1, 1993.

## The Wound

Powerful parties -- corporations, developers, government officials, wealthy individuals -- discovered they could silence critics not by winning lawsuits but by filing them. The lawsuit itself was the weapon. The goal was not to prevail on the merits but to impose the cost of defense on someone who had spoken out, petitioned the government, or participated in a public proceeding.

These became known as SLAPP suits -- Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation. A neighborhood activist who spoke at a city council meeting would get sued for defamation. A consumer who posted a negative review would get sued for trade libel. A journalist who investigated a company would face a fraud claim. The claims were meritless, but the cost of defending them was real. The message was clear: speak up, and we will bury you in legal fees.

The chilling effect was documented and measurable. People stopped showing up to public hearings. Journalists dropped investigations. Community groups dissolved rather than face litigation. The constitutional rights of free speech and petition were being nullified not by government censorship but by private litigation abuse.

## The Promise

If you are sued for exercising your right of free speech or petition on a public issue, you can file a special motion to strike the claim early in the case. The court must hear the motion quickly (within 30 days of service). All discovery is stayed while the motion is pending. The test is two steps:

1. The defendant shows the claim arises from protected activity (speech, petition, or conduct in furtherance of those rights on a public issue)
2. The burden shifts to the plaintiff to demonstrate a probability of prevailing on the claim

If the defendant wins the motion, the plaintiff's claim is stricken and the defendant recovers attorney's fees and costs. If the motion is frivolous, the plaintiff recovers fees.

The statute says it "shall be construed broadly." The Legislature meant it.

## Why This Statute Exists

The First Amendment protects speech from government restriction. But it does not protect speech from private litigation designed to punish the speaker. CCP 425.16 fills that gap. It creates a procedural mechanism to identify and dispose of meritless claims targeting protected speech before the litigation costs accomplish the plaintiff's real objective.

The anti-SLAPP statute is one of the most frequently litigated provisions in California civil procedure. It has been amended many times since 1992, each time expanding its reach or clarifying its application.
