# CCP 425.16 -- Diff from 01_origin_1992

## Prior State (1992 Original)

Protected activity was defined generally as acts "in furtherance of the person's right of petition or free speech under the United States or California Constitution in connection with a public issue." The statute listed two specific categories:

- (e)(1): written or oral statements made before a legislative, executive, or judicial proceeding, or any other official proceeding authorized by law
- (e)(2): written or oral statements made in connection with an issue under consideration or review by a legislative, executive, or judicial body, or any other official proceeding authorized by law

Both categories were tied to government proceedings. If the speech did not connect to a formal government body, it was arguably outside the statute.

## What Changed

Two new subdivisions added:

- **(e)(3) added**: "any written or oral statement or writing made in a place open to the public or a public forum in connection with an issue of public interest"
- **(e)(4) added**: "any other conduct in furtherance of the exercise of the constitutional right of petition or the constitutional right of free speech in connection with a public issue or an issue of public interest"

**"Shall be construed broadly" directive added** to subdivision (a).

## What This Means in Practice

Before: A defendant had to show their speech was connected to a government proceeding.
After: A defendant only had to show their speech or conduct related to a public issue or issue of public interest, regardless of whether any government body was involved.

Before: Courts could (and did) read the statute narrowly.
After: The Legislature expressly directed broad construction.

## What Did Not Change

- The two-step test remained the same
- The 30-day hearing timeline remained the same
- The discovery stay remained the same
- The fee-shifting provisions remained the same
- Subdivisions (e)(1) and (e)(2) remained unchanged
