## The Arc

The anti-SLAPP statute follows a clear legislative arc:

1. **1992 -- Creation.** The Legislature identified a real harm: meritless lawsuits used to punish people for speaking on public issues. It created a fast, cost-shifting procedural mechanism to kill those suits early.

2. **1997 -- Expansion.** Courts read the statute too narrowly. The Legislature broadened it to cover all public-issue speech and conduct, not just government petitions.

3. **2003 -- Correction.** The broad statute was being weaponized by commercial defendants against legitimate claims. The Legislature carved out exemptions and created a penalty for frivolous use.

4. **Current.** The statute remains one of the strongest anti-SLAPP protections in the country. The core two-step test is unchanged since 1992. The definition of protected activity is unchanged since 1997. The exemptions are unchanged since 2003. The framework is stable.

## Additional Amendments Not Separately Staged

Several additional amendments have been made between 2003 and the present that refined procedural details without changing the statute's fundamental structure:

- Filing deadlines adjusted (60-day window to file the motion)
- Immediate appealability of anti-SLAPP rulings codified in subdivision (i)
- Judicial Council reporting requirements added
- Public-entity enforcement exemptions clarified
- Discovery stay provisions refined

These are documented in the current text but do not represent structural changes to the statute's operation.
