# CCP §425.16 — Historical Chain (Anti-SLAPP)

---

### I. The Wound

By the early 1990s, a pattern had emerged: powerful parties — developers, corporations, public officials — were filing meritless lawsuits against individuals and organizations who spoke out on public issues. A citizen who testified at a city council hearing about a development project would be sued for defamation. A community group that circulated a petition would be sued for tortious interference. The suits weren't designed to win — they were designed to silence. The cost of defending a lawsuit, even a frivolous one, was enough to deter most people from exercising their First Amendment rights.

These suits were called SLAPPs — Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation.

### II. The Promise (1992)

CCP §425.16 was enacted in 1992. The promise: **If you are sued because you exercised your right of petition or free speech on a public issue, you can bring a special motion to strike the case early — and if you win, the plaintiff pays your attorney's fees.**

The two-step analysis:
1. **Defendant's burden (Step 1):** Show the claim arises from activity protected under the right of petition or free speech in connection with a public issue.
2. **Plaintiff's burden (Step 2):** If Step 1 is met, the plaintiff must demonstrate a "probability of prevailing" on the claim. If the plaintiff cannot, the case is struck.

### III. Key Features

- **Discovery stay.** All discovery is stayed upon filing an anti-SLAPP motion. This prevents the SLAPP plaintiff from using the discovery process itself as a weapon — depositions, document demands, interrogatories all stop until the motion is resolved.
- **Mandatory fees.** A prevailing defendant gets fees. A prevailing plaintiff gets fees only if the anti-SLAPP motion was frivolous.
- **Interlocutory appeal.** An order granting or denying the motion is immediately appealable under §904.1.
- **Broad construction.** The statute mandates broad construction: "this section shall be construed broadly."

### IV. The Sword and the Shield

The anti-SLAPP statute is both a shield (protecting speakers from meritless suits) and, controversially, a sword. Defendants in legitimate cases sometimes file anti-SLAPP motions to delay proceedings, stay discovery, and force plaintiffs to prove their case at the pleading stage — before any discovery has occurred.

This tension is the statute's central doctrinal challenge: distinguishing genuine SLAPPs (meritless suits designed to silence speech) from legitimate claims that happen to arise from speech activity.

### V. Why This Statute Matters

For the steward's work, the anti-SLAPP statute cuts both ways:
- **Defensive:** If the steward is sued for public advocacy, petitioning government, or speaking out about corruption, §425.16 provides an early strike mechanism with mandatory fee recovery.
- **Offensive risk:** If the steward sues a defendant who engaged in protected speech activity (e.g., filing government reports, making statements in official proceedings), the defendant may bring an anti-SLAPP motion.

Understanding which claims arise from protected activity and which do not is critical to any California civil rights litigation strategy.
