ANTI-SLAPP — COMMERCIAL SPEECH AND "PUBLIC ISSUE" REQUIREMENT:

The § 425.16(e)(3)-(4) protected activity categories require that the statement be "in connection with a public issue or an issue of public interest." California courts have grappled with when commercial activity qualifies as a matter of public interest for anti-SLAPP purposes.

THE OVERARCHING PRINCIPLE (from Isaac v. Lamar Advertising and related cases):
A statement is not automatically a matter of public interest simply because it relates to a business or because the public finds it interesting. A business's services are a matter of public interest when:
1. The business has inserted itself into a public controversy, OR
2. The subject matter of the statement is a topic of ongoing public debate beyond the specific parties, OR
3. The statement addresses a matter of significant concern to a substantial number of people

COMMERCIAL CRITICISM AND THE PUBLIC ISSUE TEST:
When one company publishes reports rating or evaluating other companies' services — and those evaluations are shared publicly — the reporting function may constitute speech on a matter of public interest (commercial integrity, advertising accuracy) qualifying under § 425.16(e)(3)-(4).

THE FILMON-TYPE HOLDING (general):
In ad tech / digital advertising cases, where a third-party verification company publishes reports about a platform's ad inventory quality, courts have analyzed whether the reports address matters of public interest (fraudulent advertising inventory is a matter of public concern) or are merely commercial business-to-business communications.

FOR ANTI-SLAPP PUBLIC ISSUE ANALYSIS:
The "public issue" requirement is satisfied for:
- Government accountability claims — clearly public interest
- Constitutional rights claims — public interest
- Law enforcement practices — public interest
- Business fraud affecting consumers — public interest (post-FilAmon analysis of commercial speech)

The "public issue" requirement is NOT satisfied for:
- Private commercial disputes between two businesses
- Internal personnel matters
- Private financial transactions
