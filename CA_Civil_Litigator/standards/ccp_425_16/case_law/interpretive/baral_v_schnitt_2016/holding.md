# Baral v. Schnitt, 1 Cal.5th 376 (2016)

## The Case

Schnitt sued Baral for breach of fiduciary duty. The complaint alleged both protected activity (statements and communications) and unprotected activity (non-communicative conduct). Baral filed an anti-SLAPP motion under §425.16 to strike the entire complaint. The question was how courts handle a "mixed" cause of action — one that rests on both protected and unprotected conduct.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **Anti-SLAPP applies to mixed causes of action, but only to the protected-activity allegations within them.** A court does not have to choose between striking the entire cause of action or denying the motion entirely. It can surgically strike specific allegations that arise from protected activity while leaving the rest intact.

2. **At step one, the court identifies which specific allegations arise from protected activity.** Not every allegation in a cause of action counts — only those that describe conduct falling within §425.16(e)'s definition of protected activity (speech/petition in a public forum, in connection with a public issue, etc.).

3. **At step two, for the allegations that do arise from protected activity, the plaintiff must show a probability of prevailing on those specific claims.** If the plaintiff cannot, those allegations get struck. The remaining allegations — the ones based on unprotected conduct — survive untouched.

## The Rule

**When a cause of action mixes protected and unprotected activity, the court must identify and analyze only the allegations arising from protected activity, then strike those specific allegations if the plaintiff cannot show a probability of prevailing on them. The unprotected allegations survive regardless.**

## Why This Case Matters for §425.16

Before Baral, courts were split on how to handle mixed claims. Some struck entire causes of action; some denied motions entirely if any unprotected conduct was alleged. Baral resolved the split by requiring claim-by-claim, allegation-by-allegation analysis. This is now the mandatory approach statewide.
