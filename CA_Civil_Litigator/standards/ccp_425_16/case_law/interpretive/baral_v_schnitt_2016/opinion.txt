BARAL v. SCHNITT
1 Cal.5th 376 (2016)
California Supreme Court
Decided: August 1, 2016

---

FACTS:

Andrew Baral filed a lawsuit against Carol Schnitt that contained both claims arising from protected anti-SLAPP activity and claims that did not arise from protected activity. Schnitt filed an anti-SLAPP motion. The question was whether the anti-SLAPP motion could target only the protected-activity-based claims, or whether a mixed complaint either survived or fell entirely.

---

HOLDING:

Justice Kruger wrote for the Court.

ANTI-SLAPP CAN TARGET INDIVIDUAL CLAIMS OR ALLEGATIONS WITHIN A COMPLAINT:
Prior cases had divided on whether § 425.16 applied to an entire action (meaning mixed complaints were either saved or dismissed entirely) or could target discrete claims.

Baral resolved: "The anti-SLAPP statute may be used to strike only those claims or allegations in a complaint that arise from protected activity. The statute does not require striking an entire complaint where only some claims arise from protected activity."

THE CLAIM-BY-CLAIM ANALYSIS:
1. Identify which specific claims or allegations arise from protected activity
2. Apply the two-step § 425.16 analysis to only those specific claims/allegations
3. Claims that do not arise from protected activity are not subject to anti-SLAPP and proceed normally
4. If protected-activity claims are struck, only those claims are dismissed; the remainder proceed

THE GRAVAMEN TEST:
The "gravamen" of each claim determines whether it arises from protected activity. A claim whose central wrong is protected speech or petitioning activity is subject to anti-SLAPP. A claim whose central wrong is non-protected conduct (even if some protected activity is incidentally mentioned) is not.

"[I]n assessing the gravamen of a claim, courts must identify the specific acts that are alleged to constitute the defendant's wrongdoing. If those acts include both protected and unprotected conduct, the anti-SLAPP motion reaches only the claims arising from the protected acts."

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Baral's claim-by-claim analysis has multiple implications:

1. **Plaintiff's advantage**: Even if one or more of the steward's claims could be anti-SLAPP'd (because they arise from another party's protected petitioning activity), the remaining non-protected claims proceed. Anti-SLAPP cannot knock out the entire case unless every claim arises from protected activity.

2. **Strategic pleading**: Organize claims so that the gravamen of each claim is the non-protected wrongdoing (the false reports, the physical conduct, the financial fraud) — not the protected activity (filing court documents). The court looks at what the central wrong is, not whether protected activity is mentioned.

3. **Defendant's filing strategy**: If defendants file anti-SLAPP motions against the steward, Baral requires them to identify which specific claims arise from their protected activity. Vague motions targeting the entire complaint will fail under Baral.

4. **California Rules of Court timing**: Anti-SLAPP motions must be filed within 60 days of service of the complaint (unless the court permits later filing for good cause). Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(f). Monitor the 60-day window.
