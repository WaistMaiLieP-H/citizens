# Varian Medical Systems v. Delfino, 35 Cal.4th 180 (2005)

## The Case

Varian Medical Systems sued two former employees, Michelangelo Delfino and Mary Day, for defamation and trade libel based on thousands of internet postings they made on Yahoo message boards criticizing Varian, its executives, and its stock. Delfino and Day filed an anti-SLAPP motion under CCP § 425.16, arguing the postings were protected activity on a public issue (corporate governance of a public company).

## What the Court Held

Justice Moreno, writing for the majority:

1. **Internet postings on a public forum about a publicly traded company's governance and business practices constitute protected activity under the anti-SLAPP statute.** The postings concerned a "public issue" because they addressed the conduct of a public company, its officers, and its business practices — matters of public interest to shareholders and the investing public.

2. **However, the plaintiff demonstrated a probability of prevailing on the merits.** The postings included false statements of fact (not opinion), and evidence supported the defamation and trade libel claims. The anti-SLAPP motion was therefore properly denied despite the protected-activity finding.

3. **The two-step anti-SLAPP analysis is confirmed:** (1) Did the defendant show the claims arise from protected activity? (2) If so, did the plaintiff demonstrate a probability of prevailing? Both steps must be satisfied — a defendant cannot win on step one alone.

## The Rule

**Internet postings about a public company's governance are protected activity under anti-SLAPP, but the motion fails if the plaintiff shows a probability of prevailing with admissible evidence of actual defamation. The statute protects the right to speak, not the right to defame.**
