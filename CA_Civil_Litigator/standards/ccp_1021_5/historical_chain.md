# CCP §1021.5 — Historical Chain (Private Attorney General)

---

### I. The Wound

California follows the "American Rule" — each side pays its own attorney's fees unless a statute or contract provides otherwise. This means that a plaintiff who wins a case enforcing an important public right — stopping a government abuse, enforcing an environmental regulation, vindicating civil liberties — bears the full cost of the litigation. The public benefits; the plaintiff pays.

This creates a structural disincentive to bring cases that serve the public interest but offer little or no financial recovery to the individual plaintiff. Environmental cases, government accountability cases, civil rights cases — all cases where the benefit is diffuse and public rather than concentrated and private — are economically irrational for private litigants under the American Rule.

### II. The Promise (1977)

CCP §1021.5 codified the "private attorney general" doctrine. The promise: **When a private litigant enforces an important right affecting the public interest and confers a significant benefit on the general public, the court may shift the attorney's fees to the losing party.**

The three-part test:
1. **Significant benefit** conferred on the general public or a large class of persons
2. **Necessity and financial burden** of private enforcement make the award appropriate
3. **Fees should not come from the recovery** (i.e., the plaintiff shouldn't be expected to fund their own fee from damages)

### III. The Relationship to Federal §1988

CCP §1021.5 is the state-law analog to federal §1988, but broader in scope. §1988 is limited to specific enumerated civil rights statutes. §1021.5 applies to **any action** enforcing an important right affecting the public interest — environmental, consumer, government accountability, civil rights, anything. It is the most flexible fee-shift provision in California law.

### IV. Key Doctrinal Features

- **"Successful party"** — the plaintiff must achieve results. Unlike §1988, there is no statutory "prevailing party" language, and the Buckhannon "judicial imprimatur" requirement does not directly apply. California courts have their own catalyst-theory analysis under §1021.5.

- **Against public entities only** — when a public entity is a party, fees go against but not in favor of public entities. A government defendant who wins does not get §1021.5 fees from the plaintiff.

- **No multiplier for public-entity awards** — the Serrano v. Priest multiplier (enhancing fees based on risk, complexity, or quality) does not apply to fee awards against public entities.

### V. Why This Statute Is Load-Bearing

CCP §1021.5 is what makes government accountability litigation viable in California. Without it, a plaintiff who sues a city for violating open-meeting laws, or a county for illegal spending, or a state agency for failing to comply with environmental regulations, would bear the full cost of litigation while the public reaps the benefit. The fee shift aligns the economic incentives: if the case serves the public interest, the defendant — typically a government entity — pays the plaintiff's lawyer.

For the steward's work, §1021.5 is relevant to any state-court action that enforces public rights — government accountability claims, taxpayer standing actions under §526a, and Bane Act claims that vindicate public civil rights interests.
