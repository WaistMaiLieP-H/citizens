# Los Angeles Police Protective League v. City of Los Angeles, 102 Cal.App.4th 85 (2002)

## The Case

The Los Angeles Police Protective League (LAPPL) challenged the City of Los Angeles's implementation of the federal consent decree governing LAPD reforms after the Rampart scandal. LAPPL argued the consent decree's provisions exceeded what was necessary and sought attorney's fees under CCP § 1021.5 as a private attorney general.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **CCP § 1021.5 fees require that the litigation conferred a significant benefit on the general public or a large class of persons.** The "significant benefit" test looks at the real-world impact of the litigation, not just the legal ruling.

2. **The necessity and financial burden of private enforcement must be shown.** The party seeking fees must demonstrate that the cost of the litigation transcends the individual plaintiff's stake — meaning the fees are disproportionate to any personal benefit obtained.

3. **Police union challenges to consent decree implementation, where they protect officer procedural rights, can qualify as private attorney general actions** when the result benefits the public interest in constitutional policing — not just union members' employment interests.

## The Rule

**CCP § 1021.5 private attorney general fees require a significant public benefit, necessity of private enforcement, and financial burden disproportionate to personal stake. The "public benefit" must be real and concrete, not theoretical.**
