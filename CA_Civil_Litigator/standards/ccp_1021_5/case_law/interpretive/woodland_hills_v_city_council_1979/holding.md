# Woodland Hills Residents Assn. v. City Council, 23 Cal.3d 917 (1979)

## The Case

A homeowners' association in Woodland Hills sued the Los Angeles City Council over a zoning decision. After winning, the association asked for attorney fees under the newly enacted CCP §1021.5, arguing it had acted as a private attorney general enforcing zoning laws that protect the public.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court established the three-factor test that every §1021.5 fee motion must satisfy:

1. **The lawsuit enforced an important right affecting the public interest.** Not every lawsuit qualifies. The right at stake must matter to the public, not just the individual plaintiff.

2. **A significant benefit was conferred on the general public or a large class of persons.** The victory must have done something meaningful for people beyond the plaintiff. A purely private win does not qualify.

3. **The necessity and financial burden of private enforcement make the fee award appropriate.** The court asks: was private enforcement necessary (i.e., the government was not going to do it), and was the financial burden on the plaintiff such that it would have been unreasonable to expect them to bear the cost without fee-shifting?

The court also held that the "successful party" requirement does not demand total victory — substantial success on a significant issue is enough.

## The Rule

**To recover fees under §1021.5, a party must show: (1) an important public right was enforced, (2) a significant benefit flowed to the public or a large class, and (3) the financial burden of private enforcement justifies the award. This three-factor test is THE framework for every §1021.5 motion.**

## Why This Case Matters for §1021.5

Woodland Hills is the operational manual. Serrano created the doctrine and §1021.5 codified it, but Woodland Hills tells courts and lawyers exactly how to apply the statute. Every §1021.5 fee motion in California is structured around these three factors.
