GRAHAM v. DAIMLERCHRYSLER CORP.
34 Cal.4th 553 (2004)
California Supreme Court
Decided: November 1, 2004

---

FACTS:

Plaintiffs brought a consumer protection class action against DaimlerChrysler relating to vehicle defects. After the defendant made partial remedial changes during litigation, the case was settled without a court judgment. Plaintiffs sought attorney's fees under CCP § 1021.5 based on the catalyst theory — their lawsuit caused the defendant to provide remedial relief.

DaimlerChrysler argued that after the U.S. Supreme Court's decision in Buckhannon (which rejected the federal catalyst theory for § 1988 fees), California should adopt the same rule for § 1021.5.

---

HOLDING:

Chief Justice George wrote for the Court.

CALIFORNIA REJECTS BUCKHANNON FOR § 1021.5 — THE CATALYST DOCTRINE SURVIVES:
"We conclude that the catalyst theory remains a valid basis for fee awards under § 1021.5 and that California courts need not follow Buckhannon in this regard."

The Court held that Buckhannon interpreted federal fee-shifting statutes (§ 1988) and was driven by federal separation-of-powers concerns about Congress's intent regarding fee awards. Those concerns do not apply to California's § 1021.5, which was enacted by the California Legislature with different textual, historical, and policy considerations.

THE CALIFORNIA CATALYST TEST (GRAHAM STANDARD):
A plaintiff may recover § 1021.5 fees under the catalyst theory if:
1. The lawsuit was a substantial factor causing the defendant to provide the relief obtained;
2. The relief obtained was worth more than a nominal amount; AND
3. The plaintiff had a reasonable basis to bring the lawsuit — the action was not frivolous.

"The test is objective and focused on causation: did the lawsuit, in fact, cause the defendant to act? Courts consider circumstantial evidence including the timing of the defendant's actions relative to the filing of the suit."

EVIDENCE OF CATALYST CAUSATION:
- Defendant made changes after the lawsuit was filed
- Defendant expressed awareness of the lawsuit in communications
- Changes made corresponded to the relief specifically sought in the complaint
- No other convincing explanation for the change

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Graham v. DaimlerChrysler is directly controlling for California § 1021.5 fee claims:

1. **No court judgment required**: The steward can recover § 1021.5 fees in California state court even if:
   - The government defendants settle without a judgment
   - The case is mooted by policy changes
   - The federal § 1983 case settles without a court order

2. **Catalyst causation evidence**: If APD, Contra Costa County, or CalVCB make policy or procedural changes after the litigation is filed, that change is evidence of catalyst causation supporting § 1021.5 fees.

3. **Objectively reasonable basis**: The steward's claims — supported by 16 years of documented conduct and a detailed factual record — are far from frivolous. The "reasonable basis" element is easily satisfied.

4. **Strategic use**: Filing state court claims in parallel with the federal § 1983 complaint preserves the § 1021.5 catalyst-theory fee claim that is unavailable under the federal § 1988 / Buckhannon framework.
