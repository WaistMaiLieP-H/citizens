# Graham v. DaimlerChrysler Corp., 34 Cal.4th 553 (2004)

## The Case

Graham sued DaimlerChrysler under the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (lemon law). Before any judgment, DaimlerChrysler gave Graham the relief he sought — a vehicle replacement. Graham then moved for attorney fees under §1021.5, arguing his lawsuit caused the result even though no court order made it happen. DaimlerChrysler argued Graham was not a "successful party" because there was no favorable judgment.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **A plaintiff can be a "successful party" under §1021.5 even without a favorable judgment, if the lawsuit was the catalyst that caused the defendant to provide the relief sought.** This is the "catalyst theory." The lawsuit does not have to end with a court order in the plaintiff's favor — it just has to be the reason the defendant changed its behavior.

2. **California law rejects the federal approach.** The U.S. Supreme Court in Buckhannon Board & Care Home v. West Virginia DHHR (2001) rejected the catalyst theory for federal fee-shifting statutes (42 USC §1988). California's Supreme Court explicitly declined to follow Buckhannon, holding that §1021.5's purpose — encouraging private enforcement of public rights — requires recognizing catalyst success.

3. **The plaintiff must still satisfy the Woodland Hills three-factor test.** Catalyst status alone does not guarantee fees. The case must still involve an important public right, a significant public benefit, and a financial burden justifying the award.

## The Rule

**Under §1021.5, a plaintiff who files suit and causes the defendant to provide the desired relief is a "successful party" entitled to fees, even without a favorable judgment — provided the Woodland Hills three-factor test is met. California explicitly rejects the federal Buckhannon rule.**

## Why This Case Matters for §1021.5

Graham is critical because it means §1021.5 has teeth even when the defendant capitulates before judgment. Without the catalyst theory, defendants could moot fee claims by giving in early, stripping the financial incentive for private enforcement. Graham closes that loophole in California.
