SERRANO v. PRIEST
20 Cal.3d 25 (1977)
California Supreme Court
Decided: December 30, 1977

---

FACTS:

This was the third in the series of Serrano v. Priest cases challenging California's school finance system as unconstitutional. The litigation resulted in a major restructuring of California school funding. After the plaintiffs achieved substantial success, they sought attorney's fees under the "private attorney general" doctrine — the common law principle that became the basis for CCP § 1021.5 (which was enacted in 1977 in part to codify this doctrine).

The question: under what circumstances is it appropriate for courts to award attorney's fees to a plaintiff who successfully vindicated the public interest?

---

HOLDING:

Justice Tobriner wrote for the Court.

THE SERRANO FOUR-FACTOR TEST (EXPANDED WOODLAND HILLS):
To award attorney's fees under the private attorney general doctrine, a court must find:

1. The litigation has resulted in the enforcement of an important right affecting the public interest;
2. A significant benefit has been conferred on the general public or a large class of persons;
3. The necessity and financial burden of private enforcement make an award appropriate; AND
4. The "vindication of the right" test: the award should not prove financially burdensome to the defendant to a degree inconsistent with the importance of the right enforced.

[Note: The fourth factor is largely absorbed into § 1021.5's discretionary standard.]

THE CATALYST THEORY (CONFIRMED IN SERRANO):
"An attorney's fee award is appropriate under the private attorney general doctrine when the plaintiff's lawsuit was a substantial factor in causing the defendant to provide the relief that mooted the case."

If a plaintiff's litigation caused the defendant to change its behavior — even if the case was settled, dismissed, or mooted — the private attorney general doctrine (now § 1021.5) supports a fee award if the other elements are satisfied.

This California catalyst theory directly contradicts the federal Buckhannon rule (which rejects catalyst theory for federal fee statutes). California's § 1021.5 has a catalyst doctrine; § 1988 (federal) does not.

---

WHY SERRANO MATTERS FOR CALIFORNIA FEE AWARDS:
1. California expressly RETAINED the catalyst doctrine for § 1021.5 claims even after Buckhannon
2. A plaintiff who caused beneficial change through litigation can recover fees under § 1021.5 even without a court judgment
3. The "important right" element is interpreted broadly — it includes any right that has been recognized as important by statute, constitution, or public policy

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

The California catalyst doctrine preserved by Serrano means:
1. If the § 1983 federal complaint, combined with California state law claims, causes APD or Contra Costa County to change policies, provide remediation, or settle — attorney fees are available under § 1021.5 even if no judgment is entered.
2. The steward's pro se status does not automatically bar § 1021.5 fees for future attorney representation — if any attorney participates in the litigation, their fees are recoverable.
3. Parallel state court claims (Bane Act, Art. I § 1, § 1021.5) can support fee awards even if the federal § 1983 action settles without a judgment.
