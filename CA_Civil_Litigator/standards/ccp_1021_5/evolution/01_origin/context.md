# CCP 1021.5 -- Origin Context

## Enactment

Stats. 1977, Ch. 1197. Added to the Code of Civil Procedure as Section 1021.5. Effective January 1, 1978.

## The Wound

California follows the American Rule: each side pays its own attorney's fees. A plaintiff who wins a case enforcing an important public right -- stopping illegal government spending, enforcing environmental regulations, vindicating civil liberties -- bears the full cost of the litigation. The public benefits. The plaintiff pays.

This created a structural problem. Cases that serve the public interest but offer little or no financial recovery to the individual plaintiff -- environmental cases, government accountability cases, civil rights cases -- were economically irrational to bring. The benefit is diffuse and public. The cost is concentrated and private. Important rights went unenforced because no one could afford to enforce them.

Before this statute, California courts had developed a judicially-created "private attorney general" doctrine to address this problem. But the doctrine was inconsistent in application and lacked a clear statutory foundation.

## The Promise

If you enforce an important right that benefits the public, the court can order the opposing party to pay your attorney's fees.

Three conditions:
1. A significant benefit -- pecuniary or nonpecuniary -- was conferred on the general public or a large class of persons
2. The necessity and financial burden of private enforcement make the fee award appropriate
3. The fees should not in the interest of justice be paid out of the recovery

## Why This Statute Exists

The California Legislature codified the private attorney general doctrine because the judicial version was not enough. The Legislature wanted a clear, statutory signal that fee-shifting is available in any action that enforces an important right affecting the public interest. Not just civil rights. Not just environmental. Any important public right.

This is broader than the federal counterpart (42 USC 1988), which is limited to enumerated civil rights statutes. Section 1021.5 applies to any action, any subject matter, as long as the three conditions are met.

## Source

Stats. 1977, Ch. 1197 (S.B. 947). Codifying and expanding the judicially-created private attorney general doctrine recognized in Serrano v. Priest (1977) 20 Cal.3d 25.
