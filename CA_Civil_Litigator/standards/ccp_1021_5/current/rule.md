# Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 1021.5 — Private Attorney General Fees

**Standard ID:** CCP_1021_5_PRIVATE_AG_FEES
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

Upon motion, a court may award attorneys' fees to a successful party against one or more opposing parties in any action which has resulted in the enforcement of an important right affecting the public interest if:

**(a)** a significant benefit, whether pecuniary or nonpecuniary, has been conferred on the general public or a large class of persons,

**(b)** the necessity and financial burden of private enforcement, or of enforcement by one public entity against another public entity, are such as to make the award appropriate, and

**(c)** such fees should not in the interest of justice be paid out of the recovery, if any.

With respect to actions involving public entities, this section applies to allowances against, but not in favor of, public entities, and no claim shall be required to be filed therefor.

Attorneys' fees awarded to a public entity pursuant to this section shall not be increased or decreased by a multiplier based upon extrinsic circumstances, as discussed in *Serrano v. Priest*, 20 Cal.3d 25, 49.

---

## Three-Part Test

All three elements must be satisfied:

1. **Important public right enforced** — The right vindicated must be qualitatively significant, not just important to the parties.
2. **Significant benefit to public or large class** — Pecuniary or nonpecuniary benefit; breadth of class matters.
3. **Financial burden makes award appropriate** — The cost of litigation must be disproportionate to any individual plaintiff's financial stake — making private enforcement unlikely without the fee incentive.

---

## Plain English

If you win a lawsuit that vindicates a significant public right — even if you recover little or nothing for yourself personally — the court can order the other side to pay your attorney fees. This makes it financially viable to bring cases that serve the public but wouldn't otherwise pencil out for a private attorney. It's California's primary tool for incentivizing civil rights and public interest litigation.
