# Evolution Stage 02: Current

The current text of CCP §1094.5 is at `../../current/ccp_1094_5_leginfo.txt`.

## Summary of current state

The statute has been amended multiple times since 1945, primarily to add specialized provisions:

- **Subdivision (d)** (hospital boards): Added to address the specific context of private hospital peer review decisions. Uses substantial evidence standard, except in discrimination cases where independent judgment applies.
- **Subdivision (h)** (APA stay standards): Added to create a stricter stay standard for agencies operating under the Administrative Procedure Act — the petitioner must show the agency is unlikely to prevail on the merits.
- **Subdivision (j)** (State Bargaining Unit 5): Added 1996 to limit judicial review of certain state employee disciplinary decisions.

## The core remains

Despite amendments, the core structure from 1945 is intact: subdivisions (a) through (c) define the scope of review, the three questions (jurisdiction, fair trial, abuse of discretion), and the two standards of review (independent judgment vs. substantial evidence). These are the provisions litigated most often and the ones that matter most.

## Key doctrinal development since enactment

The major post-enactment development came from the courts, not the legislature: the California Supreme Court in **Bixby v. Pierno, 4 Cal.3d 130 (1971)** and **Strumsky v. San Diego County Employees Retirement Assn., 11 Cal.3d 28 (1974)** established the "fundamental vested right" doctrine that determines which standard of review applies. When an agency decision affects a fundamental vested right — typically a professional license or retirement benefits — the independent judgment standard applies. Everything else gets substantial evidence.

This judge-made doctrine is not in the statutory text. The statute says "in cases in which the court is authorized by law to exercise its independent judgment on the evidence." The courts determined what "authorized by law" means.
