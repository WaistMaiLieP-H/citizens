# Topanga Assn. for a Scenic Community v. County of Los Angeles, 11 Cal.3d 506 (1974)

## What This Case Stands For

An agency cannot just announce a decision. It has to explain how it got there.

The agency's written findings must bridge the gap between the raw evidence in the record and the ultimate decision. If someone reading the findings cannot trace the logical path from "here is what the evidence showed" to "here is what we decided," the decision fails.

This is not a technicality. Without adequate findings, a court cannot perform its review function under CCP 1094.5 -- the court has nothing to review. And without adequate findings, the parties cannot know whether to challenge the decision or how.

## The Rule

Under CCP 1094.5, an administrative agency must make findings that:

1. Identify the evidence in the record that supports the decision.
2. Show the analytic route from that evidence to the conclusion.
3. Are specific enough that a reviewing court can determine whether the evidence supports the findings, and whether the findings support the decision.

If the findings do not bridge the analytic gap, the decision is an abuse of discretion regardless of what standard of review applies.

## What It Means in Practice

- An agency that says "application denied" without explaining why based on the evidence has committed an abuse of discretion.
- The findings requirement applies under BOTH the independent judgment and substantial evidence standards. You cannot review whether evidence supports findings if there are no findings.
- This case is the basis for challenging any administrative decision where the agency failed to explain its reasoning.
- In writ petitions, Topanga is cited whenever the agency's findings are conclusory, boilerplate, or disconnected from the evidence actually presented.
- The remedy is typically remand to the agency to make proper findings, not reversal of the decision outright.
