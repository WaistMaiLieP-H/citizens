# Statute Version Cited: CCP 1094.5 as of 1974

## The Relevant Text

The court was reading CCP 1094.5 as a whole, particularly subdivisions (b) and (c):

Subdivision (b) sets out the grounds for granting a writ: the agency acted without or in excess of jurisdiction, there was no fair trial, or there was an abuse of discretion.

Subdivision (c) defines abuse of discretion in terms of whether the findings are supported by the evidence -- under either the independent judgment or substantial evidence standard.

## What the Court Was Deciding

The statute assumes there ARE findings to review. Subdivision (c) asks whether the findings are "supported by the evidence." But what happens when the agency makes no meaningful findings at all, or makes findings that are so vague they do not connect to the evidence?

Topanga held that the statute implicitly requires the agency to make findings sufficient to enable the review that subdivision (c) contemplates. Without adequate findings, the entire review framework breaks down.

## What Changed (and What Did Not)

The text of CCP 1094.5 in 1974 is substantively the same as the current version with respect to subdivisions (b) and (c). The findings requirement announced in Topanga is a judicial gloss on the statute -- it was read into the existing text, not added by amendment.

## Source

CCP 1094.5 as enacted by Stats. 1945, Ch. 868, with amendments through 1974. Current version available at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov.
