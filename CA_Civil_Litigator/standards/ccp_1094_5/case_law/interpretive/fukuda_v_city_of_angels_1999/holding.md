# Fukuda v. City of Angels, 20 Cal.4th 805 (1999)

## What This Case Stands For

Land use and zoning decisions are reviewed under the substantial evidence standard, not independent judgment. A property owner challenging a zoning decision does not get the court to reweigh the evidence. The court only asks whether any reasonable person could have reached the same conclusion as the agency.

This is because land use and zoning decisions do not affect "fundamental vested rights" as defined in Bixby. A zoning classification or land use permit is a regulatory decision about how property may be used -- it does not take away a right that has already vested in the way that revoking a professional license does.

## The Rule

Under CCP 1094.5(c), judicial review of local land use and zoning decisions is limited to the **substantial evidence** standard. The court does not exercise independent judgment.

Substantial evidence means: is there evidence in the record that a reasonable person could accept as adequate to support the agency's conclusion? If yes, the agency's decision stands -- even if the court would have decided differently.

## What It Means in Practice

- If a city denies your building permit or rezoning application, and there is any substantial evidence supporting that denial, the court will uphold it.
- The practical difference is enormous. Under independent judgment, the court reweighs everything and can substitute its own view. Under substantial evidence, the court defers heavily to the agency.
- This means land use challenges are much harder to win than license revocation challenges.
- The distinction: your medical license is a fundamental vested right (independent judgment). Your request to rezone your property is not (substantial evidence).
- Fukuda resolved confusion in the lower courts about whether certain property-related decisions might qualify for independent judgment review. They do not.
