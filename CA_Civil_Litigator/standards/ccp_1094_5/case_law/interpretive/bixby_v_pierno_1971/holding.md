# Bixby v. Pierno, 4 Cal.3d 130 (1971)

## What This Case Stands For

When a government agency makes a decision that takes away or substantially affects something you already have -- like a professional license, a pension, or a permit you've been relying on -- the court does not just rubber-stamp the agency's decision. The court looks at all the evidence itself and makes its own independent call about whether the agency got it right.

This is called the "independent judgment" standard of review.

For everything else -- things that are not fundamental vested rights -- the court uses a much more deferential standard called "substantial evidence." Under that standard, the court only asks: could any reasonable person have reached the same conclusion as the agency? If yes, the agency wins, even if the court would have decided differently.

## The Rule

Under CCP 1094.5(c), when an administrative decision substantially affects a **fundamental vested right**, the trial court must exercise its **independent judgment on the evidence**. The court reweighs the evidence and substitutes its own judgment for the agency's.

When the decision does **not** affect a fundamental vested right, the court applies the **substantial evidence** test -- it defers to the agency's findings if they are supported by substantial evidence in light of the whole record.

## What It Means in Practice

- A doctor whose license is revoked gets independent judgment review -- the court looks at everything fresh.
- A contractor whose license application is denied for the first time gets substantial evidence review -- the court defers to the agency unless no reasonable person could have reached that conclusion.
- The distinction turns on whether you already had the right (vested) and whether it is fundamental (important enough to warrant full judicial scrutiny).
- This is the most important case for determining which standard of review applies in any CCP 1094.5 writ petition.
