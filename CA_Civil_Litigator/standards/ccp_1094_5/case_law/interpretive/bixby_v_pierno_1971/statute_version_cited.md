# Statute Version Cited: CCP 1094.5 as of 1971

## The Relevant Text

The court in Bixby was reading CCP 1094.5(c), which even in 1971 contained the same core language that exists today:

> "Where it is claimed that the findings are not supported by the evidence, in cases in which the court is authorized by law to exercise its independent judgment on the evidence, abuse of discretion is established if the court determines that the findings are not supported by the weight of the evidence."

> "In all other cases, abuse of discretion is established if the court determines that the findings are not supported by substantial evidence in the light of the whole record."

## What Changed (and What Did Not)

The subdivision (c) language the court interpreted in 1971 has remained **substantively unchanged** through all subsequent amendments. The dual-track review framework -- independent judgment for some cases, substantial evidence for the rest -- is the same today as when the court decided Bixby.

## Why This Matters

Bixby answered the question the statute left open: WHEN does the court get to exercise independent judgment? The statute said "in cases in which the court is authorized by law" but did not say which cases those were. Bixby established the test: when the decision substantially affects a fundamental vested right.

## Source

CCP 1094.5 as enacted by Stats. 1945, Ch. 868, with amendments through 1971. Core text of subdivision (c) is substantially identical to the current version available at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov.
