# Statute Version Cited: CCP 1094.5 as of 1974

## The Relevant Text

The court was reading the same CCP 1094.5(c) dual-track language that Bixby interpreted three years earlier:

> "Where it is claimed that the findings are not supported by the evidence, in cases in which the court is authorized by law to exercise its independent judgment on the evidence, abuse of discretion is established if the court determines that the findings are not supported by the weight of the evidence."

## What the Court Was Deciding

The statute says "in cases in which the court is authorized by law to exercise its independent judgment." Bixby had said this means cases involving fundamental vested rights. Strumsky answered the follow-up question: does "authorized by law" include local agency decisions, or only state agency decisions?

The court held it includes both. The phrase "authorized by law" is triggered by the nature of the right, not the source of the decision.

## What Changed (and What Did Not)

The statutory text of subdivision (c) was the same in 1974 as it was in 1971 when Bixby was decided, and it remains substantively the same today. No amendments changed the independent judgment / substantial evidence framework.

## Source

CCP 1094.5 as enacted by Stats. 1945, Ch. 868, with amendments through 1974. Core text of subdivision (c) is substantially identical to the current version available at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov.
