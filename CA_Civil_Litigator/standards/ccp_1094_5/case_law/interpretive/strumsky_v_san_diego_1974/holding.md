# Strumsky v. San Diego County Employees Retirement Assn., 11 Cal.3d 28 (1974)

## What This Case Stands For

The independent judgment standard of review applies to ALL administrative decisions that affect fundamental vested rights -- regardless of whether the decision was made by a state agency or a local agency.

Before Strumsky, there was a question: Bixby established the fundamental vested right doctrine, but did it only apply to state-level agencies? Could a county retirement board strip your pension and get away with deferential review just because it was a local body, not a state one?

The California Supreme Court said no. The standard of review depends on the nature of the right at stake, not on which level of government made the decision.

## The Rule

When a local administrative agency makes a decision that substantially affects a fundamental vested right, the trial court must exercise its independent judgment on the evidence under CCP 1094.5(c). The same standard that applies to state agencies applies to counties, cities, and special districts.

## What It Means in Practice

- A county retirement board denying your pension gets the same scrutiny as a state licensing board revoking your license.
- A city planning commission revoking a previously-granted use permit affecting vested property rights gets independent judgment review.
- The question is always: what kind of right is at stake? Not: who made the decision?
- This closed a loophole that would have let local agencies escape meaningful judicial review by virtue of being local rather than state-level.
