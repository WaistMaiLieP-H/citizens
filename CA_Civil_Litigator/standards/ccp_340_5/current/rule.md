# Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 340.5 — Medical Malpractice Statute of Limitations (MICRA)

**Standard ID:** CCP_340_5_MICRA_SOL
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

In an action for injury or death against a health care provider based upon such person's alleged professional negligence, the time for the commencement of action shall be **three years after the date of injury** or **one year after the plaintiff discovers, or through the use of reasonable diligence should have discovered, the injury**, whichever occurs first.

In no event shall the time for commencement of legal action exceed three years unless tolled for any of the following: (1) upon proof of **fraud**, (2) **intentional concealment**, or (3) the presence of a **foreign body** which has no therapeutic or diagnostic purpose or effect, in the person of the injured person.

Actions by a minor shall be commenced within three years from the date of the alleged wrongful act except that actions by a minor under the full age of six years shall be commenced within three years or prior to his eighth birthday whichever provides a longer period.

**Definitions:**
- "Health care provider" — any person licensed under Division 2 of the Business and Professions Code; any clinic, health dispensary, or health facility licensed under Division 2 of the Health and Safety Code.
- "Professional negligence" — a negligent act or omission in the rendering of professional services within the scope of the provider's license.

---

## SOL Structure — MICRA

| Trigger | Limitations Period |
|---------|--------------------|
| Date of injury | 3 years |
| Discovery of injury (whichever is first) | 1 year from discovery |
| Fraud / intentional concealment / foreign body | Tolled — 3-year cap lifted |
| Minor under 6 years old | 3 years OR until 8th birthday, whichever is longer |

---

## Plain English

Medical malpractice claims must be filed within one year of when you discovered (or reasonably should have discovered) the injury, or three years from the date of injury — whichever comes first. If the provider committed fraud or hid the injury, the three-year cap is lifted. Discovery rule tolling is critical: if you had no reason to know the injury was caused by malpractice, the one-year clock doesn't start until you reasonably should have known.
