# Evolution Stage 02: Current

The current text of CCP §340.5 is at `../../current/ccp_340_5_leginfo.txt`.

## Summary of current state

The statute has been amended since 1975 but the core structure is intact: three years from injury, one year from discovery, whichever is first. The tolling provisions (fraud, concealment, foreign body) and the minor provisions remain substantively the same.

## MICRA reform — AB 35 (2022, effective 2023)

The most significant post-1975 development in MICRA was AB 35 (Stats. 2022, Ch. 17), which raised the noneconomic damages cap from $250,000 to $350,000 (with scheduled increases over time). AB 35 addressed the damages cap (Civ. Code §3333.2), not the statute of limitations. §340.5's limitations periods were not changed by AB 35.

## The discovery rule in practice

The discovery rule under §340.5 is narrower than the general discovery rule. Under the general personal injury statute (CCP §335.1), the period runs from discovery — there is no outside limit. Under §340.5, the one-year-from-discovery clock starts when the patient discovers or should have discovered the injury. But the three-year-from-injury clock runs regardless.

The practical effect: a patient who could not reasonably have discovered their injury within three years of the date it occurred is barred from suit UNLESS fraud, concealment, or foreign body tolling applies. The discovery rule is not a safety net — it is an additional trigger that can start the clock even earlier than three years if the patient discovers the injury quickly.

## Key judicial developments

- **Sanchez v. South Hoover Hospital, 18 Cal.3d 93 (1976):** Upheld MICRA's constitutionality
- **Knowles v. Superior Court, 118 Cal.App.4th 1290 (2004):** Discovery rule applies — one year from when the patient knew or should have known of the injury AND its negligent cause
- **Young v. Haines, 41 Cal.3d 883 (1986):** The discovery rule requires suspicion of wrongdoing, not certainty

## Relevance to the steward's medical fraud timeline

The fraud and intentional concealment tolling provisions are directly applicable to the steward's documented medical fraud timeline. When a health care provider commits fraud or intentionally conceals an injury, the three-year outside limit does not apply. The clock does not start until the patient discovers or should have discovered the fraud/concealment.
