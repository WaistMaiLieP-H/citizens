# CCP §340.5 — Origin Context

## Enactment

Stats. 1975, Ch. 1, 2d Ex. Sess. (AB 1XX). Part of the Medical Injury Compensation Reform Act (MICRA). Effective September 23, 1975.

## The wound — two sides

### The legislative wound (1975 insurance crisis)

In 1975, California experienced a medical malpractice insurance crisis. Malpractice insurance premiums were skyrocketing. Major insurers were withdrawing from the market or refusing to write new policies. Some doctors threatened to stop practicing. Hospitals faced closure. The Legislature convened a special session to address the crisis.

MICRA was the response. The Legislature determined that the litigation system was driving insurance costs. The reforms included: capped damages (Civ. Code §3333.2 — $250,000 cap on noneconomic damages, later raised to $350,000 in 2023), attorney fee limits (Bus. & Prof. Code §6146), and a shortened statute of limitations (CCP §340.5).

### The counter-wound (patient access to justice)

MICRA's reforms made it harder for injured patients to obtain justice. The shortened statute of limitations (§340.5) gives less time than the general personal injury statute (CCP §335.1 — two years from discovery). The damages cap limits recovery for the most severely injured patients. The fee limits make it harder for attorneys to take complex cases on contingency. Critics have argued for decades that MICRA protects the medical industry at the expense of injured patients.

Both wounds are real. The standard documents both.

## The statute of limitations structure

§340.5 creates a dual-trigger limitations period:

- **Three years from the date of injury** — the outside limit (absent tolling)
- **One year from discovery** — the discovery trigger

Whichever comes FIRST. This is the critical word. Under the general personal injury statute (CCP §335.1), the period runs from discovery. Under §340.5, the three-year outside limit runs from the date of injury regardless of whether the patient knows they were injured. If the injury happened more than three years ago and none of the tolling exceptions apply, the claim is time-barred even if the patient just discovered the injury yesterday.

## The tolling exceptions

The three-year outside limit is not absolute. Three things toll it:

1. **Fraud** — the health care provider committed fraud
2. **Intentional concealment** — the provider actively hid the injury
3. **Foreign body** — a non-therapeutic foreign body was left in the patient

These tolling provisions are narrow. They require affirmative misconduct, not mere failure to follow up or failure to discover an error.

## The minor provisions

Minors get additional protection:

- Three years from the wrongful act (not from discovery)
- Children under six: three years from the act OR until the child's eighth birthday, whichever is longer
- Tolling when the parent/guardian AND the defendant's insurer or provider have committed fraud or collusion in failing to bring the minor's claim

The parent/guardian collusion provision is significant. It recognizes that the adults responsible for protecting the child may themselves be part of the problem.
