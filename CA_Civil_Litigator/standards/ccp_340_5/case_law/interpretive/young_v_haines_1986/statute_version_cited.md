# Statute Version Cited: CCP 340.5 as of 1986

## The Relevant Text

The court was reading the discovery provision of CCP 340.5:

> "In an action for injury or death against a health care provider based upon such person's alleged professional negligence, the time for the commencement of action shall be three years after the date of injury or one year after the plaintiff discovers, or through the use of reasonable diligence should have discovered, the injury, whichever occurs first."

## What the Court Was Deciding

The critical phrase is "discovers, or through the use of reasonable diligence should have discovered, the injury." But what does "discovers the injury" mean in practice? Does it mean discovering you are hurt? Discovering that someone caused the hurt? Discovering exactly what the doctor did wrong?

Young held that discovery occurs when the plaintiff has a "suspicion of wrongdoing" -- awareness that the injury may have been caused by someone's fault. This is a lower threshold than certainty. The clock starts at suspicion, not confirmation.

## What Changed (and What Did Not)

The discovery language in CCP 340.5 has remained substantively the same since MICRA's enactment in 1975. Young interpreted the existing text -- it did not prompt a statutory amendment. The "suspicion of wrongdoing" standard announced in Young remains the governing test for when the one-year discovery period begins.

## Source

CCP 340.5 as enacted by Stats. 1975, 2d Ex. Sess., Ch. 1 (MICRA), with amendments through 1986. Current version available at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov.
