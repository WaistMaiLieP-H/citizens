# Statute Version Cited: CCP 340.5 as of 2004

## The Relevant Text

The court was reading the same discovery provision of CCP 340.5 that Young v. Haines interpreted:

> "In an action for injury or death against a health care provider based upon such person's alleged professional negligence, the time for the commencement of action shall be three years after the date of injury or one year after the plaintiff discovers, or through the use of reasonable diligence should have discovered, the injury, whichever occurs first."

## What the Court Was Deciding

The specific question was what "discovers the injury" means. Young had said "suspicion of wrongdoing." Knowles clarified that "suspicion of wrongdoing" means awareness of the injury and its factual cause -- not awareness that the factual cause constituted professional negligence.

The distinction: a patient who knows their arm stopped working after surgery and knows the surgery caused it has "discovered the injury" -- even if they believe (incorrectly or correctly) that the outcome was just a known risk of the procedure rather than a mistake.

## What Changed (and What Did Not)

The statutory text was the same in 2004 as when Young was decided in 1986 and when MICRA was enacted in 1975. The core discovery language has remained unchanged. Knowles added a layer of interpretation to the existing framework: discovery = injury + cause, not injury + cause + legal negligence.

## Source

CCP 340.5 as enacted by Stats. 1975, 2d Ex. Sess., Ch. 1 (MICRA), with amendments through 2004. Current version available at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov.
