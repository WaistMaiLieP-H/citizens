# Statute Version Cited: CCP 340.5 as Enacted by MICRA (1975)

## The Relevant Text

CCP 340.5 was enacted as part of MICRA -- the Medical Injury Compensation Reform Act of 1975 (Stats. 1975, 2d Ex. Sess., Ch. 1). The court was reading the statute shortly after enactment:

The statute provides that an action for injury or death against a health care provider based on professional negligence must be commenced within:

- **One year** after the plaintiff discovers, or through reasonable diligence should have discovered, the injury; OR
- **Three years** after the date of the injury;

whichever occurs first.

Exceptions are provided for fraud, concealment, the presence of a foreign body with no therapeutic purpose, and actions by minors under age six (who get until their eighth birthday).

## What the Court Was Deciding

Whether this entire framework -- shorter than the general personal injury limitations period -- violated constitutional guarantees of due process, equal protection, access to courts, and jury trial rights.

The court held it did not. The Legislature had a rational basis (the medical malpractice insurance crisis) and the means chosen (shortened limitations) were reasonably related to the legislative purpose.

## What Changed Since

CCP 340.5 has been amended several times since 1975, including adjustments to the minor's tolling provision and clarifications of the discovery rule. The core one-year/three-year framework upheld in Sanchez remains intact.
