# Evolution Stage 02: Current

The current text of CCP §583.310 is at `../../current/ccp_583_310_leginfo.txt`.

## Summary of current state

The text is one sentence: "An action shall be brought to trial within five years after the action is commenced against the defendant."

It has not been amended since its enactment in its current form in 1984. The rule itself — five years to trial — has not changed since at least 1933 under former CCP §583.

## The companion statutes

§583.310 does not operate alone. The full five-year rule framework includes:

- **§583.310:** The rule — five years to trial.
- **§583.340:** Tolling exceptions — the time during which the following are excluded: (a) impossibility, impracticability, or futility of bringing the action to trial; (b) the time a stay of the action was in effect; (c) the time during which the action was suspended by court order.
- **§583.350:** The effect of impossibility/impracticability/futility — these are questions of fact, determined by the court.
- **§583.360:** Computation of time — the five years is computed from the date of filing, not from the date of service.
- **§583.130:** The policy preference — California favors trial on the merits. But the five-year rule is mandatory, and when the two conflict, the five-year rule controls.
- **§583.140:** The rule cannot be extended by stipulation or court order — the parties cannot agree to more time, and the court cannot grant it.
- **§583.360(b):** Mandatory dismissal on motion or the court's own motion if the five-year period has expired.

## Key judicial developments

The courts have been strict in applying the five-year rule:

- **Hughes v. Board of Architectural Examiners, 17 Cal.4th 763 (1998):** The five-year period is mandatory and jurisdictional. Once it expires, the court has no power to try the case.
- **Bruns v. E-Commerce Exchange, Inc., 51 Cal.4th 717 (2011):** The impossibility/impracticability exception is narrow; it requires circumstances truly beyond the parties' control.
- **Sanchez v. City of Los Angeles, 109 Cal.App.4th 1262 (2003):** Court congestion alone does not establish impossibility.

## What has NOT changed

The five-year rule has been five years since its origins. The exceptions have remained narrow. The mandatory nature has never been softened. This is one of the most stable rules in California civil practice.
