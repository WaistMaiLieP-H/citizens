# Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 583.310 — Five-Year Mandatory Dismissal Rule

**Standard ID:** CCP_583_310_FIVE_YEAR_RULE
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

An action shall be brought to trial within five years after the action is commenced against the defendant.

*(Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 583.310)*

---

## Related Provisions

**§ 583.360** — Mandatory dismissal: If an action is not brought to trial within the time prescribed by § 583.310, the court shall dismiss it on motion of the defendant or on the court's own motion. The dismissal is mandatory, not discretionary.

**§ 583.340** — Tolling: In computing the time within which an action must be brought to trial, the following periods shall be excluded:
- (a) The time during which the jurisdiction of the court to try the action was suspended;
- (b) The time during which prosecution or trial of the action was stayed or enjoined;
- (c) The time during which, for any other reason beyond the control of the plaintiff, trial could not be had.

**§ 583.350** — Minimum time: If the parties agree, by stipulation filed in court, to an extension of time, the action must be brought to trial within the extended time.

---

## Key Rules

- **Mandatory, not discretionary** — Once five years pass without trial, dismissal is mandatory on defendant's motion. The court has no discretion to excuse the failure.
- **Tolling is narrow** — Plaintiff bears burden of showing time was beyond their control. Difficulty finding witnesses, settlement negotiations, and counsel's busy schedule do not toll the period.
- **Measured from commencement** — The five years run from the date the action was commenced (complaint filed) against the defendant.

---

## Plain English

File a lawsuit and fail to bring it to trial within five years, and the defendant can get it dismissed — permanently, with no discretion for the court to let you try again. The rule exists to protect defendants from indefinite litigation clouds and to clear dormant cases from court dockets. Tolling exceptions exist but are narrow.
