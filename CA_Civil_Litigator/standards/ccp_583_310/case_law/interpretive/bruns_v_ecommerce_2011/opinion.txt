BRUNS v. E-COMMERCE EXCHANGE, INC.
196 Cal.App.4th 1472 (2011)
California Court of Appeal, Fourth District
Decided: July 29, 2011

---

FACTS:

Bruns filed a civil action that remained pending for over five years. The defendant moved to dismiss under CCP § 583.310. Bruns argued that certain time periods should be excluded from the five-year calculation because bringing the action to trial was "impossible, impracticable, or futile" under § 583.340(c).

The court analyzed what circumstances qualify as "impossible, impracticable, or futile" and what evidence is required to establish the exception.

---

HOLDING:

THE "IMPOSSIBLE, IMPRACTICABLE, OR FUTILE" STANDARD — OBJECTIVE TEST:
"The test for whether a period qualifies as impossible, impracticable, or futile under § 583.340(c) is objective, not subjective. The question is whether, in the actual circumstances of the litigation, it was objectively impossible, impracticable, or futile to bring the case to trial — not whether the plaintiff subjectively found it difficult."

THE THREE-PART OBJECTIVE STANDARD:
1. **Impossible**: The action literally could not have been tried — a legal bar, a stay order, a jurisdiction problem, or a physical impossibility prevented trial.

2. **Impracticable**: Circumstances made trial unreasonably difficult — not merely inconvenient or expensive. This requires showing that a reasonably prudent plaintiff pursuing the action diligently could not have brought the case to trial within the remaining time.

3. **Futile**: Proceeding would accomplish nothing of value — for example, proceeding during a bankruptcy stay where no recovery was available.

WHAT DOES NOT QUALIFY:
- Plaintiff's own delays in discovery
- Difficulty finding witnesses
- Plaintiff's financial hardship in pursuing litigation
- The case being complex

WHAT DOES QUALIFY:
- Court-ordered stays
- Automatic stays (bankruptcy, appeal)
- Natural disasters or public health emergencies affecting court operations (COVID-related closures)
- Opposing party's intentional delay tactics that the court failed to sanction

THE BRUNS BURDEN OF PROOF:
The plaintiff bears the burden of proving the excluded period with "reasonably specific evidence" — not conclusory assertions. The evidence must be:
- Specific dates of the impossible/impracticable period
- The specific cause of the impossibility
- That the plaintiff was diligently pursuing the action but was prevented

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Bruns is the practical guide for calculating § 583.310 exclusions:

1. **COVID emergency orders**: The California Supreme Court issued emergency orders during 2020-2021 that tolled certain civil limitations periods. These orders may extend the five-year calculation for cases filed before or during COVID.

2. **APD/Government entity delays**: If any government defendant delayed producing discovery, failed to comply with discovery orders, or caused other discovery-related delays — and those delays prevented the case from reaching trial — document them specifically for § 583.340(c) purposes.

3. **Building the exclusion record**: For every case, maintain a contemporaneous log of:
   - Court closures and emergency orders
   - Discovery disputes and their resolution dates
   - Court scheduling delays attributable to the court
   - Any formal stays or injunctions
   These records are the evidence needed to prove a § 583.340(c) exclusion.

4. **Five-year deadline management**: The optimal practice is to set internal deadlines at three years (§ 583.420 discretionary dismissal risk) and four years (one year to trial preparation before the § 583.310 mandatory bar). Do not wait until year five to assess the timeline.
