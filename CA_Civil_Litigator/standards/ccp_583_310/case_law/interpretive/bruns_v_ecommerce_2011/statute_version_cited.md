# Statute Version Cited: CCP 583.310 and 583.340 as of 2011

## The Relevant Text

The court was reading two companion provisions:

**CCP 583.310:**
> "An action shall be brought to trial within five years after the action is commenced against the defendant."

**CCP 583.340(c):**
> "In computing the time within which an action must be brought to trial pursuant to this article, there shall be excluded the time during which any of the following conditions existed: ... (c) Bringing the action to trial, for any other reason, was impossible, impracticable, or futile."

## What the Court Was Deciding

The scope of the word "impracticable" in subdivision (c). How hard does it have to be to get to trial before the exception kicks in? Is court congestion enough?

The court held that impracticability requires more than difficulty. It requires circumstances beyond the plaintiff's control that made it effectively impossible -- not just harder -- to bring the case to trial. Court congestion is a predictable feature of the system, not an extraordinary obstacle.

## What Changed (and What Did Not)

CCP 583.310 and 583.340 have been substantively unchanged since their enactment in 1984 (Stats. 1984, Ch. 1705). Bruns interpreted the existing text without prompting any statutory amendment. The narrow construction of "impracticable" is a judicial interpretation that remains the governing standard.
