# Statute Version Cited: CCP 583.310 and 583.340 as of 2003

## The Relevant Text

The court was reading the same two provisions at issue in Hughes and Bruns:

**CCP 583.310:**
> "An action shall be brought to trial within five years after the action is commenced against the defendant."

**CCP 583.340(c):**
> "In computing the time within which an action must be brought to trial pursuant to this article, there shall be excluded the time during which any of the following conditions existed: ... (c) Bringing the action to trial, for any other reason, was impossible, impracticable, or futile."

## What the Court Was Deciding

Whether chronic court congestion -- a systemic problem, not a case-specific obstacle -- satisfies the impossibility/impracticability exception. The court held it does not, at least not without a showing that the plaintiff exercised reasonable diligence despite the congestion.

The reasoning: court congestion is a known, predictable condition. Plaintiffs must account for it when managing their case timeline. If you know the courts are backed up, you need to start pushing for trial dates early, not at the end of the five-year period.

## What Changed (and What Did Not)

CCP 583.310 and 583.340 were unchanged in 2003 from their 1984 enactment. The statutory text has remained stable. Sanchez applied the existing framework to a common fact pattern (court congestion) and reached the conclusion that most practitioners expected: congestion is not enough.

## Source

CCP 583.310 and 583.340 as enacted by Stats. 1984, Ch. 1705. Current version available at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov.
