# Sanchez v. City of Los Angeles, 109 Cal.App.4th 1262 (2003)

## What This Case Stands For

Court congestion -- even severe court congestion -- does not by itself establish impossibility or impracticability sufficient to toll the five-year mandatory dismissal period. The plaintiff must show they exercised reasonable diligence in prosecuting the case despite the congestion.

This is the practical application of the rule from Hughes and Bruns: you cannot point to a crowded court calendar as your excuse for failing to get to trial within five years. Every plaintiff faces the same congestion. The five-year rule exists precisely because the system needs a hard backstop against cases that languish indefinitely.

## The Rule

Under CCP 583.340(c):

1. **Court congestion alone** does not constitute impossibility or impracticability.
2. The plaintiff must demonstrate **reasonable diligence** in prosecuting the case -- requesting trial dates, opposing continuances, keeping the case moving.
3. If the plaintiff sat on the case for years and then blames court congestion for the final delay, the tolling exception will not save them.
4. The test looks at the plaintiff's conduct THROUGHOUT the five-year period, not just at the end.

## What It Means in Practice

- If you are a plaintiff approaching the five-year mark, you need a documented record of diligence: requests for trial dates, objections to continuances, motions to advance the trial.
- Passive prosecution -- filing a case and waiting for something to happen -- is a death sentence when the five-year clock is running.
- Even if the court genuinely cannot accommodate your trial within five years, you will need to show you tried everything possible to get there sooner.
- This is an appellate court decision, but it is consistent with the California Supreme Court's treatment of the issue in Hughes and Bruns, and it is widely cited.
- The practical lesson: treat the five-year deadline as a countdown from the moment you file. Do not wait until year four to start pushing for trial.
