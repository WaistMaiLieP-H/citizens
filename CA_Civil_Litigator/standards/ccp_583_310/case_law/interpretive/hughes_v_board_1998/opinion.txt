FIVE-YEAR RULE IN ADMINISTRATIVE MANDATE CONTEXT:
In administrative mandamus proceedings under CCP § 1094.5, the five-year dismissal rule of § 583.310 applies. However, the timing of the five-year period in agency review cases requires specific analysis because:

1. The action is typically filed in superior court to review an already-completed agency decision
2. The "trial" in § 1094.5 matters is typically a court hearing on the administrative record
3. The five-year period runs from the filing of the mandamus petition

THE FIVE-YEAR RULE AND ADMINISTRATIVE MANDAMUS:
Courts have generally held that the § 583.310 five-year rule applies to administrative mandamus proceedings just as to ordinary civil cases. The petitioner must bring the matter to trial (hearing on the merits) within five years of filing the petition.

DELAYED ADMINISTRATIVE PROCEEDINGS:
If an agency delays in transmitting the administrative record, or if the agency's own delays prevented the petitioner from proceeding to hearing, those delays may qualify as "impossible, impracticable, or futile" under § 583.340(c) — tolling the five-year period.

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

The five-year rule in administrative mandamus cases:
1. Any § 1094.5 administrative mandamus petition (CalVCB, DDS, professional license) must be brought to hearing within five years of filing
2. Agency-caused delays in producing the administrative record toll the period under the "impossible/impracticable" exception
3. The petitioner must document any agency delay that prevented timely hearing
