# Hughes v. Board of Architectural Examiners, 17 Cal.4th 763 (1998)

## What This Case Stands For

The five-year rule in CCP 583.310 is not a suggestion. It is mandatory and jurisdictional. When five years pass from the date you filed your complaint without the case being brought to trial, the court loses the POWER to try the case. Not the discretion -- the power.

No judge can waive it. No stipulation between the parties can extend it. No showing of good cause can excuse it. The only things that can extend the five-year period are the specific tolling provisions listed in CCP 583.340 (impossibility, impracticability, or futility).

## The Rule

Under CCP 583.310:

1. An action must be brought to trial within five years after the complaint is filed.
2. The five-year period is **mandatory and jurisdictional** -- the court loses power to act.
3. The period cannot be waived by stipulation, excused by good cause, or extended by judicial discretion.
4. The ONLY exceptions are the tolling provisions in CCP 583.340: impossibility, impracticability, or futility of bringing the case to trial.

## What It Means in Practice

- If your case has been pending for five years and has not gone to trial, it will be dismissed. Period.
- The plaintiff bears the burden of prosecuting the case diligently enough to reach trial within five years.
- Busy court calendars, settlement negotiations, and discovery disputes do not excuse missing the deadline.
- The only tolling that works is showing a genuine impossibility or impracticability beyond the plaintiff's control -- and even that is narrowly construed.
- This is one of the hardest deadlines in California civil litigation. There is no safety valve.
