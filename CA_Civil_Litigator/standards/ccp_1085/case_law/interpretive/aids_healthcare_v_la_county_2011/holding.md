# AIDS Healthcare Foundation v. Los Angeles County Dept. of Public Health, 197 Cal.App.4th 693 (2011)

## The Case

The AIDS Healthcare Foundation sued the Los Angeles County Department of Public Health, seeking mandamus to compel the department to enforce workplace safety regulations requiring condom use in adult film productions. The department argued it had discretion over how and whether to enforce those regulations. The Foundation argued the department's own regulations made enforcement mandatory — not optional.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **Mandamus under §1085 can compel a government agency to follow its own regulations, even when the underlying statute grants some discretion.** When an agency adopts regulations that bind itself — using mandatory language like "shall" — those self-imposed rules create ministerial duties enforceable by mandamus.

2. **An agency cannot hide behind statutory discretion when its own regulations eliminate that discretion.** If the Legislature gave the department discretion about how to implement a program, but the department then adopted regulations saying "we will do X when Y happens," the department must do X when Y happens. It chose to bind itself, and mandamus holds it to that choice.

3. **The distinction matters because many statutes delegate broad authority while the implementing regulations create specific, mandatory procedures.** A practitioner must look beyond the statute to the regulations to determine whether the duty is ministerial.

## The Rule

**When a government agency adopts regulations using mandatory language, those regulations create ministerial duties enforceable by §1085 mandamus — even if the underlying statute grants the agency broad discretion. The agency's own rules can convert discretionary authority into ministerial obligation.**

## Why This Case Matters for §1085

This case fills an important gap. Manjares (1966) said mandamus reaches ministerial duties. Common Cause (1989) said "shall" in a statute creates ministerial duty. AIDS Healthcare Foundation adds: "shall" in an agency's own regulations also creates ministerial duty. This means practitioners must read the regulations, not just the statute, to determine whether mandamus is available.
