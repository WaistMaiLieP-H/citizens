# Common Cause v. Board of Supervisors, 49 Cal.3d 432 (1989)

## The Case

Common Cause, a government-accountability organization, sued the Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors for violating the Ralph M. Brown Act (Government Code §54950 et seq.) — California's open-meeting law. The Board had conducted closed-session discussions on redistricting that Common Cause argued should have been public. Common Cause sought mandamus under CCP §1085 to compel compliance with the Brown Act's open-meeting requirements.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **Mandamus under §1085 is the appropriate remedy to compel a public body to comply with the Brown Act's open-meeting requirements.** When a statute says public meetings "shall" be open to the public, that is a ministerial duty — the public body has no discretion to close the meeting outside the specific statutory exceptions.

2. **The word "shall" in a statute creates a ministerial duty that mandamus can enforce.** When the Brown Act says meetings "shall" be open, that word is not advisory. Mandamus gives "shall" its teeth — it turns a legislative command into an enforceable obligation.

3. **The Board's redistricting discussions did not fall within any closed-session exception.** The Brown Act lists specific topics that may be discussed in closed session (pending litigation, personnel, labor negotiations, real estate). Redistricting is not on the list, so the meetings had to be public.

## The Rule

**Mandamus under §1085 enforces statutory "shall" commands against public bodies. When a statute requires a public body to do something and uses mandatory language, the duty is ministerial and mandamus is the proper tool to compel compliance.**

## Why This Case Matters for §1085

Common Cause is the leading case connecting §1085 mandamus to open-government laws. It demonstrates that mandamus is not just for individual rights — it is the enforcement mechanism for structural transparency requirements. Any time a government body ignores a "shall" in a statute, §1085 mandamus is the tool that forces compliance.
