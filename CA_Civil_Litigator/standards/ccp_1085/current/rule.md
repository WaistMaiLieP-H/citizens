# Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 1085 — Traditional Mandamus (Writ of Mandate)

**Standard ID:** CCP_1085_TRADITIONAL_MANDAMUS
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** A writ of mandate may be issued by any court to any inferior tribunal, corporation, board, or person, to compel the performance of an act which the law specially enjoins, as a duty resulting from an office, trust, or station, or to compel the admission of a party to the use and enjoyment of a right or office to which the party is entitled, and from which the party is unlawfully precluded by that inferior tribunal, corporation, board, or person.

**(b)** The appellate division of the superior court may grant a writ of mandate directed to the superior court in a limited civil case or in a misdemeanor or infraction case.

---

## Two-Part Test (Traditional Mandamus)

1. **Clear, present, ministerial duty** — The respondent must have a clear, present duty to perform the act. Ministerial means the duty is prescribed by law with no discretion; the official has no choice but to act.

2. **Clear, present right to relief** — The petitioner must have a beneficial interest in the performance of the duty — a direct stake in the outcome.

Distinguished from § 1094.5 (Administrative Mandamus): § 1085 compels the performance of a ministerial act. § 1094.5 reviews the correctness of a quasi-judicial decision made after a required hearing.

---

## Plain English

When a government official or body has a legal duty to do something — issue a permit, process an application, release a record, certify a result — and they refuse or ignore the duty, a writ of mandate under § 1085 is the mechanism to compel them. The court orders: do the thing the law requires you to do. No discretion is involved — either the law requires the act or it doesn't.
