# Felder v. Casey, 487 U.S. 131 (1988)

## What This Case Stands For

If you are suing under 42 U.S.C. 1983 for a civil rights violation, you do NOT have to comply with state tort claim notice-of-claim requirements -- even if you file the 1983 action in state court.

This is a Supremacy Clause case. The U.S. Supreme Court held that state notice-of-claim statutes (like California's Government Claims Act requirement to file a claim before suing) are preempted when applied to federal civil rights actions. These state procedural hurdles would burden the vindication of federal rights, and Congress did not intend for 1983 claims to be subject to state-created prerequisites.

## The Rule

State tort claim presentation requirements -- including the Government Claims Act's requirement to file a claim with the public entity before filing suit -- **do not apply** to actions brought under 42 U.S.C. 1983. This is true whether the 1983 action is filed in federal court or in California state court.

The Supremacy Clause of the U.S. Constitution preempts state procedural requirements that would burden the enforcement of federal civil rights.

## What It Means in Practice

- If a government employee violates your constitutional rights (excessive force, due process, equal protection), you can file a 1983 suit WITHOUT first filing a government tort claim under Gov. Code 910 et seq.
- This is critical in California because the Government Claims Act normally requires you to present a claim to the public entity within six months of the injury (Gov. Code 911.2). Missing that deadline normally bars your state law tort claims entirely. But 1983 claims are exempt.
- You still need to meet the 1983 statute of limitations (which borrows the state personal injury limitations period -- two years under CCP 335.1 in California). But you do NOT need to present a government claim first.
- This is the single most important case for anyone asserting federal civil rights claims against California government actors. It removes the Government Claims Act as a barrier to 1983 litigation.
