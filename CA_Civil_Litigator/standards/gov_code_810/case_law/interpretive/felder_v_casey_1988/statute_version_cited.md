# Statute Version Cited: Gov. Code 810 et seq. and Wisconsin Notice-of-Claim Statute (1988)

## The Relevant Text

Felder involved Wisconsin's notice-of-claim statute (Wis. Stat. 893.80), but the holding applies to ALL state notice-of-claim requirements, including California's Government Claims Act (Gov. Code 900-935.6, particularly 910-911.2).

The California Government Claims Act requires that before suing a public entity for money damages, you must present a claim to the entity:

- **Gov. Code 910**: Contents of the claim.
- **Gov. Code 911.2**: Claim must be presented within six months of accrual for most causes of action.
- **Gov. Code 945.4**: No suit may be brought against a public entity until the claim is acted upon or deemed rejected.

## What the Court Was Deciding

Whether these state procedural prerequisites apply to federal 1983 claims filed in state court. The answer: they do not.

## The Preemption

42 U.S.C. 1983 provides a federal cause of action enforceable in state court under the Supremacy Clause. State procedural requirements that condition the right to bring a 1983 action -- like presenting a tort claim first -- are preempted because they impose burdens on federal rights that Congress did not authorize.

## What Changed (and What Did Not)

The Government Claims Act text was not changed by this decision. The statute still requires claim presentation for state law tort claims against public entities. But as a matter of federal constitutional law, that requirement cannot be enforced against 1983 plaintiffs.

## Source
