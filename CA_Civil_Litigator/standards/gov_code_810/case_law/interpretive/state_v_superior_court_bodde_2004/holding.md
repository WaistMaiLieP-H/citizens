# State of California v. Superior Court (Bodde), 32 Cal.4th 1234 (2004)

## The Case

Robert Bodde was injured while riding his bicycle when he hit a pothole on a state highway. He sued the State of California for dangerous condition of public property under Government Code § 835. The State argued Bodde's claim was barred because his government tort claim filed under Government Code § 910 et seq. was defective — it failed to describe the indebtedness or injury with sufficient specificity.

## What the Court Held

Justice Brown, writing for the majority:

1. **The Government Claims Act (Gov. Code § 810 et seq.) is a prerequisite to suit — not merely a procedural formality.** The claim presentation requirement serves multiple purposes: it gives the public entity an opportunity to investigate, evaluate, and settle meritorious claims before litigation; it allows for fiscal planning; and it protects public entities from surprise.

2. **Substantial compliance with the claim requirements is sufficient.** A claim need not be perfect — it is adequate if it gives the public entity enough information to investigate and respond. The purpose of the statute is notice, not technical perfection.

3. **However, a claim that fails to identify the public entity's alleged act or omission, or the nature of the injury, is not substantially compliant.** The entity must be able to determine what happened, where, when, and what injury resulted.

## The Rule

**The Government Claims Act requires substantial compliance — the claim must provide enough information for the public entity to investigate and evaluate the claim. Technical perfection is not required, but the entity must be able to identify the incident, the alleged wrongdoing, and the nature of the injury.**
