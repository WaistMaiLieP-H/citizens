STATE OF CALIFORNIA v. SUPERIOR COURT (BODDE)
32 Cal.4th 1234 (2004)
California Supreme Court
Decided: December 20, 2004

---

FACTS:

Plaintiffs (Bodde and others) filed complaints against the State of California for injuries arising from government conduct without first presenting a claim under the Government Claims Act. The State moved for judgment on the pleadings, arguing that failure to allege compliance with the Government Claims Act claim presentation requirement required dismissal.

The trial court denied the motion. The Court of Appeal reversed. The California Supreme Court granted review to resolve the fundamental question of pleading requirements in government entity tort actions.

---

HOLDING:

THE BODDE RULE — CLAIM PRESENTATION MUST BE PLEADED AND PROVED:
"The Government Claims Act requirement that a plaintiff must present a claim to the relevant government entity before filing suit is not merely an affirmative defense — it is an element of the plaintiff's cause of action that must be pleaded in the complaint."

"A plaintiff who fails to allege timely claim presentation in the complaint, or who fails to allege facts showing that claim presentation was excused or tolled, has not stated a cause of action against the government entity and the complaint is subject to demurrer."

PLEADING REQUIREMENT — WHAT MUST APPEAR IN THE COMPLAINT:
Under Bodde, a complaint against a California government entity for a state tort must affirmatively allege:
1. That a Government Claims Act claim was timely presented to the entity; OR
2. Facts establishing that presentation was excused (late claim relief under § 946.6, or an applicable accrual rule); OR
3. That the cause of action falls within an exception to the claim presentation requirement

FAILURE TO PLEAD = FAILURE TO STATE A CAUSE OF ACTION:
"The complaint need not attach the claim or set forth its contents verbatim, but it must allege that a timely claim was presented. Omission of this allegation is a pleading defect that can be raised by demurrer, by motion for judgment on the pleadings, or at any stage of the litigation."

THE BODDE DISTINCTION — DEMURRER vs. AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE:
Prior cases treated failure to comply with the Claims Act as an affirmative defense. Bodde resolved this question definitively: claim presentation compliance is an element of the plaintiff's cause of action, not a defense. The practical consequence is that the plaintiff must plead it, not wait for the defendant to raise it.

---

WHAT CONSTITUTES TIMELY CLAIM PRESENTATION:

Under the Government Claims Act framework:
- Claims for personal injury, property damage, and wrongful death: must be presented within six months of accrual (Gov. Code § 911.2(a))
- Claims for breach of contract and other causes: within one year of accrual (Gov. Code § 911.2(b))
- Accrual for purposes of the Act generally follows the discovery rule (not the date of occurrence)

THE LATE CLAIM PETITION PATHWAY:
If a claimant misses the six-month deadline, § 946.6 allows filing a petition in superior court for leave to present a late claim. The petition must be filed within six months of the expiration of the claim presentation period and must show:
- Mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect; AND
- The government entity would not be prejudiced by late presentation

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Bodde is mandatory pleading doctrine for every California state tort claim against a government entity:

1. **Every complaint against a California government defendant must affirmatively plead Government Claims Act compliance**: For Civil C25-01403 (Hartmann v. Hillberg) and any related state court filings against City of Antioch, Contra Costa County, CalVCB, or APD — the complaint must allege in the opening paragraphs that a timely Government Claims Act claim was presented and was rejected (or deemed rejected).

2. **Demurrer trap**: Failure to plead Government Claims Act compliance gives the government defendant a demurrer weapon. Curable by amendment — but it delays the case and signals a pleading gap.

3. **Allegations that satisfy Bodde**:
   Example: "On [date], Plaintiff presented a Government Claim to the City of Antioch in compliance with Government Code § 910. The claim was rejected on [date] [or: the claim was deemed rejected on [date] by passage of 45 days without action]. This action is timely filed within six months of the rejection."

4. **CalVCB application**: CalVCB is a state agency. Petitions for writ of mandate challenging CalVCB decisions under CCP § 1094.5 also require timely administrative exhaustion. Bodde's principle (plead compliance) applies to the administrative record completeness allegations in the mandamus petition.

5. **Coordination with § 340.5 and § 583.310**: The claim presentation deadline is separate from the statute of limitations and the five-year trial deadline. Claim presentation (six months) → rejection → six months to file suit (§ 945.6). This compressed timeline means the actual window for filing a state court complaint against a government entity may be as short as 12 months from injury.
