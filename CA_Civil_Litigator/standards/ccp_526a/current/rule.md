# Cal. Code of Civil Procedure § 526a — Taxpayer Standing to Sue Local Government

**Standard ID:** CCP_526A_TAXPAYER_STANDING
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** An action to obtain a judgment, restraining and preventing any illegal expenditure of, waste of, or injury to, the estate, funds, or other property of a local agency, may be maintained against any officer thereof, or any agent, or other person, acting in its behalf, either by a **resident** therein, or by a corporation, who is assessed for and is liable to pay, or, within one year before the commencement of the action, has paid, a tax that funds the defendant local agency.

**(b)** This section does not affect any right of action in favor of a local agency, or any public officer; provided, that no injunction shall be granted restraining the offering for sale, sale, or issuance of any municipal bonds for public improvements or public utilities.

**(c)** An action brought pursuant to this section to enjoin a public improvement project shall take special precedence over all civil matters on the calendar of the court.

**Definitions (§ 526a(d)):**
- "Local agency" — a city, town, county, city and county, district, public authority, or other political subdivision.
- "Resident" — a person who lives, works, owns property, or attends school in the jurisdiction of the defendant local agency.

---

## Standing Requirements

To sue under § 526a, the plaintiff must establish:

1. **Taxpayer status** — assessed for, liable to pay, or paid within the past year a tax that funds the defendant agency.
2. **Local agency defendant** — only applies to local agencies, not state agencies.
3. **Illegal expenditure, waste, or injury** — the challenged conduct must be illegal (beyond lack of wisdom or poor policy).

---

## Plain English

If a local government is spending money illegally — unauthorized spending, spending in excess of appropriations, outright waste — any taxpayer who pays taxes to that agency can sue to stop it, even if their individual harm is identical to every other taxpayer's. This bypasses the general standing doctrine that would otherwise block such suits. "Resident" is broadly defined: you qualify if you live, work, own property, or go to school in the jurisdiction.
