# Wirin v. Parker, 48 Cal.2d 890 (1957)

## The Case

A.L. Wirin, a civil liberties attorney and taxpayer, sued Los Angeles Police Chief William Parker, alleging the LAPD was conducting illegal intelligence-gathering operations — mass surveillance, infiltration of lawful organizations, and maintaining dossiers on law-abiding citizens. Wirin brought the suit under CCP §526a as a taxpayer, arguing public funds were being spent on illegal activities. The defendants argued §526a only covered financial waste, not challenges to government conduct itself.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **Section 526a taxpayer standing extends beyond pure fiscal waste to challenges of illegal government conduct.** A taxpayer does not have to show the government spent money unwisely in a bookkeeping sense. If the government is spending money to do something illegal, that is "wasteful" expenditure within the meaning of §526a.

2. **Illegal conduct by public officials, funded by taxpayer money, is itself a form of waste.** The court reasoned that any expenditure on an unlawful program is inherently wasteful because the government has no authority to spend public funds on illegal operations.

3. **Taxpayers have standing under §526a to challenge unconstitutional police practices.** The suit was not just about the dollar amount spent — it was about the legality of what the money was buying.

## The Rule

**Section 526a standing reaches any challenge to illegal government conduct funded by taxpayer money. The illegality of the conduct is itself the waste — taxpayers need not prove separate financial harm beyond the expenditure itself.**

## Why This Case Matters for §526a

Before Wirin, §526a could have been read narrowly as a fiscal-watchdog statute — only useful when the government overpaid for something or spent money without authorization. Wirin expanded it into a tool for challenging unconstitutional government programs, opening the door for taxpayer suits against illegal police practices, civil rights violations, and other unlawful government conduct.

## Citation
