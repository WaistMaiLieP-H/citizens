# Coshow v. City of Escondido, 132 Cal.App.4th 687 (2005)

## The Case

Coshow, a taxpayer, sued the City of Escondido challenging a city policy related to day laborers and immigration enforcement. The question on appeal included whether §526a provides taxpayer standing to challenge state government spending, or only local government spending.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **Section 526a taxpayer standing is limited to challenges against local agencies — counties, cities, towns, and similar local government entities.** The statute's text lists specific types of local government bodies. It does not include the state government.

2. **A taxpayer cannot use §526a to challenge state-level spending or state government conduct.** For challenges to state action, other mechanisms exist (such as declaratory relief, or standing doctrines under the California Constitution), but §526a is not one of them.

3. **The limitation is textual, not policy-based.** The Legislature wrote §526a to cover local government. If it wanted to extend taxpayer standing to state government challenges, it could amend the statute, but courts cannot read that extension in.

## The Rule

**Section 526a provides taxpayer standing only against local government entities (counties, cities, towns, districts). It does not provide standing to challenge state government spending or conduct. Use other legal mechanisms for state-level challenges.**

## Why This Case Matters for §526a

This is the boundary case. Wirin and Blair expanded what §526a can reach (illegal conduct, intangible interests), but Coshow draws the line on who can be sued under it. Practitioners must know that §526a is a local-government tool. Filing a §526a suit against a state agency is a jurisdictional mistake.

## Citation

Coshow v. City of Escondido, 132 Cal.App.4th 687 (2005). Court of Appeal, Fourth Appellate District, Division One.
