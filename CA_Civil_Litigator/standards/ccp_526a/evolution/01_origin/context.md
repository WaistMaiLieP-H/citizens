# CCP 526a -- Origin Context

## Enactment

Originally enacted 1909. Stats. 1909, Ch. 348. Substantially amended multiple times, most recently by Stats. 2021, Ch. 224 (A.B. 1578), which modernized the definition of "resident" and "local agency."

## The Wound

Local government spends taxpayer money. Sometimes that spending is illegal -- unauthorized by law, in excess of appropriations, or constituting waste of public funds. Under ordinary standing rules, a private citizen has no right to sue unless they can show a direct, personal injury different from the injury to the general public. This means that illegal government spending -- no matter how blatant -- could continue unchecked unless the government chose to sue itself, or the Attorney General chose to act.

Taxpayers were paying for illegal expenditures and had no way to stop them. The government was spending their money in violation of the law, and the courts would not hear their complaints because they lacked "standing" -- they could not show they were injured differently than every other taxpayer.

## The Promise

Any taxpayer of a local agency can sue to stop illegal expenditure, waste, or injury to public property. You do not need to show individual injury. You do not need to show you were harmed differently than anyone else. Your status as a taxpayer is your standing.

The statute gives taxpayers the power to be the check on local government that elections alone cannot provide. Elections happen every few years. Illegal spending happens every day. Section 526a bridges the gap.

## What Counts as a "Taxpayer"

The statute was modernized in 2021 to define "resident" broadly: a person who lives, works, owns property, or attends school in the jurisdiction. The tax that confers standing can be income tax, sales tax, property tax, or a business license tax. Even a sales tax paid as a consumer to a retailer counts.

## Why This Statute Exists

Without taxpayer standing, government accountability depends entirely on internal controls and the willingness of government officers to police themselves. Section 526a creates an external check. It says: the people who pay the taxes have the right to ensure those taxes are spent legally.

This is one of the oldest government accountability statutes in California. It has survived for over a century because the problem it addresses -- illegal government spending -- never goes away.

## Source

Stats. 1909, Ch. 348. Substantially rewritten by Stats. 2021, Ch. 224 (A.B. 1578), effective January 1, 2022.
