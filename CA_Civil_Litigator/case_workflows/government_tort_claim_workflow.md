# Government Tort Claim Workflow

## The Gateway to Suing California Public Entities

---

### Phase 1: DETERMINE IF CLAIM PRESENTATION IS REQUIRED

**Required for:** ALL state-law claims against public entities and their employees
**NOT required for:** Federal §1983 claims (Felder v. Casey)

**Decision tree:**
- Is the defendant a public entity (city, county, state, school district, special district)? → Claim required
- Is the defendant a public employee acting within scope? → Claim required against the entity; may be required against the employee
- Is the claim federal (§1983, ADA, §504)? → NO claim required
- Is the claim state (Bane Act, negligence, state constitutional)? → Claim REQUIRED

**Critical split:** A complaint with both §1983 and Bane Act claims has TWO tracks:
- §1983 → no claim needed → file directly in court
- Bane Act → claim needed → must present and wait for rejection before filing

---

### Phase 2: CALCULATE DEADLINES

| Claim Type | Deadline | Code Section |
|---|---|---|
| Personal injury / death | **6 months** from accrual | Gov. Code §911.2(a) |
| Property damage | **6 months** from accrual | Gov. Code §911.2(a) |
| All other claims | **1 year** from accrual | Gov. Code §911.2(a) |
| Late-claim application | **1 year** from accrual | Gov. Code §911.4 |

**Accrual = discovery rule:** Clock starts when the plaintiff knew or should have known of the injury AND its cause.

**If deadline missed:**
- Apply for late-claim relief under Gov. Code §946.6
- Must show: excusable neglect, minority, incapacity, or that the entity was not prejudiced
- File application within 1 year of accrual (absolute outer limit)
- If denied → petition the court under §946.6(a)

---

### Phase 3: PREPARE AND PRESENT THE CLAIM

**Required contents (Gov. Code §910):**
1. Name and address of claimant
2. Address to which notices should be sent
3. Date, place, and circumstances of the occurrence
4. Description of the injury, damage, or loss
5. Names of public employees causing the injury (if known)
6. Amount claimed (if under $10,000, state the amount; if over, indicate whether the claim would be a limited or unlimited civil case)

**Present to:**
- City claims → City Clerk
- County claims → County Clerk or Board of Supervisors
- State claims → Department of General Services (Victim Compensation and Government Claims Board) — $25 filing fee (fee waiver available)
- Special district → Clerk or Secretary of the governing board

**Proof of presentation:** Certified mail, return receipt requested. Keep the receipt.

---

### Phase 4: WAIT FOR RESPONSE

| Response | Timeline | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Written rejection | Starts 6-month litigation clock | Must sue within 6 months of rejection notice |
| No response within 45 days | Deemed rejected | 2-year litigation window from deemed rejection |
| Written acceptance | Claim resolved | No lawsuit needed |

**The deemed-rejection route (no response in 45 days) gives the LONGEST litigation window — 2 years instead of 6 months.** Some practitioners prefer no response over rejection for this reason.

---

### Phase 5: FILE THE LAWSUIT

**After rejection or deemed rejection:**
- File in Superior Court (state claims) or include as supplemental claims in federal court (if paired with §1983)
- Allege compliance with the Government Claims Act in the complaint
- Attach the claim and the rejection (or allege deemed rejection)

**If no claim was presented:**
- State claims are BARRED (§945.4)
- Federal claims survive (Felder v. Casey)
- The complaint can proceed on federal claims only, with state claims dismissed

### Cross-Citizen Handoff

This workflow feeds into:
- **US Federal Civil Rights Litigator** → §1983 claims proceed independently of tort claim
- **CA Civil Rights Litigator** → Bane Act claims require tort claim; this workflow provides the prerequisite
- **CA Conservator Investigator** → Claims against public conservators or court investigators require tort claim
