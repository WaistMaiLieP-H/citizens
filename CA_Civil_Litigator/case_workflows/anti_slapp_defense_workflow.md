# Anti-SLAPP Defense Workflow (CCP §425.16)

## When You Are Hit With a SLAPP — or When the Defendant Hits You With an Anti-SLAPP

---

### Scenario A: STEWARD IS DEFENDANT — Someone Sues to Silence

**If someone sues the steward for exercising free speech or petition rights (filing complaints, testifying, publicizing government misconduct):**

1. **File anti-SLAPP motion within 60 days of service** (§425.16(f))
2. **Discovery is automatically stayed** upon filing (§425.16(g))
3. **Two-step analysis:**
   - Step 1 (defendant's burden): Show the claim arises from protected activity — speech or petition in connection with a public issue (§425.16(e))
   - Step 2 (plaintiff's burden): If Step 1 met, plaintiff must show probability of prevailing
4. **If motion granted:** Mandatory fee award to the steward (§425.16(c)(1))
5. **Immediately appealable** (§425.16(i))

### Scenario B: STEWARD IS PLAINTIFF — Defendant Files Anti-SLAPP Against Your Claims

**If the steward sues and the defendant brings an anti-SLAPP motion:**

1. **Discovery stays automatically** — the steward cannot conduct discovery until the motion is resolved
2. **The steward must show "probability of prevailing"** with evidence available at the pleading stage
3. **Request limited discovery** under §425.16(g) — "good cause shown" for specific, targeted discovery necessary to oppose the motion
4. **Baral v. Schnitt (2016):** Courts analyze specific allegations, not entire causes of action. Some allegations may be struck (arising from protected activity) while others survive.
5. **If the anti-SLAPP motion is frivolous:** The steward recovers fees under §425.16(c)(1) + CCP §128.5

### Key Exemptions

- **Government enforcement actions** are exempt (§425.16(d)) — AG, DA, city attorney actions cannot be anti-SLAPPed
- **Government transparency claims** (Brown Act, Public Records Act) — fees are not awarded against the plaintiff even if the anti-SLAPP motion succeeds (§425.16(c)(2))

### For the Steward's Cases

The steward's activities — filing complaints, requesting public records, petitioning government agencies, publicizing findings of government misconduct — are core protected activity under §425.16(e). If anyone sues to stop these activities, the anti-SLAPP statute is the first line of defense.

Conversely, if the steward sues and the defendants claim their conduct was "protected activity" (filing police reports, making statements in official proceedings), the steward must be prepared to show that the claims are based on the CONDUCT (false reports, fabricated evidence) not the SPEECH (the act of reporting itself).
