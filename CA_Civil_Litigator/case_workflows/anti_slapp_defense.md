# Anti-SLAPP Motion (CCP Section 425.16)

Operational workflow for filing an anti-SLAPP special motion to strike under the California Code of Civil Procedure.

---

## Step 1: File Within 60 Days

The motion must be filed within **60 days of service** of the complaint, cross-complaint, or amended complaint that contains the claims you want to strike (CCP Section 425.16(f)).

The court may extend this deadline for good cause, but do not rely on that. Calendar the 60-day deadline immediately upon service.

Corpus reference: `standards/ccp_425_16/current/ccp_425_16_leginfo.txt`

---

## Step 2: Meet Defendant's Burden — Show the Claim Arises from Protected Activity

This is the first prong. The defendant must demonstrate that the claim being challenged arises from an act in furtherance of the defendant's right of petition or free speech in connection with a public issue.

Section 425.16(e) defines four categories of protected activity:

1. **(e)(1)** — A written or oral statement made before a legislative, executive, or judicial proceeding, or any other official proceeding authorized by law.
2. **(e)(2)** — A written or oral statement made in connection with an issue under consideration or review by a legislative, executive, or judicial body, or any other official proceeding.
3. **(e)(3)** — A written or oral statement made in a place open to the public or a public forum in connection with an issue of public interest.
4. **(e)(4)** — Any other conduct in furtherance of the exercise of the constitutional right of petition or free speech in connection with a public issue or an issue of public interest.

Identify which subsection applies. Most litigation-related claims fall under (e)(1) or (e)(2). Public commentary claims fall under (e)(3) or (e)(4).

Corpus reference: `standards/ccp_425_16/case_law/interpretive/navellier_v_sletten_2002/holding.md`

---

## Step 3: Shift the Burden — Plaintiff Must Show Probability of Prevailing

If the defendant meets the first prong, the burden shifts to the plaintiff. The plaintiff must demonstrate a probability of prevailing on the merits of the claim.

This requires the plaintiff to present admissible evidence that, if credited, would be sufficient to sustain a favorable judgment. It is similar to the standard for opposing summary judgment — the plaintiff cannot rely on the allegations in the complaint alone but must produce evidence.

The court does not weigh the evidence or resolve factual disputes at this stage. It only asks whether the plaintiff has presented enough evidence that a reasonable trier of fact could find in the plaintiff's favor.

---

## Step 4: Discovery Is Automatically Stayed

Upon the filing of an anti-SLAPP motion, all discovery in the action is **automatically stayed** (Section 425.16(g)).

The stay remains in effect until the court rules on the motion, unless the court orders otherwise on noticed motion and for good cause.

This is a significant tactical advantage. It means the plaintiff cannot take depositions, serve interrogatories, or request documents while the motion is pending, which limits the plaintiff's ability to develop evidence to meet the probability-of-prevailing burden.

---

## Step 5: Mandatory Attorney's Fees If Defendant Prevails

If the motion is granted (defendant wins), the defendant is entitled to **mandatory attorney's fees and costs** under Section 425.16(c)(1). The word "shall" in the statute means the court must award fees — there is no discretion.

Fees are calculated using the lodestar method and can be substantial, especially if the motion required significant briefing and a hearing.

---

## Step 6: Plaintiff's Fee Recovery If Motion Is Frivolous

If the anti-SLAPP motion is found to be frivolous or solely intended to cause unnecessary delay, the plaintiff can recover attorney's fees under CCP Section 128.5.

This cuts both ways. Do not file anti-SLAPP motions that have no basis just to get the discovery stay. The fee exposure works in both directions.

---

## Step 7: Immediately Appealable

An order granting or denying an anti-SLAPP motion is **immediately appealable** under Section 425.16(i). This is an exception to the normal rule that only final judgments are appealable.

This means:

- If the motion is denied, the defendant can appeal immediately without waiting for trial.
- If the motion is granted, the plaintiff can appeal immediately.
- The appeal does not stay the proceedings unless a stay is separately obtained.

---

## Step 8: Mixed Claims — Apply Baral v. Schnitt

When a cause of action includes both protected and unprotected activity, the court applies the framework from Baral v. Schnitt (2016). The court does not strike the entire cause of action — it strikes only the allegations that arise from protected activity.

This means:

- Identify which specific allegations within each cause of action arise from protected activity.
- The anti-SLAPP analysis applies only to those allegations.
- The unprotected allegations survive the motion and proceed to trial.

Corpus reference: `standards/ccp_425_16/case_law/interpretive/baral_v_schnitt_2016/holding.md`

---

## Step 9: Commercial Speech Exception

Section 425.17 creates a limited exception for claims arising from commercial speech. If the defendant's speech was made to promote the sale of goods or services, the anti-SLAPP statute may not apply.

Check whether the challenged speech was purely commercial before filing. If it was, the motion may be denied on this basis alone.

Corpus reference: `standards/ccp_425_16/case_law/interpretive/filmon_v_doubleverify_2019/holding.md`
