# Grafilo v. Wolfsohn, 33 Cal.App.5th 1024 (2019)

## The Case

A patient sought access to their medical records from a health care provider. The provider either delayed production, produced incomplete records, or imposed conditions on access that the patient argued violated CMIA §56.20. The case addressed the scope and enforceability of the patient's statutory right to inspect and copy medical records and the provider's affirmative obligations under the statute.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal held:

1. **The patient's right to access medical records under §56.20 is mandatory, not discretionary.** The statute uses "shall" — the provider must permit inspection and provide copies within the statutory timeframe. A provider cannot condition access on the patient's stated reason for the request, impose additional requirements beyond a written request, or selectively withhold portions of the record.

2. **The 5-working-day deadline is enforceable.** A provider that fails to produce copies within 5 working days of receiving a written request has violated §56.20(b). The statute does not provide for extensions, and the provider's internal administrative difficulties do not excuse non-compliance.

3. **The right to access is a substantive patient right, not merely a procedural convenience.** Denial of access to one's own medical records is an injury in itself — it deprives the patient of the ability to verify their medical history, seek second opinions, and participate meaningfully in their own care decisions.

## The Rule

**Providers must produce copies of medical records within 5 working days of a written request. The obligation is mandatory. Failure to comply is a violation of the patient's substantive right to access their own medical information.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case: every provider who held the steward's medical records — Providence St. Joseph, Dr. Wiseman's practice, Walgreens, Blue Shield, any psychiatrist or evaluator — was obligated to produce copies within 5 working days of a written request. If any provider delayed, refused, or produced incomplete records, Grafilo confirms that the provider violated the steward's substantive right under §56.20. The violation is actionable regardless of why the provider failed to comply.
