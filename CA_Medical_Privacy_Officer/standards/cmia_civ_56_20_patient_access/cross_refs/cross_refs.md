# CMIA Employer Confidentiality Obligations — Cross-References

| Standard | Relationship | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| CMIA_CIV_56_10_DISCLOSURE | SUPPLEMENTS | § 56.10 governs provider/plan/contractor disclosures; § 56.20 governs employer use and disclosure of employee medical information — both apply to the same patient but from different entity perspectives; a physician sharing a patient's records with the patient's employer may violate both § 56.10 and § 56.20 simultaneously |
| CMIA_CIV_56_11_AUTHORIZATION | SUPPLEMENTS | § 56.20(c) requires a signed § 56.11 authorization for employer use or disclosure outside the four enumerated exceptions — the § 56.11 validity requirements apply to employer-obtained authorizations as well as provider-obtained ones |
| CMIA_CIV_56_35_DAMAGES | ENFORCES | A patient who suffered economic loss or personal injury from an employer's § 56.20 violation may recover under § 56.35 — § 56.35 explicitly lists § 56.20 as a covered violation alongside § 56.10 |
| CMIA_CIV_56_36_REMEDIES | ENFORCES | § 56.36 penalties apply to employer violations of § 56.20 — employers who knowingly and willfully misuse employee medical information face the same nominal damages, civil penalties, and criminal exposure as health care providers |
| CMIA_CIV_56_05_DEFINITIONS | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.20 uses § 56.05's definition of "medical information" — the employer's obligation attaches when it possesses "medical information" as CMIA defines it, not merely when it holds a document labeled as a medical record |
