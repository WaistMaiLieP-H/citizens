# CMIA Definitions — Medical Information, Provider, Patient — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Civ. Code § 56.05
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** 1981 (most recent amendment: Stats. 2020, Ch. 190, Sec. 1, AB 713, effective January 1, 2021)

## The Rule

Section 56.05 establishes the vocabulary that determines who and what is covered by the CMIA. Getting the definitions right determines whether the CMIA applies at all — if the information at issue is "medical information," if the disclosing entity is a "provider," "plan," or "contractor," and if the person is a "patient," then the entire CMIA protection framework attaches. If any element falls outside the definitions, the CMIA may not apply.

The three most legally significant definitions are:

**Medical information (§ 56.05(j)):** Any individually identifiable information, in any form — electronic or physical — in the possession of or derived from a provider, plan, pharmaceutical company, or contractor regarding a patient's medical history, mental or physical condition, or treatment. "Individually identifiable" means it includes any element sufficient to identify the person (name, address, email, phone, SSN) or that, combined with public information, reveals identity. Immigration status and place of birth, if known or collected by a covered entity, are treated as medical information.

**Patient (§ 56.05(m)):** A natural person, whether or not still living, who received health care services from a provider and to whom medical information pertains.

**Provider of health care (§ 56.05(p)):** Any person licensed or certified under Division 2 of the Business and Professions Code; any person licensed under the Osteopathic Initiative Act or Chiropractic Initiative Act; any person certified under Division 2.5 of the Health and Safety Code; or any clinic, health dispensary, or health facility licensed under Division 2 of the Health and Safety Code.

## Key Elements

1. **"Medical information" is format-neutral:** Paper, electronic, digital, handwritten — all covered. The CMIA was written before EHRs and has been amended to explicitly cover electronic form.
2. **Derivation counts:** Information derived from a provider's records is protected even if it is not itself a medical record — an insurance company's actuarial summary derived from patient records is "medical information" if it is individually identifiable.
3. **Pharmaceutical companies are covered:** Unlike HIPAA, which covers only providers, plans, and clearinghouses, CMIA explicitly covers pharmaceutical companies.
4. **Contractor is broadly defined:** Any medical group, IPA, pharmaceutical benefits manager, or medical services organization that is not itself a provider or plan — the entity does not need to be licensed to fall within CMIA coverage.
5. **Mental health application information and reproductive health application information** are added as specific protected categories under subdivisions (k)-(r) — digital health apps collecting this data are covered.

## What This Means in Practice

The CMIA's definition of medical information is broader than HIPAA's definition of Protected Health Information in several respects. CMIA covers pharmaceutical companies and digital health applications. The definition's breadth means that any document, data point, or communication in the possession of a covered entity that relates to a patient's health history, condition, or treatment — and that can be linked to that patient — is fully protected by the CMIA's disclosure prohibitions and civil remedies.
