# Eisenhower Medical Center v. Superior Court, 226 Cal.App.4th 430 (2014)

## The Case

Plaintiffs brought a class action alleging that Eisenhower Medical Center improperly disclosed patient medical information to third parties. The case turned on the scope of the CMIA's definition of "medical information" under §56.05(f) — specifically, whether certain types of data collected by health care providers qualify as individually identifiable medical information subject to CMIA protection.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal held:

1. **"Medical information" under §56.05(f) is broadly defined.** It encompasses any individually identifiable information regarding a patient's medical history, mental or physical condition, or treatment that is in the possession of or derived from a provider of health care. The definition is not limited to clinical diagnoses or treatment records — it includes any information from which a patient's identity and health status can be determined.

2. **The "individually identifiable" element is the key threshold.** Information that can be linked to a specific patient — through name, address, social security number, or any combination of data points that reveals identity — is medical information subject to CMIA protection.

3. **Providers cannot narrow the definition by characterizing data differently.** A hospital cannot avoid CMIA obligations by relabeling patient data as "administrative" or "operational" information. If the information pertains to a patient's medical history, condition, or treatment and identifies the patient, it is medical information regardless of how the provider categorizes it internally.

## The Rule

**The CMIA's definition of "medical information" is broad and includes any individually identifiable information regarding a patient's medical history, condition, or treatment held by a provider. Providers cannot evade CMIA obligations through internal relabeling.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case: every record held by every provider — surgical notes, imaging, pharmacy records, insurance correspondence, billing data that includes diagnosis codes, appointment records — is "medical information" under §56.05(f) as interpreted by this court. No provider can claim that certain records fall outside CMIA protection by calling them "administrative." The Blue Shield records naming the phantom Dr. Dilworth are medical information. The SSA/DDS records are medical information. The SIRVA records are medical information. All are protected.
