# CMIA Definitions — Cross-References

| Standard | Relationship | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| CMIA_CIV_56_10_DISCLOSURE | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.05 definitions are the jurisdictional prerequisites for § 56.10 — "medical information," "provider," "patient," "contractor," and "pharmaceutical company" must each be established before a § 56.10 violation analysis begins |
| CMIA_CIV_56_11_AUTHORIZATION | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.05 defines "authorization" (subdivision (a)) and "authorized recipient" (subdivision (b)) — the definitions that § 56.11's validity requirements attach to |
| CMIA_CIV_56_36_REMEDIES | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.36 penalties apply to violations of "this part" — meaning violations by entities within § 56.05's definitions; if a disclosing entity is not a "provider," "plan," "contractor," or "pharmaceutical company," § 56.36 civil penalties do not apply (though other remedies may) |
| CMIA_CIV_56_20_EMPLOYER_CONFIDENTIALITY | CROSS_REFERENCE | § 56.20 uses § 56.05 definitions — an "employer" receiving "medical information" about its "patients" (employee-patients) triggers § 56.20 obligations; the definitions determine who is covered |
| HIPAA_45CFR_164_502_USES_DISCLOSURES | CROSS_REFERENCE | HIPAA's definition of "protected health information" (45 C.F.R. § 160.103) is narrower than § 56.05's "medical information" — CMIA covers pharmaceutical companies and digital health apps outside HIPAA's coverage; § 56.05(j)(2) treating immigration status as medical information has no HIPAA analog |
