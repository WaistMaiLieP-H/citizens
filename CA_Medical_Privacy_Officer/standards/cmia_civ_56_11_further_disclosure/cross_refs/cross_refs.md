# CMIA Authorization Requirements — Cross-References

| Standard | Relationship | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| CMIA_CIV_56_10_DISCLOSURE | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.11 defines what makes a valid "authorization" within the meaning of § 56.10(a) — a disclosure made in reliance on an authorization that fails § 56.11's formal requirements is a § 56.10(a) violation because no valid authorization existed |
| CMIA_CIV_56_05_DEFINITIONS | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.05(a) defines "authorization" as permission granted in accordance with § 56.11 or § 56.21 — the definition is circular in that it points to § 56.11 for substance; § 56.05(b) defines "authorized recipient" as someone authorized under § 56.10 or § 56.20 |
| CMIA_CIV_56_36_REMEDIES | SUPPLEMENTS | A disclosure made under an invalid § 56.11 authorization is an unauthorized disclosure subject to § 56.36 civil and criminal penalties — the formal requirements of § 56.11 are enforced through § 56.36's penalty structure |
| CMIA_CIV_56_35_DAMAGES | SUPPLEMENTS | Where a patient suffered actual harm from a disclosure made under an invalid authorization, both § 56.35 compensatory damages and § 56.36 nominal damages are available — § 56.35 requires the patient prove actual harm; § 56.36 requires only that the violation occurred |
| CMIA_CIV_56_20_EMPLOYER_CONFIDENTIALITY | CROSS_REFERENCE | § 56.20(c) requires employer to obtain a § 56.11 authorization for uses and disclosures outside the four enumerated exceptions — the same nine validity requirements apply whether the authorization is obtained by a health care provider or an employer |
