# Brown v. Superior Court, 37 Cal.4th 947 (2006)

## The Case

The California Supreme Court addressed whether medical information obtained through litigation discovery could be freely disclosed outside the litigation context. A party who had obtained an opposing party's medical records through court-authorized discovery sought to use those records for purposes beyond the pending lawsuit. The question was whether the CMIA's further-disclosure restriction under §56.11 applied to limit what a litigation recipient could do with medical information obtained through legal process.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court held:

1. **Medical information obtained through litigation discovery remains subject to CMIA protections.** The fact that medical records were disclosed pursuant to a court order or discovery process does not strip them of CMIA protection for purposes of further disclosure. The litigation exception permits the initial disclosure; it does not authorize unlimited downstream use.

2. **Section 56.11 applies to litigation recipients.** A party who receives medical information through discovery cannot further disclose it to non-parties, use it in unrelated proceedings, or disseminate it publicly without either (a) a new authorization from the patient or (b) a specific legal provision permitting the further disclosure.

3. **The CMIA's privacy protections are not waived by participating in litigation.** A plaintiff who puts their medical condition at issue in a lawsuit authorizes disclosure of relevant medical information for purposes of that litigation. That authorization does not extend to all future uses of the information by all parties who receive it.

## The Rule

**Medical information obtained through litigation discovery remains subject to CMIA §56.11's further-disclosure restriction. Litigation recipients cannot use or disclose that information beyond the scope of the proceeding in which it was obtained.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case: if medical records were obtained through family court proceedings, custody disputes, conservatorship hearings, or any other litigation, §56.11 prohibited the recipients from further disclosing those records outside the litigation. If Christina, the mother, or their attorneys obtained the steward's medical records through court proceedings and then shared them with third parties, each further disclosure is a separate §56.11 violation — regardless of whether the initial litigation disclosure was lawful.
