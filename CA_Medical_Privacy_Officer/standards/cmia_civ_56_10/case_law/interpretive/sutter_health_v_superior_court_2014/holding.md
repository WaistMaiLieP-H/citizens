# Sutter Health v. Superior Court, 227 Cal. App. 4th 1546 (2014)

## The Case

A class action alleged that Sutter Health disclosed patients' medical information to third-party vendors without proper authorization under the CMIA. The question was whether the CMIA's confidentiality requirements apply to disclosures made to business associates and contractors who process medical information on behalf of the provider.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **The CMIA applies to disclosures to third-party vendors and business associates.** A health care provider cannot circumvent the CMIA by outsourcing the handling of medical information to contractors. The provider remains responsible for ensuring all disclosures comply with §56.10.

2. **Each unauthorized disclosure is a separate CMIA violation.** Statutory damages under §56.35 apply per disclosure, not per patient or per incident. In a class action involving thousands of patients, this can result in substantial aggregate damages.

3. **The CMIA creates a private right of action (§56.35) with statutory damages** — the patient does not need to prove actual harm from the unauthorized disclosure. The violation itself is the injury.

## The Rule

**Health care providers are liable under the CMIA for unauthorized disclosures to third-party vendors. Each disclosure is a separate violation. Statutory damages available without proof of actual harm.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case: every entity that handled his medical records — Blue Shield (the insurer), Walgreens (the pharmacy/vaccine provider), Providence St. Joseph (the hospital), Dr. Wiseman (the surgeon), any lab or billing service — was bound by the CMIA. If any of them disclosed his medical information without proper §56.10 authorization, each disclosure is a separate violation carrying statutory damages.
