# CMIA Disclosure Prohibitions and Permitted Uses — Cross-References

| Standard | Relationship | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| CMIA_CIV_56_05_DEFINITIONS | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.10 applies only when the disclosing entity is a "provider," "plan," "pharmaceutical company," or "contractor" and the information is "medical information" as defined in § 56.05 — the definitions are the jurisdictional threshold for every § 56.10 analysis |
| CMIA_CIV_56_11_AUTHORIZATION | SUPPLEMENTS | § 56.10(a) requires authorization for voluntary disclosures; § 56.11 defines what makes an authorization valid — an authorization that fails § 56.11's nine requirements is not a valid authorization under § 56.10(a) |
| CMIA_CIV_56_36_REMEDIES | ENFORCES | Every § 56.10 violation is subject to the civil and criminal penalties in § 56.36 — the nominal damages ($1,000 per violation), knowing/willful penalties ($25,000), and financial-gain penalties ($250,000 + disgorgement) attach to § 56.10 violations |
| CMIA_CIV_56_35_DAMAGES | SUPPLEMENTS | Where a § 56.10 violation caused actual economic loss or personal injury, § 56.35 provides compensatory and punitive damages + attorney fees in addition to § 56.36 civil penalties |
| CMIA_CIV_56_20_EMPLOYER_CONFIDENTIALITY | CROSS_REFERENCE | § 56.20 governs employer use and disclosure of employee medical information; § 56.10 governs provider, plan, and contractor disclosures — both sections prohibit unauthorized disclosure but apply to different entity types holding the information |
| HIPAA_45CFR_164_502_USES_DISCLOSURES | CROSS_REFERENCE | § 164.502 is the federal floor; § 56.10 provides California's stricter standard. CMIA is not preempted by HIPAA — both apply to California providers, and CMIA's broader scope (pharmaceutical companies, digital health apps) covers entities outside HIPAA's definition of "covered entity" |
