# CMIA Disclosure Prohibitions and Permitted Uses — Statutory Text

**Source:** Cal. Civ. Code § 56.10
**Retrieved:** 2026-04-12
**URL:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=56.10.&lawCode=CIV

## Text

*Operative subsections quoted verbatim. Subsection (c)(1)–(c)(24) items are listed by label only due to length; full text retrieved 2026-04-12 and available in current/cal_civ_56_10_leginfo.txt.*

**(a) Default prohibition:**

A provider of health care, health care service plan, or contractor shall not disclose medical information regarding a patient of the provider of health care or an enrollee or subscriber of a health care service plan without first obtaining an authorization, except as provided in subdivision (b) or (c).

**(b) Compelled disclosures — 9 categories:**

A provider of health care, a health care service plan, or a contractor shall disclose medical information if the disclosure is compelled by any of the following:

(b)(1)(A) A court order issued by a court of this state or a federal court, including a court order issued pursuant to Section 2029.300 of the Code of Civil Procedure relating to a foreign subpoena.
(b)(1)(B) A provider shall not comply with a court order that constitutes a foreign subpoena, absent a court order issued pursuant to Section 2029.300.
(b)(2) A board, commission, or administrative agency for purposes of adjudication pursuant to its lawful authority.
(b)(3) A party to a proceeding before a court or administrative agency pursuant to a subpoena, subpoena duces tecum, notice to appear served pursuant to Section 1987 of the Code of Civil Procedure, or any provision authorizing discovery in a proceeding before a court or administrative agency.
(b)(4) A board, commission, or administrative agency pursuant to an investigative subpoena issued under Article 2 (commencing with Section 11180) of Chapter 2 of Part 1 of Division 3 of Title 2 of the Government Code.
(b)(5) An arbitrator or arbitration panel, when arbitration is lawfully requested by either party, pursuant to a subpoena duces tecum issued under Section 1282.6 of the Code of Civil Procedure, or another provision authorizing discovery in a proceeding before an arbitrator or arbitration panel.
(b)(6) A valid search warrant issued by a judicial officer, including a magistrate, to a governmental law enforcement agency.
(b)(7) The patient or the patient's representative pursuant to Chapter 1 (commencing with Section 123100) of Part 1 of Division 106 of the Health and Safety Code.
(b)(8) A medical examiner, forensic pathologist, or coroner, when requested in the course of an investigation [scope limited to identification, next of kin, and specified public health/criminal death contexts].
(b)(9) When otherwise specifically required by law.

**(c) Permitted disclosures — 24 categories:**

A provider of health care or a health care service plan may disclose medical information as follows [categories listed by subject]:

(c)(1) Other providers/plans/contractors for diagnosis or treatment
(c)(2) Insurers/payors — to the extent necessary for payment
(c)(3) Billing, claims management, data processing administrative services
(c)(4) Organized committees of professional societies, peer review, utilization review
(c)(5) Licensing/accrediting body review
(c)(6) Medical examiner/coroner outside (b)(8) scope
(c)(7) Public agencies, clinical investigators, accredited research organizations for bona fide research
(c)(8) Employer — employment-related care at employer's request, limited to specified uses
(c)(9) Sponsor/insurer/administrator for sponsor-funded eligibility evaluation
(c)(10) Health care service plan administration
(c)(11) Insurance institution under Insurance Code Article 6.6
(c)(12) Probate court investigator in a conservatorship proceeding under Guardianship-Conservatorship Law (Probate Code § 1400), or a probate court investigator, probation officer, or domestic relations investigator engaged in determining the need for an initial guardianship or continuation of an existing guardianship
(c)(13) Organ procurement organization or tissue bank
(c)(14) When otherwise specifically authorized by law (FDA adverse events, child abuse reporting)
(c)(15) State-recognized or federally recognized disaster relief organization (basic info only)
(c)(16) Third party for encoding, encrypting, or anonymizing data
(c)(17) Disease management programs and services
(c)(18) Local health department for disease/injury/disability prevention or control
(c)(19) Psychotherapist for imminent threat to health or safety of identifiable victim
(c)(20) As described in § 56.103
(c)(21) Employee welfare benefit plan (Taft-Hartley) under specific conditions
(c)(22) Elder/dependent adult abuse reporting under Welfare and Institutions Code § 15633.5
(c)(23) School-linked services coordinator with written authorization
(c)(24) Mental health records of correctional/state hospital patients per Penal Code § 5073

**(d) Anti-marketing rule (verbatim):**

Except to the extent expressly authorized by a patient, enrollee, or subscriber, or as provided by subdivisions (b) and (c), a provider of health care, health care service plan, contractor, or corporation and its subsidiaries and affiliates shall not intentionally share, sell, use for marketing, or otherwise use medical information for a purpose not necessary to provide health care services to the patient.

**(e) Contractor downstream-disclosure limit (verbatim):**

Except to the extent expressly authorized by a patient or enrollee or subscriber or as provided by subdivisions (b) and (c), a contractor or corporation and its subsidiaries and affiliates shall not further disclose medical information regarding a patient of the provider of health care or an enrollee or subscriber of a health care service plan or insurer or self-insured employer received under this section to a person or entity that is not engaged in providing direct health care services to the patient or the patient's provider of health care or health care service plan or insurer or self-insured employer.

**(f) Immigration enforcement prohibition (verbatim):**

Except to the extent expressly authorized by a patient, enrollee, or subscriber, or as required by subdivision (b), or as permitted by subdivision (c), a provider of health care, health care service plan, contractor, or corporation and its subsidiaries and affiliates shall not disclose medical information for immigration enforcement.
