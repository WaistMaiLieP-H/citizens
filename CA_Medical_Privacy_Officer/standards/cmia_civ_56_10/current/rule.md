# CMIA Disclosure Prohibitions and Permitted Uses — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Civ. Code § 56.10
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** 1981 (most recent amendment: SB 81, effective September 20, 2025)

## The Rule

A provider of health care, a health care service plan, or a contractor cannot disclose a patient's medical information without first obtaining that patient's written authorization. There are two categories of exception: compelled disclosures — where the law requires the provider to hand over records (court orders, search warrants, patient's own request) — and permitted disclosures — where the law allows but does not require sharing (treatment coordination, billing, public health reporting). Everything outside those categories requires the patient's signed consent.

No provider, plan, or contractor may use medical information for marketing, sell it, or use it for any purpose not necessary to provide health care to that patient — regardless of whether any other exception applies. Disclosure of medical information for immigration enforcement purposes is categorically prohibited.

## Key Elements

1. **Default rule (§ 56.10(a)):** No disclosure without authorization — the burden is on the entity seeking to disclose to identify a valid exception.
2. **Compelled disclosures (§ 56.10(b)):** 9 categories where disclosure is legally required — court orders, subpoenas, search warrants, patient's own request (HSC § 123100), coroner/medical examiner, and others specifically required by law.
3. **Permitted disclosures (§ 56.10(c)):** 24 categories where disclosure is allowed without authorization — treatment, payment, peer review, licensing, probate court investigators in conservatorship proceedings (§ 56.10(c)(12)), organ procurement, public health, disease management, and others.
4. **Anti-marketing rule (§ 56.10(d)):** Providers, plans, and contractors may not share, sell, or use medical information for any purpose not necessary to deliver health care to that patient.
5. **Contractor downstream limit (§ 56.10(e)):** Recipients of medical information under this section cannot re-disclose to non-care entities.
6. **Immigration enforcement prohibition (§ 56.10(f)):** Medical information cannot be disclosed for any immigration enforcement purpose unless the patient authorizes it.

## What This Means in Practice

Every medical record, diagnosis, treatment note, prescription, lab result, and imaging study about a patient in California is protected from disclosure unless the disclosing entity can point to an authorization signed by the patient or a specific enumerated exception. The exception in § 56.10(c)(12) — allowing disclosure to a probate court investigator in a conservatorship proceeding — is the statutory mechanism by which a court-authorized investigation can lawfully obtain a patient's records without the patient's consent or knowledge.
