# CMIA Civil Remedies and Criminal Penalties — Cross-References

| Standard | Relationship | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| CMIA_CIV_56_10_DISCLOSURE | ENFORCES | § 56.36 penalties attach to § 56.10 violations — every unauthorized disclosure by a provider, plan, or contractor triggers the civil penalty tracks; the criminal misdemeanor track applies where the violation caused economic loss or personal injury |
| CMIA_CIV_56_35_DAMAGES | SUPPLEMENTS | § 56.35 provides compensatory + punitive damages for patients who suffered actual harm; § 56.36 provides nominal damages ($1,000) without proof of harm — both may be pursued simultaneously; § 56.36(c) civil penalties are separate from § 56.35 damages |
| CMIA_CIV_56_05_DEFINITIONS | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.36 penalties apply to violations of "this part" — meaning violations of CMIA Part 2.6 generally; § 56.05 definitions determine whether the violator is a covered entity and whether the disclosed information qualifies as "medical information" |
| CMIA_CIV_56_20_EMPLOYER_CONFIDENTIALITY | CROSS_REFERENCE | § 56.36 penalties apply to employer violations of § 56.20 — employer misuse of employee medical information triggers the same penalty structure as provider/plan violations |
| HIPAA_45CFR_164_502_USES_DISCLOSURES | CROSS_REFERENCE | HIPAA violations by California providers are typically simultaneous CMIA violations — § 56.36's civil penalty structure provides a private enforcement track that HIPAA itself does not have; the HIPAA affirmative defense in § 56.36(e) specifically applies to HIPAA-compliant covered entities |
