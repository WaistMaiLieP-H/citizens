# CMIA Compensatory and Punitive Damages — Cross-References

| Standard | Relationship | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| CMIA_CIV_56_10_DISCLOSURE | SUPPLEMENTS | § 56.35 compensatory and punitive damages attach to violations of § 56.10 — the unauthorized disclosure is the predicate act; § 56.35 is the damages remedy for patients who can prove that the disclosure caused actual economic loss or personal injury |
| CMIA_CIV_56_20_EMPLOYER_CONFIDENTIALITY | SUPPLEMENTS | § 56.35 explicitly covers violations of § 56.20 — an employee harmed by an employer's misuse of their medical information can sue for compensatory damages, punitive damages ($3,000 cap), and attorney fees under § 56.35 |
| CMIA_CIV_56_36_REMEDIES | SUPPLEMENTS | § 56.35 (requires actual harm) and § 56.36(b) (nominal damages, no actual harm required) operate in parallel — a plaintiff may pursue both simultaneously; § 56.36 civil penalties are separate government-enforcement mechanisms; a patient typically brings both § 56.35 and § 56.36(b) claims in a single action |
| CMIA_CIV_56_05_DEFINITIONS | IMPLEMENTS | § 56.35 applies to violations of §§ 56.10 and 56.20 — which in turn require that the disclosed information is "medical information" and the disclosing entity is a covered entity under § 56.05; the definitions are prerequisites |
| CMIA_CIV_56_11_AUTHORIZATION | CROSS_REFERENCE | A disclosure made in reliance on an invalid § 56.11 authorization is a § 56.10 violation — which in turn triggers § 56.35 damages if the patient suffered harm; invalid authorization + actual harm = § 56.35 claim |
