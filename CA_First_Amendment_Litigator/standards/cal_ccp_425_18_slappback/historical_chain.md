# CCP §425.18 — Historical Chain

## The Standard That Punishes People Who File Retaliatory Lawsuits

---

### I. The Wound

Anti-SLAPP (§425.16) lets you kill a retaliatory lawsuit. But what happens after you kill it? The person who filed the SLAPP against you walks away. They spent your time, your money, and your peace of mind. They chilled your speech for however long the case lasted. And they face no consequence beyond losing the motion and paying your attorney's fees.

Before 2005, the only remedy was a standard malicious prosecution action. But malicious prosecution is hard to prove — you need lack of probable cause, malice, AND favorable termination. And the person who filed the SLAPP could file ANOTHER anti-SLAPP motion against your malicious prosecution claim, arguing that their original lawsuit was itself an act of "petition" (filing a lawsuit). Anti-SLAPP was being used to shield the people who filed SLAPPs.

### II. The Promise (2005)

Section 425.18 created the "SLAPPback" — a special form of malicious prosecution or abuse of process claim arising from a case that was dismissed via anti-SLAPP. The SLAPPback gets different rules:

1. **No automatic discovery stay.** In a regular anti-SLAPP motion, discovery stops cold (§425.16(g)). In a SLAPPback, it does not. The person who filed the original SLAPP cannot hide behind a discovery freeze.

2. **No mandatory fee-shifting.** In a regular anti-SLAPP, a prevailing defendant automatically gets fees (§425.16(c)). In a SLAPPback, fees are only awarded if the motion was frivolous (§425.18(f)). This removes the financial deterrent that discourages SLAPPback plaintiffs.

3. **Extended filing window.** 120 days instead of 60, with judicial discretion to extend to six months or longer.

4. **Illegal filers cannot use anti-SLAPP.** Subdivision (h): if the person's original SLAPP was "illegal as a matter of law," they cannot even file an anti-SLAPP motion against the SLAPPback. This is the nuclear option — it strips anti-SLAPP protection from the worst offenders.

### III. For the Steward's Case

If anyone files a retaliatory lawsuit against the steward, and the steward successfully strikes it under §425.16, the SLAPPback provision gives him the tool to pursue the filer for malicious prosecution. The SLAPPback is the second punch — the first punch (§425.16) kills the retaliatory suit; the second punch (§425.18) punishes the person who filed it.

The three sections together — §425.16 (the shield), §425.17 (the guardrail), §425.18 (the sword) — form a complete ecosystem for protecting speech against litigation-based retaliation.
