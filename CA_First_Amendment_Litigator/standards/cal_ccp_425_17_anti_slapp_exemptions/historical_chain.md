# CCP §425.17 — Historical Chain

## The Standard That Prevents Anti-SLAPP From Being Weaponized

---

### I. The Wound

Anti-SLAPP was designed to protect citizens who speak about public issues from being silenced by retaliatory lawsuits. But by 2003, lawyers had discovered that §425.16 could be weaponized. Businesses hit with consumer protection suits filed anti-SLAPP motions arguing their advertising was protected speech. Defendants in public interest litigation used anti-SLAPP to force plaintiffs into expensive procedural fights before they could even get to discovery. The tool designed to protect speakers was being used to silence them.

The Legislature found "a disturbing abuse of Section 425.16" — their words, in subdivision (a). They used the same framing as §425.16 itself: participation in public affairs "should not be chilled through abuse of the judicial process." The abuse was now coming from inside the house.

### II. The Promise (2003)

Section 425.17 carves two categories of cases out of anti-SLAPP:

**Public interest suits** (subdivision (b)): If a case is brought solely in the public interest, seeks no personal relief beyond what the public gets, would enforce an important public right, and places a disproportionate burden on the plaintiff — anti-SLAPP does not apply. This protects the private attorney general. The person who sues not for personal gain but because someone has to.

**Commercial speech** (subdivision (c)): If a business is making factual representations about its products to customers — advertising, sales pitches, commercial claims — anti-SLAPP does not apply. Commercial speech is not the kind of speech anti-SLAPP was designed to protect.

### III. The Press Carve-Back (subdivision (d))

The exemptions in (b) and (c) do NOT apply to journalists, media, publishers, or artistic works. This is critical. Even if a journalist's speech looks commercial or a news organization's investigation could be characterized as not "solely in the public interest," anti-SLAPP protection remains available. The Legislature was clear: the press keeps its shield.

### IV. For the Steward's Case

If the steward's compliance audits and public statements prompt a retaliatory lawsuit, §425.16 is the primary defense. But if an adversary argues that §425.17(b) exempts their suit because it's "in the public interest," the steward can counter that §425.17(d) preserves anti-SLAPP for persons engaged in gathering, receiving, or processing information for communication to the public — which is exactly what the steward does.

Section 425.17 is the guardrail that keeps anti-SLAPP honest. It exists because every tool that protects speech can be turned against speech if there is no limit on who can use it.
