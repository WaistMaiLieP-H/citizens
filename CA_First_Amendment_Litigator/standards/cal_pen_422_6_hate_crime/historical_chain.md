# Penal Code §422.6 — Historical Chain

## The Standard That Draws the Line Between Speech and Violence

---

### I. The Wound

Hate crimes are not just crimes against individuals — they are crimes against the communities those individuals belong to. When someone is attacked because of who they are, every person who shares that characteristic receives the message: you are not safe here. California recognized that rights secured by the Constitution are meaningless if people are terrorized out of exercising them.

### II. The Promise

Section 422.6 criminalizes interference with constitutional or statutory rights by force or threat of force when motivated by the victim's actual or perceived characteristics (race, gender, sexual orientation, disability, and other characteristics listed in §422.55). The statute reaches conduct by ANY person — whether or not acting under color of law. This is unusual. Most civil rights statutes require state action. Section 422.6 applies to private actors.

### III. The Speech Protection (subdivision (c))

The critical provision for First Amendment purposes is the speech-alone exception in subdivision (c): "a person shall not be convicted of violating subdivision (a) based upon speech alone, except upon a showing that the speech itself threatened violence against a specific person or group of persons and that the defendant had the apparent ability to carry out the threat."

This is the Legislature drawing the constitutional line that the First Amendment requires. Pure speech — no matter how hateful, offensive, or disturbing — cannot be the basis of a §422.6 conviction unless it constitutes a true threat. The statute explicitly requires both (1) that the speech threatened violence against a specific person or group and (2) that the speaker had the apparent ability to carry out the threat.

This is the California statutory implementation of the true-threat doctrine established in Virginia v. Black (2003) and refined in Counterman v. Colorado (2023).

### IV. For the Steward's Case

Section 422.6 is relevant in two directions:

**As protection:** If anyone interferes with the steward's exercise of his constitutional rights (speech, petition, property, privacy, liberty) by force or threat of force because of his actual or perceived characteristics, §422.6 provides a criminal enforcement mechanism.

**As a boundary:** The steward's own speech — compliance audits, published findings, pro se filings, public statements — is protected by the speech-alone exception. No matter how critical his statements are of specific individuals or agencies, they cannot form the basis of a §422.6 prosecution unless they constitute true threats of violence. Publishing the truth about government misconduct is not a hate crime. It is not even close.

Section 422.6 is the criminal-law marker of the boundary between protected speech and prohibited conduct. It exists in this Citizen's corpus because the boundary must be known precisely — both to protect the steward's speech and to identify when others have crossed the line against him.
