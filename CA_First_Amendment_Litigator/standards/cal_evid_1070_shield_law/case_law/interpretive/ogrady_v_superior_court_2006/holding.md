# O'Grady v. Superior Court, 139 Cal.App.4th 1423 (2006)

## The Case

Jason O'Grady operated PowerPage, an online news site that published leaked information about upcoming Apple products. Apple sued unnamed "Does" for trade secret misappropriation and sought discovery from O'Grady's email service provider to identify the source of the leaks. O'Grady moved to quash, invoking the shield law (Evidence Code §1070 and Article I, §2(b)).

The trial court denied the motion, holding that online publishers were not covered by the shield law. O'Grady sought a writ of mandate.

## What the Court Held

The Sixth District Court of Appeal reversed, holding:

1. **Online publishers are protected by the shield law.** The court held that the shield law protects "any person connected with or employed upon a newspaper, magazine, or other periodical publication" — and that an online news publication is a "periodical publication" within the meaning of the statute. The court rejected the argument that the shield law was limited to traditional print and broadcast media.

2. **The shield law protects the publisher's sources and unpublished information regardless of the subject matter.** Apple argued that trade secret information should not be protected. The court held that the shield law contains no subject-matter exception — it protects source identity and unpublished information regardless of what the information is about.

3. **The constitutional shield (Art. I, §2(b)) provides independent protection** that cannot be overridden by statutory trade secret law. Even if a statute purported to create an exception to the shield, the constitutional provision would control.

## The Rule O'Grady Stands For

**The California shield law extends to online publishers.** A person who gathers, receives, or processes information for communication to the public through an online publication is within the shield's protection. The medium of communication does not determine whether the shield applies — the function does.

For the steward's case: O'Grady is directly relevant. The steward operates an online compliance platform that publishes audit findings to the public. If his activities constitute "gathering, receiving or processing of information for communication to the public," O'Grady supports shield law protection for his unpublished working materials, source documents, and draft audit findings.
