# Delaney v. Superior Court, 50 Cal. 3d 785 (1990)

## The Case

A criminal defendant sought to compel a journalist to reveal confidential sources. The journalist invoked California's shield law (Evidence Code §1070), which provides a privilege against being held in contempt for refusing to disclose sources or unpublished information.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **The shield law (§1070) provides an absolute privilege against contempt** — a journalist cannot be held in contempt for refusing to disclose the identity of a source or unpublished information, even in a criminal case.

2. **However, the shield law does not create an absolute bar to disclosure in all circumstances.** In criminal cases, the defendant's Sixth Amendment right to a fair trial may require balancing against the shield law privilege. The court must conduct a balancing test when the defendant demonstrates the information is critical to the defense.

3. **The balancing test requires the defendant to show:** (a) the information is relevant and material, (b) there is a reasonable possibility the information will assist the defense, and (c) the defendant has exhausted all alternative sources for the information.

## The Rule

**The shield law (§1070) provides absolute protection from contempt but is subject to balancing against a criminal defendant's constitutional fair-trial rights. The defendant must demonstrate materiality, potential assistance, and exhaustion of alternatives before the court can compel disclosure.**
