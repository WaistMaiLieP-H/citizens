# Shield Law Constitutional Elevation — 1980 Context

## What Happened

On June 3, 1980, California voters passed Proposition 5 (Res.Ch. 77, 1978), which added subdivision (b) to Article I, Section 2 of the California Constitution. The text of Article I §2(b) tracks the language of Evidence Code §1070 almost exactly — because it was modeled directly on the statute.

## Why the Voters Did This

A statute can be repealed by the Legislature. A constitutional provision can only be amended by the voters. The elevation of the shield law from Evidence Code §1070 to Article I §2(b) was a deliberate act of constitutional entrenchment: the voters decided that journalist privilege was too important to leave to legislative discretion.

The timing is significant:
- 1972: Branzburg v. Hayes held there is no federal constitutional journalist privilege
- 1974: California Legislature strengthened §1070 with "unpublished information" protection
- 1978: Legislature placed the shield law on the ballot as a proposed constitutional amendment (Res.Ch. 77)
- 1980: Voters approved — the shield law became constitutional

The progression shows a six-year arc from federal retrenchment (Branzburg) to state constitutional entrenchment (Prop. 5).

## The Two-Level Shield

After 1980, California's journalist privilege operates at two levels:
1. **Statutory:** Evidence Code §1070 (amendable by Legislature)
2. **Constitutional:** Article I, §2(b) (amendable only by voters)

Even if the Legislature repealed §1070, the constitutional shield in §2(b) would stand. The two provisions are redundant by design — the constitutional provision is the backstop.
