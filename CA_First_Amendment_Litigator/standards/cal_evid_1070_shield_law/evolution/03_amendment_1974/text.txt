Evidence Code Section 1070 (as amended by Stats. 1974, Ch. 1323)

[NOTE: The exact verbatim text of the 1974 amendment is believed to be substantially identical
to the CURRENT text, since §1070 has not been amended since 1974.]

The 1974 amendment is the most consequential amendment to §1070. According to secondary sources
(Wikipedia, RCFP Compendium), the 1974 amendment:

1. Added protection for "unpublished information" — not just source identity
2. Defined "unpublished information" broadly to include "all notes, outtakes, photographs,
   tapes or other data of whatever sort not itself disseminated to the public through a
   medium of communication"

The 1974 amendment was enacted in the same year as:
- Proposition 7 (November 5, 1974), which comprehensively revised Article I of the Constitution
- Two years after Branzburg v. Hayes (1972), in which the U.S. Supreme Court held 5-4 that
  the First Amendment does not protect journalists from grand jury subpoenas seeking source
  identity — making state shield laws the primary protection
