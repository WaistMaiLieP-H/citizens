# Current — Internal Revenue Code of 1986, § 7201

**Recodified:** 1986 (Tax Reform Act of 1986, Pub. L. 99-514) — renamed the 1954 Code as the "Internal Revenue Code of 1986" with no change to § 7201 text
**Current statutory face text:** $100,000 individual / $500,000 corporation / 5 years imprisonment
**Effective fine via 18 U.S.C. § 3571:** $250,000 individual / $500,000 organization (in practice, courts apply § 3571 because it sets a higher ceiling than the face-value figures for individual defendants)

## Intermediate amendments to penalty figures

1. **1954** — $10,000 / 5 years (enacted)
2. **1982** (TEFRA, Pub. L. 97-248 §329(a)) — raised to $100,000 individual / $500,000 corporation / 5 years
3. **1984** (Comprehensive Crime Control Act / Sentencing Reform Act) — enacted 18 U.S.C. § 3571 alternative fine schedule; effective 1987
4. **1987 forward** — 18 U.S.C. § 3571 governs in practice: up to $250,000 individual / $500,000 organization for felonies, overriding the face-value § 7201 fine for individual defendants

## What is in the current text, verbatim (subject to steward verification)

> Any person who willfully attempts in any manner to evade or defeat any tax imposed by this title or the payment thereof shall, in addition to other penalties provided by law, be guilty of a felony and, upon conviction thereof, shall be fined not more than $100,000 ($500,000 in the case of a corporation), or imprisoned not more than 5 years, or both, together with the costs of prosecution.

## Substantive doctrine — stable since 1954

Because the § 7201 text has not changed in substance since 1954, the doctrinal surface is governed by:

- *Spies v. United States*, 317 U.S. 492 (1943) — affirmative-act requirement
- *Sansone v. United States*, 380 U.S. 343 (1965) — three-element test
- *Cheek v. United States*, 498 U.S. 192 (1991) — willfulness as voluntary intentional violation of known legal duty
- *United States v. Bishop*, 412 U.S. 346 (1973) — willfulness standard is the same across §§7201, 7203, 7206
- *Boulware v. United States*, 552 U.S. 421 (2008) — §7201 defendant may rebut deficiency element by showing constructive dividends traced to earnings and profits
- *Marinello v. United States*, 584 U.S. ___, 138 S. Ct. 1101 (2018) — interpreting §7212(a) narrowly; relevant cross-reference because §7212 is the "omnibus" companion to §7201

## How practitioners litigate § 7201 today

### Government side
1. Build the deficiency via specific-item, net-worth, bank-deposits, or cash-expenditures method
2. Identify affirmative acts matching Spies list or later analogues (false invoices, concealed accounts, nominee structures, digital-asset obfuscation, cash structuring, transferable-credit resale without reporting)
3. Establish willfulness through badges of fraud (Bradford factors) and taxpayer conduct reflecting knowledge of duty
4. Charge — alone or with §§7206, 7212(a), 18 U.S.C. §§371, 1343

### Defense side
1. Attack the deficiency element (Boulware; method-of-proof challenges; stipulate civil liability to kill the criminal tax due)
2. Attack the affirmative-act element — distinguish from §7203 (omission)
3. Raise Cheek good-faith belief — rare success, but mandatory jury instruction when evidence supports it
4. Attack willfulness via taxpayer's reliance on professional advice (*United States v. Boyle*, 469 U.S. 241 (1985) — tax-advice reliance doctrine)
5. Attack SOL under §6531(2)

## Notable modern §7201 developments (post-1990)

- **Tempel v. Commissioner**, 136 T.C. 341 (2011) — state transferable tax credit sale produces §1001 gain with zero basis if earned; implicates §7201 where the gain is not reported. Especially relevant to the steward's GA film tax credit investigation vector.
- **Digital asset reporting** — Notice 2014-21 treats virtual currency as property; §6045 broker reporting expanded via Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act of 2021 (effective date contested)
- **FBAR / FATCA** — offshore account non-disclosure regularly charged as §7201 predicate act
- **Klein conspiracy** (18 U.S.C. §371 to defraud US) — frequently paired with §7201 where concealment involved multiple actors
