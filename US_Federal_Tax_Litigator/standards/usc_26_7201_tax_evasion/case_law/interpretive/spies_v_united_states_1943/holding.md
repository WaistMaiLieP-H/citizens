# Spies v. United States, 317 U.S. 492 (1943)

## The rule

Felony tax evasion under what is now § 7201 requires an *affirmative act* to evade or defeat tax, not merely a willful omission. Failure to file and failure to pay, without more, are misdemeanors under § 7203. The felony requires some positive step the likely effect of which is to mislead or to conceal.

## Plain language

The court drew a bright line between *doing nothing that you were supposed to do* (misdemeanor — failure to file, failure to pay) and *doing something affirmative to hide or defeat the tax* (felony — evasion). Filing nothing is not enough to be a felon. You must take a step. The court gave examples: keeping two sets of books, making false entries, destroying records, hiding assets, concealing sources of income, handling affairs in a way that avoids the normal paper trail, or any conduct whose likely effect is to mislead or conceal.

## Statute version cited

§ 145(b) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1939 (immediate predecessor to 1954 § 7201; identical operative language "wilful attempt in any manner to evade or defeat"). The doctrine carries forward without modification because the 1954 recodification preserved the "attempts in any manner" language.

## Why it matters for the corpus

Spies is the case every § 7201 prosecution and every defense motion reads first. It defines the line between §7201 and §7203, which is the most-litigated distinction in criminal tax practice. Without Spies, §7201 would either swallow §7203 entirely (and make every non-filer a felon) or collapse into a mere intent standard. Spies gave it spine.

## Contemporary application vectors (from steward's file)

- **Concealment of sources of income** — Treasury securities attributed to "another individual" under the steward's SSN, if engineered by a third party to route income away from the true earner, is concealment of a source within the Spies meaning.
- **Handling affairs to avoid usual records** — 19 unsigned/undated disclosure documents at the Honeysuckle closing fit the Spies frame of conduct "the likely effect of which would be to mislead or to conceal."
- **False entries / destruction of records** — missing OPD reports, phantom SSA contacts, and fabricated competency evaluations would fit Spies affirmative-act categories if the purpose was tax-advantageous or collection-evading.

The Spies list is non-exhaustive on purpose. Any conduct whose *likely effect* is to mislead or conceal qualifies.
