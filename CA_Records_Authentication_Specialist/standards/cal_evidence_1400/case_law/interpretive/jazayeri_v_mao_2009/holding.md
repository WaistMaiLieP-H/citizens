# Jazayeri v. Mao, 174 Cal. App. 4th 301 (2009)

## The Case

Jazayeri submitted documents in support of a motion. The opposing party challenged the documents' authenticity under Evidence Code §1400, arguing insufficient foundation for authentication.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **Evidence Code §1400 requires authentication before any writing may be received in evidence.** This applies to all documents — contracts, letters, emails, printouts, records, photographs — without exception.

2. **Authentication under §1401 requires "the introduction of evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that it is the writing that the proponent of the evidence claims it is."** The standard is not certainty — it is sufficiency. Enough evidence that a reasonable trier of fact could find the document is genuine.

3. **Authentication can be established through multiple means under §§1410-1421:** testimony of a witness who saw execution, handwriting comparison, content and circumstances, reply doctrine, official seals, or any other means provided by law.

4. **A document submitted without any authentication foundation is inadmissible.** The court has no obligation to accept a document at face value simply because it is offered.

## The Rule

**Every document must be authenticated before it can be used as evidence. Authentication requires sufficient evidence that the document is what it claims to be. No authentication = inadmissible.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case, every document that was relied on in any proceeding — custody evaluations, medical reports, police reports, financial records, court filings — must have been properly authenticated. An unsigned report submitted without authentication foundation was inadmissible under §1400. If it was relied on anyway, the proceeding was based on evidence that should not have been admitted.
