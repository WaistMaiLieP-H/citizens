# Context — California Evidence Code § 1552 Computer Printouts / Digital Records

## The statute in brief

Section 1552 creates a rebuttable presumption that a printed representation of computer information accurately represents the computer data it purports to represent. The companion provision, § 1553 (added 1998), extends the same presumption to printed representations of video or digital images.

**The operational rule:** A printout of computer data is presumed accurate — the proponent does not need expert testimony or a live custodian to authenticate it as an accurate copy of the underlying electronic data. But the presumption is rebuttable: once the opponent introduces evidence that the printout is inaccurate or unreliable, the burden shifts to the proponent to prove accuracy by a preponderance of the evidence.

## Presumption affecting burden of producing evidence

Section 1552 expressly creates a "presumption affecting the burden of producing evidence" — not a conclusive presumption and not a presumption affecting the burden of proof. Under California's presumption framework:
- Presumption affecting burden of producing evidence: the presumption disappears once the opponent introduces any competent evidence to the contrary
- The proponent then must prove accuracy by a preponderance

This is a relatively weak presumption. Any credible challenge to the accuracy or reliability of the computer printout — an expert opinion that the system was unreliable, evidence of data manipulation, or evidence that the printout was modified after printing — is sufficient to rebut it and require the proponent to prove accuracy affirmatively.

## § 1552(b) exception for certified official records

Section 1552(b) provides that the § 1552 presumption does not apply to computer-generated official records that are certified under § 452.5 or § 1530. Those records are handled under the official records framework (§§ 1280, 1450-1451, 1530), not the general computer printout presumption. This prevents parties from using § 1552 to authenticate an uncertified printout of official data — the official certification framework must be used for official records.

## Section 1553 — Digital Images

Section 1553 extends the same presumption to printed representations of video or digital images (photographs, video captures, screenshots). A screenshot of a webpage, a printed photograph from a digital camera, or a printout of a video frame is presumed to accurately represent the image it purports to represent — subject to the same rebuttable-presumption structure.

For electronically stored social media posts, text messages, emails, or website content, § 1553 provides the presumption basis. The opponent who challenges a screenshot must introduce evidence of inaccuracy or unreliability, at which point the proponent bears the preponderance burden.

## Application to contested electronic records

**Pension system records:** A printout of the UA Local 342 pension database showing a "retired" status is a § 1552 printout. It is presumed to accurately represent the database contents at the time printed. But the steward can challenge this presumption by introducing evidence that the database entry was unauthorized, was entered based on a fraudulent election, or was altered after the fact. Once challenged, the Trust must prove by a preponderance that the printout accurately represents genuine, authorized data.

**Electronic communications:** Printed copies of emails, text messages, or system logs relating to the identity replacement scheme are § 1553 presumptions (digital images) or § 1552 presumptions (electronic data). The presumption helps the proponent of those records — the steward — admit them without live authentication testimony, unless the opponent challenges accuracy.

**The digital evidence attack:** When attacking computer printouts offered by opposing parties, the § 1552 challenge is: introduce evidence that the data was entered without authorization, that the system was manipulated, or that the printout was modified. This shifts the burden of proof to the proponent of the record — requiring them to prove accuracy, not just assert it.

## California vs. federal framework

California's § 1552 applies in California state court. In federal court, computer records are authenticated under FRE 901(b)(9) (process or system producing an accurate result) or FRE 902(13)/(14) (certified electronic records). The federal framework is generally more flexible for electronic records; California's § 1552 is a simpler presumption structure that still requires a live challenge to trigger the burden shift.
