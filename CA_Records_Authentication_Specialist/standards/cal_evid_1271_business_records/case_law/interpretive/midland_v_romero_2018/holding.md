# Midland Funding, LLC v. Romero, No. B274892 (Cal. Ct. App. 2018)

## The Case

Midland Funding, a debt buyer, sued to collect on a purchased consumer debt. Midland offered records from the original creditor as business records under Evidence Code § 1271 to establish the existence and amount of the debt. The defendant challenged the records' admissibility, arguing that Midland — as a debt buyer that purchased the account — could not lay a proper § 1271 foundation for records it did not create and whose creation system it had no personal knowledge of.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **A debt buyer's records must independently qualify under § 1271.** The debt buyer cannot simply offer the original creditor's records and claim they are business records of the debt buyer. The debt buyer must establish that the records were integrated into the debt buyer's own record-keeping system and that the debt buyer's system meets the § 1271 requirements — or the debt buyer must produce a qualified witness from the original creditor.

2. **The custodian-witness requirement cannot be satisfied by a witness who is only familiar with the debt buyer's system and has no knowledge of the original creditor's record-keeping practices.** When a debt buyer offers records created by the original creditor, the foundation witness must be able to testify about the original creditor's system — how records were created, when they were created, what sources of information were used, and whether the system was trustworthy. A debt buyer's employee who only knows "we bought this account and these records came with it" does not satisfy § 1271(c).

3. **Records that pass through multiple hands without independent verification at each stage are vulnerable to trustworthiness challenges under § 1271(d).** The more steps between the original creation of the record and the party offering it in court, the greater the trustworthiness concern. A debt buyer that cannot trace the record-keeping chain back to a reliable original source fails the trustworthiness test.

## The Rule

**A debt buyer must independently establish a § 1271 foundation for records created by the original creditor. The debt buyer's witness must have knowledge of the original creditor's record-keeping system, or the original creditor's records must have been genuinely integrated into the debt buyer's own system. Records that pass through multiple hands without verification at each stage fail the trustworthiness requirement.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case, Midland v. Romero is directly relevant to the Jaguar debt collection ($19,985) and any other debt collection action. The entity attempting to collect the debt must establish a § 1271 foundation for the records it relies on. If the collecting entity is not the original creditor, it cannot simply offer the original creditor's records — it must show that it has a qualified witness who understands the original record-keeping system or that the records were genuinely integrated into the collector's own verified system. This is the same principle that applies to any financial record that has passed through multiple parties: Treasury records, insurance records, and medical billing records that were transferred between entities all face the same foundation challenge.
