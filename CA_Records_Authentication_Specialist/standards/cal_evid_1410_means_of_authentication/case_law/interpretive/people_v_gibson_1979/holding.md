# People v. Gibson, 90 Cal.App.3d 468 (1979)

## The Case

The prosecution offered documentary evidence whose authentication did not fit neatly into any of the specific means enumerated in Evidence Code §§ 1411-1421. The defendant argued the evidence was inadmissible because the prosecution had not authenticated it through one of the enumerated statutory methods.

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **The means of authentication listed in §§ 1411-1421 are illustrative, not exhaustive.** Section 1410 expressly provides that nothing in Article 2 shall be construed to limit the means by which a writing may be authenticated or proved. The enumerated methods are a starting point, not a closed list.

2. **A writing may be authenticated by any means that produces evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that the writing is what its proponent claims it is.** The test is always the § 1400 definition: evidence sufficient to sustain a finding of authenticity. The method of producing that evidence is open.

3. **Courts should not exclude reliable evidence merely because the method of authentication does not match one of the enumerated categories.** The purpose of the authentication requirement is to ensure that evidence is genuine before the trier of fact relies on it. Any reliable method of establishing genuineness serves that purpose.

## The Rule

**The authentication methods in §§ 1411-1421 are illustrative. Section 1410 guarantees that any method capable of producing evidence sufficient to sustain a finding of authenticity under § 1400 is acceptable. Courts may not restrict authentication to the enumerated categories.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case, Gibson is the foundational authority for using non-traditional authentication methods — digital forensic analysis, metadata examination, hash comparison, system audit logs, database record timestamps — in California state court. When the steward's document forensics engine identifies inconsistencies in a document's metadata, timestamp, or digital signature, that analysis is admissible as authentication evidence under § 1410 as interpreted by Gibson. The authentication methods are not frozen in 1965.
