# Rupf v. Yan, 85 Cal.App.4th 411 (2000)

## The Case

A party offered an official record that technically satisfied the first two requirements of § 1280 — it was made by a public employee within the scope of duty, and it was made at or near the time of the event. However, the circumstances surrounding the record's creation raised serious questions about its reliability. The opposing party argued that despite technical compliance with requirements (a) and (b), the record should be excluded because the circumstances indicated untrustworthiness under requirement (c).

## What the Court Held

The Court of Appeal:

1. **Section 1280 does not apply where the circumstances indicate untrustworthiness, even when the record technically satisfies the public-employee and contemporaneity requirements.** The trustworthiness requirement in § 1280(c) is an independent, substantive gate that is not automatically satisfied by meeting (a) and (b).

2. **The trial court has discretion to exclude an official record under § 1280(c) when the circumstances of its preparation indicate untrustworthiness.** This discretion is not arbitrary — it is guided by an examination of the sources of information, the method of preparation, and the timing. But it is real discretion, meaning a party can challenge the trustworthiness of any official record and the court must evaluate the challenge on its merits.

3. **Trustworthiness is not presumed from the official character of the record.** The proponent must affirmatively show that the sources and methods indicate trustworthiness. The burden is on the proponent, not on the opponent to show untrustworthiness.

## The Rule

**Technical compliance with § 1280(a) and (b) does not guarantee admissibility. The trustworthiness requirement in § 1280(c) is an independent gate. An official record may be excluded when the circumstances of its creation indicate it is untrustworthy, even though it was created by a public employee within the scope of duty and near the time of the event. The proponent bears the burden of showing trustworthiness.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case, Rupf is the authority that prevents the government seal from being treated as a guarantee of reliability. When official records in the steward's case were created under circumstances that indicate untrustworthiness — police reports based on uncorroborated allegations from interested parties, court records with documented procedural deficiencies, CLETS entries that may have been based on unverified information, CalVCB records with 13 documented audit failures — Rupf authorizes the court to exclude them despite their official character. The seal of the state does not override the requirement that the record actually be trustworthy.
