# People v. Goldsmith, 59 Cal.4th 258 (2014)

## The Case

The prosecution offered photographs from an automated red-light camera system as evidence that the defendant ran a red light. The defendant challenged the photographs' authentication, arguing that digital images generated by an automated system — with no human photographer — could not be properly authenticated under Evidence Code §§ 1400-1401.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **Digital evidence — photographs, videos, computer printouts, and other electronically generated writings — is authenticated the same way as any other writing under Evidence Code §§ 1400-1401.** There is no separate, heightened authentication standard for digital evidence. The proponent must introduce evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that the writing is what it claims to be.

2. **Circumstantial evidence is sufficient to authenticate digital evidence.** The proponent does not need a witness who personally observed the creation of the digital file. Evidence about the system that generated the file, the procedures for maintaining the system, the chain of custody, and the consistency of the evidence with other known facts can all support authentication.

3. **The authentication threshold is not high.** The proponent need only produce evidence sufficient to sustain a finding — not proof beyond a reasonable doubt, not even a preponderance. If reasonable minds could conclude the evidence is what it purports to be, the authentication requirement is met, and the evidence goes to the jury.

4. **Once authenticated, the weight and credibility of digital evidence is for the trier of fact.** Authentication is a threshold question for the court; what the evidence means is a question for the jury.

## The Rule

**Digital evidence is subject to the same authentication requirement as all other writings under §§ 1400-1401. Authentication may be established through circumstantial evidence, including evidence about the system that generated the record, maintenance procedures, chain of custody, and contextual consistency. The threshold is low: sufficient to sustain a finding.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case, Goldsmith establishes that all digital evidence — email printouts, electronic court records, CLETS entries, digital financial records, social media posts, computer-generated reports — must be authenticated, but that authentication can be accomplished circumstantially. This cuts both ways: (1) the steward can authenticate his own digital evidence through circumstantial means; (2) the opposing party's digital evidence must also be authenticated, and if the circumstances show the evidence is unreliable (altered timestamps, missing metadata, system that generated the record was compromised), authentication may fail.
