California Evidence Code § 1450 — Official Seals and Signatures

A writing purporting to be signed or certified by a public employee of any jurisdiction described in Section 1600, and appearing by its terms to be executed within the jurisdiction of the public employee, is presumed to be what it purports to be. Unless the signer is a court officer, the court may require reasonable proof that the signature is genuine.

(Enacted by Stats. 1965, Ch. 299.)

---

California Evidence Code § 1451 — Certified Copies

A copy of a writing certified to be a correct copy by a public employee of any jurisdiction purporting to have custody of the original, where such jurisdiction is described in Section 1600, is presumed to be a correct copy of the original.

(Enacted by Stats. 1965, Ch. 299.)

---

California Evidence Code § 1452 — Official Publications

Official books, pamphlets, or other publications purporting to be issued by a public entity and purporting to contain statutes, administrative rules, court rules, decisions, or other official matter of any jurisdiction described in Section 1600 are presumed to be what they purport to be.

(Enacted by Stats. 1965, Ch. 299.)

---

California Evidence Code § 1453 — Printed Materials

A writing purporting to be a newspaper, periodical, book, pamphlet, or other printed publication, not within the scope of Section 1452, is presumed to be what it purports to be.

(Enacted by Stats. 1965, Ch. 299.)

---

California Evidence Code § 1454 — Trade Inscriptions and Labels

Inscriptions, signs, tags, or labels purporting to have been affixed in the course of business and indicating ownership, control, or origin are presumed to be what they purport to indicate.

(Enacted by Stats. 1965, Ch. 299.)

---
SOURCE: California Evidence Code §§ 1450-1454 (Self-Authentication — Article 3). Enacted Stats. 1965, Ch. 299. Sections are in their original enacted form; verify at leginfo.legislature.ca.gov for any amendments. The self-authentication category was originally limited; federal FRE 902 (enacted 1975) is broader and courts occasionally look to FRE 902 for guidance on analogous California situations.
