# Context — California Evidence Code §§ 1450-1454 Self-Authentication

## The statutes in brief

Sections 1450-1454 define five categories of "self-authenticating" documents — writings that are presumed to be what they purport to be without requiring the proponent to introduce separate extrinsic authentication evidence under § 1400(a). The five categories are:

1. **§ 1450 — Official seals and signatures:** Writings signed or certified by a public employee, within that employee's jurisdiction
2. **§ 1451 — Certified copies:** Copies certified by the custodian of the official record
3. **§ 1452 — Official publications:** Statutes, administrative rules, court decisions, and similar official matter
4. **§ 1453 — Printed publications:** Newspapers, periodicals, books, and other printed matter
5. **§ 1454 — Trade inscriptions:** Labels, tags, and inscriptions indicating business ownership or origin

## How the presumption works

A "presumed" writing under §§ 1450-1454 is admitted without a separate authentication foundation — the proponent need not call a witness to testify that the document is genuine. The presumption is rebuttable: the opponent may introduce evidence that defeats the presumption (e.g., showing that the official signature is forged, that the certified copy was altered, or that the document does not actually originate from the claimed source).

Once the presumption is rebutted by competent evidence, the document is no longer self-authenticating and the proponent must satisfy § 1400(a) through extrinsic evidence.

## § 1450 — Official signatures: the load-bearing category

For the steward's cases, § 1450 is the most operationally significant provision. Many documents in contested litigation claim to be government records bearing official signatures: police reports signed by officers, court orders signed by judges, agency determinations signed by officials, pension records certified by plan administrators.

Under § 1450, such a document is presumed authentic unless rebutted. The rebuttal mechanism is critical: evidence that the claimed official did not sign the document, that the document does not match the agency's records, or that the document was altered after signing defeats the § 1450 presumption and throws the proponent back to § 1400(a).

**The OPD records context:** A purported Oakland Police Department report bearing an officer's signature is entitled to the § 1450 presumption until rebutted. The October 2025 OPD records pickup, which confirmed that the purported 6/2/2009 OPD report does not exist in OPD's records system, is competent rebuttal evidence against the § 1450 presumption. Once rebutted, the proponent must satisfy § 1400(a) with extrinsic evidence — which, without OPD's records, is impossible.

## § 1451 — Certified copies

Certified copies of official records are presumed authentic under § 1451. A certified copy must bear the custodian's certification that it is a correct copy of the original. An uncertified copy does not benefit from § 1451 and must be authenticated under § 1400(a).

This distinction matters for pension records and court records: a certified copy of a UA Local 342 retirement election application is self-authenticating under § 1451; an uncertified photocopy is not.

## California vs. Federal (FRE 902)

FRE 902 (the federal analog) contains a broader list of self-authenticating categories and was amended in 2017 to add certified electronic records. California's §§ 1450-1454 are narrower. In federal court proceedings, FRE 902 may be available for categories not covered by California §§ 1450-1454.

## Strategic significance

Self-authentication is a double-edged sword in contested-document cases:
- The opponent can use it to streamline admission of official records that support their case
- The proponent who introduces a document claiming § 1450 self-authentication faces a rebuttal attack if the claimed originating agency's records contradict the document's claimed authenticity
- When the steward shows that OPD's records do not contain a purported OPD report, he is successfully rebutting the §1450 presumption — defeating self-authentication AND forcing the proponent to §1400(a) extrinsic evidence (which does not exist)
