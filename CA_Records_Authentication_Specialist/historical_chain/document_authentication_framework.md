# CA Records Authentication Specialist — Citizen-Level Historical Chain

## The Document Must Prove It Is What It Claims to Be

---

### I. The Foundation

California Evidence Code §1400 states the principle: **Authentication of a writing is required before it may be received in evidence.** A document cannot be used against someone until the party offering it proves it is genuine.

This is not a technicality. It is a protection against fraud. A forged document, an altered record, an unsigned report, a fabricated authorization — each of these is a weapon if the court treats it as authentic without verification. The authentication requirement is the first line of defense.

### II. The Standards This Citizen Carries

**§1400 — Authentication required.** No writing is admissible without authentication. Period.

**§1401 — What authentication means.** Authentication requires sufficient evidence to sustain a finding that the writing is what the proponent claims it is. Not proof beyond doubt — but enough that a reasonable trier of fact could find it genuine.

**§1410-1421 — How to authenticate.** Multiple methods available: testimony of a witness who saw it executed (§1413), expert comparison of handwriting (§1418), content and circumstances (§1421), official seal or attestation (§1411-1412).

**§1280 — Official records exception.** A writing made by a public employee in the course of duty is admissible as an exception to the hearsay rule IF: (1) it was made by or under the direction of a public employee, (2) it was made within the scope of duty, and (3) the sources of information and method of preparation were such as to indicate its trustworthiness.

### III. For the Steward's Case

The document manipulation findings in the steward's case raise authentication questions at every level:

- **Unsigned reports submitted as evidence.** An unsigned report fails basic authentication under §1401 — there is no way to verify who prepared it or whether it is complete without a signature or other authenticating evidence.

- **The Wiita CST report.** If only two sources were reviewed for a capacity determination, the report's trustworthiness under §1280(c) is questionable — a professional evaluation based on two sources may not meet the "trustworthiness" requirement for official records.

- **Blue Shield authorizations naming a non-existent doctor.** A medical authorization that names "Dilworth" when no such provider exists in NPI fails authentication on its face — the document does not appear to be what it purports to be (a legitimate authorization from a real provider).

- **Financial documents in the house sale.** Nineteen unsigned documents in a $605K real estate transaction. Each unsigned document is an authentication defect.

The authentication framework is the lens through which every document in the case must be evaluated. The question is always: is this document what it claims to be, and can that be proven?
