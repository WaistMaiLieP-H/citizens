# Reasoning — Cal. Rules of Court, Rule 5.215 (DV Protocols in Mediation)

**Standard ID:** CAL_RULES_COURT_5_215_DV_PROTOCOLS
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why Rule 5.215 Exists Alongside § 3172

Section 3172 establishes the statutory mandate for separate sessions in DV cases. Rule 5.215 operationalizes that mandate with specific procedural requirements: screening before any session, separate waiting areas, separate entry/exit, no direct communication requirements, safety planning, and documentation.

The gap between § 3172 and rule 5.215 is the gap between a mandate and a protocol. A mediator who technically conducts "separate sessions" but puts the parties in the same waiting room, requires them to pass each other at the door, or refuses to discuss safety planning has complied with the letter of § 3172 while violating rule 5.215. Rule 5.215 closes that gap.

---

## Why Pre-Session Screening Is the Critical First Gate

Rule 5.215 requires screening before any session — before the parties are in any proximity. This is not a formality. DV victims often do not disclose abuse in joint settings, in the presence of authority figures, or when they believe the disclosure will make things worse. A screening conducted in a private, separate setting before any mediation begins is the only environment where honest disclosure is possible.

A mediator who skips pre-session screening — or conducts it in the presence of the other party — has contaminated the process from the start. Any subsequent finding about the absence of DV is founded on a flawed screening process.

---

## Why the Documentation Requirement Is the Audit Tool

Rule 5.215(f) requires the mediator to document that screening occurred. This documentation requirement exists precisely to create an auditable record. Without it, a mediator could claim screening happened when it did not, or deny that DV was disclosed when it was.

Absence of screening documentation = failure to conduct or document screening = rule 5.215(f) violation. The absence is the finding.

---

## Application in Practice

For any mediation record in a DV-affected case: confirm screening documentation exists. Confirm separate sessions were conducted. Confirm no joint communication was required. Confirm safety planning occurred. Each unconfirmed element is an auditable gap. In a case with a protective order in effect, any joint session is a per se violation.
