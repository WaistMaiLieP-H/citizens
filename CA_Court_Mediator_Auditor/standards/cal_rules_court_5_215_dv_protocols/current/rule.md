# Domestic Violence Protocols in Custody Mediation — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Rules of Court, rule 5.215
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** January 1, 2012 (current form)

## The Rule

Rule 5.215 implements the mandatory domestic violence safety protocols for all child custody mediation and recommending counseling proceedings where domestic violence is a factor.

**Mandatory DV screening (rule 5.215(b)):**
Before any mediation session, the mediator or recommending counselor shall screen each party separately for a history of domestic violence. Screening must occur before the parties are brought together or placed in proximity.

**Separate sessions — mandatory in DV cases (rule 5.215(c)):**
If domestic violence is identified through screening or is otherwise apparent:
- Sessions shall be conducted separately and at different times
- Parties shall not be required to wait in the same waiting area
- Parties shall not be required to enter or exit through the same location simultaneously
- Parties shall not be required to communicate directly with each other

**The mediator shall not:**
- Conduct joint sessions when either party reports fear of the other
- Apply pressure to reach agreement when one party expresses fear
- Treat the case as a mutual dispute when one party has documented the other's violence
- Ignore protective orders that are in effect

**Safety planning (rule 5.215(d)):**
The mediator shall provide information about DV resources, shelters, legal protections, and safety planning options to the party who has experienced or is at risk of domestic violence.

**Confidentiality of DV screening (rule 5.215(e)):**
The results of DV screening are confidential and may not be disclosed to the other party or used against the screened party in any proceeding.

**Documentation requirement (rule 5.215(f)):**
The mediator shall document that DV screening was conducted. The documentation does not need to include the screening results but must confirm the screening occurred.

## Key Elements

1. **Screening is mandatory before any session** — not optional; failure to screen is a rule 5.215 violation regardless of whether DV exists.
2. **Joint sessions are prohibited when DV is present** — "shall not require" means the prohibition is absolute; proceeding with joint sessions after DV is disclosed is a per se rule violation.
3. **Fear is sufficient** — even if formal DV finding has not been made, a party's expressed fear of the other triggers the separate session requirement.
4. **Documentation of screening must exist** — absence of documentation = unverifiable compliance = auditable gap.
5. **Screening results are confidential** — a mediator who discloses one party's screening answers to the other has violated rule 5.215(e).
