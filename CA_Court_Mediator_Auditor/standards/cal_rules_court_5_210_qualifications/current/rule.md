# Child Custody Recommending Counselor — Qualifications — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Rules of Court, rule 5.210
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** January 1, 2012 (current form); prior versions from 1999

## The Rule

Rule 5.210 establishes the minimum qualifications for any person appointed by a California court to perform child custody recommending counseling (formerly called "mediation" in recommending counties).

**Minimum educational qualifications (rule 5.210(b)):**
A recommending counselor must have a master's or doctoral degree in:
- Psychology, psychiatry, or social work; OR
- Marriage, family, and child counseling; OR
- A behavioral science field substantially related to marriage and family interpersonal relationships; OR
A juris doctor degree with demonstrated competency in behavioral issues affecting children and families.

**Minimum experience requirement (rule 5.210(c)):**
- At least two years of experience providing mental health services, counseling, or psychotherapy to families and children; AND
- Practical experience in child custody and visitation matters.

**Domestic violence training — mandatory (rule 5.210(d)):**
All recommending counselors must complete:
- A minimum of **40 hours** of domestic violence training before conducting any child custody recommending counseling where DV is alleged or apparent; AND
- Ongoing continuing education in DV issues (minimum 16 hours per year)

**Child abuse training (rule 5.210(e)):**
Training in the dynamics of child abuse, child sexual abuse, and child abduction.

**Substance abuse training (rule 5.210(f)):**
Training in the assessment of substance abuse issues affecting child custody and visitation.

**Continuing education (rule 5.210(g)):**
- Minimum 16 hours per year of continuing education in family mediation, child development, domestic violence, and related subjects.

**Verification requirement:** Courts must verify that recommending counselors meet these standards before appointment. The counselor's qualifications must be documented in court records and are subject to PRA request.

## Key Elements

1. **40 hours DV training is mandatory** — not optional, not suggested; a counselor without this training cannot legally conduct recommending counseling in a DV case.
2. **Two-year experience minimum** — must be in mental health services to families and children; general counseling experience in other fields does not satisfy this requirement.
3. **Qualifications are court-verifiable** — the county court maintains qualification records; these are obtainable via PRA.
4. **Non-compliant appointment = ultra vires report** — a recommendation issued by a person who does not meet rule 5.210 standards carries no legal authority.
