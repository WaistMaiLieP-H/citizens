# Reasoning — Cal. Fam. Code §§ 3160–3164 (Mediation Act Foundation)

**Standard ID:** FAM_CODE_3160_3164_MEDIATION_ACT
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why This Standard Exists

The Mediation of Custody and Visitation Disputes Act was enacted to reduce the adversarial burden on children caught in custody disputes. The Legislature recognized that children are harmed not only by custody outcomes but by the litigation process itself — watching parents fight, testifying, being subjected to evaluations. Mediation was intended to substitute a cooperative problem-solving process for adversarial litigation.

The qualification requirements of § 3164 exist because the stakes of an incompetent mediator are measured in children's lives. A mediator who does not understand child development may propose arrangements that harm children. A mediator who does not understand domestic violence may expose a DV victim to danger. The Legislature set minimum qualifications precisely because the consequences of getting it wrong fall on children who had no voice in the selection.

---

## Why an Unqualified Mediator's Report Is Ultra Vires

Section 3164 is not a preference — it is a threshold. A person who does not meet the § 3164 qualifications does not have the legal authority to perform court-ordered mediation. Any report they issue is issued without authority — the same as a judgment entered by someone without judicial authority.

Courts that appoint unqualified mediators are not exercising discretion. They are violating the statute. Any party who receives an adverse recommendation from an unqualified mediator is entitled to object on ultra vires grounds before the report is considered by the court.

---

## Why DV Knowledge Is a Mandatory Qualification

Section 3164(a)(4) requires knowledge of domestic violence as one of the four mandatory qualification components. This is not a specialty add-on — it is a baseline. The Legislature understood that family court cases frequently involve DV histories, and a mediator who lacks DV knowledge will misidentify the dynamics of DV cases as mutual conflict, apply inappropriate joint-session procedures, and produce recommendations that place DV victims at risk.

A mediator without DV knowledge who handles a DV-affected case has both violated § 3164 (by lacking a mandatory qualification) and § 3172 (by misapplying DV protocols). The failures compound.

---

## Application in Practice

For any mediator or recommending counselor: obtain their qualification documentation via PRA. Verify each § 3164 element: degree, experience, DV knowledge, child development knowledge. If any element is missing, the mediator lacked authority. Any report they issued is subject to challenge.
