# Reasoning — Cal. Fam. Code §§ 3170–3172 (Mandatory Mediation and DV Protocols)

**Standard ID:** FAM_CODE_3170_3172_MANDATORY_MEDIATION
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why § 3172 Separate Sessions Are Non-Negotiable

Section 3172 uses the word "shall" — the most unambiguous term of legal obligation in California statutory drafting. "Shall make reasonable efforts to meet with the parties separately" means the mediator must do this. There is no escape hatch for a mediator who believes the parties can handle a joint session, or who thinks the DV allegation is unsubstantiated, or who finds joint sessions more efficient.

The Legislature chose mandatory separate sessions in DV cases for a documented reason: joint mediation sessions in DV relationships recreate the coercive dynamic that produced the violence. A DV victim in a joint session with their abuser cannot freely express their interests, cannot advocate for the children's safety, and cannot refuse an agreement that places them at risk. The "agreement" reached in a joint DV session is not a product of free negotiation — it is a product of coercion conducted in the mediator's presence.

---

## Why "Alleged" DV Is Sufficient to Trigger § 3172

Section 3172 does not require a court finding, conviction, or sustained DV determination. It requires a "history of domestic violence between the parties." History means what has occurred, not what has been proven in court. When a party alleges DV — in pleadings, in screening, in communications to the mediator — that allegation is sufficient to trigger the mandatory separate session requirement.

A mediator who waits for "proof" of DV before applying § 3172 protocols has set the wrong threshold. The protection is prospective: it applies before the joint session occurs, not after the harm is done.

---

## Why a Protective Order Automatically Triggers § 3172

Section 3172 lists protective orders as an independent trigger alongside "history of domestic violence." A DVRO, an Emergency Protective Order, or any other court-issued protective order between the parties is, by itself, sufficient to require separate sessions — regardless of whether the parties agree to meet jointly, regardless of the mediator's assessment of current danger.

A court has already determined that a protective order was necessary. The mediator cannot second-guess that determination by proceeding with joint sessions.

---

## Application in Practice

In any case with: DV allegations in the pleadings, a protective order of any kind, or a party's expressed fear of the other party — separate sessions are mandatory. The audit question is simple: did the mediator conduct separate sessions? If not, § 3172 was violated. The finding does not require proof of harm to the parties — the violation is the failure to follow the mandatory procedure.
