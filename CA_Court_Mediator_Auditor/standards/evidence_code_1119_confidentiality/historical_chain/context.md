# Historical Chain — Cal. Evid. Code § 1119

**Pre-1997:** California mediation confidentiality governed by a patchwork of case law and individual mediation agreements. No uniform statutory protection. Courts reached inconsistent results on whether mediation communications could be used in subsequent litigation.

**1997 (Stats. Ch. 772):** Legislature enacts uniform mediation confidentiality provisions, Evidence Code §§ 1115-1128. Section 1119 establishes the general confidentiality rule. The Legislature explicitly chose a broad protection rule — erring on the side of confidentiality to encourage open mediation.

**Rojas v. Superior Court (2004) 33 Cal.4th 407:** California Supreme Court holds § 1119 mediation confidentiality is broad and strictly applied. The court declines to recognize equitable exceptions not in the statute. For exceptions to apply, they must be in the Evidence Code.

**Post-Rojas:** Legislature has added specific statutory exceptions (§§ 1120-1123) where needed. The mandatory reporting exception for child abuse was clarified as falling outside § 1119 protection because Pen. Code § 11166 is a separate statutory obligation.

**Present:** Section 1119 is one of California's most strictly applied evidentiary privileges. Its limits are precisely defined by the statutory exceptions.
