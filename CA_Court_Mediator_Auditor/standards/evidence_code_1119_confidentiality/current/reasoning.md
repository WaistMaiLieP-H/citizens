# Reasoning — Cal. Evid. Code § 1119 (Mediation Confidentiality)

**Standard ID:** EVIDENCE_CODE_1119_CONFIDENTIALITY
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why Mediation Confidentiality Exists

Evidence Code § 1119 protects mediation communications to encourage frank disclosure. If parties knew their statements in mediation could be used against them in the same litigation, they would never negotiate openly. The entire value of mediation — the ability to explore creative solutions without the constraints of the evidentiary record — depends on confidentiality.

The Legislature made an explicit policy choice: privileging the mediation process over the evidentiary completeness of litigation. This is a significant trade-off. The question for the auditor is whether that trade-off was properly applied — whether the confidentiality shield was used correctly, and whether its limits were respected.

---

## Why the Mediator's Court Report Is Not Confidential

The § 3174 report submitted to the court is a public document in a court proceeding. It is not a mediation communication — it is the mediator's official filing. Section 1119 does not protect it. A mediator who claims that their court report is confidential has misapplied § 1119. A mediator who refuses to provide their report to both parties citing confidentiality has violated § 3176 (distribution requirement).

The distinction matters: what was said in mediation (protected) is different from what the mediator wrote in their official report (not protected). Parties have full access to the report and can challenge every element of it.

---

## Why Mandatory Reporting Overrides § 1119

Penal Code § 11166 requires mandatory reporters — which mediators in California are — to report known or reasonably suspected child abuse regardless of how they learned of it. This obligation is not suspended because the disclosure occurred in mediation. A mediator who fails to report suspected child abuse because of § 1119 has committed a mandatory reporting violation.

This exception is critical in cases where child welfare concerns are disclosed during mediation. The mediator cannot hide behind confidentiality to avoid a legal reporting obligation.

---

## Application in Practice

For any mediation record: identify every document. Determine whether each document is a protected mediation communication (§ 1119) or a non-protected court filing or agreement (§§ 1120-1123). Flag any claim of § 1119 confidentiality applied to a court report — that is a misapplication of the statute. Flag any failure to report child abuse disclosed in mediation — that is a mandatory reporting violation that § 1119 does not excuse.
