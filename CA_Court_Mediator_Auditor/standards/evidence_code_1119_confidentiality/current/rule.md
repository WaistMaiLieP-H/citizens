# Mediation Confidentiality — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Evid. Code §§ 1115–1128 (primary: § 1119)
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** Stats. 1997, Ch. 772; amendments through 2012

## The Rule

**§ 1115 — Definitions:**
- "Mediation" means a process in which a neutral person or persons facilitate communication between the disputants to assist them in reaching a mutually acceptable agreement.
- "Mediation consultation" means a communication between a person and a mediator for the purpose of initiating, considering, or reconvening a mediation.

**§ 1119 — General Confidentiality Rule:**
Except as otherwise provided:

(a) No evidence of anything said or any admission made during the course of a mediation or mediation consultation is admissible or subject to discovery in any civil action or proceeding.

(b) No writing made during the course of a mediation or mediation consultation is admissible or subject to discovery.

(c) All communications, negotiations, or settlement discussions by and between participants in the course of a mediation are confidential.

**§ 1120 — Exceptions — What is NOT protected:**
- An agreement that is intended by the parties to be enforceable (a written mediation agreement signed by all parties, subject to § 1123)
- Evidence otherwise admissible or subject to discovery that does not lose that status solely because it was used in a mediation
- A report, assessment, or evaluation prepared by a mediator pursuant to court order (the mediator's § 3174 report is NOT confidential as to its contents)
- Communications that are required to be disclosed by statute (e.g., mandatory child abuse reports under Pen. Code § 11166)

**§ 1122 — Disclosure by agreement:**
A communication or writing made in the course of a mediation is not confidential if all parties to the mediation and the mediator consent in writing to its disclosure.

**§ 1123 — Written settlement agreements:**
A written agreement prepared during or pursuant to a mediation is not confidential if it satisfies the requirements of § 1123 (signed, written, states it is enforceable).

**§ 1128 — Sanctions for improper disclosure:**
Any party or mediator who discloses confidential mediation communications without authorization may be subject to sanctions.

## Key Elements

1. **§ 1119 confidentiality is broad but not absolute** — oral statements, writings, and negotiations during mediation are protected; but the exceptions are real and enforceable.
2. **The mediator's court report is not confidential** — the § 3174 report submitted to the court is a public court document; § 1119 does not shield it.
3. **Mandatory reporter obligations override confidentiality** — child abuse disclosures must be reported regardless of § 1119; a mediator who fails to report because of confidentiality concerns has misapplied the rule.
4. **A mediator who discloses protected communications can be sanctioned** — and a mediator who withholds reportable disclosures behind § 1119 is also in violation.
5. **Settlement agreements can be made enforceable** — by meeting the § 1123 requirements; an unsigned mediation agreement does not bind anyone.
