# Reasoning — Cal. Fam. Code §§ 3174–3177 (Mediator Report and Witness Limitations)

**Standard ID:** FAM_CODE_3174_3177_MEDIATOR_REPORT
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why the Mediator's Report Has Bounded Scope

Sections 3174-3177 reflect a structural design choice: the mediator is not a judge, not an evaluator, and not a fact-finder. The mediator's report is a proposed parenting plan with supporting rationale — not a determination of who is telling the truth, not a finding that one parent is dangerous, and not a credibility assessment of DV allegations.

When a mediator's report includes adjudicative findings — conclusions about whether abuse occurred, which parent's account is credible, whether DV allegations are "founded" — the mediator has exceeded the scope of §§ 3174-3175. These findings belong in the exclusive domain of the court, which has the authority, the procedural protections, and the evidence rules to make them properly.

---

## Why the 10-Day Notice Rule Matters

Section 3176 requires parties to receive the mediator's report at least 10 days before any hearing where it will be considered. This is not a formality — it is a due process protection. A party who receives a mediator's report at the hearing has no meaningful opportunity to prepare a response, obtain expert input, or challenge specific findings.

Violating the 10-day rule is not merely a procedural irregularity. It is a due process violation that compromises the fairness of the entire hearing. Any hearing proceeding on a report provided fewer than 10 days earlier was conducted without proper foundation.

---

## Why § 3177 Limits the Mediator as Witness

Section 3177's limit on mediator testimony reflects the confidentiality framework of Evidence Code § 1119. If the mediator could testify freely about what was said in mediation, § 1119 would be meaningless — parties would be deterred from frank discussion if their statements could be used against them in the same proceeding. The mediator's testimony is therefore bounded by the written report.

A mediator who testifies beyond the report — offering opinions, characterizations of party conduct, or assessments of credibility that were not in the written report — has exceeded § 3177 authority. Any such testimony should be objected to as beyond the mediator's permitted scope.

---

## Application in Practice

Read the mediator's report against §§ 3174-3175: every finding, every recommendation, every characterization. For each element: is this a parenting plan proposal (authorized) or an adjudicative finding (not authorized)? Flag every excess of scope. Cross-reference against the 10-day notice requirement. If the report was delivered fewer than 10 days before a hearing, flag the due process violation.
