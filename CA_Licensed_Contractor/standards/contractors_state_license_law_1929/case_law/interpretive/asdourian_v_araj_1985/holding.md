# Asdourian v. Araj, 38 Cal. 3d 276 (1985)

## The Case

A contractor performed work without a valid license. When sued by the property owner for defective work, the contractor counterclaimed for payment. The property owner invoked BPC §7031, which bars unlicensed contractors from suing for compensation.

## What the Court Held

The California Supreme Court:

1. **BPC §7031 bars an unlicensed contractor from recovering any compensation for work performed — period.** The contractor cannot sue for the contract price, quantum meruit, or any other theory of recovery. The bar is absolute.

2. **The purpose of §7031 is to deter unlicensed contracting by removing the economic incentive.** If an unlicensed contractor could recover for work performed, the licensing requirement would be toothless — contractors would have no financial reason to get licensed.

3. **Substantial compliance with the licensing requirement is not enough.** The contractor must have been duly licensed at all times during performance. A lapsed license, an expired license, or a license obtained after the work was performed does not satisfy the requirement.

## The Rule

**An unlicensed contractor cannot recover any compensation for work performed, under any legal theory. The bar is absolute and substantial compliance does not save the claim. BPC §7031.**

## Why This Case Matters

For the steward's case: if someone performed plumbing work using the steward's license without authorization, every payment that person received was potentially obtained through fraud. And if that person's license was actually invalid (because the true licensee — the steward — didn't authorize the use), every contract that person entered is subject to the §7031 bar.
