# Independent Medical Review System — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Health & Safety Code § 1374.30
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** 2001 (operative July 1, 2015 per subdivision (o)); established January 1, 2001

## The Rule

When a health care service plan (Knox-Keene plan under DMHC jurisdiction) denies, modifies, or
delays health care services based on medical necessity, the enrolled patient has the right to
request an Independent Medical Review (IMR) through the California Department of Managed Health
Care (DMHC). The IMR decision is binding on the plan.

**Eligibility requirements (§1374.30(j)):**
1. The enrollee's provider has recommended the service as medically necessary, OR the enrollee
   received urgent/emergency care the provider determined was necessary, OR the enrollee has been
   seen by an in-plan provider for the condition
2. The plan has denied, modified, or delayed the service based on medical necessity
3. The enrollee has filed a grievance with the plan and either the grievance was upheld or it
   remained unresolved after 30 days (or 3 days for expedited cases)

**The IMR process:** Plan must provide the independent reviewer all relevant medical records within
3 business days of the DMHC notice. The enrollee pays no fees. The IMR organization is independent —
it has no financial relationship with the plan.

**Binding effect:** An IMR determination that the service is medically necessary is binding on the
plan. A plan that refuses to provide the service after an IMR determination in the enrollee's favor
is in violation of Knox-Keene Act and subject to DMHC enforcement.

**Distinction — medical necessity vs. coverage decision (§1374.30(b)-(c)):**
- A medical necessity dispute: "Is this service appropriate for this patient's condition?" → IMR
- A coverage decision: "Does this patient's contract include this service?" → DMHC §1368 review
- DMHC is the final arbiter when there is a question about which category applies (§1374.30(d)(3))

## Key Elements

1. **Grievance prerequisite** — must have filed a plan grievance and waited 30 days (3 days expedited)
2. **Six-month application window** — from the qualifying event; director may extend
3. **No fee** — enrollee pays nothing for IMR
4. **Binding decision** — IMR finding overrides the plan's medical necessity determination
5. **Medical necessity only** — IMR does not reach coverage exclusions (those go to §1368)

## What This Means in Practice

For the spine surgery fraud cases: if the Blue Shield denial was for medical necessity (not a
coverage exclusion), and if Blue Shield is a Knox-Keene plan or its UR was conducted under
Knox-Keene-equivalent rules, the steward or any California enrollee has the right to demand IMR.
The grievance window may have tolled — §1374.30(k) allows the director to extend the six-month
application window. The IMR is the fastest path to a binding reversal of a wrongful medical
necessity denial, independent of litigation.
