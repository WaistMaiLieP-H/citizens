# Historical Chain — HSC_1374_30_INDEPENDENT_MEDICAL_REVIEW
## Origin: AB 55 (2000) — Independent Medical Review System

## The Pre-2001 Problem

Before IMR, California patients denied coverage for medical necessity had two options:
1. Internal grievance — reviewed by the same plan that denied the claim (structural conflict of interest)
2. Litigation — expensive, slow, and typically occurred after the health crisis that the denied service was meant to prevent

California courts were flooded with bad faith insurance claims in the 1990s — Wilson v. Blue Cross (1990), Sarchett v. Blue Shield (1987) — establishing that arbitrary denials without adequate investigation were tortious. But litigation as the only remedy meant that many patients died or suffered serious harm before any judicial intervention.

## The Legislative Response

AB 55 (2000) created IMR as a real-time independent clinical review mechanism. The key legislative findings:
- Internal grievance processes are inherently conflicted
- Medical necessity determinations require clinical expertise independent of financial incentives
- Patient access to independent review is a right, not a privilege
- The cost of external review is properly borne by the plan (who denies the claim), not the patient (who is harmed by the denial)

## What IMR Changed

Before IMR: deny internally → patient either gives up or sues (2+ year timeline)
After IMR: deny internally → patient requests IMR → independent physician reviews in 30 days → binding determination

The binding nature of the IMR determination is the critical feature: it is not advisory. A plan that denies an IMR determination is in defiance of a DMHC-binding order.

## The Wound

The wound is the gap between the right and the notice: §1374.30(i) requires prominent display of IMR rights in every denial letter. A denial letter that fails to include IMR notice effectively suppresses the right — the patient does not exercise a right they were never told they had. The silence in the denial letter is itself a §1374.30 violation.
