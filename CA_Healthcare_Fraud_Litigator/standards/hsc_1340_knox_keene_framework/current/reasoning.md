# Reasoning — HSC_1340_KNOX_KEENE_FRAMEWORK

## Why Knox-Keene Matters

Knox-Keene (1975) was California's response to the Federal HMO Act of 1973 (Pub. L. 93-222), which incentivized HMO formation nationally. California recognized that managed care organizations operating under capitation contracts would have powerful financial incentives to deny care — the less care delivered, the more of the capitation payment the plan retained.

The legislature's response was not to ban managed care — it was to regulate it. Knox-Keene:
1. Required DMHC oversight of all HMOs operating in California
2. Imposed explicit duties on health plans (delivery, timeliness, non-interference)
3. Created an enforcement mechanism (DMHC license suspension/revocation)

The non-interference duty in §1367(g) is the foundational rule: the plan is the financial administrator; the physician is the medical decision-maker. When the plan's UR apparatus overrides the physician's medical necessity determination, it violates this boundary.

## The Structural Problem with Prior Authorization

Prior authorization as administered by health plans in 2020-2026 routinely violates §1367(g):
- UR criteria are proprietary (not developed with practicing providers as §10123.135(b) requires)
- Non-physicians or algorithms make initial reviews
- Physicians who attempt to appeal face systematic administrative barriers
- Denial rates correlate with financial incentives, not clinical variation

This is not a California-specific observation: the 2023 U.S. Senate Finance Committee investigation of Medicare Advantage prior authorization denials found that 13% of Medicare Advantage prior authorization denials were for services that met CMS coverage criteria — meaning the denials were not medically appropriate, they were financially motivated.

## DMHC vs. CDI Jurisdiction

**DMHC** regulates Knox-Keene entities (HMOs, health care service plans) — Blue Shield Health is a DMHC-licensed entity.  
**CDI** regulates disability insurers and traditional health insurance products — Blue Shield's disability products fall under CDI and Ins. Code §10123.135.

For Case #11: the operative contract determines which regulatory agency has jurisdiction. If it was a Knox-Keene plan product, DMHC / §1367 applies. If it was a disability insurance product, CDI / §10123.135 applies. Both may apply concurrently.
