# Rule — HSC_1340_KNOX_KEENE_FRAMEWORK

**Citation:** Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 1340 (Act title), 1367 (health plan duties), 1367.01 (timely access)  
**Standard:** Knox-Keene Health Care Service Plan Act — Health Plan Duties and Non-Interference

## The Rule

The Knox-Keene Health Care Service Plan Act of 1975 (Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 1340 et seq.) establishes DMHC as the regulatory authority over health care service plans (HMOs) operating in California. Blue Shield of California is a Knox-Keene regulated entity.

## The Core Non-Interference Duty (§ 1367(g))

A health care service plan shall not interfere with the practice of medicine by any licensed physician or health care practitioner. Medical decisions affecting covered benefits shall be rendered by qualified medical providers acting in the interest of the enrollee, unhindered by fiscal and administrative management.

**What this prohibits:**
- Administrative overrides of physician medical necessity determinations
- UR criteria designed to systematically deny clinical decisions
- Non-physician review that supplants physician judgment (mirrors §10123.135(e))
- Algorithmic denial systems that remove clinical discretion from licensed providers

## The Delivery Duty (§ 1367(a), (b))

A Knox-Keene plan must:
1. Provide all health care services the plan contracted to provide
2. Provide services in a timely fashion appropriate to the enrollee's condition

## DMHC Enforcement

The DMHC has authority to:
- Issue cease and desist orders
- Assess administrative penalties up to $100,000 per violation
- Suspend or revoke the plan's Knox-Keene license
- Appoint a conservator to operate the plan

## Application to Case #11

Blue Shield's prior authorization denial of spine surgery, if made by a non-physician using algorithmic UR without physician review, violates:
1. §1367(g) — interference with the spine surgeon's medical necessity determination
2. §10123.135(e) — non-physician denial of health care service
3. §1374.30(i) — denial letter without IMR notice

The three violations work together: the denial was procedurally defective (§10123.135), interfered with medical practice (§1367(g)), and failed to disclose the remedy (§1374.30(i)).
