# Historical Chain — PEN_550_INSURANCE_FRAUD
## Origin: California Health Care Fraud Provisions (1994 era)

## Pre-History: The 1970s-1980s Gap

Before §550's health care provisions were strengthened, California health care fraud was prosecuted under the general fraud statutes (PC §487 grand theft by false pretense, PC §532 obtaining money by false pretense). These statutes required proof of the victim's reliance and were difficult to apply to insurance fraud where the insurer — not an individual — was the direct victim.

## The 1990s Healthcare Fraud Epidemic

California's 1990s managed care expansion created an entirely new fraud landscape:
- Capitated billing (per-member per-month) incentivized under-treatment
- Fee-for-service billing incentivized over-treatment and duplicate billing
- Neither system had adequate fraud controls

CDI estimates that health care fraud in California exceeded $1 billion annually by 1993. The legislature responded with §550's strengthened health care provisions — creating specific health care fraud counts (§550(a)(6)-(9)) independent of the general fraud statutes.

## What Was Lost Before §550(a)(6)-(9)

Without health care-specific fraud provisions:
- Duplicate billing required proof the insurer actually relied on the duplicate claim — difficult when computer systems auto-processed claims
- Non-rendered services claims required individual proof for each transaction
- Pattern evidence was difficult to aggregate into a single charge

§550's "knowing" standard removed reliance as an element for health care counts. Each false claim is independently chargeable without proving what the insurer actually believed.

## The Wound

The wound is this: the same UR system that denied a legitimate spine surgery claim with a non-physician reviewer and an AI algorithm, is the same system that would be reviewing the provider's billing submissions. The insurer's fraud (denying legitimate claims with false physician review statements) and the provider's potential fraud (duplicate MRI billing) operate in the same transaction — and both are §550 violations.
