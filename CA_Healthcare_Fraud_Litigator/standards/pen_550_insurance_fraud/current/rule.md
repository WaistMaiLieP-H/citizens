# Insurance Fraud — Health Care Benefit Fraud — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Pen. Code § 550
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** 1989 (original); health care benefit provisions added over time; current form reflects AB/SB consolidations through 2021

## The Rule

California Penal Code § 550 criminalizes insurance fraud. For health care benefit fraud
specifically, subdivisions (a)(6) through (a)(9) are the operative provisions:

- **§550(a)(6):** Knowingly making or causing to be made a false or fraudulent claim for payment
  of a health care benefit — felony if amount exceeds $950
- **§550(a)(7):** Knowingly submitting a claim for a health care benefit that was not used by or
  on behalf of the claimant — felony if amount exceeds $950
- **§550(a)(8):** Knowingly presenting multiple claims for payment of the same health care benefit
  with intent to defraud
- **§550(a)(9):** Knowingly presenting undercharges for health care benefits without simultaneously
  reconciling known overcharges for the same claimant

Subdivision (b) covers written statements: presenting or preparing any written or oral statement
in support of a claim knowing it contains false or misleading information is a separate offense.
Subdivision (b)(3) specifically covers concealing the occurrence of an event that affects the right
to an insurance benefit.

**Penalties:** For §(a)(6)-(9) when the amount exceeds $950: 2, 3, or 5 years in state prison, or
up to one year in county jail, plus fine up to $50,000 or double the fraud amount, whichever is
greater. Mandatory restitution ordered in all convictions.

## Key Elements

1. **Knowing** — defendant must know the claim is false, not merely negligent or mistaken
2. **Health care benefit** — includes any claim submitted under a private insurance policy, workers'
   comp health benefit (§550(a)(10)), or federal program
3. **$950 threshold** — below $950 is a misdemeanor unless aggregate in any 12-month period exceeds $950
4. **False or fraudulent** — the specific false statement or misrepresentation must be identified
5. **Aid/abet/conspire** — the statute expressly reaches those who aid, abet, solicit, or conspire

## What This Means in Practice

Four duplicate MRI pages in a medical billing file are a potential §550(a)(8) issue — multiple
presentations of the same film can be billed as separate studies. The handwritten annotations on
those duplicates may constitute the "written statement containing false or misleading information"
under §550(b)(1). The question is not whether the MRIs were duplicates — it is whether the billing
reflected multiple charges for the same study. That requires comparing the billing records to the
radiology report count. This Citizen maps the document evidence to the statute's elements. The
criminal referral decision belongs to the steward and counsel.
