# Prior Authorization — Utilization Review Requirements — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Ins. Code § 10123.135
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** 2000 (original); AI/algorithm prohibition subdivision (j) added 2024

## The Rule

A California disability insurer that uses utilization review (UR) or utilization management (UM)
functions — whether performed in-house or through a contracted entity — must follow specific rules
before it can deny, modify, or delay a health care service on medical necessity grounds.

The core prohibition is in subdivision (e): a non-physician may not deny or modify a request for
authorization of health care services for reasons of medical necessity. The individual making the
denial must be a licensed physician or a licensed health care professional who is competent to
evaluate the specific clinical issues involved. A nurse reviewer denying a spine surgery authorization
without being a licensed physician competent in spine is a per se violation of this subdivision.

The timeline rules are mandatory: standard decisions within 5 business days of receiving necessary
information; urgent decisions within 72 hours. The insurer must communicate its decision to the
requesting provider within 24 hours of the decision. Denial, delay, or modification must be
communicated to the insured in writing within 2 business days.

The 2024 AI prohibition (subdivision (j)) adds that an artificial intelligence, algorithm, or other
software tool may not deny, delay, or modify health care services based on medical necessity.
The determination of medical necessity must be made by a licensed physician or licensed health care
professional — not by an algorithm — reviewing the requesting provider's recommendation and the
patient's individual clinical circumstances.

## Key Elements

1. **Physician reviewer requirement** — Only a licensed physician or licensed health care professional
   competent in the specific clinical issue may deny or modify for medical necessity (§10123.135(e))
2. **Timeline compliance** — Standard: 5 business days; urgent: 72 hours; communication to provider: 24 hours
3. **Criteria disclosure** — The specific criteria or guidelines used must be disclosed to the provider
   and insured in any denial, delay, or modification communication (§10123.135(h)(4))
4. **AI prohibition** — Effective 2024, an AI, algorithm, or software tool may not make the medical
   necessity determination; it may assist but may not supplant provider decision-making (§10123.135(j)(2))
5. **Written policies required** — Insurer must maintain and file written UR/UM policies with CDI;
   must disclose to insureds and providers on request (§10123.135(b))
6. **No MICRA exposure** — Compliance with this section does not make the insurer a health care provider
   for purposes of MICRA or any other liability cap (§10123.135(k))

## What This Means in Practice

When a prior authorization denial arrives, the first two questions are: (1) Who signed it — a licensed
physician competent in the specific specialty, or someone else? and (2) Was it issued within the
statutory timeline? If either answer fails, the denial is a per se violation regardless of whether
the underlying clinical decision was correct. A nurse reviewer denying lumbar spine surgery is
not a disputable medical judgment — it is an unauthorized act. The remedy is CDI complaint under
§10123.135(h)(6), appeal, and IMR under HSC §1374.30.
