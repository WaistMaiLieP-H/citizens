# Wilson v. Blue Cross of Southern California

**Citation:** 222 Cal.App.3d 660 (1990)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second District
**Year:** 1990
**Status:** PROPOSED

## Holding

A health insurer that unreasonably denies medically necessary inpatient care in bad faith may be
held liable in tort for the foreseeable harm resulting from that denial, including death. The insurer's
bad faith denial can be a proximate cause of patient harm.

## Why It Matters

Wilson closes the causation gap that insurers used to argue after Sarchett: that even if a denial
was in bad faith, the insured had to prove that a different insurer would have approved care and
that care would have saved the patient. Wilson holds that foreseeability of harm from a bad faith
denial is the standard, not certainty of different outcome. For the spine surgery fraud cases,
Wilson supports the argument that Blue Shield's §10123.135(e) violation (nurse reviewer denial)
is not just a regulatory infraction — it is a tortious act that caused foreseeable harm.
