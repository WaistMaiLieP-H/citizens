# Sarchett v. Blue Shield of California

**Citation:** 43 Cal.3d 1 (1987)
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Year:** 1987
**Status:** PROPOSED

## Holding

A health insurer that arbitrarily denies coverage without adequate investigation of the claim
violates the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing inherent in every insurance contract.
The insured's vulnerability and inability to negotiate on equal terms creates a special duty of
fair dealing that goes beyond ordinary contract breach.

## Why It Matters

Sarchett is the pre-statutory anchor. Before §10123.135 codified the physician-reviewer requirement,
Sarchett established that arbitrary UR denials — including those made without qualified clinical
review — are actionable in tort, not just contract. This means a §10123.135(e) violation (non-physician
denial) is not only a statutory per se violation subject to CDI penalties; it is also evidence of
the bad faith conduct Sarchett defines. The case gives the insured a concurrent bad faith claim
alongside the statutory claim.
