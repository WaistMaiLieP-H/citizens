# Knox-Keene Health Care Service Plan Act — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Health & Safety Code § 1340 et seq.
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** 1975 (original); substantially amended through 1999–2024

## The Rule

The Knox-Keene Act is California's comprehensive regulatory framework for health care service plans.
The DMHC administers it. Core obligations:

- **Timely access** (§§1367, 1367.01): Primary care within 10 business days non-urgent, urgent care
  within 48 hours, emergency immediately
- **Grievance procedures** (§1368): Mandatory system; DMHC direct complaint if plan fails to resolve
  within 30 days
- **Medical necessity decisions**: Same physician-reviewer requirement as Ins. Code §10123.135 applies
  through parallel HSC provisions
- **Independent Medical Review** (§1374.30): Binding IMR for medical necessity disputes — see
  dedicated standard HSC_1374_30_IMR

**The DMHC/CDI split:** Knox-Keene applies to health care service plans (HMOs). Ins. Code §10123.135
applies to disability insurers. Blue Shield operates both. The correct agency depends on which
product the denial came under — this determination is load-bearing for every enforcement strategy.

## Key Elements

1. Plan license required for all Knox-Keene plans in California
2. Timely access standards — enforceable, not aspirational
3. Grievance system mandatory — DMHC direct complaint available
4. DMHC enforcement: administrative fines, license suspension, license revocation
5. No MICRA application — Knox-Keene plans are not health care providers

## What This Means in Practice

First question on every managed care denial: DMHC or CDI? Check whether the policy is a health
care service plan contract (DMHC/Knox-Keene) or a disability insurance policy (CDI/Insurance Code).
The answer determines which enforcement pathway, which complaint form, and whether IMR applies.
