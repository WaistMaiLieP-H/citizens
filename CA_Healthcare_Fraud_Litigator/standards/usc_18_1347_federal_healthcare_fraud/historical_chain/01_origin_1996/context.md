# Federal Health Care Fraud — Origin 1996

## The Wound

By the early 1990s, federal investigations into Medicare and Medicaid fraud had documented
systematic billing fraud reaching into the billions. The specific documented patterns that drove
§1347: home health agencies billing for patients who were not homebound and not actually receiving
services; durable medical equipment suppliers billing for devices never delivered; clinical labs
billing for tests never ordered; hospitals upcoding diagnoses to maximize DRG reimbursement.

The General Accounting Office reported in 1992 that Medicare and Medicaid fraud losses were
estimated at 10% of total expenditures — roughly $20-$30 billion annually at the time.
Federal prosecutors were using mail fraud and wire fraud statutes but facing increasing difficulty
proving the incidental use of mail or wires for each scheme. Fraud was adapting to electronic
billing systems that did not always involve traditional mail.

The wound was not abstract: patients were enrolled in home health fraud schemes without their
knowledge; Medicare beneficiaries were billed for wheelchairs they did not need and never received;
physicians' NPI numbers were used without their knowledge to submit false claims. The victims
were the programs, the taxpayers, and ultimately the patients whose legitimate claims competed
for resources with fraudulent ones.

## The Legislative Response

HIPAA (1996) created §1347 as a dedicated federal health care fraud statute — the first comprehensive
federal criminal provision targeting health care fraud specifically, without requiring mail or wire
predicates. Congress also created the Health Care Fraud and Abuse Control Program (HCFAC) under
HIPAA, directing a portion of fraud recovery proceeds to fund the program — creating a self-financing
enforcement mechanism.

The ACA (2010) strengthened §1347 by adding the §1347(b) provision eliminating the need to prove
knowledge of the federal prohibition, by expanding the definition of covered programs, and by
creating enhanced sentencing for large-scale health care fraud conspiracies.

## What Changed

Before §1347: Federal health care fraud prosecution required either a federal program predicate
(Medicare/Medicaid statute violations) or proof of mail/wire fraud elements. Private insurer fraud
was largely a state prosecution matter.

After §1347: Any scheme targeting any health care benefit program — public or private — affecting
commerce is federal health care fraud. Private insurers contracting with federal programs are covered.
The "death results" enhancement added consequences proportionate to the harm: health care fraud
that kills patients is treated as severely as other crimes resulting in death.
