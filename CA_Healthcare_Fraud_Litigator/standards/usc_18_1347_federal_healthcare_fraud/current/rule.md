# Federal Health Care Fraud — Rule

**Authority:** 18 U.S.C. § 1347
**Jurisdiction:** Federal
**Effective:** 1996 (HIPAA); 2010 (ACA enhanced penalties)

## The Rule

The federal health care fraud statute criminalizes two distinct acts: (1) executing or attempting
to execute a scheme to defraud any health care benefit program, and (2) obtaining, by false pretenses
or fraudulent representations, money or property owned by or under the custody or control of any
health care benefit program, in connection with the delivery of or payment for health care benefits,
items, or services.

The penalty is up to 10 years imprisonment. If the violation results in serious bodily injury, up
to 20 years. If the violation results in death, any term of years or life.

**Health care benefit program** is defined at 18 USC §24(b) to include any public or private plan
or contract under which any medical benefit, item, or service is provided to any individual, and
includes any individual or entity who is providing a medical benefit, item, or service for which
payment may be made under the plan or contract. This reaches Blue Shield, Medicare Advantage plans,
Medi-Cal managed care, and any insurer contracting with a federal program.

Under §1347(b): a person need not have actual knowledge of this section or specific intent to commit
a violation. This means the government does not need to prove the defendant knew the conduct was
federally prohibited — only that the defendant knowingly engaged in the scheme.

## Key Elements

1. **Knowing and willful** — the scheme must be executed knowingly and willfully
2. **Scheme or artifice** — a systematic plan, not merely a single false submission
3. **Health care benefit program** — must be a covered program under 18 USC §24(b)
4. **Defraud or false pretenses** — either defraud the program or obtain property by false statements
5. **Connection to delivery or payment** — must be in connection with health care benefits, items, or services
6. **No knowledge of law required** — §1347(b) eliminates the defense that defendant did not know the conduct was federally prohibited

## What This Means in Practice

Blue Shield of California participates in Medicare Advantage and contracts with federal programs —
making it a health care benefit program for §1347 purposes. A scheme to submit false or duplicate
claims to Blue Shield under a Medicare Advantage contract is federal health care fraud, not just
California insurance fraud under §550. The two statutes can be charged concurrently. Federal
prosecution adds DOJ jurisdiction, federal sentencing guidelines, and the serious bodily injury
and death enhancement — relevant where the denial-caused harm was a failed surgery or delayed
treatment that resulted in permanent injury.
