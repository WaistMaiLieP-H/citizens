# Federal Health Care Fraud — Reasoning

## Why This Law Exists

18 USC §1347 was enacted as part of the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996
(HIPAA, Pub. L. 104-191). Before HIPAA, federal health care fraud prosecutions required prosecutors
to fit health care fraud into general mail fraud (§1341) or wire fraud (§1343) statutes, which
required proof that the US mail or interstate wire communications were used. This was technically
achievable but required additional elements that let fraudsters escape prosecution on technicalities.

HIPAA created a dedicated federal health care fraud statute because the scale of health care fraud —
estimated by the federal government at $100 billion per year in 1996 — demanded a comprehensive
federal response that did not depend on incidental use of the mails. The statute was designed
to reach any scheme targeting any health care benefit program, public or private.

## Legislative Intent

Congress found in HIPAA that health care fraud imposed massive costs on the federal government,
on private insurers, and on patients. The specific intent in creating §1347 was to give federal
prosecutors a direct tool — not reliant on mail or wire fraud predicates — against health care
fraud schemes of any structure. The "serious bodily injury" and "death" enhancements were included
because Congress recognized that health care fraud is not merely financial crime: when people are
denied medically necessary care through fraud or when fraudulent care is delivered, people get hurt
and people die.

The Affordable Care Act (2010) strengthened §1347 by adding the knowledge of law provisions and
expanding the definition of covered programs. The 2010 amendments reflected continued congressional
concern that health care fraud was adapting faster than prosecution tools could keep pace.

## Doctrinal Basis

Section 1347 is grounded in the Commerce Clause and the federal power to regulate interstate commerce
in health care services. The definition of "health care benefit program" at §24(b) is deliberately
broad — reaching any public or private plan providing medical benefits. The federal health care
market is interstate commerce by its structure: Blue Shield contracts with federal Medicare Advantage
programs, and any fraud in that context is federal fraud. The statute does not require that any
specific transaction cross state lines — program participation is the jurisdictional hook.
