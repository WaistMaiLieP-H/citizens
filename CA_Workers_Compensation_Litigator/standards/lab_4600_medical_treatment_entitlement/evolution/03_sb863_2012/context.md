# LAB §4600 — Stage 03: SB 863 (2012) — Independent Medical Review

**Stage:** 03_sb863_2012
**Enactment:** SB 863 (Stats. 2012, Ch. 363)
**Effective:** January 1, 2013

---

## What Changed

SB 863 added Independent Medical Review (IMR) as the mandatory dispute resolution mechanism for UR denials of treatment. Before SB 863, a UR denial could be challenged at the WCAB through expert testimony — the same expensive litigation process that SB 228 was supposed to replace.

SB 863 changes:
1. UR denial → worker requests IMR within 30 days
2. IMR is conducted by a physician selected by a DWC-contracted organization (Maximus Federal Services)
3. IMR determination is binding on WCAB absent fraud, conflict of interest, or clear legal error
4. WCAB review of IMR is extremely limited — this was the most controversial aspect

## The IMR Binding-Determination Problem

SB 863's IMR system was challenged on due process grounds: the WCAB's limited review of IMR determinations arguably denied workers a meaningful hearing. The California Supreme Court upheld IMR in *Stevens v. WCAB* (2015) 30 Cal.App.5th 394 (later cases clarified scope), but the binding nature of IMR determinations remains the primary systemic challenge workers face in treatment disputes.

## §4610(g)(3)(A) — The Physician-Only Rule Strengthened

SB 863 strengthened the physician-only rule for UR modifications/denials: only a licensed physician competent to evaluate the clinical issues may modify, delay, or deny a treatment request. A non-physician UR reviewer's denial is void. This is the most important enforcement provision in the post-SB 863 system.
