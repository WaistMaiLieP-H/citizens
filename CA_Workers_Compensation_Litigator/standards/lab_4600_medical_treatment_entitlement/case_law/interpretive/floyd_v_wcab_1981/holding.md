## Holding

The Court addressed the physician-selection hierarchy under §4600 and the consequences of employer interference with the worker's medical care. Key holdings:

1. **First 30 days — employer controls physician selection (absent pre-designation)** — where the worker has not designated a personal physician in writing before injury, the employer controls physician selection for the first 30 days post-injury. This is the employer's medical narrative window.

2. **After 30 days — worker may change physicians** — after the initial 30-day period, the worker has the right to change treating physicians within the MPN (Medical Provider Network) or, if no MPN exists, to a physician of the worker's choosing.

3. **Interference with medical care creates additional liability** — employer conduct that interferes with the worker's access to medical treatment (including coercive pressure to not treat, threats related to employment status, or manipulation of the MPN to exclude necessary specialists) is not protected by the exclusive remedy doctrine and may create additional liability.

## Application

The Floyd interference principle is relevant to the UA342 scenario: if the identity replacement scheme involved preventing Michael from accessing WC medical treatment by manipulating his employment status or communications, that interference itself creates additional WCAB exposure beyond the underlying benefit denial.
