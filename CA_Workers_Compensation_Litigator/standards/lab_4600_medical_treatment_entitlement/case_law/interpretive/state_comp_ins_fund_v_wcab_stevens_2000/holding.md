## Holding

The Court addressed the scope of the employer's §4600 medical treatment obligation and the consequences of breach. Key holdings:

1. **"Reasonably required to cure or relieve" is the operative standard** — the employer's obligation extends to all treatment that a reasonable physician would consider appropriate for the condition, not merely the minimum necessary to stabilize the worker.

2. **The "cure or relieve" disjunction** — treatment that will not cure the injury (e.g., chronic pain management for a permanent condition) is still required if it relieves the worker from the effects of the injury. Palliative and maintenance treatment for permanent industrial conditions falls within §4600.

3. **§4600(a) self-procured treatment liability** — when the employer fails or refuses to provide reasonably required treatment, the worker may obtain it independently. The employer is then liable for the reasonable cost of self-procured treatment, even if the specific provider or treatment was not pre-approved.

## Application to Active Cases

**Bilateral ankles:** If insurer UR denials resulted in Michael self-procuring ankle treatment (orthopedic care, physical therapy, pain management), the insurer is liable for those costs under §4600(a). The denial does not eliminate the obligation — it triggers direct liability.
