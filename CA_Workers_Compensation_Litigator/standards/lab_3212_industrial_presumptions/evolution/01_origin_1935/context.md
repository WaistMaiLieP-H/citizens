# LAB §3212 — Origin: 1935 (Narrow Peace Officer Presumption)

**Stage:** 01_origin_1935
**Enactment:** Stats. 1935 (early predecessor provision; recodified in 1937 Labor Code as §3212)

---

## The Original Narrow Scope

The original §3212 covered a narrow category: peace officers and firefighters for city and county police/fire departments. The conditions covered were hernia, heart trouble, and pneumonia. The rationale was straightforward: these workers face unique occupational exposures — heavy physical exertion (hernia), cardiovascular stress and smoke inhalation (heart/pneumonia) — that make causal proof inherently difficult and unfair to require.

## The Original No-Attribution Rule

The no-attribution rule was present from the beginning: the covered condition "shall in no case be attributed to any disease existing prior to that development or manifestation." This absolute bar against prior-condition attribution was the defining feature of §3212 — it was the legislature's recognition that these workers' prior health history was irrelevant in the context of their high-exposure service.

## Post-Termination Extension Origins

The post-termination extension (3 months per year of service, max 60 months) was added to recognize that occupationally-caused diseases often have long latency periods. A firefighter who develops heart disease 2 years after retirement may have been developing the condition throughout their career.
