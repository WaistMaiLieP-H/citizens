## Holding

The Court of Appeal addressed the post-termination extension under §3212 and the scope of the no-attribution rule. Key holdings:

1. **Post-termination extension is self-executing** — the worker does not need to file a claim during active service to preserve the post-termination extension. The extension period runs from the last date actually worked, and any claim filed within the extension window is timely.

2. **The no-attribution rule is absolute** — the employer may not introduce evidence that the covered condition was attributable to pre-service disease. The no-attribution rule in §3212(a)(3) forecloses this defense entirely for the covered conditions.

3. **The disputable presumption standard** — the employer CAN rebut the presumption, but only with evidence that the specific condition arose from non-occupational causes. The employer cannot simply point to a prior medical history and ask the WCAB to draw an inference; the employer must introduce affirmative evidence of non-industrial causation.

## Application

The absolute no-attribution rule in Garcia establishes that for §3212-covered workers, the prior-condition defense is simply unavailable for the covered conditions. This is the starkest contrast with the §4663 apportionment framework for non-§3212 workers like Michael — and illustrates why legislative expansion of presumption coverage to additional occupational categories (plumbers, construction workers) would be so significant.
