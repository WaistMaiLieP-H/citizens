# LAB §3212 — Reasoning

## Why the Presumption Exists

Public safety workers face unique occupational exposures that are inherently difficult to separate from civilian health conditions. A firefighter's heart condition may have been caused by decades of smoke inhalation, extreme physical exertion, and cardiovascular stress — or it may have been caused by diet and genetics. The legislature determined that requiring the worker to prove industrial causation for these conditions, in the context of these occupations, was unfair and impractical.

The presumption shifts the burden: the employer/insurer must prove the condition is NOT industrial. This is the §3202 liberal construction principle made specific for the highest-risk occupational categories.

## The No-Attribution Rule — §3212(a)(3)

The no-attribution rule is the strongest provision in §3212: the covered condition "shall in no case be attributed to any disease existing prior to that development or manifestation." This is absolute. Unlike the general §4663 apportionment framework, §3212 conditions cannot be reduced based on any prior disease — even if the employer can prove a prior condition existed. The prior condition is irrelevant. The industrial period of service is the only relevant period.

## Legislative History

§3212 has been expanded repeatedly since its origin as a narrow provision covering only certain peace officers. Each expansion reflects the legislature's response to evidence that specific occupational categories face heightened exposure to specific conditions. The 2026 expansion to federal installation firefighters is the most recent addition.
