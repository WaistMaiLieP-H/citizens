## The Rule

Permanent disability apportionment is based on causation. The physician must determine what percentage of the permanent disability was caused by the industrial injury versus other factors (pre-existing conditions, subsequent conditions, prior industrial injuries). The insurer pays only for the industrial percentage. The physician's report must include an apportionment determination to be complete.

---

## The Causation-Based Apportionment Framework

### Step 1 — What Is the Total Permanent Disability?

The qualified medical evaluator (QME) or agreed medical evaluator (AME) determines the whole-person impairment rating under the AMA Guides (5th Ed.) as modified by the PDRS (Permanent Disability Rating Schedule). This rating is the total impairment — before apportionment.

### Step 2 — Causation Analysis

The physician must answer: what caused this permanent disability? The analysis identifies contributing causes:
- Industrial injury (the current claim)
- Prior industrial injuries (previously compensated or not)
- Non-industrial factors (genetics, pre-existing conditions, aging, non-work activities)
- Subsequent injuries (occurred after the industrial injury)

**The physician must give percentages.** Not narrative descriptions — specific percentages attributable to each cause. A report that states "some apportionment is appropriate" without giving percentages is not a complete apportionment determination under §4663(c).

### Step 3 — The Apportionment Split

If the physician finds 60% industrial, 40% non-industrial:
- The permanent disability award is 60% of what it would be without apportionment
- The insurer pays 60%; the employee bears 40% of their own permanent disability
- The medical treatment obligation is NOT apportioned — the employer must still treat the industrial component

### The Reporting Requirement (§4663(c))

A permanent disability report that does not include an apportionment determination is **incomplete**. An incomplete report cannot support a permanent disability award. This is a procedural tool:
- If the employer's QME report apportions without adequate causation analysis, challenge the report as incomplete
- If the employer apportions to a condition the physician cannot specifically identify in the medical records (speculative apportionment), challenge the causation foundation

## Challenge Grounds — Attacking Apportionment

### Ground 1 — Lack of Substantial Medical Evidence
Apportionment must be based on "substantial medical evidence" — not speculation, not surmise, not "it's likely this person had pre-existing problems." The physician must identify specific conditions in the medical record, specific prior injuries documented in the record, or specific findings at examination that support the apportionment.

**Vigil standard (Escobedo v. Marshall's):** An apportionment opinion that is speculative — based on what "commonly" happens, not on what the record shows for this specific worker — is not substantial medical evidence and cannot support apportionment. California WCAB decisions applying this principle: apportionment without documented prior condition is impermissible.

**Attack:** Request the physician's records basis for the apportionment. If the pre-existing condition was never documented, never treated, never caused prior impairment — what is the evidence it causally contributed to the current disability?

### Ground 2 — Apportioning to Non-Disabling Conditions
Pre-SB 899, apportionment required a prior "permanent disability." Post-SB 899, §4663 allows apportionment to conditions that never caused prior permanent disability. But there is a limit from case law: apportionment cannot be based on a condition that was asymptomatic (causing no impairment) before the industrial injury. If the pre-existing condition did not cause any disability before the industrial event, the industrial event caused the disability — not the underlying condition.

**The asymptomatic prior condition rule:** A degenerative spinal condition that was present but never caused symptoms or impairment is not a "cause" of the post-injury permanent disability in the sense §4663 requires. The industrial event was the cause of the disability.

### Ground 3 — Physician Qualifications and Methodology
The apportionment opinion is only as good as the physician who makes it. Attack grounds: (a) physician is not qualified to evaluate the specific body system being apportioned; (b) physician did not review adequate records; (c) physician used an improper methodology (e.g., equal apportionment without causation analysis).

### Ground 4 — §4663(e) Exemption
If the industrial injury falls under §3212 (law enforcement/firefighter presumptive conditions), causation-based apportionment does not apply.

## Application to Active Cases

### Michael — Bilateral Ankle Injuries

**The apportionment threat:** Any prior ankle injuries, surgeries, or degenerative conditions can be cited by the employer's QME as grounds for apportionment. If Michael had prior ankle treatment, those records will be requested under §4663(d) (employee must disclose prior permanent disabilities).

**The defense:**
1. Were the prior ankle conditions actually causing impairment before the industrial event? Asymptomatic prior conditions cannot be apportioned.
2. What is the specific medical basis for the apportionment percentage? Speculative apportionment is not substantial medical evidence.
3. Is the QME's report complete — does it actually give percentages and causation analysis, or does it state apportionment conclusions without explanation?

**The MTUS/PDRS interaction:** The industrial component's rating under the AMA Guides and PDRS is the starting point. Apportionment then adjusts downward. Challenging both the base rating and the apportionment affects the total award.

### UA342 Identity Replacement — Pension/Disability

If the identity replacement scheme involved falsified injury records or fraudulent workers comp claims filed in Michael's name, the §4663 reports for those claims are relevant evidence — they contain medical findings, physician signatures, and dates that can be cross-checked against the actual medical record.

## Practical Apportionment Checklist

| Issue | Action |
|---|---|
| QME apportions to undocumented prior condition | Challenge as speculative; demand medical record basis |
| QME apportions to asymptomatic pre-existing condition | Apply asymptomatic-prior rule; condition never caused disability = not a cause |
| QME report lacks specific percentages | Report is incomplete under §4663(c); inadmissible for PD determination |
| Apportionment to subsequent non-industrial event | Analyze: was the subsequent event a natural consequence of the industrial injury? |
| §3212 presumption applies | §4663(e) exemption from causation apportionment |
