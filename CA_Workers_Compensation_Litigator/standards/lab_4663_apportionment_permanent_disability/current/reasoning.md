## Why This Standard Exists — The SB 899 Bargain

The apportionment statute was rewritten in 2004 by SB 899 under intense pressure from employer and insurer lobbying. The insurers' argument: workers compensation costs were spiraling because California was paying 100% of permanent disability for conditions that were partly caused by age, genetics, prior injuries, and non-work factors. The Legislature accepted this argument and rewrote §4663.

The shift from compensation-based to causation-based apportionment was framed as a fairness correction: employers should pay only for what they caused. The mechanism: physician-determined causation percentages.

---

## The Policy Structure — And Its Costs

**The argument for causation-based apportionment:** If a worker had a prior back injury that caused 30% of their current disability, why should the current employer pay for that 30%? The employer did not cause the prior condition. Requiring full payment for a mixed-cause disability over-charges the current employer.

**The cost of causation-based apportionment — The Wound:**

As the source prep documents: the insurers celebrated SB 899. What the Legislature did not fully account for was that causation-based apportionment would be applied to conditions the worker had never sought compensation for — conditions that never caused disability at all.

A worker who had knee cartilage wear from 25 years of physical labor — never injured, never treated, never disabled — has that wear identified on MRI as a "pre-existing condition" that the QME apportions against the current claim. The worker never had a knee problem. The industrial injury is what created the disability. But the apportionment says: 30% pre-existing.

**The person counting those percentages** is a physician hired by the insurer. The structural conflict of interest is built in: the insurer-selected QME has an economic relationship with the insurer. Higher apportionment = lower award = lower cost to the insurer. The physician's career in insurer UR and QME work depends on being the kind of physician the insurer retains.

**The WCAB response — The Escobedo/Vigil standard:** California WCAB panels developed case law limiting speculative apportionment. An apportionment opinion must be based on substantial medical evidence — specific findings, documented conditions, actual record. "It's likely this person had some pre-existing degeneration" is not substantial medical evidence.

---

## The Historical Loss — What Causation Apportionment Costs Workers

Before SB 899: a worker with undocumented pre-existing conditions received 100% of their permanent disability award for a legitimate industrial injury. After SB 899: the same worker, with the same industrial injury and the same outcomes, can have their award reduced by 30%, 40%, or 50% based on conditions they never knew about, never sought treatment for, and never experienced as disability.

The historical loss is paid in percentages. Each percentage point of apportionment is a dollar reduction in the permanent disability award. For a worker with a significant permanent disability, a 40% apportionment can mean the difference between a meaningful settlement and poverty.

The tool for fighting back: the Escobedo/Vigil substantial evidence requirement for apportionment opinions. Speculative, records-unsupported, asymptomatic-condition apportionment must be challenged at the WCAB level through the appropriate procedural mechanism.
