# LAB §4663 — Stage 02: SB 899 (2004) — Causation-Based Apportionment

**Stage:** 02_sb899_2004
**Enactment:** SB 899 (Stats. 2004, Ch. 34, §§ 34-35)
**Effective:** April 19, 2004

---

## The Fundamental Shift

SB 899 rewrote §4663 from the ground up. The new rule: apportionment is to the CAUSATION of the permanent disability, not to the disability itself. Any prior condition — including an asymptomatic prior condition — that causally contributed to the current disability may be apportioned.

**New §4663(a):** "Apportionment of permanent disability shall be based on causation."

**New §4663(b):** The physician must "make an apportionment determination" in every case involving permanent disability, specifying what percentage of the disability is caused by the current industrial injury versus other factors.

**New §4663(c):** If the physician is unable to make an apportionment determination, the entire disability is industrial. This provision — the inability-to-apportion default — is one of the most important in post-2004 practice.

## The Asymptomatic Prior Condition Problem

SB 899's most controversial change: an asymptomatic pre-existing condition that a physician can credibly identify as a causative factor in the current disability IS now apportionable. A plumber with a prior disc bulge that was non-symptomatic can have the current spine disability apportioned to that prior condition.

The Brodie (2007) Supreme Court case confirmed this is constitutional. But Brodie also imposed the substantial medical evidence requirement: the physician's apportionment opinion must be based on substantial medical evidence, not speculation about asymptomatic conditions.

## §4663(e) — The Exemption for Presumptive Conditions

Conditions subject to a §3212 presumption (hernia, heart trouble, pneumonia for specified workers) are exempt from apportionment under §4663(e). If a condition is presumed industrial under §3212, it cannot be apportioned away.
