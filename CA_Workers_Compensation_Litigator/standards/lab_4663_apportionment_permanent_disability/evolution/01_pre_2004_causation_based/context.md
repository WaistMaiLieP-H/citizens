# LAB §4663 — Pre-2004: Compensation-Based Apportionment

**Stage:** 01_pre_2004_causation_based
**Period:** Pre-SB 899 (before April 19, 2004)

---

## The Pre-2004 Apportionment System

Before SB 899 (2004), apportionment was governed by a different standard: apportionment was to the disability, not to the causation of the disability. Courts asked: what percentage of the current permanent disability was caused by the industrial injury versus pre-existing conditions?

The critical distinction: a pre-existing condition that was ASYMPTOMATIC before the industrial injury — that had caused no disability before the injury — could NOT be apportioned. If the worker had arthritic knees but had been working without restriction, the arthritis was not "causing" disability before the injury; the industrial accident was the cause. Under the pre-2004 rule, no apportionment to a non-disabling pre-existing condition was permitted.

## The Employer-Side Problem With Pre-2004 Apportionment

By 2004, California's workers compensation system faced a genuine cost crisis. Medical costs had increased dramatically, and insurers argued that the non-apportionability of asymptomatic pre-existing conditions created unfunded liability for injuries that were, in their view, partially caused by the worker's constitution or prior history. The Workers' Compensation Reform Act of 2004 (SB 899) was the legislature's response.

## The Wound the Old System Protected

The pre-2004 rule protected workers from the consequences of their own biology. A plumber with genetically predisposed arthritis, or a construction worker with a prior asymptomatic disc bulge, was not penalized for their constitutional vulnerability — the rule was: if you were working without restriction before the industrial injury, your pre-existing condition is irrelevant to apportionment. SB 899 reversed this protection.
