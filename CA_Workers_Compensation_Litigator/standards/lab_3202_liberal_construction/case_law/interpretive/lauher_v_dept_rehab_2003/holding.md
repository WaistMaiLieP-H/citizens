# Department of Rehabilitation v. Workers' Comp. Appeals Bd. (Lauher) (2003) 30 Cal.4th 1281

**Citation:** (2003) 30 Cal.4th 1281
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Standard:** lab_3202_liberal_construction
**Status:** PROPOSED — verify against primary source before filing reliance

---

## Holding

The California Supreme Court clarified the limits of §3202's liberal construction mandate. Key holdings:

1. **§3202 applies to ambiguous provisions only** — the liberal construction mandate does not authorize courts to rewrite clear statutory text. Where a statute unambiguously addresses a situation, §3202 does not permit a court to adopt a different interpretation simply because it would be more beneficial to the worker.

2. **§3202 is not a license for judicial legislation** — the Court distinguished between (a) resolving ambiguity in the worker's favor (proper application of §3202) and (b) extending statutes beyond their text on policy grounds (improper; legislative function).

3. **The limit is ambiguity, not outcome** — §3202 requires liberal construction "for the purpose of extending benefits." Where a provision has no ambiguity, there is nothing to construe; the text governs.

## Why This Case Matters

Lauher is the case insurers cite to limit §3202. The proper response is: Lauher does not eliminate §3202 — it defines its domain. The question in any given dispute is whether the provision at issue is genuinely ambiguous. If it is, §3202 applies and the worker prevails. Lauher only matters when the employer can prove there is no ambiguity.

## Application

In the bilateral ankle and UA342 cases, if an insurer argues that §3202 does not apply because the statute is clear, the response is: (1) identify the specific provision; (2) identify the two plausible readings; (3) demonstrate that both readings are textually supportable. Once ambiguity is established, Lauher drops out and §3202 governs.
