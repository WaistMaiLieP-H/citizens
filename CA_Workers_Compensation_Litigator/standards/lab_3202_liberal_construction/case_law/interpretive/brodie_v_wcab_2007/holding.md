# Brodie v. Workers' Comp. Appeals Bd. (2007) 40 Cal.4th 1313

**Citation:** (2007) 40 Cal.4th 1313
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Standard:** lab_3202_liberal_construction (also: lab_4663_apportionment_permanent_disability)
**Status:** PROPOSED — verify against primary source before filing reliance

---

## Holding

The California Supreme Court upheld the 2004 SB 899 apportionment reforms (Lab. Code §4663) against constitutional challenge, but in doing so confirmed the continued force of §3202's liberal construction mandate. The Court held:

1. **§4663's causation-based apportionment is constitutional** — the legislature may rationally require apportionment to all causes of permanent disability, including pre-existing conditions.

2. **§3202 remains operative within §4663** — where the evidentiary record on apportionment is ambiguous (i.e., a physician provides a range of apportionment percentages rather than a single figure), the ambiguity is resolved in the worker's favor under §3202. The employer bears the burden of proving apportionment; ambiguity does not shift to the worker.

3. **Substantial medical evidence standard governs** — apportionment determinations must be based on substantial medical evidence, not speculation. A physician's apportionment opinion that relies on speculation about an "inherent predisposition" without documented clinical support does not constitute substantial medical evidence.

## Application to Active Standards

**§4663 + §3202 interaction:** The Brodie framework means that in any disputed apportionment case, the worker holds two lines of defense: (1) the physician's opinion lacks substantial medical evidence (Escobedo/Brodie), and (2) any remaining ambiguity in the medical record resolves in the worker's favor under §3202.

**Steward relevance:** For the bilateral ankle cases, if the insurer's QME attributes a percentage of permanent disability to a pre-existing asymptomatic ankle condition, Brodie requires: (a) the QME's apportionment opinion must be based on substantial medical evidence, not speculation about "constitutional predisposition"; (b) any range in the QME's opinion resolves toward the lower apportionment percentage (more to the worker) under §3202.
