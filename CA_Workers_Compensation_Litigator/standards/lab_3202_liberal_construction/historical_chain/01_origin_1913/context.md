# Historical Chain — Origin: Liberal Construction Rule (1913)

**Standard:** lab_3202_liberal_construction
**Era:** 1913 — California Workers Compensation Act

---

## The 1913 Choice

When the California Legislature enacted the workers compensation system in 1913, it made a deliberate structural choice: the statute would be liberally construed in favor of injured workers. This was not incidental — it was written into the Act from the beginning. The Legislature understood that it was creating a remedial statute, and that remedial statutes must be interpreted broadly or they are whittled away by narrow construction.

The liberal construction rule reflects the same foundational judgment as the rest of the 1913 Act: that the balance of power between employer and employee favors the employer in adversarial settings, and that statutory design must compensate for this imbalance.

## Why a Liberal Construction Rule Is Necessary in Workers Compensation

Workers compensation statutes are technically complex. They define terms — "employee," "employer," "injury," "arising out of employment," "managing representative" — that must be applied across an enormous variety of employment relationships and injury circumstances. Many of these applications are contested.

Without a liberal construction rule, contested interpretations are resolved by whoever makes the more persuasive legal argument. In workers compensation, that usually means the employer — which has institutional resources, recurring litigation experience, and counsel. The injured worker, often appearing pro se or with limited representation, is structurally disadvantaged.

The 1913 Legislature built §3202 into the system to address this. It is not a statement that workers are always right — it is a statement about which direction ambiguity resolves.

## The Scope — Divisions 4 and 5

§3202 covers the entire Division 4 (workers compensation benefits and procedure) and Division 5 (Cal/OSHA and workplace safety). This is the full scope of the workers compensation and workplace safety statutory scheme. The liberal construction rule is not limited to particular sections — it applies wherever interpretation is required.
