# LAB § 3202 Liberal Construction — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** lab_3202_liberal_construction
**Citizen:** CA_Workers_Compensation_Litigator
**Layer:** 2 — Operative Rule
**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

Courts must liberally construe all provisions of Labor Code Divisions 4 and 5 in favor of extending workers compensation benefits to injured workers. When a statutory provision is ambiguous — when it could be read to cover or exclude a worker, a condition, or a benefit — the court must choose the interpretation that extends protection to the injured worker.

---

## What "Liberal Construction" Requires

### Ambiguity Resolution
When two reasonable interpretations of a workers compensation statute are available, §3202 mandates the interpretation that is more beneficial to the injured worker. This is not a mere tiebreaker — it is the operative rule for every ambiguous provision in Division 4 and Division 5.

### Scope — What §3202 Covers
The liberal construction rule applies across the entire workers compensation scheme:
- Whether an injury arises out of and in the course of employment (AOE/COE analysis)
- What constitutes "injury" for compensation purposes
- Who qualifies as an "employee" under the Act
- What conditions satisfy §4553 "serious and willful misconduct" (including ambiguities in "managing representative")
- What constitutes "medical treatment reasonably required to cure or relieve" under §4600
- What evidence is sufficient to meet a compensation threshold

### Scope — What §3202 Does Not Do
- §3202 does not create rights that the statute does not grant — it construes existing provisions liberally, it does not extend the statute beyond its text
- §3202 does not override unambiguous provisions — it applies only where ambiguity exists
- §3202 does not eliminate the burden of proof — the worker still bears the burden on each element

---

## Application to Active Standards

### LAB §4553 — "Managing Representative"
The definition of "managing representative" is not specifically defined in §4553. Under §3202, ambiguity in who qualifies as a managing representative for purposes of the serious and willful misconduct enhancement is resolved in the worker's favor — a broader reading of "managing representative" is required, not a narrower one.

### LAB §4553 — "Serious and Willful" Threshold
Where the evidence of employer misconduct is sufficient to support either an ordinary negligence reading or a serious and willful misconduct reading, §3202 directs the court toward the more protective interpretation — that the misconduct meets the §4553 threshold.

### LAB §4600 — Scope of Required Treatment
Where §4600's "reasonably required to cure or relieve" standard is ambiguous as applied to a specific treatment, §3202 requires the interpretation that extends medical treatment to the injured worker.

### LAB §4663 — Apportionment Methodology
Where an apportionment methodology is ambiguous in its application — particularly whether a prior asymptomatic condition is a "causative factor" under §4663 — §3202 directs resolution in the worker's favor.

## Tactical Use at the WCAB

§3202 is not merely an interpretive background rule — it is a tactical tool:

1. **Identify the ambiguity.** State clearly which word or phrase in the statute has two plausible readings.
2. **State both readings.** Show that the employer's reading and the worker's reading are both textually supportable.
3. **Invoke §3202.** Argue that the mandatory liberal construction rule requires the court to adopt the worker's reading.
4. **Tie to purpose.** The statute's purpose is "extending benefits for the protection of persons injured in the course of their employment." The employer's reading contracts benefits; the worker's reading extends them. §3202 resolves this.

**Format for WCAB brief:**
> "To the extent §[X] is susceptible to two readings, Labor Code §3202 mandates that the court adopt the construction that extends the Act's benefits to the injured worker. [Employer's reading] would narrow benefits. [Worker's reading] extends them. §3202 requires the Court to adopt [worker's reading]."
