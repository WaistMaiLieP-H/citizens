# LAB § 3202 Liberal Construction — Reasoning and Historical Loss

**Citizen:** CA_Workers_Compensation_Litigator
**Layer:** 3 — Reasoning / Historical Loss

## Why This Standard Exists — The Structural Inequality Problem

Workers compensation statutes are written in general terms. They must be — the Legislature cannot anticipate every injury, every industry, every employment relationship, every mechanism of harm. When a statute is written in general terms, interpretation is required. And interpretation is where the power relationship between employer and worker is most exposed.

Employers have lawyers. Employers have insurance companies with legal departments. Employers litigate interpretation questions regularly and have developed institutional knowledge about how to read ambiguous provisions in ways that limit their liability. Workers — particularly injured workers — often lack equivalent resources.

LAB §3202 is the Legislature's answer to this structural inequality. It does not eliminate the need for interpretation — it determines who wins when interpretation is genuinely contested. The injured worker wins. Not because workers are always right, but because the purpose of the entire statute is "extending benefits for the protection of persons injured in the course of their employment." When the statute is ambiguous, the interpretation that serves that purpose must prevail.

---

## The 1913 Foundation

LAB §3202 was part of the original 1913 workers compensation framework. The Legislature recognized from the outset that a remedial statute without a liberal construction rule would be systematically eroded by employers reading ambiguous provisions narrowly. The liberal construction rule was built in as structural protection against interpretive erosion.

---

## The Historical Loss — Interpretive Erosion Without §3202

Without §3202, every ambiguous provision in the workers compensation statute is a contested question. Employers can argue for narrow interpretations. Courts can resolve ambiguities either way. Over time, a body of narrow interpretations would accumulate — each individually defensible — that collectively contracts the scope of workers compensation protection far below what the Legislature intended.

§3202 prevents this drift. It does not eliminate contested interpretation — it does not operate on clear statutory text. But for every genuinely ambiguous provision, it sets the direction: the interpretation that protects the injured worker is the legally required interpretation.

The historical loss when §3202 is not properly applied:
- Workers with legitimate claims are excluded based on narrow readings of "employee" or "arising out of employment"
- Serious and willful misconduct enhancements are denied based on narrow readings of "managing representative"
- Medical treatment is denied based on narrow readings of "reasonably required to cure or relieve"
- Apportionment is over-applied based on generous readings of "causative factor" under §4663

Each of these losses represents a case where the Legislature's purpose — extending benefits to injured workers — was defeated by an interpretive choice that §3202 was designed to prevent.
