# LAB §3202 — Stage 02: Labor Code Recodification 1937

**Stage:** 02_labor_code_1937
**Enactment:** California Labor Code (Stats. 1937, Ch. 90)
**Effective:** August 27, 1937

---

## What Changed

The 1937 Labor Code recodification reorganized California's labor statutes into the unified Labor Code. Workers compensation provisions, previously scattered across multiple session laws (1913, 1917, 1919, 1921 Acts), were consolidated into Division 4 (Workers Compensation and Insurance) and Division 5 (Safety in Employment).

The liberal construction provision was recodified as **Labor Code §3202** and its scope was updated to expressly cover both Division 4 AND Division 5:

**"This division and Division 5 (commencing with Section 6300) shall be liberally construed by the courts with the purpose of extending their benefits for the protection of persons injured in the course of their employment."**

## The Significance of the Division 5 Addition

Division 5 covers occupational safety and health requirements. Including Division 5 in the liberal construction mandate means that ambiguities in employer safety obligations — including what constitutes a "safe place of employment" (§6400) and what constitutes a "serious and willful" violation of safety orders (which feeds §4553) — are also resolved in the worker's favor.

For §4553 cases: because the "serious and willful misconduct" standard in §4553 applies to employer violations of Division 5 safety requirements, the §3202 liberal construction rule applies both to the underlying safety obligation AND to the §4553 standard itself.

## Text Stability Since 1937

The text of §3202 has remained substantively unchanged since the 1937 codification. The provision is stable — legislative attention has focused on the substantive standards in Division 4, not the interpretive canon.
