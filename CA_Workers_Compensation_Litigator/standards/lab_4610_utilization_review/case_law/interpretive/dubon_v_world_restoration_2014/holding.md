# Dubon v. World Restoration Inc. (2014)

**Citation:** (2014) 79 Cal.Comp.Cases 313 (WCAB en banc)
**Court:** Workers' Compensation Appeals Board (en banc)
**Standard:** lab_4610_utilization_review
**Status:** PROPOSED — verify citation

---

## Holding

The WCAB en banc addressed the consequences of a UR denial that fails to comply with the procedural requirements of §4610. Key holdings:

1. **Procedurally deficient UR is void** — a UR denial that does not comply with the mandatory requirements of §4610 (timeliness, physician reviewer, criteria disclosure) is void and of no legal effect. The underlying treatment request stands as if never denied.

2. **WCAB has jurisdiction over procedural UR challenges** — challenges to UR procedure (as opposed to the medical necessity determination itself) are within WCAB jurisdiction, not subject to the IMR exclusivity rule. IMR determines medical necessity; WCAB determines UR compliance.

3. **The distinction matters critically** — if the challenge is "the UR reviewer was not a physician" (procedural), it goes to WCAB. If the challenge is "the UR physician was wrong about medical necessity" (substantive), it goes to IMR. This is the most important jurisdictional distinction in post-2013 WC practice.

## Application

This is the primary authority for attacking procedurally deficient UR denials. In any case involving denied treatment:
1. First question: Was the denial issued by a licensed physician? If not → void under §4610(g)(3)(A) → WCAB jurisdiction → Dubon
2. Second question: Was the denial timely? If not → void under §4610 timeliness requirements → WCAB jurisdiction → Dubon
3. Third question: Were criteria disclosed? If not → void → WCAB
4. Only if all procedural requirements met: medical necessity challenge → IMR
