# Maxham v. California Dept. of Corrections and Rehabilitation (2014)

## Holding

The WCAB addressed the "competent to evaluate" requirement in §4610(g)(3)(A) — that the physician conducting UR must be competent in the relevant specialty. Key holdings:

1. **Specialty competence is required, not just licensure** — a licensed physician who is not competent to evaluate the specific clinical issues involved in the treatment request cannot issue a valid UR denial. A general internist cannot deny a request for spinal surgery by a neurosurgeon unless the general internist has competence in spinal conditions.

2. **The insurer bears the burden of establishing reviewer competence** — if competence of the UR reviewer is challenged, the burden is on the insurer to establish that the reviewing physician had the relevant specialty qualifications.

3. **Incompetent UR is procedurally deficient** — a UR denial by a non-competent physician falls within the Dubon void-UR framework: it is procedurally deficient, jurisdiction goes to WCAB, and the underlying §4600 treatment entitlement stands.

## Application

For the bilateral ankle cases: if the insurer's UR denials for orthopedic ankle treatment were issued by a physician without orthopedic specialty competence, those denials are void under §4610(g)(3)(A) as interpreted in Maxham. The underlying §4600 entitlement stands as to each void denial.
