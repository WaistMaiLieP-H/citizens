# Historical Chain — SB 899 (2004): UR Reform

**Standard:** lab_4610_utilization_review
**Era:** 2004 — SB 899 Reform

---

## SB 899 and the §4610 Overhaul

SB 899 (2004) was a comprehensive workers compensation reform package. Among its major changes, it significantly restructured §4610 to create the modern UR framework:

1. **Physician-only rule codified:** SB 899 strengthened the requirement that modifications and denials of treatment recommendations be made by licensed physicians competent in the relevant specialty — not administrative staff or non-physician reviewers.

2. **MTUS created:** The Medical Treatment Utilization Schedule was established as the objective standard for evaluating treatment recommendations. UR decisions must be consistent with the MTUS; criteria used must be MTUS-consistent.

3. **Timeliness requirements tightened:** SB 899 established the 5-day/14-day framework for prospective UR decisions, preventing employers from using indefinite delay as a denial mechanism.

4. **Criteria disclosure required:** The obligation to disclose the specific criteria and guidelines used in a denial decision — to both the treating physician and the injured worker — was reinforced.

The 2004 reforms were the Legislature's judgment that UR is a legitimate cost-management and evidence-based medicine tool, but only when conducted by qualified physicians on a timely basis using objective, disclosed criteria. UR that fails these requirements is not legitimate medical review.

## Post-2004 Amendments — First 30 Days Exemption (2018)

Subsequent amendments (effective January 1, 2018) created the first-30-days prospective UR exemption in §4610(b): treatment rendered within the first 30 days post-injury for accepted compensable conditions is authorized without prospective UR (subject to §4610(c) exceptions for surgery, psychological treatment, DME over $250, imaging, etc.).

The 2018 amendment reflected experience that UR-induced delays in the first 30 days after injury were causing avoidable harm — delays in early treatment that increased long-term disability.
