# LAB § 4610 Utilization Review — Operative Rule

**Standard ID:** lab_4610_utilization_review
**Citizen:** CA_Workers_Compensation_Litigator

**Build date:** 2026-04-13

---

## The Rule

Employers must conduct utilization review (UR) of physician treatment recommendations through a compliant process governed by strict procedural requirements. A UR decision that does not comply with §4610 — whether because it was made by a non-physician, missed timeliness deadlines, lacked required criteria disclosure, or was made outside the permitted UR window — is procedurally deficient and subject to challenge at the WCAB and through the independent medical review (IMR) process under §4610.5.

---

## The Five Critical Compliance Requirements

### Requirement 1 — Physician-Only Modification/Denial [§4610(g)(3)(A)]

The single most important enforcement provision in §4610:

**Only a licensed physician — competent to evaluate the specific clinical issues — may modify or deny treatment recommendations for reasons of medical necessity.**

A UR denial issued by:
- A non-physician UR reviewer
- A nurse acting as the decision-maker
- A physician who is not competent in the relevant specialty
- An administrative staff member

...is **void as a matter of law** under §4610(g)(3)(A). This is not a technicality — it is the fundamental compliance requirement that ensures medical decisions are made by qualified medical professionals.

**Practice note:** When attacking a deficient UR denial, obtain the UR company's internal documentation showing who made the modification/denial decision. If the decision-maker was not a licensed California physician with specialty-appropriate competence, the denial fails.

### Requirement 2 — Timeliness [§4610(i)]

| Type of UR | Decision Deadline |
|---|---|
| Prospective (non-formulary treatment) | 5 business days from receipt, max 14 days from physician recommendation |
| Prospective (formulary-covered treatment) | 5 business days from receipt |
| Urgent/imminent threat to health | 72 hours from receipt of necessary information |
| Communication of decision (all types) | 24 hours by phone/fax/secure email |
| Written confirmation of modification/denial | 24 hours (concurrent) / 2 business days (prospective) |

A UR denial issued outside these timeframes is procedurally deficient. Late denials may be challenged as a basis for ordering the employer to authorize the treatment.

### Requirement 3 — First 30 Days Exemption [§4610(b)]

For injuries on or after January 1, 2018: treatment rendered within the first **30 days** post-injury by an MPN physician, predesignated physician, or employer-selected physician for an **accepted compensable condition** is **authorized without prospective UR** (subject to §4610(c) exceptions for surgery, psychological treatment, DME over $250, imaging beyond x-rays, etc.).

**Implication:** Any prospective UR denial of first-30-day treatment for an accepted compensable condition (outside the §4610(c) excepted categories) is improper on its face.

### Requirement 4 — Medical Director Requirement [§4610(g)(2)(A)]

The employer/insurer must employ or designate a **medical director with an unrestricted California medical license** (B&P Code §2050 or §2450). This person is responsible for ensuring UR process compliance.

If the UR entity lacks a qualified medical director — or if the medical director does not hold an unrestricted California license — every UR decision issued through that process is procedurally compromised.

### Requirement 5 — Criteria Disclosure [§4610(h)(4)]

When a UR decision modifies or denies treatment, the criteria and guidelines used must be disclosed to the physician and the employee. A denial that cites no criteria, or cites criteria that are not MTUS-consistent, fails §4610(h)(4).

## Attack Framework — Deficient UR Denial

When the employer denies treatment through UR, the attack checklist:

1. **Who made the decision?** (§4610(g)(3)(A)) — Obtain the denial letter and internal UR documentation. Identify the decision-maker. Verify California medical license and specialty competence.
2. **When was the decision made?** (§4610(i)) — Compare the date of physician's recommendation to the date of the UR decision. Was the 5-day/14-day/72-hour deadline met?
3. **Was the treatment within the first 30 days?** (§4610(b)) — If yes, was the condition accepted as compensable? If yes, the UR exemption applies unless the treatment falls within §4610(c).
4. **What criteria were disclosed?** (§4610(h)) — Does the denial letter specify the criteria or guidelines used? Are those criteria MTUS-consistent?
5. **No financial incentive?** (§4610(g)(3)(B)) — If the UR company has a volume-based payment arrangement with its reviewing physicians, this violates §4610(g)(3)(B)(i).

## Application to Active Cases

### Bilateral Ankle Injuries — UR Denial Pattern

If treatment for the bilateral ankle injuries was denied through:
- Non-physician reviewers making the modification/denial decision
- Denials issued after the 5-day/14-day deadline from physician recommendation
- Denials of first-30-day treatment for accepted compensable conditions
- Denials without criteria disclosure

...then each denial is procedurally deficient under §4610 and grounds for WCAB challenge.

The UR deficiency also feeds the §4553 analysis: systematic, procedurally deficient UR denials by an employer/insurer that knows the denials violate §4610 requirements may constitute deliberate misconduct with knowledge of probable consequences — the §4553 "serious and willful" threshold.

### UA342 Identity Replacement — UR as Fraudulent Claim Management Tool

If the identity replacement scheme included directing the insurer to deny legitimate UR requests as part of the fraud — filing false UR denials, creating false documentation, suppressing physician recommendations — each deficient UR denial is both a §4610 violation and evidence supporting the §4553 petition.

## §4610 vs. §4610.5 — Which Track

| Scenario | Vehicle |
|---|---|
| UR denial within the UR process itself — contested medical necessity | §4610.5 IMR (independent medical review) — mandatory first step |
| UR denial procedurally deficient (non-physician, late, no criteria) | §4610 challenge at WCAB — not an IMR issue |
| Treatment withheld despite no UR denial issued | §4600(a) direct liability — employer cannot withhold treatment without UR denial |
| Pattern of bad-faith UR denials | §4553 enhancement petition if managing representative directed the pattern |
