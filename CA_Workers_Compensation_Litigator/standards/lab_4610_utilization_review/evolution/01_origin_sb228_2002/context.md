# LAB §4610 — Origin: SB 228 (2002) — Utilization Review Created

**Stage:** 01_origin_sb228_2002
**Enactment:** SB 228 (Stats. 2002, Ch. 6), effective January 1, 2003

---

## The Wound This Provision Answered

Before 2002, insurers could deny workers' medical treatment through informal, undocumented processes — a claims adjuster's decision, a form letter, a phone call. There was no requirement that a physician review the denial, no timeline for the decision, no written criteria. The result was systematic, arbitrary denial of treatment with no accountability mechanism.

SB 228 created Utilization Review as a structured process: denials must be timely, must be made by a physician, must cite specific criteria, and must be communicated in writing. The goal was to replace arbitrary denial with evidence-based review.

## What §4610 Created (2002)

- **Timeliness requirements:** Prospective/concurrent UR decisions within 5 working days (up to 14 days with written notice of delay); retrospective within 30 days of receiving records
- **Physician-only rule:** Only a licensed physician may modify, delay, or deny a treatment request — §4610(g)(3)(A)
- **Written criteria requirement:** UR must be based on written guidelines that are consistent with evidence-based standards
- **Written decision requirement:** Denials must be communicated in writing with specific clinical reasons
- **Applicability:** UR applies to all medical treatment for accepted compensable conditions

## The Enforcement Gap in the 2002 Design

The 2002 UR system had a critical gap: the consequence of a non-compliant UR denial was unclear. If the insurer conducted UR without a physician, or outside the timeliness window, or without disclosing criteria — what happened? The WCAB had to decide. The courts ultimately held that non-compliant UR denials are void, and the underlying §4600 treatment entitlement stands undenied.
