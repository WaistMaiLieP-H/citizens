# LAB §4610 — Stage 02: SB 863 (2012) — IMR Added; Binding Determination

**Stage:** 02_sb863_2012_imr
**Enactment:** SB 863 (Stats. 2012, Ch. 363), effective January 1, 2013

---

## What Changed

SB 863 added §4610.5 (Independent Medical Review) and amended §4610 to integrate IMR as the mandatory appeal pathway for UR denials. The key change: IMR determinations are binding on the WCAB with only limited exceptions (fraud, conflict of interest, clear legal error — §4610.6(h)).

### §4610(g)(3)(A) — Physician-Only Rule Clarified

SB 863 explicitly clarified that ONLY a licensed physician may modify, delay, or deny a treatment request. The statute specifies "competent to evaluate the specific clinical issues involved in the medical treatment services." This means:
- A generalist physician cannot deny a specialty procedure if not competent in that specialty
- A nurse, claims adjuster, or non-physician UR reviewer's denial is legally void
- The employer cannot cure a physician-less denial retroactively

### The 30-Day First-Aid Exemption

SB 863 also confirmed that first aid treatment for the first 30 days following injury is not subject to UR for accepted compensable conditions. This is a critical protection: in the first month, the treating physician's recommendations stand without UR challenge.

### Strategic Importance Post-SB 863

The IMR binding-determination rule makes procedural compliance at the UR stage critical. A UR denial that is void (non-physician, untimely, no criteria disclosure) cannot be cured through IMR — the underlying treatment entitlement under §4600 stands. The time to challenge the UR process is BEFORE IMR, not after.
