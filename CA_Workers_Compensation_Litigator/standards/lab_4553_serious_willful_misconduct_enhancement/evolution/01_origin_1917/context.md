# LAB §4553 — Origin: 1917 Workmen's Compensation Act Amendment

**Stage:** 01_origin_1917
**Enactment:** Stats. 1917, Ch. 586 (amendment to the 1913 Workers' Compensation Act)
**Effective:** 1917

---

## The Wound This Provision Answered

The original 1913 workers compensation system was a no-fault bargain: the worker gives up tort rights; the employer pays fixed benefits regardless of fault. This worked for ordinary industrial accidents. But it created a perverse incentive: an employer who deliberately endangered workers faced the same compensation cost as one who took reasonable safety precautions. The no-fault system, without a penalty provision, made deliberate misconduct economically rational.

The 1917 amendment added the serious and willful misconduct enhancement precisely to close this gap. If the employer (or its managing agents) deliberately endangered workers with knowledge that injury was the probable consequence, the compensation award increases by 50%. The amendment reintroduced a fault element — not to recreate tort liability, but to give the WCAB system a deterrent mechanism against deliberate employer wrongdoing.

## Original Provision

The 1917 amendment established that compensation would be increased "one-half" where injury resulted from the "serious and willful misconduct" of the employer, partners, officers, managing agents, or general superintendents.

## The Three-Level Employer Structure

The 1917 drafters made a precise choice about who could trigger the enhancement: not just the employer-entity, but specific individuals within the employer hierarchy — managing representative, partner, corporate executive/managing officer, general superintendent. This structure ensured that small-business employers (partners) and large-corporation employers (officers/managers) were equally covered while ordinary supervisory negligence was not.

## Why This Matters Today

The 1917 structure — employer/managing representative/officer/general superintendent — is the operative text in current §4553. Understanding the 1917 origin establishes that the enhancement was designed to reach deliberate managerial decisions, not accidental supervisory errors. In the UA342 identity replacement scenario: if management-level actors directed the falsification of WC records or manipulation of Michael's employment status, the 1917 purpose of §4553 is directly implicated.
