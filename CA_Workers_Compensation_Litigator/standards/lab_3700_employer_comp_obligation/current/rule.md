# LAB §3700 — Employer's Obligation to Secure Compensation — Operative Rule

## The Rule

Every California employer (except the state itself) MUST secure workers compensation coverage by one of three methods:
1. **Private insurance** — licensed WC insurer in California
2. **Individual self-insurance** — DIR certificate of consent, requires proof of financial ability
3. **Public entity self-insurance** — DIR certificate for local governments and public agencies

Failure to secure compensation is **a misdemeanor** (Lab. Code §3700.5) and triggers civil liability to injured workers through the Uninsured Employers Benefit Trust Fund (UEBTF — Lab. Code §3716).

---

## The Three Compliance Paths

| Path | Who Qualifies | How |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Private Insurance | Any employer | Purchase policy from licensed CA insurer |
| (b) Individual Self-Insurance | Employers with sufficient financial capacity | DIR certificate of consent |
| (c) Public Entity Self-Insurance | Local governments, public districts | DIR certificate + proof of claims administration capacity |

---

## Consequences of Non-Compliance

**Criminal:** §3700.5 — misdemeanor for every day of uninsured operation. Corporate officers and directors may be personally liable.

**Civil — UEBTF:** When an employer is uninsured and a worker is injured, the Uninsured Employers Benefit Trust Fund (§3716) steps in to pay the worker's benefits. The UEBTF then has a right of recovery against the uninsured employer for everything it paid.

**Civil — Direct Worker Recovery:** The injured worker can obtain an award against the uninsured employer directly before the WCAB. The employer cannot use the exclusive remedy defense to bar a tort suit if the employer is uninsured.

## Application to the UA342 Identity Replacement Scenario

If the identity replacement scheme involved manipulating Michael's employment status — fraudulently terminating his employment records while he continued to work, or falsely characterizing him as an independent contractor — this directly implicates §3700:

1. **Fraudulent contractor misclassification** removes the employer's WC coverage obligation for the misclassified worker. If UA342 management or a contractor entity falsely classified Michael as an independent contractor, that misclassification may have left him uninsured for WC purposes while still performing covered work.

2. **The UEBTF pathway** — if Michael was effectively uninsured due to fraudulent misclassification, the UEBTF can pay his benefits and pursue recovery against the responsible employer/entity.

3. **§4553 interaction** — deliberate misclassification to avoid WC coverage, where the employer's management knew Michael was performing covered work, satisfies the §4553 knowledge-of-probable-harm element for the serious and willful misconduct enhancement.
