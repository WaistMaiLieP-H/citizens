# LAB §3700 — Origin: 1913 Compulsory Insurance Requirement

**Stage:** 01_origin_1913
**Enactment:** California Workmen's Compensation, Insurance and Safety Act of 1913 (Stats. 1913, Ch. 176)

---

## The Wound

Without compulsory insurance, the workers compensation promise is illusory. An employer who operates without coverage has received the benefit of the exclusive remedy bargain (tort immunity) without providing the consideration (guaranteed benefits). The injured worker, denied tort recovery and left with no WC benefits, falls through entirely.

The 1913 legislature enacted compulsory coverage because it understood that an optional system would be exploited. Employers most likely to injure workers — those operating on thin margins with dangerous conditions — would be least likely to carry insurance voluntarily. Compulsory coverage was the system's integrity requirement.

## The 1913 Three-Path Structure

The 1913 Act already provided three coverage paths: private insurance, self-insurance (for large employers), and public entity provisions. This structure has remained fundamentally unchanged for 113 years.

## The Criminal Penalty Origins

The misdemeanor penalty for uninsured operation (now §3700.5) was part of the original Act. The legislature treated non-compliance not as a civil matter but as a crime — employers who operated without coverage were committing an offense against the public safety framework the Act created.
