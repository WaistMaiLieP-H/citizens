## Holding (Doctrinal)

Early California cases interpreting the compulsory insurance requirement established foundational principles that remain operative:

1. **§3700's coverage obligation is strict** — there is no good-faith exception or reasonable reliance defense to the misdemeanor. An employer who believed it was covered but was not (e.g., policy lapsed, broker error) is still criminally liable under §3700.5 and civilly liable to injured workers.

2. **The exclusive remedy does not protect uninsured employers** — the exclusive remedy provision is a bargained exchange. An employer that has not provided the guaranteed benefits side of the bargain cannot invoke the immunity side. Uninsured employers face both WCAB liability AND potential tort exposure.

3. **Definition of "employer"** — the coverage obligation attaches to the statutory definition of employer, not to common-law employment. Employers who structure arrangements to avoid the employer label (misclassification as independent contractor) are subject to WCAB jurisdiction to determine actual employer status.
