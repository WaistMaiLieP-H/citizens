## The Wound §3700 Answers

### Pre-1913 California: The No-Coverage Hell

Before compulsory coverage, an injured worker faced a three-part catastrophe:

**1. No guaranteed payment source.**
An employer could simply have no money. A construction laborer crushed under a beam had a tort claim — against a sole proprietor who had already spent the season's revenue. Winning a lawsuit against a judgment-proof employer produced a piece of paper, not a penny. The worker bore 100% of the economic risk of the employer's insolvency.

**2. The three-defense wall.**
Even before insolvency, the employer's legal defenses made recovery nearly impossible:
- **Assumption of risk:** You knew the job was dangerous. You took the job anyway. Your loss.
- **Contributory negligence:** If you were even 1% at fault, you recovered nothing.
- **Fellow-servant rule:** If a coworker's negligence contributed, the employer was not liable.

These three defenses, used together, defeated most injured worker claims before reaching the merits. Workers' compensation was designed to eliminate all three simultaneously. But without compulsory coverage, even the new WC system could be gamed: an employer could accept the no-fault obligation on paper while having nothing to pay it with.

**3. The civil court delay.**
Tort litigation took years. An injured worker without income had to survive litigation time while disabled. Medical costs accumulated. Families dissolved. Many workers settled for pennies on the dollar just to eat.

### The Structural Gap the Legislature Saw

The 1913 WC Act created the right to compensation. But a right against an insolvent, uninsured employer is a hollow right. The legislature understood that the WC bargain — workers give up tort rights in exchange for guaranteed, no-fault benefits — only works if the "guaranteed" part is real. A right that depends on the employer's continued solvency and goodwill is not guaranteed. It is contingent.

§3700 answered this by making insurance (or demonstrated self-insurance capacity) a precondition to operating with employees. The coverage obligation is not an option. It is not a suggestion. It is a structural requirement of the entire WC system.

### What Happens Without §3700

The exclusive remedy provision (§3601) strips workers of their tort rights. If §3700 were voluntary, an uninsured employer could:
- Receive the benefit of the tort immunity (§3601 bars the civil lawsuit)
- Provide nothing in exchange (uninsured = no WC benefits)
- Leave the worker with neither remedy

This is the trap §3700 is designed to close. When §3700 fails — through fraud, misclassification, or deliberate non-compliance — the law's response is:
1. Criminal liability: §3700.5 misdemeanor (knowing violation) to felony
2. Civil exposure: §3706 strips the three defenses; negligence presumed; full tort damages available
3. UEBTF backstop: §§3715-3717 ensure the worker still receives benefits even against judgment-proof employers

### The Misclassification Wound

The modern form of this wound is fraudulent independent contractor misclassification. The mechanism is identical to pre-1913 structural avoidance:
- Employer removes the worker from the covered workforce on paper
- Worker continues to perform covered work under employer direction and control
- Worker is injured
- Employer invokes the "independent contractor" label to deny WC coverage
- WCAB must first determine the true employment relationship before adjudicating the underlying claim

The legislature's response is the same: §3700 cannot be evaded by labeling. WCAB jurisdiction to determine actual employer status exists independent of the contractual label. The misclassification itself is the §3700 violation.

## Summary of Loss Without §3700

| Pre-1913 Problem | §3700 Solution |
|---|---|
| Insolvent employer = no recovery | Compulsory insurance = guaranteed fund |
| Three-defense wall | §3706 strips assumption of risk, contributory negligence, fellow servant |
| WC right against judgment-proof employer | UEBTF backstop (§§3715-3717) |
| Fraudulent misclassification | WCAB jurisdiction over employer status |
| Criminal avoidance by sophisticated employers | §3700.5 criminal liability regardless of good faith |

The wound §3700 answers is not just historical — it recurs in every misclassification case, every lapsed policy, every employer who calculated that the fine is cheaper than the premium. §3700 answers by making those calculations irrelevant to the worker's right to receive benefits.
