# Layer 3 — Historical Loss
## CAL_EVID_1235 | The Wound: Evidence Lost When Prior Statements Are Used Only for Impeachment

**Standard ID:** CAL_EVID_1235
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The Wound: Treating Substantive Evidence as Merely Credibility Evidence

The failure mode § 1235 is designed to prevent: a witness gave a more honest account earlier (closer in time to the events, without a lawyer, without adversarial pressure), and now tells a different story under oath. The earlier account is closer to the truth. The common law and the federal rule say: that earlier account can only be used to show the witness is unreliable. The jury is instructed not to use it for the truth of what it says.

**The result of that failure:** The party with the more credible early account loses the substantive weight of that account. The factfinder decides between the two versions, is told the prior account is merely an impeachment tool, and may give disproportionate weight to the sworn in-court account. Injustice follows.

**§ 1235's correction:** That prior account is evidence. It proves what it says. The jury compares both and decides.

---

## Documented Failure Patterns

### Pattern 1 — The evolving police account

**The failure:** An officer writes an incident report immediately after an event. The report says X. Months later, at trial, the officer testifies not-X (often after consulting with prosecutors and reviewing the case file). The report is introduced as impeachment — "Officer, didn't you write X?" — and the officer explains the discrepancy. The jury is told to use the inconsistency only for credibility. The original, contemporaneous account — the one most likely to be accurate — is not treated as proof of anything.

**§ 1235's remedy:** The original report, if offered in compliance with § 770, is substantive evidence of what actually happened. The jury can find that the report accurately described events and that the trial testimony is the distorted version.

**Importance for criminal case 04-23-01959:** Every incident report by every APD officer who responded on June 16, 2023, is a prior statement. If the trial testimony differs from the report, the report itself — through the § 770 foundation — is substantive evidence in California court. Not just a credibility challenge. Proof of what actually happened.

### Pattern 2 — The recanting informant / witness recantation

**The pattern:** A witness makes a statement to police identifying a suspect or describing events. Before trial, the witness recants. At trial, the witness testifies consistent with the recantation, not the original statement. Under pre-§ 1235 law, the prior statement is only impeachment. The prosecution or civil plaintiff loses the substantive force of the earlier, more credible statement.

**§ 1235's remedy:** The prior statement to police is substantive evidence. The prosecution (or civil plaintiff) can argue that the recantation itself is the lie — the prior statement is the truth. The jury decides.

### Pattern 3 — Prior declarations in related proceedings

**The pattern:** A party files a declaration in a prior family law proceeding making factual representations. Later, in a civil fraud case or criminal case, the same party testifies to different facts. Without § 1235, the prior declaration is only impeachment. The party admits inconsistency, explains it away, and continues.

**§ 1235's remedy:** The prior declaration (sworn or unsworn — California does not require oath) is substantive evidence of the facts it asserted. The factfinder can find those facts true based on the declaration. The in-court testimony to the contrary is assessed against the prior record.

**Importance for the steward's cases:** Declarations filed by opposing parties in family law proceedings (RF09456481), in CalVCB proceedings, and in any prior litigation are § 1235 substantive evidence in California proceedings when they contradict the party's current positions.

### Pattern 4 — Agency correspondence recanting earlier positions

**The pattern:** A government agency (CalVCB, a child protective services unit, an investigating police department) sends correspondence establishing facts or making admissions. Later, a representative testifies inconsistently at a hearing. Without § 1235, the correspondence is impeachment only. The agency representative explains the discrepancy. The hearing officer accepts the explanation.

**§ 1235's remedy:** The agency's prior written representations are substantive evidence via § 1235 (prior inconsistent statement) and § 1220 (party admission by a party's agent). The hearing officer or court must treat the correspondence as proof, not merely as a credibility challenge.

**Importance for CalVCB A25-10117946:** CalVCB's denial letter, its case notes, and any prior correspondence are prior statements. If CalVCB representatives testify inconsistently at the § 13959 hearing, those prior documents are § 1235 substantive evidence AND § 1220 party admissions.

## The California Advantage in the Steward's Cases

The steward's cases involve a 16-year record of events. Every prior declaration, every prior court filing by opposing parties, every police report, every agency letter — all are potential § 1235 substantive evidence in California court. In federal court, only the sworn ones carry substantive weight. In California state court, they all do, provided the § 770 foundation is laid.

**This is the most powerful evidentiary difference between California and federal proceedings for the steward's claims.** HERALD maintains a prior statements log for each case — every prior statement by every opposing party and material witness — and classifies each as:
- Substantive in California (§ 1235) and federal (FRE 801(d)(1)(A)) — if under oath
- Substantive in California only (§ 1235) — if informal/unsworn
- Impeachment only in federal (FRE 613) — if informal/unsworn
