## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

California Evidence Code § 1235:

> Evidence of a statement made by a witness is not made inadmissible by the hearsay rule if the statement is inconsistent with his testimony at the hearing and is offered in compliance with Section 770.

*(California Evidence Code § 1235; source text retrieved 2026-04-12 via VernenLegal MCP from leginfo.legislature.ca.gov)*

---

## The Foundation Requirement — § 770 (Verbatim)

California Evidence Code § 770, which § 1235 requires compliance with:

> Unless the interests of justice otherwise require, extrinsic evidence of a statement made by a witness that is inconsistent with any part of his testimony at the hearing shall be excluded unless:
>
> (a) The witness was so examined while testifying as to give him an opportunity to explain or deny the statement; or
>
> (b) The witness has not been excused from giving further testimony in the action.

*(California Evidence Code § 770; source text retrieved 2026-04-12 via VernenLegal MCP from leginfo.legislature.ca.gov)*

## What These Rules Do — Plain Language

**§ 1235 rule:** A prior statement that is inconsistent with a witness's current testimony is not hearsay. It is admissible as substantive evidence — the jury may use it to find that the prior statement was true. This is not merely impeachment (credibility); it is evidence of the facts asserted in the prior statement.

**§ 770 foundation:** Before you can introduce extrinsic evidence (a document, a recording, a second witness) to prove the witness made the prior inconsistent statement, you must give the original witness an opportunity to explain or deny it — either while the witness is on the stand or before they are permanently excused.

---

## The California Advantage — No Oath Required

This is the single most important feature of California § 1235:

**Federal rule (FRE 801(d)(1)(A)):** A prior inconsistent statement is substantive evidence only if it was made under oath at a trial, hearing, other official proceeding, or deposition.

**California rule (§ 1235):** No oath required. ANY prior inconsistent statement — text message, phone call, informal conversation, written letter, unsworn statement to a friend — is substantive evidence if it contradicts the witness's current testimony and the § 770 foundation is laid.

| Prior Statement Type | Federal (FRE) | California (§ 1235) |
|---|---|---|
| Prior deposition testimony | Substantive (under oath) | Substantive |
| Prior § 1746 / § 2015.5 declaration | Substantive (under oath) | Substantive |
| Prior police interview statement | Impeachment only (not under oath) | **SUBSTANTIVE** |
| Text message | Impeachment only (not under oath) | **SUBSTANTIVE** |
| Informal conversation witnessed by third party | Impeachment only | **SUBSTANTIVE** |
| Written letter, email, unsigned statement | Impeachment only | **SUBSTANTIVE** |

**HERALD's application:** In every California proceeding, HERALD treats ALL prior inconsistent statements as candidates for § 1235 substantive evidence, not merely impeachment. The § 770 foundation requirement — opportunity to explain or deny — must be laid.

---

## The "Inconsistency" Standard Under California Law

California courts define inconsistency broadly:

- **Direct contradiction** — witness previously said A; now says not-A
- **Omission** — witness's prior statement did not mention X; witness now testifies prominently about X; the omission of X from a statement where it would have been natural to include it is an inconsistency (*People v. Cowan* (2010) 50 Cal.4th 401)
- **Certainty vs. uncertainty** — witness previously described event with certainty; now claims inability to remember
- **Degree of detail** — prior statement was highly specific; current testimony is vague about the same facts

**Key: § 1235 covers inconsistency with "any part" of the testimony.** A prior statement does not need to contradict the witness's entire account — inconsistency on any individual point satisfies the threshold.
