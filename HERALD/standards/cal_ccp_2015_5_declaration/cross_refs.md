## Prior Inconsistent Statements — Offensive Use of § 2015.5

Under California Evidence Code § 1235, a prior statement of a witness that is inconsistent with their current testimony is admissible as **substantive evidence** (not just impeachment). When the prior statement was made in a § 2015.5 declaration, it was made under penalty of perjury — which means one of two things is true: the prior declaration was perjury, or the current testimony is false.

HERALD applies this framework to:
1. **Christina's declarations** — any prior § 2015.5 declarations filed in family law proceedings that contradict her subsequent statements or the steward's documented record
2. **Social worker / evaluator reports** — if signed under § 2015.5 and their findings contradict the documented record, § 1235 preserves the prior inconsistent statement for substantive use
3. **Opposing party responses to the Treasury and CalVCB inquiries** — if any were made under oath or penalty of perjury and contradict each other, § 1235 (or FRE 801(d)(1)(A) in federal court) applies

## The § 2015.5 / § 1746 Decision Tree (HERALD's Standard Protocol)

```
Is this declaration for a California state court or California agency?
  YES → Use CCP § 2015.5 Form (b):
        "I certify (or declare) under penalty of perjury under the laws of
         the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct."
         [Date]   [Signature]

  NO → Is this declaration for a federal court or federal agency?
       YES → Use 28 U.S.C. § 1746(2):
             "I declare (or certify, verify, or state) under penalty of perjury
              that the foregoing is true and correct. Executed on (date).
              (Signature)"

       NO → Identify the jurisdiction's applicable rule before proceeding.
```

**Note on federal court / state claims:** If a California state-law claim is litigated in federal court (e.g., state-law fraud claim joined to the § 1983 complaint in NDCA), federal procedural rules govern declarations — use § 1746, not § 2015.5.
