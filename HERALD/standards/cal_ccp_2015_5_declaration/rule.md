## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

California Code of Civil Procedure § 2015.5:

> Whenever, under any law of this state or under any rule, regulation, order or requirement made pursuant to the law of this state, any matter is required or permitted to be supported, evidenced, established, or proved by the sworn statement, declaration, verification, certificate, oath, or affidavit, in writing of the person making the same (other than a deposition, or an oath of office, or an oath required to be taken before a specified official other than a notary public), such matter may with like force and effect be supported, evidenced, established or proved by the unsworn statement, declaration, verification, or certificate, in writing of such person which recites that it is certified or declared by him or her to be true under penalty of perjury, is subscribed by him or her, and (1), if executed within this state, states the date and place of execution, or (2), if executed at any place, within or without this state, states the date of execution and that it is so certified or declared under the laws of the State of California.
>
> The certification or declaration may be in substantially the following form:
>
> (a) If executed within this state:
> "I certify (or declare) under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct":
> _____________   _________
> (Date and Place)  (Signature)
>
> (b) If executed at any place, within or without this state:
> "I certify (or declare) under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct":
> _____________   _________
> (Date)  (Signature)

## What This Rule Requires — Plain Language

**The rule:** California's parallel to 28 U.S.C. § 1746. Any sworn statement required in a California state proceeding can be replaced by a written declaration that includes: "under penalty of perjury," the words "true and correct," a date, a place (if executed in California), and a signature. No notary required.

**Key difference from § 1746:** California adds a **place of execution** requirement when signed within California. 28 U.S.C. § 1746 does not require place — only date. A § 2015.5 declaration signed in California that omits the place of execution is defective.

**Two forms:**
- **Within California:** Date + place + signature + "I certify (or declare) under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct"
- **Outside California (or universally):** Date (no place required) + signature + "I certify (or declare) under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct"

---

## The Six Elements (Form (a) — Executed Within California)

| # | Element | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In writing | § 2015.5 (implied — "in writing of such person") |
| 2 | "Certified or declared to be true under penalty of perjury" | § 2015.5 core text |
| 3 | "True and correct" (or substantially similar) | § 2015.5 form (a) |
| 4 | Date of execution | § 2015.5 form (a) — "Date and Place" |
| 5 | **Place of execution** (within California only) | § 2015.5 form (a) — required for in-state execution |
| 6 | Signature | § 2015.5 form (a) |

## The Five Elements (Form (b) — Outside California or Universal)

| # | Element | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In writing | § 2015.5 |
| 2 | "Under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California" | § 2015.5 form (b) — the "laws of California" phrase is required for out-of-state execution |
| 3 | "True and correct" | § 2015.5 form (b) |
| 4 | Date of execution | § 2015.5 form (b) |
| 5 | Signature | § 2015.5 form (b) |

**Note:** Form (b) drops the place requirement but adds "under the laws of the State of California" to the perjury clause. Form (a) omits the "laws of California" phrase but requires place. HERALD standard: use Form (b) universally — it is accepted both in and outside California and eliminates the place-omission defect risk.

---

## What § 2015.5 Does Not Cover

Same carve-outs as 28 U.S.C. § 1746:
- Depositions
- Oaths of office
- Oaths before a specified official other than a notary public

---

## Jurisdiction

California state courts, California state agencies, California administrative proceedings.
Does NOT apply in federal court (28 U.S.C. § 1746 governs in federal court, even for state-law claims litigated in federal court).

**CalVCB application:** CalVCB (Government Code § 13950 et seq.) is a California state agency. The § 13959 appeal procedure is governed by California law. **CCP § 2015.5 is the correct declaration form for the CalVCB appeal — not 28 U.S.C. § 1746.** See provenance.md flag.
