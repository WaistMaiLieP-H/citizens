# Layer 3 — Historical Loss
## CAL_CCP_2015_5 | Cases Lost or Damaged by Declaration Defects in California Proceedings

**Standard ID:** CAL_CCP_2015_5
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The Core Failure Mode

A CCP § 2015.5 declaration defective on its face — missing the perjury phrase, missing a date, missing the place when executed in California, or missing a signature — can be stricken by the court or hearing officer. In summary judgment practice, a stricken declaration eliminates the evidentiary support for a motion. In administrative appeals (including CalVCB), a defective declaration may be rejected outright, causing the appeal to proceed without evidentiary support.

---

## Documented Failure Patterns

### 1. The missing-place trap (California-specific)

**Pattern:** Declarant signs a declaration in California with date and signature but does not state the city or place of execution. Declaration challenged. Court finds form (a) of § 2015.5 requires "date and place." Declaration stricken.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** Every declaration the steward signs in Antioch or elsewhere in California must include the place. The steward's address in Antioch, California is the place of execution. Omitting it creates a curable but costly defect — and in time-sensitive filings (like a CalVCB appeal with a strict deadline), a defective declaration may not be cureable before the deadline.

**HERALD solution:** Universal form (b) — include "under the laws of the State of California" and omit the place requirement. Valid everywhere. Zero risk of the place-omission defect.

---

### 2. The administrative agency rejection

**Pattern:** Pro se claimant submits a written statement to a California agency (analogous to CalVCB) that recites facts but does not include "under penalty of perjury." Agency rejects the statement as unsworn. Claimant argues substantial compliance. Agency holds the perjury-phrase requirement is not a technicality — it is the only mechanism giving the declaration legal weight. Appeal denied.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** The CalVCB § 13959 appeal is an administrative proceeding. The standard for declarations in administrative proceedings tracks § 2015.5. A written factual statement submitted without the perjury phrase is an unsworn letter, not sworn evidence.

---

### 3. The summary judgment declaration stripped to nothing

**Pattern:** Plaintiff opposing summary judgment submits a § 2015.5 declaration that mixes personal knowledge facts with "information and belief" statements, and also attaches documents without authentication. Court strikes all "information and belief" portions under California Evidence Code § 702 (personal knowledge requirement), and strikes the unauthenticated attachments. The declaration survives but provides no material fact sufficient to defeat summary judgment. MSJ granted.

**Historical teaching:** *Raghavan v. Boeing Co.* and similar California cases: California courts apply a rigorous personal knowledge screen to declarations on summary judgment. The § 2015.5 form is necessary but not sufficient — the content must also satisfy the personal knowledge requirement.

**HERALD note:** This is a compound failure: § 2015.5 compliance + Cal. Evid. Code § 702 compliance are both required. HERALD flags any "information and belief" language in declarations it drafts.

---

### 4. The custody declaration — pattern of fabricated content

**Pattern:** In the steward's own family law case, declarations were filed by opposing parties and third parties (social workers, custody evaluators) that contained factual assertions the steward contends are false. Under California Penal Code § 118, false material statements in § 2015.5 declarations are perjury. Unsigned reports (which cannot be sworn declarations at all) were treated as evidence.

**Historical teaching from the steward's record:** Three police reports identified by HERALD (OPD 09-011438, OPD 09-040089, APD 23-004494) are **unsigned** — they cannot be sworn declarations and lack the legal foundation to be treated as sworn evidence. HERALD applies this in reverse: documents submitted in the steward's cases that purport to be sworn but lack the § 2015.5 elements are defective as sworn evidence and should be challenged on that ground.

---

### 5. The CalVCB-specific deadline trap

**Pattern:** Victim submits an appeal under § 13959 without a sworn declaration of the procedural errors. CalVCB hearing officer finds no sworn evidentiary basis for the procedural objections. Appeal fails not on the merits but on the evidentiary foundation.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** The CalVCB application A25-10117946 has documented procedural failures — including a crime date error (6/16/2018 vs. 6/16/2023) that is a standalone reversal ground. That finding must be put in sworn form under § 2015.5 in the appeal declaration or it is an unsworn allegation. HERALD's CalVCB chronology (case_calvcb_A25-10117946_chronology.md) is the factual foundation — the declaration converts that chronology into admissible sworn evidence.
