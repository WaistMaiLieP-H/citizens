# Layer 2 — Reasoning
## CAL_CCP_2015_5 | Why This Standard Exists and How California Courts Apply It

**Standard ID:** CAL_CCP_2015_5
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## Why California Enacted § 2015.5

California enacted § 2015.5 for the same access-to-justice reasons as 28 U.S.C. § 1746: to eliminate the notary requirement for sworn statements while preserving criminal exposure for false statements. The California legislature recognized that requiring notarization created an unnecessary barrier for litigants who were already burdened by the cost and logistics of litigation.

The perjury deterrent in California is **California Penal Code § 118** (perjury) — up to 4 years in state prison for a willfully false material statement made under oath or penalty of perjury. The § 2015.5 declaration substitutes for a notarized affidavit but carries the same Penal Code § 118 exposure.

---

## The Critical California-Specific Distinction: Place of Execution

The most important difference between § 2015.5 and 28 U.S.C. § 1746 is **place of execution**:

- If the declaration is signed **within California**, § 2015.5 form (a) requires both the date **and place** of execution.
- If signed outside California, form (b) requires only the date but adds "under the laws of the State of California" to the perjury clause.

**Why this matters:** A declaration signed in Antioch, California that states only "Executed on April 12, 2026" without the city/place is defective under form (a). Courts have stricken declarations for this omission.

**HERALD's universal form solution:** Use form (b) for all California declarations regardless of where signed. Form (b) ("under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California") is accepted everywhere — in-state and out-of-state — and does not require a place. It is the safer universal form.

---

## How California Courts Apply "Substantially the Following Form"

California courts follow similar analysis to federal courts:

**Permissive:**
- "I declare under penalty of perjury under the laws of the State of California that the foregoing is true and correct" — accepted universally
- "I certify under penalty of perjury that the following is true and correct" (executed within CA) — accepted with date and place

**Strict:**
- Missing "penalty of perjury" — declaration stricken; "I affirm" or "I swear" does not substitute
- Missing "true and correct" (or substantial equivalent) — declaration defective
- Missing place when executed within California (form (a)) — defective; courts have stricken on this ground
- Declaration signed but not dated — defective regardless of form

---

## The "Laws of the State of California" Phrase — When Required

The phrase "under the laws of the State of California" is **required** for out-of-state execution. It is what gives the California perjury statute (Penal Code § 118) jurisdiction over a declarant who signed outside California.

If a declaration is signed outside California and omits "under the laws of the State of California," the declarant has not subjected themselves to California perjury law — the declaration has no enforceable perjury deterrent under California law and is defective.

**HERALD's standard:** Always include "under the laws of the State of California" regardless of where signed. It is required for out-of-state execution; it is harmless (and still accepted) for in-state execution.

---

## Verification — Self-Verification Rule

The declarant must personally sign. A declaration signed by someone "on behalf of" another person without that person's signature is not a § 2015.5 declaration — it is hearsay at best. The signature is the declarant's personal certification.

## Perjury Exposure Under California Law

| Statute | Offense | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Penal Code § 118 | Perjury — willfully false material statement under penalty of perjury | Felony — up to 4 years state prison |
| Penal Code § 127 | Subornation of perjury — inducing another to make a false § 2015.5 declaration | Felony — up to 4 years state prison |
