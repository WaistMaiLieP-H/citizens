## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

Federal Rule of Evidence 901:

> **(a) In General.** To satisfy the requirement of authenticating or identifying an item of evidence, the proponent must produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims it is.
>
> **(b) Examples.** The following are examples only — not a complete list — of evidence that satisfies the requirement:
>
> (1) **Testimony of a Witness with Knowledge.** Testimony that an item is what it is claimed to be.
>
> (2) **Nonexpert Opinion About Handwriting.** A nonexpert's opinion that handwriting is genuine, based on a familiarity with it that was not acquired for the current litigation.
>
> (3) **Comparison by an Expert Witness or the Trier of Fact.** A comparison with an authenticated specimen by an expert witness or the trier of fact.
>
> (4) **Distinctive Characteristics and the Like.** The appearance, contents, substance, internal patterns, or other distinctive characteristics of the item, taken together with all the circumstances.
>
> (5) **Opinion About a Voice.** An opinion identifying a person's voice — whether heard firsthand or through mechanical or electronic transmission or recording — based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances that connect it with the alleged speaker.
>
> (6) **Evidence About a Telephone Conversation.** Evidence that a call was made to the number assigned at the time to a particular person or business, if: (A) in the case of a person, circumstances, including self-identification, show that the person answering was the one called; or (B) in the case of a business, the call was made to a business and the call related to business reasonably transacted over the telephone.
>
> (7) **Evidence About Public Records.** Evidence that a writing was authorized by law to be recorded or filed and was in fact recorded or filed in a public office, or that a purported public record or statement is from the office where items of that kind are kept.
>
> (8) **Evidence About Ancient Documents or Data Compilations.** For a document or data compilation, evidence that it: (A) is in a condition that creates no suspicion about its authenticity; (B) was in a place where, if authentic, it would likely be; and (C) is at least 20 years old when offered.
>
> (9) **Evidence About a Process or System.** Evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result.
>
> (10) **Methods Provided by a Statute or Rule.** Any method of authentication or identification allowed by a federal statute or a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court.

## What This Rule Requires — Plain Language

**The rule:** Before any item of evidence — a document, a recording, a photograph, a physical object — can be admitted, the proponent must show it is what they say it is. This is the authentication requirement. It is a condition precedent to admissibility — no authentication, no admission.

**The threshold is "sufficient to support a finding":** Courts do not require proof beyond a reasonable doubt that the item is authentic. The question is only whether a reasonable factfinder could conclude the item is what it is claimed to be. Once that threshold is cleared, the item goes to the jury; authenticity goes to weight.

**The list in (b) is illustrative, not exhaustive:** FRE 901 provides 10 methods as examples. Other methods can authenticate evidence if they produce "evidence sufficient to support a finding."

## The Authentication Methods Most Used in the Steward's Cases

| Method | Rule | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Witness with knowledge | FRE 901(b)(1) | Steward testifies: "This is the closing disclosure I received at the Honeysuckle escrow closing on [date]." Personal knowledge authenticates documents the steward received or signed. |
| Distinctive characteristics | FRE 901(b)(4) | Contents, letterhead, account number, formatting characteristics of documents — taken together with circumstances |
| Telephone call / electronic communication | FRE 901(b)(5)/(6) | Identifying voice in phone recordings; identifying phone number as assigned to a party |
| Public records | FRE 901(b)(7) | APD/OPD reports, court filings, agency letters — authentic because from the office that keeps them |
| Process or system | FRE 901(b)(9) | Cell records, AT&T account records generated by automated systems |

## The Unsigned Document Problem — FRE 901 Applied to the Steward's Cases

An unsigned document presents a specific authentication challenge. Who can authenticate it? The author cannot sign off on it as their work product if it has no signature. Under FRE 901(b)(1), the person who received the document can testify it is the document they received — but that authenticates receipt, not authorship or accuracy.

**HERALD's position:** The three unsigned police reports in the steward's cases can be authenticated as documents received from the respective agencies (FRE 901(b)(7) — from the office that keeps them) but cannot be authenticated as accurate, complete, or signed by the reporting officer. Their admissibility as official records is weakened by the absence of the officer's signature.
