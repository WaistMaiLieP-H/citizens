# Layer 3 — Historical Loss
## FRE_901 | Cases Lost or Damaged by Authentication Failures

**Standard ID:** FRE_901
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The Core Failure Mode

A party offers a document without adequate authentication. The opposing party objects. The court excludes the document. The party's case collapses because the document was the linchpin of their claim. This failure is entirely avoidable — FRE 901 provides multiple authentication pathways, and a declaration under § 1746 from a witness with knowledge can usually clear the threshold.

---

## Documented Failure Patterns

### 1. The pro se litigant's unauthenticated document pile

**Pattern:** Pro se plaintiff attaches 50 documents to a summary judgment opposition without authentication declarations. Each document is offered as proof of specific facts. Defense moves to strike all exhibits as unauthenticated. Court grants. Plaintiff's opposition is stripped of all documentary evidence. Summary judgment granted.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** HERALD's role is to build authentication declarations for all key documents before they are needed in any filing. Every document in the steward's cases must have an authentication path mapped in advance. "I received this document on [date] from [source]" under § 1746 is the minimum — but it must be in place before filing.

---

### 2. The electronic communication authentication failure

**Pattern:** Party offers screenshots of text messages as evidence of threats. No authentication foundation — just the screenshots printed from a phone. Opposing counsel objects. Court finds screenshots could have been fabricated, altered, or taken from a different device. Excluded.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** The SIM swap / communications fraud track involves phone records, text message history, and account records. Screenshots alone are not sufficient. Authentication requires: the phone number was assigned to the party at the time (carrier records — FRE 901(b)(9) process/system); the message content is consistent with the thread and circumstances (FRE 901(b)(4) distinctive characteristics); and ideally, carrier-certified records of the communication.

---

### 3. The escrow document authentication gap — who received what

**Pattern:** In a real estate fraud case, the plaintiff offers escrow closing documents. Plaintiff's counsel relies on the documents being self-evidently authentic — the escrow company's letterhead is on them. Opposing counsel challenges: these could be altered copies. No witness testimony establishes which version of each document was actually in the plaintiff's hands at closing. Key discrepancy between the plaintiff's copies and the "official" closing package is excluded because plaintiff can only authenticate what the plaintiff received, not what the closing package contains.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases (Honeysuckle):** The 19 unsigned documents in the Honeysuckle closing package are the core of the real estate fraud claim. Authentication requires: (1) steward's testimony that these are the documents received at closing (FRE 901(b)(1)); (2) comparison of steward's copies to the escrow company's official records (FRE 901(b)(3) or (b)(4)); (3) escrow company custodian declaration for the official package (FRE 803(6) business records). The authentication declaration from HERALD's case file must establish which documents were and were not presented to the steward for signature.

---

### 4. The police report self-authentication attempt — fails on unsigned

**Pattern:** Party relies on FRE 902(4) (certified copies of public records are self-authenticating) for a police report. But the certification from the records custodian certifies that this is a copy of what is in their files — it does not certify that the original was signed, accurate, or complete. Challenge to the content (not the authenticity of the copy) must go through FRE 803(8) trustworthiness, not FRE 901.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** OPD and APD reports can be certified as authentic copies of agency records. HERALD does not dispute that they are authentic copies of what the agencies have on file. HERALD's challenge is to their trustworthiness as sworn accounts — FRE 803(8) — and to their status as signed instruments — CCP § 2015.5. These are distinct challenges from authentication.

---

### 5. The voice recording — failure to lay foundation

**Pattern:** Party offers audio recording of phone call. Fails to: (1) identify the voices on the recording, (2) establish the recording was complete and unaltered, (3) provide a chain of custody from recording to presentation. Recording excluded.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** Any recordings relevant to the SIM swap or MDM track (voicemail messages altered or intercepted, calls redirected) require foundation: identification of voices under FRE 901(b)(5), chain of custody under FRE 901(b)(9) (the recording process produces accurate results), and either the steward's personal authentication (heard the call; recognized the voice) or a technical foundation (the recording system's reliability).
