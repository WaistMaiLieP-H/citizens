## FRE 902 — Self-Authentication (Companion Rule)

FRE 902 provides that certain documents are self-authenticating — they require no extrinsic evidence of authenticity:

| FRE 902 Subdivision | Document Type | Application to Steward's Cases |
|---|---|---|
| 902(1) | Domestic public documents under seal | Government documents with official seal |
| 902(4) | Certified copies of public records | Certified APD/OPD reports, certified court records, certified CalVCB records |
| 902(5) | Official publications | Published government reports |
| 902(7) | Trade inscriptions | Labels on products (RedJag vehicle) |
| 902(11)/(12) | Certified records of regularly conducted activities | Business records certifications from custodians — AT&T, GM Financial, escrow companies |

**HERALD's use:** FRE 902(4) and FRE 902(11)/(12) are the primary self-authentication tools for the steward's documentary evidence. A business records certification under FRE 902(11) from a carrier custodian authenticates phone records without the custodian appearing at trial.

## Authentication × Hearsay — Two Separate Gates

The most important conceptual point for the steward's cases:

Authentication (FRE 901) and hearsay (FRE 801-807) are two separate admissibility gates. A document must pass both:

1. **FRE 901:** Is this document what it is claimed to be?
2. **FRE 802/803/804:** Is its content admissible (not hearsay, or within an exception)?

A document can be perfectly authenticated but still excluded as hearsay. Example: a letter from Christina, authenticated as her letter (FRE 901(b)(1) — steward received it, recognized her handwriting), offered to prove the truth of its contents — that is hearsay. It is admissible only if it falls under FRE 801(d)(2) (party admission) or another exception.

HERALD always maps both: the authentication path AND the hearsay path for every document.
