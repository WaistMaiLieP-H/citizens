## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

California Evidence Code § 1400:

> Authentication of a writing means (a) the introduction of evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that it is the writing that the proponent of the evidence claims it is or (b) the establishment of such facts by any other means provided by law.

## What This Rule Does — Plain Language

California's authentication requirement for writings. Identical in standard to FRE 901(a). A document is authenticated when the proponent introduces evidence sufficient for a trier of fact to find that the document is what the proponent claims it is. The standard is "sufficient to sustain a finding" — a low threshold; the factfinder decides weight.

**Note:** California Evidence Code uses "writing" broadly. Cal. Evid. Code § 250 defines "writing" to include: "handwriting, typewriting, printing, photostating, photographing, photocopying, transmitting by electronic mail or facsimile, and every other means of recording upon any tangible thing, any form of communication or representation, including letters, words, pictures, sounds, or symbols, or combinations thereof, and any record thereby created, regardless of the manner in which the record has been stored."

This covers: documents, photographs, emails, text messages, audio recordings, video recordings, digital files.

## Authentication Methods Under California Law

Cal. Evid. Code §§ 1410–1421 provide specific methods:

| Section | Method |
|---|---|
| § 1410 | No subscribing witness required (general rule) |
| § 1411 | Subscribing witness testimony |
| § 1412 | Proof of handwriting by one who has seen it |
| § 1413 | Proof of handwriting by any witness |
| § 1414 | Admission or prior authentication |
| § 1415 | Authenticated copy of authenticated writing |
| § 1416 | Handwriting comparison by trier of fact |
| § 1417 | Expert comparison of handwriting |
| § 1418 | Comparison by trier of fact (general) |
| § 1419 | Ancient documents (30+ years, unsuspicious condition) |
| § 1421 | Distinctive characteristics / contents |

## The Unsigned Document — California's Application

Under § 1400, an unsigned document can be authenticated. Absence of signature is not automatic exclusion — it goes to weight. But authentication of an unsigned document is more difficult because the most common authentication method (testimony by the signer that they signed it) is unavailable.

For unsigned documents in the steward's cases, authentication must rely on:
- Distinctive characteristics (§ 1421) — letterhead, account numbers, format
- Custody chain — where the document was found and by whom
- Witness testimony about receiving the document (§ 1413)

This authenticates the document as received/located. It does not authenticate authorship or accuracy of content.
