# Layer 2 — Reasoning
## CAL_EVID_1400 | Why California Authentication Matters

**Standard ID:** CAL_EVID_1400
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The § 1400 / FRE 901 Parallel

California Evidence Code § 1400 and FRE 901(a) are identical in standard. The analysis is the same: introduce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the document is what it is claimed to be.

The methods differ in enumeration (California uses §§ 1410–1421; federal uses FRE 901(b)(1)-(10)) but the underlying logic is the same.

HERALD applies the same authentication analysis in California proceedings as in federal proceedings, substituting § 1400 for FRE 901(a) and §§ 1410–1421 for FRE 901(b) methods.

---

## The Broad "Writing" Definition — Cal. Evid. Code § 250

California's authentication requirement applies to any "writing" — and § 250's definition is expansive. It covers digital documents, emails, text messages, audio recordings, and video recordings. This means:

- AT&T account records (printed from digital systems) — writings requiring authentication
- Screenshots of text messages — writings requiring authentication
- Email correspondence (the SIM swap / MDM email accounts) — writings requiring authentication
- The CARFAX report — writing requiring authentication

For each, the proponent must produce evidence sufficient to sustain a finding that it is what the proponent claims.

---

## The Distinctive Characteristics Method — § 1421

Section 1421 is the California equivalent of FRE 901(b)(4). Letterhead, account numbers, unique formatting, content that only the purported author would know — taken together with circumstances, these authenticate a document.

**Application to the Honeysuckle documents:** The unsigned closing documents have letterhead, account numbers, escrow numbers, and property descriptions that link them to the Honeysuckle transaction. These distinctive characteristics, taken with the circumstances of the steward's presence at closing, satisfy § 1421 for authentication purposes.

---

## The Steward as Authenticating Witness

Under § 1413 (testimony by any witness with knowledge), the steward can authenticate any document the steward received, signed, or observed. The steward testifies: "This is the document I received at the October 6, 2023 closing." That testimony is sufficient to support a finding under § 1400.

**HERALD's authentication declaration structure for California proceedings:**
1. Identify the document
2. State how the declarant came to have it (received it, signed it, obtained it from X)
3. State the circumstances connecting the document to the claimed transaction or event
4. Authenticate distinctive characteristics if needed

This structure satisfies § 1400 + § 1413 + § 1421.
