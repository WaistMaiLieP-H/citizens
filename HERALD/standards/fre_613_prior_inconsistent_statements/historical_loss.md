# Layer 3 — Historical Loss
## FRE_613 | Cases Lost or Damaged by Prior Inconsistent Statement Failures

**Standard ID:** FRE_613
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The Core Failure Modes

**Failure Mode 1 — Impeachment squandered by disclosure:** Under common law, showing the document to the witness before asking about it allowed the witness to explain it away before the jury saw the full confrontation. FRE 613 fixed this — but practitioners who reflexively hand the document over before asking about it still lose the impact advantage.

**Failure Mode 2 — Extrinsic evidence excluded for lack of foundation:** The "opportunity to explain or deny" requirement for FRE 613(b) extrinsic evidence is a procedural trap. Extrinsic evidence (the document, the recording, the second witness) is inadmissible unless the original witness was given an opportunity to explain or deny. If you go straight to extrinsic evidence without first confronting the witness, the court may exclude it.

**Failure Mode 3 — Treating all prior inconsistent statements as substantive:** The federal oath requirement (FRE 801(d)(1)(A)) is frequently misunderstood. A party submits a contradictory text message as evidence "proving" the witness said something, but the court instructs the jury it is impeachment only. The party argues the prior statement proves their case; the jury instruction tells them it does not. The case turns on substantive proof that does not exist.

**Failure Mode 4 — Forfeiting California advantage in federal proceedings:** A practitioner using California evidence cases in federal court may argue that prior inconsistent statements are substantive — they are, in California. But in federal court, the FRE 801(d)(1)(A) oath requirement governs. The advantage California law provides does not cross the federal courthouse door.

## Documented Historical Patterns

### Pattern 1 — Police reports as definitive narrative

**The failure:** An officer testifies at trial to a version of events. The officer's contemporaneous incident report (written the night of the event) says something different. The prior inconsistent report should be devastating impeachment — but the attorney fails to confront the officer with it on cross-examination and instead waits to introduce it through extrinsic evidence without laying the 613(b) foundation. The court excludes the report as extrinsic evidence because the officer was never given an opportunity to explain.

**Result:** The most effective impeachment evidence disappears. The jury hears only the trial testimony.

**HERALD's protocol:** For each officer in the steward's criminal case, HERALD maps the incident reports against anticipated trial testimony and prepares cross-examination confrontations for each discrepancy BEFORE the officer leaves the stand.

### Pattern 2 — The "I don't recall" hedge

**The failure:** The witness denies making the prior statement or claims no memory. This is treated as sufficient explanation, and extrinsic evidence is not pursued. The court accepts the denial/non-memory, and the jury never hears the prior statement.

**What should happen:** A claim of no memory of making the statement does not prevent introduction of extrinsic evidence proving the statement was made. *United States v. Dietrich* (1980): the court held that a witness claiming no memory of the prior statement was still given an "opportunity to explain or deny" — the memory claim itself is the explanation, and extrinsic evidence is then properly admitted.

**HERALD's protocol:** Denial or claimed non-memory is documented — it satisfies the "opportunity to explain" requirement under 613(b). Proceed immediately to extrinsic evidence.

### Pattern 3 — Prior CalVCB/agency correspondence ignored

**The pattern:** A government agency (CalVCB, an officer's internal affairs unit, a social services agency) sends correspondence making factual representations. At a hearing, the agency representative testifies inconsistently with those prior representations. No one confronts the representative with the prior correspondence.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** CalVCB's denial letter, CalVCB's internal case notes, officers' prior reports in the criminal case — these are all prior written statements. When a CalVCB representative testifies at a § 13959 hearing, their testimony can be impeached with the factual representations in the denial letter. If the representations are inconsistent, FRE 613 (for federal) or § 1235 (for California) applies.
