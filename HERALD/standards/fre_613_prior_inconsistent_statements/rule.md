## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

Federal Rules of Evidence Rule 613:

> **Rule 613. Witness's Prior Statement**
>
> **(a) Showing or Disclosing the Statement During Examination.** When examining a witness about the witness's prior statement, a party need not show it or disclose its contents to the witness. But the party must, on request, show it or disclose its contents to an adverse party's attorney.
>
> **(b) Extrinsic Evidence of a Prior Inconsistent Statement.** Extrinsic evidence of a witness's prior inconsistent statement is admissible only if the witness is given an opportunity to explain or deny the statement and an adverse party is given an opportunity to examine the witness about it, or if justice so requires. This subdivision (b) does not apply to an opposing party's statement under Rule 801(d)(2).

*(Source: Federal Rules of Evidence, effective December 1, 1975, as amended through December 1, 2011; text from 28 U.S.C. Appendix; confirmed at Cornell LII — https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_613)*

---

## What This Rule Does — Plain Language

**The rule:** When confronting a witness with a prior statement that contradicts their current testimony, you do not need to disclose the statement to the witness before asking about it. BUT — if opposing counsel requests to see it, you must show it. And if you want to introduce external evidence (a document, a recording, another witness) showing the prior statement, the original witness must first be given a chance to explain or deny it.

**What FRE 613 does NOT do:** It does not make prior inconsistent statements substantive evidence. Under FRE 613 alone, the prior statement is only admissible to impeach credibility — to show the witness said something different before — not to prove the truth of what the prior statement asserts.

**The path to substantive use:** A prior inconsistent statement becomes substantive evidence only under FRE 801(d)(1)(A): "A Declarant-Witness's Prior Statement. A statement that meets the following conditions is not hearsay: The declarant testifies and is subject to cross-examination about a prior statement, and the statement: (A) is inconsistent with the declarant's testimony and was given under penalty of perjury at a trial, hearing, or other proceeding or in a deposition."

**The critical limitation:** Under federal law, a prior inconsistent statement is substantive evidence only if it was made under oath at a formal proceeding or deposition. An informal inconsistent statement — a text message, a phone call, an interview — is admissible under FRE 613 only to impeach, not as substantive evidence.

---

## The Two-Part Framework

### Part 1 — Cross-Examination (FRE 613(a))

When you examine a witness about their prior statement:
- You need not show them the statement first (you can ask "didn't you say X?" without producing the document)
- BUT: if opposing counsel asks to see the statement, you must show it to them
- This change (from common law) was to prevent surprise while still allowing effective impeachment

### Part 2 — Extrinsic Evidence (FRE 613(b))

If the witness denies or claims not to remember the prior statement, you may introduce extrinsic evidence (a document, a recording, another witness) proving the statement was made, BUT:
- The witness must be given an opportunity to explain or deny it, AND
- Opposing counsel must have an opportunity to examine the witness about it
- This requirement may be satisfied either before or after the extrinsic evidence is introduced, but the opportunity must occur at some point during the trial

**Exception:** FRE 613(b) does not apply to party admissions under FRE 801(d)(2). If the prior inconsistent statement is a party admission, extrinsic evidence is admissible without giving the party a chance to explain.

---

## FRE 613 vs. FRE 801(d)(1)(A) — Impeachment vs. Substantive Evidence

| Use | Authority | Requirement | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impeachment only | FRE 613 | Prior statement inconsistent with testimony | Shows witness is unreliable; jury may not use for truth |
| Substantive evidence | FRE 801(d)(1)(A) | Prior statement: (1) inconsistent AND (2) made under oath at formal proceeding or deposition | Jury may use prior statement for truth of its contents |

**The oath requirement is the dividing line.** A text message inconsistency → FRE 613 only (impeachment). A prior deposition statement inconsistency → FRE 801(d)(1)(A) (substantive evidence).

---

## California Contrast

California Evidence Code § 1235 removes the oath requirement. In California court, ANY prior inconsistent statement — sworn or unsworn, formal or informal — is substantive evidence for the truth of what it asserts, provided the § 770 foundation is laid (opportunity to explain or deny).

**This is one of the most significant advantages California plaintiffs have over their federal counterparts for witness impeachment.** HERALD always notes jurisdiction: federal proceedings → FRE 613/801(d)(1)(A) framework; California proceedings → § 1235 framework.
