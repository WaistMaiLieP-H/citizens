| Case | Citation | Rule Established |
|---|---|---|
| Celotex Corp. v. Catrett | 477 U.S. 317 (1986) | Defendant can shift burden by showing absence of evidence; plaintiff must then produce admissible evidence |

## § 1983 Summary Judgment Specifics

In § 1983 cases, the defendant often moves for summary judgment on qualified immunity. The motion proceeds in two steps:

**Step 1 — Constitutional violation:** Is there a genuine dispute of material fact about whether the defendants violated a constitutional right?
- Plaintiff must produce admissible evidence (FRCP 56(c)(4) declaration) establishing the factual basis for the constitutional violation
- HERALD's June 16, 2023 chronology + the steward's personal knowledge declaration establish this predicate

**Step 2 — Clearly established right:** Was the constitutional right clearly established at the time?
- This is a legal question determined by the court — not a factual showing in the declaration
- HERALD flags: the clearly-established-law argument goes in the brief, not the declaration

## Anti-SLAPP Consideration (California State Court)

For California state court filings, anti-SLAPP motions (CCP § 425.16) have their own evidentiary standard at the second step. Plaintiff must show "probability of prevailing" on the merits using admissible evidence. The CCP § 2015.5 declaration that satisfies that standard is structurally identical to the FRCP 56(c)(4) declaration — same personal knowledge requirement, same admissibility requirement.

## HERALD's Declaration Template Structure

For every summary judgment declaration:

```
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT
NORTHERN DISTRICT OF CALIFORNIA

[Case Caption]

DECLARATION OF [DECLARANT] IN [SUPPORT OF / OPPOSITION TO]
DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

I, [Name], declare:

1. I am [identify declarant]. I have personal knowledge of the facts stated herein and, if called as a witness, could and would competently testify thereto.

2. [Factual paragraph — personal knowledge, no hearsay, no legal conclusion]

3. [Factual paragraph with exhibit authentication: "Attached hereto as Exhibit A is a true and correct copy of [document], which I [received/signed/submitted] on [date] under the circumstances described below."]

[Continue for all material facts]

I declare under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct.
Executed on [date] at [location, optional].

_____________________
[Signature]
[Printed Name]
```

This template satisfies: FRCP 56(c)(4) + 28 U.S.C. § 1746 + FRE 602 + FRCP 11.
