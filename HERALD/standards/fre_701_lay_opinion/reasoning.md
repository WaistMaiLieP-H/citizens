# Layer 2 — Reasoning
## FRE_701 | Why Lay Opinion Is Permitted and Where Its Limits Matter

**Standard ID:** FRE_701
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## Why FRE 701 Exists

Witnesses do not naturally testify in pure factual statements. When asked "what happened," a witness will say "he seemed angry" rather than "his face was red, his voice was elevated 40%, and his hands were balled into fists." FRE 701 recognizes this reality. Requiring witnesses to decompose every perception into its constituent sensory inputs would make testimony artificially stilted and actually less accurate — the impression (the opinion) is often the most useful summary of multiple simultaneous observations.

The 1972 Advisory Committee Note put it plainly: the rule is designed to permit the natural expression of fact-based impressions while filtering out speculation and improperly invading the expert-testimony domain.

---

## The FRE 602 Predicate — Why (a) Is the Load-Bearing Element

FRE 701(a) requires the opinion to be "rationally based on the witness's perception." The word "perception" is doing the work of FRE 602 — the opinion must trace back to something the witness personally observed. If there is no personal observation, there is no FRE 701 opinion — there is only speculation or hearsay.

**Practical test HERALD applies:** For every lay opinion in a declaration, trace the opinion back to specific perceptions:
- "He appeared threatening" → traced to: raised voice, invasion of personal space, physical contact, facial expression
- "The document had been altered" → traced to: irregularities the witness personally observed in the document (ink color, spacing, paper type)
- "She was in control of the situation" → traced to: her observed communications, directions to others, positioning

If the chain from opinion to personal observation cannot be traced, FRE 701 does not save the opinion.

## The 2000 Amendment — The Expert Workaround Problem

Before 2000, some parties attempted to call witnesses as "lay witnesses" to give what was actually expert opinion, thereby avoiding the FRE 702 disclosure requirements (and FRCP 26(a)(2) expert report requirements). The 2000 amendment closed this gap by adding FRE 701(c): the opinion cannot be based on "scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge within the scope of Rule 702."

**Why this matters for the steward's cases:** The steward has professional knowledge (UA Local 342 plumbing — skilled trades). If the steward testifies about plumbing work, the steward may be testifying as a lay witness (things observed) or as an expert witness (specialized knowledge about trade standards). The correct category determines the disclosure requirements. HERALD flags any opinion that depends on the steward's trade expertise — those opinions may require FRE 702 disclosure.

## The Demeanor and Emotional State Exception — Most Used in the Steward's Cases

Courts have consistently held that lay witnesses can testify to their observations of another person's demeanor:
- Whether someone appeared frightened, calm, angry, intoxicated, distressed
- Whether someone's voice was raised or lowered
- Whether someone's behavior was consistent with what the witness had previously observed from them

**Application to the steward's cases:** The steward can testify to Christina's observed behavior — her demeanor at custody exchanges, her tone and affect at court appearances, her behavior patterns observed over 16 years of interaction. These are FRE 701 opinions rationally based on personal perception. The steward cannot testify to Christina's mental state (intent, knowledge, planning) — that requires inference from conduct, which must be carefully framed.

---

## Legal Conclusions — The Line FRE 701(b) Draws

The "helpful to determining a fact in issue" requirement filters out pure legal conclusions. Saying "defendant was negligent" does not help the jury — the jury determines negligence. Saying "defendant drove across three lanes without signaling, at a speed I estimated at 80 mph, while looking at a phone" helps the jury determine negligence.

**HERALD's drafting standard:** Never write a legal conclusion in a lay declaration. Convert legal conclusions to factual predicates:

| Do NOT write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "APD officers violated my constitutional rights" | "Officer Martinez placed his knee on my back while I was face down and compliant, causing sharp pain in my spine" |
| "Christina committed perjury" | "Christina testified at the June 2009 hearing that [X]. I was present at that hearing. I personally observed [specific contradicting event] on [date]." |
| "The escrow company defrauded me" | "The closing disclosure I received on [date] showed [X]. The final HUD-1 I was given at closing showed [Y]. I did not receive [document Z] at closing, though it appears in the closing package." |

## Overlap with Expert Opinion — The Boundary HERALD Watches

| Topic | Lay opinion possible? | Expert required? |
|---|---|---|
| Physical sensation of shoulder pain after vaccine | Yes — personal observation of own body | For causation: yes (FRE 702) |
| Observation that device behavior changed after unknown enrollment | Yes — observed changed behavior | For forensic attribution: yes (FRE 702) |
| Recognition that a car spare tire was in the road position | Yes — lay person can observe tire mounting | For mechanical significance: expert |
| Observation that documents in a closing package were not all signed | Yes — personal observation of signatures/blanks | For forensic authentication: expert |
| Voice identification of a person heard on a phone | Yes — personal knowledge of the person's voice | For scientific voice analysis: expert |

## California Parallel — Cal. Evid. Code § 800

California Evidence Code § 800 is the state parallel:

> "If a witness is not testifying as an expert, his testimony in the form of an opinion is limited to such an opinion as is permitted by law, including an opinion that is: (a) Rationally based on the perception of the witness; and (b) Helpful to a clear understanding of his testimony."

Identical to FRE 701(a) and (b). The FRE 701(c) expert-workaround limitation does not appear explicitly in § 800 but is enforced through Cal. Evid. Code §§ 720-730 (expert witnesses).
