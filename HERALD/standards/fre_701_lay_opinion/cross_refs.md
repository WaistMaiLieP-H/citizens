## Expert Opinion Boundary — FRE 702

FRE 701(c) explicitly references FRE 702. The lay/expert distinction is:

| Question | If Yes | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Does forming this opinion require scientific, technical, or specialized knowledge? | Probably expert | FRE 702 |
| Is the opinion naturally formed from personal observation without specialized training? | Lay opinion | FRE 701 |
| Would the opinion require an expert report under FRCP 26(a)(2)? | Expert | FRE 702 + FRCP 26(a)(2) |

## The Contradiction Framework — HERALD's Core Use of FRE 701

HERALD's primary offensive use of FRE 701 is the **behavioral contradiction**:

1. State the factual observation: "I personally observed [specific conduct] on [date] at [place]."
2. State the FRE 701 lay opinion of what the conduct meant: "This behavior was consistent with [assessment] based on [specific perceptions]."
3. Pair with the opposing claim: "This directly contradicts [opposing party's] statement that [claim]."

This three-part structure — observation + lay opinion + contradiction — is admissible, powerful, and does not invade the expert-testimony domain for behavioral observations that a lay person is competent to make.
