# Layer 3 — Historical Loss
## FRE_701 | Cases Lost or Damaged by Lay Opinion Errors

**Standard ID:** FRE_701
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The Two Failure Modes

FRE 701 produces two distinct failure patterns:

**Failure Mode 1 — Opinion improperly excluded:** A lay witness gives a permissible FRE 701 opinion. The opposing party objects. The court excludes it as "speculation" or "legal conclusion" without analyzing whether FRE 701's three elements are met. The witness's testimony is stripped of its most useful content.

**Failure Mode 2 — Expert opinion disguised as lay opinion:** A party offers expert-level opinion testimony through a lay witness to avoid FRE 702 disclosure. The court strikes it. The party has no admissible expert testimony and loses the point.

---

## Documented Failure Patterns

### 1. The "speculation" mislabel stripping valid lay opinion

**Pattern:** Lay witness testifies "he appeared to be monitoring the house." Defendant objects as speculation. Court sustains, calling it speculation. In fact, the opinion was rationally based on the witness's personal observation of defendant's repeated slow drives past the house, lingering near the driveway, and observed eye contact with the house. It was a valid FRE 701 opinion. Witness cannot re-establish it without knowing FRE 701.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** HERALD expects opposing counsel to label the steward's lay opinions as "speculation." The response is: "This opinion is rationally based on the declarant's direct perception of [specific observations] over [time period] and satisfies FRE 701(a)."

---

### 2. The mislabeled expert opinion struck at summary judgment

**Pattern:** A party submits a declaration containing what they believe is lay opinion — "in my experience, this type of device behavior is consistent with remote access." The court finds this requires specialized knowledge (FRE 702) and strikes it. The declaration no longer supports the party's position.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** The MDM enrollment evidence (xfrt35sport@gmail.com, Apple MDM + Android) involves technical conduct. The steward's personal observations of changed device behavior (apps installing without permission, settings changing, cameras activating) are FRE 701 lay observations. Attributing that behavior to a specific attacker's tools requires expert analysis. HERALD keeps these distinct in drafted declarations.

---

### 3. The credibility opinion — improper legal conclusion

**Pattern:** Declarant states: "In my opinion, Christina was lying when she testified at the June 2009 hearing." Court strikes as an improper credibility opinion (jury determines credibility) and improper legal conclusion. The far more powerful approach — which survives — is the factual contradiction statement: "Christina testified [X] at the June 2009 hearing. I personally observed [Y] on [date], which directly contradicts her testimony."

**Teaching point:** The factual contradiction is more powerful than the credibility conclusion and is admissible. HERALD never writes "she was lying" in a declaration. HERALD writes the specific factual contradiction and lets the factfinder draw the credibility conclusion.

---

### 4. The value opinion — improperly excluded

**Pattern:** Property owner testifies to the value of their own property. Court initially excludes as requiring expert appraisal. Appellate court reverses — the owner of property has personal knowledge of its value through ownership, improvements, market observation, purchase price. This is a permissible FRE 701 lay opinion.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases (Honeysuckle):** The steward owned 2958 Honeysuckle Circle and is familiar with its purchase price, improvements made, market at time of sale, and the closing figures. The steward can testify to the equity the steward expected to receive (based on personal knowledge of the mortgage, improvements, and market) vs. what was actually received ($109K on $465K equity). This is FRE 701-compliant lay opinion on value, not speculation.

---

### 5. The "appeared frightened" exclusion — wrong

**Pattern:** Witness testifies "the plaintiff appeared frightened." Defense objects. Court initially excludes as speculation about mental state. This is wrong — "appeared frightened" based on personal observation of trembling, pale color, wide eyes, and low voice is a classic FRE 701 lay opinion about observable emotional state. It is not a statement about internal mental state.

**Application to the steward's cases:** The steward can testify to the emotional state of individuals observed on June 16, 2023 — including the child's observed behavior and demeanor during the APD encounter. These are FRE 701 lay opinions based on direct parental observation.

## Offensive Application — FRE 701 Applied to Opposing Declarations

HERALD applies FRE 701 as an offensive tool to opposing declarations in the steward's cases:

| Document | FRE 701 Issue |
|---|---|
| Social worker reports asserting child was "at risk" in steward's care | "At risk" is a professional assessment — is it lay opinion or expert opinion? If expert, was it disclosed under FRE 702? Is the professional qualified as an expert? |
| Custody evaluator report | Professional psychological opinions about parenting capacity require FRE 702 expert qualification — not FRE 701 lay opinion |
| Christina's declaration asserting steward was "dangerous" | "Dangerous" is a legal/expert characterization if based on professional assessment; if based on personal observation, it is FRE 701 — but what observations? |
| APD officer report characterizing the June 16, 2023 encounter | Officer lay observations are FRE 701-compliant; officer opinions about criminal intent or legal conclusions are not |
