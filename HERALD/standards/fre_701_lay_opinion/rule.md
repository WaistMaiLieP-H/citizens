# Layer 1 — Rule
## FRE_701 | Lay Witness Opinion Testimony

**Standard ID:** FRE_701
**Popular Name:** Lay witness opinion testimony
**Citizen Owner:** HERALD
**Filed:** 2026-04-12
**Status:** PROPOSED — requires steward review

---

## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

Federal Rule of Evidence 701:

> If a witness is not testifying as an expert, testimony in the form of an opinion is limited to one that is:
> (a) rationally based on the witness's perception;
> (b) helpful to clearly understanding the witness's testimony or to determining a fact in issue; and
> (c) not based on scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge within the scope of Rule 702.

*(Pub. L. 93–595, § 1, Jan. 2, 1975; amended Mar. 2, 1987; Apr. 17, 2000; Apr. 26, 2011, eff. Dec. 1, 2011.)*

---

## What This Rule Permits — Plain Language

**The rule:** A lay witness (someone testifying from personal experience, not expertise) can give an opinion if three conditions are met: (1) the opinion is grounded in what the witness personally perceived, (2) it actually helps the factfinder understand something, and (3) it does not require specialized expertise to form.

**The threshold is low for lay opinion:** Courts recognize that witnesses naturally express themselves through opinions rather than purely factual statements. FRE 701 permits that — with the three guardrails above.

**The 2000 amendment's critical limit:** FRE 701(c) was added to prevent experts from testifying as "lay witnesses" to avoid the disclosure requirements of FRE 702 (expert testimony) and FRCP 26(a)(2) (expert report disclosure). A witness who relies on specialized knowledge to form their opinion must be disclosed as an expert under FRE 702 — they cannot use FRE 701 as a workaround.

---

## The Three Elements (All Three Required)

| # | Element | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Rationally based on perception | The opinion must flow naturally from what the witness personally observed (FRE 602 predicate) | "He seemed angry" based on tone, body language, facial expression — OK. "He intended to commit fraud" with no personal observation — not OK. |
| (b) Helpful to understanding testimony or determining a fact in issue | The opinion must actually assist the factfinder — not just restate the obvious, not invade the jury's function without good reason | "The road was slippery" helps determine negligence. "Defendant is guilty" does not help — it is a legal conclusion that invades the jury. |
| (c) Not based on specialized knowledge (FRE 702) | Cannot be scientific, technical, or specialized knowledge | "The speed looked like about 60 mph" — lay estimate OK. "Based on the vehicle dynamics, the speed was 60 mph" — that is expert opinion. |

---

## Common Permissible Lay Opinions

| Opinion | Basis |
|---|---|
| Speed, distance, time estimates | Based on direct perception |
| A person's emotional state or demeanor ("appeared nervous," "seemed frightened") | Based on direct observation of behavior |
| Identification of a familiar person | Based on personal knowledge of the person's appearance |
| Whether someone was intoxicated | Based on personal observation of slurred speech, unsteady gait, smell |
| Whether a vehicle was moving or stopped | Based on direct observation |
| Whether a statement sounded like a threat | Based on direct hearing of the statement |
| The approximate value of common property the owner is familiar with | Based on ownership experience |

---

## What FRE 701 Does NOT Permit

| Opinion | Problem |
|---|---|
| Legal conclusions ("defendant was negligent," "the contract was breached") | Not helpful — invades the factfinder's role |
| Opinions requiring specialized knowledge ("the fracture pattern indicates the bone was broken before death") | Requires FRE 702 expert |
| Opinions about another person's mental state without behavioral foundation ("she intended to deceive") | No personal knowledge of mental state; would need FRE 702 if based on behavioral analysis |
| "Information and belief" opinions | "I believe X happened" without perceptual basis is not FRE 701 opinion — it is speculation |

---

## Application to § 1746 / § 2015.5 Declarations

A lay declarant can include FRE 701 opinions in a § 1746 or § 2015.5 declaration — provided the three elements are met. HERALD marks opinions clearly in drafted declarations so they can be distinguished from factual assertions.

**Format HERALD uses:**
- Factual assertion: "Officer Smith placed his hand on my shoulder."
- FRE 701 lay opinion: "Based on Officer Smith's raised voice, clenched jaw, and aggressive movement toward me, he appeared agitated and threatening." [Clearly rationally based on personal perception, helpful, not expert knowledge]
