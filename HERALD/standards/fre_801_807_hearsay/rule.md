## Core Rules — Verbatim Text

### FRE 801 — Definitions and Exclusions from Hearsay

> **(a) Statement.** "Statement" means a person's oral assertion, written assertion, or nonverbal conduct, if the person intended it as an assertion.
>
> **(b) Declarant.** "Declarant" means the person who made the statement.
>
> **(c) Hearsay.** "Hearsay" means a statement that:
> (1) the declarant does not make while testifying at the current trial or hearing; and
> (2) a party offers in evidence to prove the truth of the matter asserted in the statement.
>
> **(d) Statements That Are Not Hearsay.**
> (1) A Declarant-Witness's Prior Statement. The declarant testifies and is subject to cross-examination about a prior statement, and the statement:
> (A) is inconsistent with the declarant's testimony and was given under penalty of perjury at a trial, hearing, or other proceeding or in a deposition;
> (B) is consistent with the declarant's testimony and is offered to rebut fabrication charges or rehabilitate credibility; or
> (C) identifies a person as someone the declarant perceived earlier.
>
> (2) An Opposing Party's Statement. The statement is offered against an opposing party and:
> (A) was made by the party in an individual or representative capacity;
> (B) the party manifested it adopted or believed to be true;
> (C) was made by an authorized person;
> (D) was made by an agent or employee within scope of that relationship; or
> (E) was made by a coconspirator during and in furtherance of the conspiracy.

### FRE 802 — The Exclusionary Rule

> Hearsay is not admissible unless any of the following provides otherwise: a federal statute; these rules; or other rules prescribed by the Supreme Court.

### FRE 803 — Exceptions (Declarant Availability Immaterial)

23 exceptions. The ones most relevant to the steward's cases:

| # | Exception | Key Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| (1) | Present sense impression | Made while perceiving or immediately after |
| (2) | Excited utterance | Made under stress of startling event |
| (3) | Then-existing mental/emotional/physical condition | Current state, not memory or belief |
| (4) | Statement for medical diagnosis/treatment | To healthcare provider; reasonably pertinent |
| (5) | Recorded recollection | When matter was fresh; witness cannot now recall |
| (6) | Business records | Regular course of business; contemporaneous; qualified custodian |
| (7) | Absence of business record | Nonoccurrence proven by absence from records |
| (8) | Public records | Official activities; legally required observations; authorized investigative findings |
| (10) | Absence of public record | Certification that diligent search found no record |
| (16) | Statements in ancient documents | Prepared before Jan. 1, 1998 |
| (22) | Judgment of previous conviction | Final felony judgment |

### FRE 804 — Exceptions (Declarant Must Be Unavailable)

Declarant is unavailable if: privileged; refuses to testify despite court order; lacks memory; death/illness; absent and unreachable by reasonable means.

Key exceptions when unavailable:
- **(b)(1)** Former testimony — prior trial, hearing, or deposition testimony, with opportunity to cross
- **(b)(3)** Statement against interest — reasonable person would only say it if it were true; corroboration required for criminal liability statements
- **(b)(6)** Forfeiture by wrongdoing — offered against party who wrongfully caused the unavailability

### FRE 805 — Hearsay Within Hearsay

> Hearsay within hearsay is not excluded under the rule against hearsay if each part of the combined statements conforms with an exception to the rule.

### FRE 807 — Residual Exception

> A hearsay statement is not excluded by the rule against hearsay even if not covered by a Rule 803 or 804 exception if: (a) the statement has equivalent circumstantial guarantees of trustworthiness; (b) it is offered as evidence of a material fact; (c) it is more probative on the point than other reasonably available evidence; and (d) admitting it will best serve the purposes of these rules and interests of justice. Notice to the adverse party is required.

## The Core Hearsay Determination — Four-Step Analysis

| Step | Question | If Yes | If No |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is it a statement (assertion)? | Go to step 2 | Not hearsay — FRE 602 governs |
| 2 | Was it made out of court? | Go to step 3 | Not hearsay (in-court testimony) |
| 3 | Is it offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted? | Go to step 4 | Not hearsay — admissible for another purpose (e.g., verbal act, effect on listener, notice) |
| 4 | Does it fit an exclusion (FRE 801(d)) or exception (FRE 803/804/807)? | Admissible | HEARSAY — EXCLUDED |

## Not Hearsay — The Critical Alternative Purposes

An out-of-court statement is NOT hearsay when offered for any purpose other than proving its truth:

| Purpose | Example | Why Not Hearsay |
|---|---|---|
| Effect on listener | "Officer told me I was under arrest" | Proves arrest was communicated, not that arrest was proper |
| Notice | "Christina sent a message saying she feared harm" | Proves she claimed fear (for DVRO purposes), not that fear was real |
| Verbal act | "The officer said 'you have the right to remain silent'" | The statement itself is the act — Miranda warning |
| Impeachment | Prior inconsistent statement used to contradict | Offered to show prior statement conflicts, not for truth |
| State of mind (speaker) | "She said she was frightened" | Proves speaker's then-existing mental state (FRE 803(3)) |
