# Layer 3 — Historical Loss
## FRE_801_807 | Cases Lost or Damaged by Hearsay Failures

**Standard ID:** FRE_801_807
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The Two Directions of Hearsay Failure

Hearsay produces losses in both directions:

**Direction 1 — Excluding damaging hearsay:** A party fails to challenge damaging hearsay that should have been excluded. The jury hears unreliable secondhand statements that never should have come in.

**Direction 2 — Failing to admit helpful hearsay:** A party fails to identify the correct exception or exclusion for admissible out-of-court statements. The factfinder never hears important evidence because it was incorrectly self-excluded.

---

## Documented Failure Patterns

### 1. The unchallenged police report — FRE 803(8) trustworthiness failure

**Pattern:** Police report contains statements sourced from an interested party (e.g., the complaining witness) recounted by the officer. Defendant fails to challenge the report's trustworthiness under FRE 803(8). The report comes in as a public record. The jury gives it substantial weight. Conviction or adverse civil judgment follows.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** APD Report #23-004494 (June 16, 2023) was prepared based on communications the officer had with Christina (the interested party who orchestrated the call) and dispatch information that originated with her. HERALD's witnessed chronology establishes this chain. The report's trustworthiness is challengeable under FRE 803(8)'s "lack of trustworthiness" caveat. HERALD has flagged this challenge.

---

### 2. The missed party admission — FRE 801(d)(2) ignored

**Pattern:** Plaintiff's counsel fails to identify prior statements by the defendant as party admissions. The statements are excluded as hearsay by a court that also misidentifies them. Plaintiff has no admissible statement from the defendant on the key issue. Case settles for less than its value.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases (offensive use):** Every statement APD officers made about the June 16, 2023 encounter that was made in their individual or official capacity is a party admission under FRE 801(d)(2)(A) (individual) or (D) (employee/agent within scope). The City of Antioch is a defendant in the § 1983 action. Officer statements in the APD report, in body camera footage, in radio communications — all are FRE 801(d)(2) admissions by a party-opponent. They are not hearsay.

---

### 3. The prior inconsistent statement used only for impeachment when it could be substantive

**Pattern:** Witness testifies at trial inconsistently with their prior deposition testimony given under oath. Counsel uses the deposition for impeachment only — to attack credibility. Counsel misses that FRE 801(d)(1)(A) makes the prior sworn statement substantive evidence — not just impeachment. The jury can use the prior testimony to decide what actually happened, not just to disbelieve the current testimony.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** If Christina or any opposing party previously testified under oath at a hearing, trial, or deposition in the family law or related proceedings, that testimony is substantive evidence under FRE 801(d)(1)(A) — if it contradicts their current position. HERALD will identify all such prior sworn testimony and flag it as both impeachment AND substantive evidence.

---

### 4. The unsigned report admitted as evidence it is not

**Pattern:** Party offers an unsigned police report as a sworn account of what happened. Opposing party fails to challenge authenticity (FRE 901), perjury-form defect (§ 2015.5), and trustworthiness (FRE 803(8)). The unsigned report carries evidentiary weight it is not entitled to.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases (defensive use):** Three unsigned police reports in the steward's cases have been admitted or treated as evidence without challenge to: (1) their authentication (FRE 901 — who can authenticate an unsigned document?); (2) their sworn-instrument status (CCP § 2015.5 — unsigned = not subscribed); (3) their FRE 803(8) trustworthiness (unsigned by the reporting officer = chain of custody break; statements from interested parties). HERALD has flagged all three for challenge.

---

### 5. The business records exception used without proper foundation

**Pattern:** Party offers financial records as business records under FRE 803(6) without a qualified custodian declaration. Court admits over objection but on appeal the foundation is found insufficient. Judgment reversed.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** GM Financial collection records (RedJag, $19,985.35 claim), AT&T account records (SIM swap track), and Honeysuckle escrow records (real estate fraud) all require FRE 803(6) business records foundation. The steward should obtain custodian certifications for all favorable records before filing.

---

### 6. The FRE 804(b)(6) forfeiture doctrine — unused in cases of witness manipulation

**Pattern:** In a domestic violence or stalking case, the primary witness recants after the defendant contacts the witness. Prosecution fails to invoke FRE 804(b)(6) (forfeiture by wrongdoing). The witness's prior statements cannot come in. Defendant acquitted.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** The SIM swap and MDM evidence establishes that communications between witnesses and the steward were actively interfered with. If the interference affected witness availability or willingness to testify — including family members, medical providers who were blocked from contact, or others whose communications were intercepted — FRE 804(b)(6) may render their prior out-of-court statements admissible against the responsible parties. HERALD flags this as a doctrine to develop factually.
