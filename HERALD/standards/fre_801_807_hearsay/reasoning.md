# Layer 2 — Reasoning
## FRE_801_807 | How the Hearsay Framework Operates in Practice

**Standard ID:** FRE_801_807
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## Why Hearsay Is Excluded

Hearsay is excluded because it cannot be tested. When someone testifies to what a third party said out of court, the factfinder cannot assess the third party's: (1) perception — did they actually observe what they reported? (2) memory — do they accurately remember it? (3) sincerity — were they telling the truth? (4) narration — did they communicate it accurately?

Cross-examination is the testing mechanism for all four. Out-of-court statements cannot be cross-examined. The exceptions are carve-outs for situations where circumstantial guarantees substitute for cross-examination — excited utterances, for example, are presumably sincere because the declarant has no time to fabricate.

---

## The Most Important Non-Hearsay Use — Effect on Listener

The most commonly missed non-hearsay use is **effect on the listener**. When a statement's significance is that it was made — not that it was true — it is not hearsay.

**Application to the steward's cases:**

- APD officer's statement to the steward on June 16, 2023: admitted not to prove the statement was accurate, but to prove what the officer communicated and what the steward understood
- Christina's threat statements (if any): admitted to prove the steward was placed in fear, not to prove Christina actually intended harm
- Dispatch communications about the June 16 call: admitted to prove what APD was told before arriving, not to prove the truth of the caller's report

## FRE 801(d)(2) — Opposing Party's Statement (Party Admission)

This is the most powerful hearsay exclusion in the steward's cases. Any statement made by an adverse party — in their individual capacity, in a declaration, in a letter, in a recorded communication, in an email — is not hearsay when offered against them. This applies to:

- Christina's statements in declarations filed in family law proceedings
- Ann Hillberg's communications about the State Farm policy
- AT&T/Sprint account communications made by or on behalf of Christina as the account controller
- APD officers' statements (statements of employees/agents of the City of Antioch offered against the City)
- Any defendant in the § 1983 action's prior statements about their conduct

**Key rule:** The statement must be offered against the party who made it. The steward's own prior statements are admissible against the steward under FRE 801(d)(2) — but the steward cannot use FRE 801(d)(2) to admit the steward's own prior statements in the steward's favor. For the steward's own prior consistent statements, FRE 801(d)(1)(B) applies (declarant-witness prior consistent statement).

---

## FRE 801(d)(1)(A) — Prior Inconsistent Statement as Substantive Evidence

This is the steward's most important hearsay exclusion for the family law and CalVCB tracks.

A prior inconsistent statement made under penalty of perjury (at a trial, hearing, or other proceeding, or in a deposition) is not hearsay. It is substantive evidence — not just impeachment.

**Application:**
- Christina's prior testimony at family law hearings (if under oath) that contradicts her current position is substantive evidence of the prior statement's truth
- Social worker or custody evaluator testimony under oath that contradicts their report is substantive evidence
- CalVCB staff testimony under oath contradicting the written determinations is substantive evidence

**The "under penalty of perjury" requirement:** The prior statement must have been made under oath or penalty of perjury at an official proceeding or in a deposition. An informal out-of-court statement, even if inconsistent, is only impeachment (not substantive evidence) unless it falls under this rule.

## FRE 803(6) — Business Records Exception

The most common exception for documents in civil litigation. Requirements:
1. Record was made at or near the time of the event
2. By someone with knowledge
3. Kept in the ordinary course of a regularly conducted activity
4. Regular practice to make such records
5. Qualified witness (custodian or other qualified person) lays the foundation

**Critical limit — FRE 803(6)(E):** A business record is not admissible if the source of information or the method or circumstances of preparation indicate a lack of trustworthiness. Courts have held that police reports containing information from non-routine investigative sources may not qualify under (6) — they qualify under FRE 803(8) (public records) instead, but FRE 803(8) has its own limits.

**Application to the steward's cases:**
- AT&T account records — business records; custodian required
- GM Financial collection records — business records
- Treasury Securities correspondence — public records (FRE 803(8)) or business records
- Walgreens vaccine administration records (SIRVA) — business records for medical treatment

## FRE 803(8) — Public Records Exception

Official records of a public agency's activities, legally-required observations, and authorized investigative findings. Key limits:

- In criminal cases against the defendant: law enforcement observations and investigative findings are excluded from FRE 803(8)(A)(ii) and (iii) (but may be offered under other exceptions)
- The record must not indicate lack of trustworthiness

**Application to the steward's cases:**
- APD Report #23-004494 — public record; but the trustworthiness issue raised by HERALD's audit (unsigned, DOC-001 FAIL) goes to the "lack of trustworthiness" exception in FRE 803(8)
- OPD Reports 09-011438 and 09-040089 — unsigned; HERALD has flagged as defective; trustworthiness challenge available
- CalVCB determination letters — public records of agency decisions
- Court orders in family law proceedings — public records

## FRE 804(b)(6) — Forfeiture by Wrongdoing

A hearsay statement is admissible against a party who wrongfully caused the declarant's unavailability to testify — intending that result. If Christina, Ann Hillberg, or others arranged for witnesses to be unavailable (through SIM swap communications, manipulation of family contacts, or other means), FRE 804(b)(6) may render those witnesses' prior statements admissible against the responsible party.

**This requires factual development.** HERALD flags this as a potentially applicable doctrine in the steward's cases but does not assert it without a specific factual showing.

## FRE 807 — The Residual Exception

The safety valve for trustworthy hearsay that doesn't fit any specific exception. Requires: equivalent trustworthiness, material fact, most probative available evidence, justice served, and advance notice to the opposing party.

**Application:** Treasury Securities contradictory response letters may be offered under FRE 807 if they don't fit squarely into FRE 803(8) — their internal contradictions are themselves strong circumstantial guarantees of trustworthiness (contradictory agency responses to the same customer establish that one or both are false, which is material).

## California Hearsay Parallel — Cal. Evid. Code §§ 1200-1380

California has its own hearsay framework:
- § 1200: The hearsay rule (parallel to FRE 802)
- § 1220: Party admission (parallel to FRE 801(d)(2))
- § 1235: Prior inconsistent statement as substantive evidence (parallel to FRE 801(d)(1)(A) — notably, California does not require the prior statement to have been under oath for it to be substantive)
- § 1271: Business records (parallel to FRE 803(6))
- § 1280: Public employee records (parallel to FRE 803(8))

**Critical California difference from federal law:** Under Cal. Evid. Code § 1235, a prior inconsistent statement is substantive evidence regardless of whether it was made under oath. This is more permissive than FRE 801(d)(1)(A), which requires the prior statement to have been under oath. In California state proceedings, prior inconsistent statements in informal contexts (conversations, letters, texts) may be substantive evidence — not just impeachment.
