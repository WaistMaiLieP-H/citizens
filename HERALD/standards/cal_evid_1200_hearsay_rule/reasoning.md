# Layer 2 — Reasoning
## CAL_EVID_1200 | How California's Hearsay Rule Operates in Practice

**Standard ID:** CAL_EVID_1200
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The § 1200 / FRE 802 Parallel

California Evidence Code § 1200 and FRE 802 establish the same hearsay exclusion. The analytical framework is identical: (1) is it a statement? (2) made out of court? (3) offered for truth? If yes to all three — hearsay, inadmissible unless an exception applies.

The differences between California and federal hearsay law are in the exceptions, not the exclusionary rule itself.

---

## The Critical California Advantage — § 1235

California's most important hearsay exception for the steward's cases is § 1235. Unlike FRE 801(d)(1)(A), which requires the prior inconsistent statement to have been made under oath, § 1235 has no oath requirement. Any prior statement by any witness that contradicts their current testimony is substantive evidence in a California proceeding.

**Why this matters for the steward's cases:**
- Christina's statements to third parties, in informal communications, in letters — any that contradict her family law testimony are substantive evidence under § 1235
- Social worker verbal statements made outside of official reports that contradict the reports are § 1235 evidence
- APD dispatch communications that contradict the officer's report are § 1235 evidence in California criminal proceedings

---

## The Party Admission Exception — § 1220

Under Cal. Evid. Code § 1220, a statement is not hearsay if it is offered against the party who made it. This is California's parallel to FRE 801(d)(2). Same function, same analysis:
- Christina's prior declarations offered against Christina: party admission
- APD officer statements offered against the City of Antioch: party admission (employee/agent)
- CalVCB staff determination letters offered in the § 13959 appeal: offered against CalVCB (the opposing party in the appeal)

---

## The "Offered for Truth" Limitation — What Is Not Hearsay

The most important analytical move in hearsay law: if the statement is not offered for truth, it is not hearsay. California follows the same non-hearsay use framework as federal law:
- Effect on listener: not hearsay
- Verbal act: not hearsay
- Notice: not hearsay
- Impeachment only: not hearsay (but also not substantive evidence — distinguished from § 1235 substantive use)

---

## Relationship to the Family Law Record

The steward's 16-year family law record contains numerous out-of-court statements — in declarations, reports, and communications — that were made and are now offered in the context of post-conviction challenges and the § 1983 complaint. California § 1200 + § 1235 + § 1220 together provide the framework for analyzing each:

1. Is it a statement offered for truth? → § 1200 hearsay question
2. Is it a party admission? → § 1220 excludes from hearsay
3. Is it a prior inconsistent statement by a witness? → § 1235 makes it substantive
4. Does it fit another § 1220-1380 exception? → Admissible despite hearsay
