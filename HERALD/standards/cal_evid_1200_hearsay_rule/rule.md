## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

California Evidence Code § 1200:

> (a) "Hearsay evidence" is evidence of a statement that was made other than by a witness while testifying at the hearing and that is offered to prove the truth of the matter stated.
>
> (b) Except as provided by law, hearsay evidence is inadmissible.
>
> (c) This section shall be known and may be cited as the hearsay rule.

## What This Rule Does — Plain Language

California's hearsay rule. Identical in effect to FRE 802. An out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of what it asserts is hearsay and is inadmissible unless a statutory exception applies.

**The three elements of hearsay under § 1200(a):**
1. A **statement** (oral, written, or assertive conduct)
2. Made **other than** while testifying at the current hearing
3. Offered **to prove the truth** of what it asserts

**"Except as provided by law"** means: California has its own hearsay exceptions (§§ 1220–1380). Those exceptions are the paths to admissibility for out-of-court statements.

| § 1250 | Statement of mental or physical state | FRE 803(3) |
| § 1270 | Business records | FRE 803(6) |
| § 1280 | Public employee records | FRE 803(8) |
| § 1290 | Former testimony | FRE 804(b)(1) |

## Key California Difference from Federal Law

The most operationally significant difference: Cal. Evid. Code § 1235 makes prior inconsistent statements **substantive evidence** (not just impeachment) **without requiring the prior statement to have been under oath.** Under FRE 801(d)(1)(A), the prior statement must have been made under oath at an official proceeding to be substantive evidence. California removes that requirement.

**Practical effect for the steward's California cases:** Any prior out-of-court statement by an opposing party or witness that contradicts their current testimony — a text message, a letter, an informal conversation witnessed by the steward — is potentially substantive evidence in California court under § 1235. In federal court, only prior sworn statements carry that weight.
