## CAL_EVID_1200 | Cases Turned on California Hearsay Analysis

**Standard ID:** CAL_EVID_1200
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## Core Failure Mode

The same failure modes as the federal hearsay analysis (FRE_801_807 historical_loss.md) apply in California proceedings. The critical California-specific failure is the missed § 1235 opportunity — failing to recognize that a prior inconsistent informal statement is substantive evidence under California law when it would only be impeachment under federal law.

---

## California-Specific Failure Patterns

### 1. The missed § 1235 informal statement

**Pattern:** Witness testifies at family law hearing that certain events did not occur. Opposing party has a text message from the witness, sent before litigation, that contradicts the testimony. Counsel does not offer the text message as substantive evidence — just uses it for impeachment (attacking credibility). Court credits the live testimony; the text is not in evidence as proof of the prior statement's truth.

**The missed opportunity:** Under § 1235, the text message is substantive evidence — not just impeachment. A competent advocate offers it as evidence that the prior statement is true, not just as a credibility attack. Two different evidentiary effects; only one was used.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** Christina's communications over 16 years of litigation likely include pre-litigation and informal statements that contradict her hearing testimony. Each is potentially § 1235 substantive evidence in California proceedings.

---

### 2. The police report offered for truth — § 1200 problem in the criminal track

**Pattern:** In the steward's criminal case (04-23-01959), the prosecution may offer the unsigned APD report for the truth of its contents — asserting that events occurred as described. The report is an out-of-court statement offered for truth: § 1200 hearsay. It must qualify under a California exception:
- § 1280 (public employee records): available but subject to trustworthiness challenge for unsigned reports
- No officer testifying = no personal knowledge authentication

**The hearsay challenge:** If the officer who prepared the report does not testify, the report is hearsay with no valid exception if the trustworthiness challenge (§ 1280) succeeds. An unsigned report fails the "made in accordance with regular official duty" requirement of § 1280 because the signature is what certifies the officer's adoption of the contents.

---

### 3. The social worker report offered in custody proceedings

**Pattern:** Social worker report submitted in family law proceeding contains assessments and observations. It is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the assessments (that the conditions it describes existed). It must qualify under § 1200 exceptions:
- If the social worker testifies: report may be used to refresh recollection (§ 771) or as prior consistent statement
- If no live testimony: the report is hearsay; § 1280 may apply (public employee records) or it may be excluded

**Why it matters:** The custody evaluator and social worker reports in the steward's family law record may have been admitted without proper hearsay analysis — and without the steward's ability to challenge the authors' personal knowledge and methodology.

## Application Summary for California Proceedings

| Document | § 1200 Analysis | Exception Path |
|---|---|---|
| Christina's prior declarations | Hearsay if offered for truth | § 1220 (party admission) — offered against Christina |
| Christina's informal prior statements | Hearsay if offered for truth | § 1220 (party admission) if against her; § 1235 (prior inconsistent) if she's now a witness |
| APD Report #23-004494 | Hearsay if offered for truth | § 1280 (public employee records) — trustworthiness challenge available for unsigned report |
| OPD Reports 09-011438, 09-040089 | Hearsay if offered for truth | § 1280 — unsigned report trustworthiness challenge |
| Social worker/evaluator reports | Hearsay if offered for truth | § 1280 if made in regular official duty; otherwise requires author to testify |
| Treasury response letters | Hearsay if offered for truth in CA proceedings | § 1220 (party admission against Treasury as opposing party) or § 1280 |
| CalVCB determination letter | Hearsay for truth in § 13959 appeal | § 1280 (public employee records) — offered to show CalVCB made the determination, not for truth of the factual recitals |
