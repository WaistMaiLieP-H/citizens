## USC_28_1746 | Unsworn Declarations Under Penalty of Perjury

**Citizen Owner:** HERALD
**Filed:** 2026-04-12
**Status:** PROPOSED — requires steward review

---

## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

28 U.S.C. § 1746 (Pub. L. 94-550, § 1(a), Oct. 18, 1976, 90 Stat. 2534):

> Wherever, under any law of the United States or under any rule, regulation, order, or requirement made pursuant to law, any matter is required or permitted to be supported, evidenced, established, or proved by the sworn declaration, verification, certificate, statement, oath, or affidavit, in writing of the person making the same (other than a deposition, or an oath of office, or an oath required to be taken before a specified official other than a notary public), such matter may, with like force and effect, be supported, evidenced, established, or proved by the unsworn declaration, certificate, verification, or statement, in writing of such person which is subscribed by him, as true under penalty of perjury, and dated, in substantially the following form:
>
> (1) If executed without the United States: "I declare (or certify, verify, or state) under penalty of perjury under the laws of the United States of America that the foregoing is true and correct. Executed on (date). (Signature)".
>
> (2) If executed within the United States, its territories, possessions, or commonwealths: "I declare (or certify, verify, or state) under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct. Executed on (date). (Signature)".

---

## What This Rule Requires — Plain Language

**The rule:** A person filing a sworn affidavit in any federal proceeding may instead file a signed written declaration that says "I declare under penalty of perjury that the foregoing is true and correct. Executed on [date]. [Signature]." That declaration has the same legal force as a notarized oath.

**What it replaces:** The requirement for a notary public or other official to administer an oath. You do not need a notary. You do not need a witness. You need: (1) the magic words — "under penalty of perjury"; (2) the words "true and correct"; (3) a date; (4) your signature.

**What it does not replace:**
- Depositions (§ 1746 explicitly excludes depositions)
- Oaths of office
- Oaths required before a specified official other than a notary public

**"Substantially the following form" means:** The statute gives a form. Courts have held this means you must use the substance — "penalty of perjury," "true and correct," date, and signature — but you are not required to use the exact words. "True and accurate" has been accepted. "True and complete" has been accepted. "True" alone, without "correct," has been rejected in some circuits.

---

## The Five Elements (Every Declaration Must Have All Five)

| # | Element | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In writing | § 1746 (implied — "in writing of such person") |
| 2 | Subscribed under penalty of perjury | § 1746 ("subscribed by him, as true under penalty of perjury") |
| 3 | The words "true and correct" (or substantially similar) | § 1746 (forms (1) and (2)) |
| 4 | Date of execution | § 1746 ("Executed on (date)") |
| 5 | Signature | § 1746 ("(Signature)") |

Missing any single element = the declaration is defective and may be stricken.

---

## Jurisdiction

Federal: All federal courts, all federal agencies, all federal proceedings.
California state court: § 1746 does not apply directly in state court. California uses CCP § 2015.5 (see CAL_CCP_2015_5 standard). However, § 1746 declarations are used in § 1983 complaints and all federal filings in NDCA.
