# Layer 2 — Reasoning
## USC_28_1746 | Why This Standard Exists and How Courts Apply It

**Standard ID:** USC_28_1746
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## Why Congress Enacted § 1746

Before § 1746, federal law required a notarized affidavit for any sworn statement in a federal proceeding. This created access barriers: people in remote areas, people without transportation, pro se litigants, and people under financial strain could be blocked from filing time-sensitive declarations because they could not reach a notary. The 1976 enactment removed the notary requirement while preserving the perjury deterrent — the declarant is still subject to 18 U.S.C. § 1621 (perjury) and 18 U.S.C. § 1001 (false statements to federal agencies) for a false § 1746 declaration.

**The tradeoff:** Less procedural burden on the declarant. Same criminal exposure for a lie.

---

## How Courts Apply "Substantially the Following Form"

Courts split on what "substantial" compliance means:

**Permissive interpretations (majority):**
- "I declare under penalty of perjury that the above is true and accurate" — accepted (*Cobell v. Norton*, D.D.C.)
- "I declare under penalty of perjury under the laws of the United States that the foregoing is true and complete" — accepted (several district courts)
- Failure to include "under the laws of the United States" when executed within the U.S. — immaterial because § 1746(2) does not require that phrase

**Strict interpretations (minority):**
- "I swear under oath" without "penalty of perjury" — rejected; oath-language does not substitute for penalty-of-perjury language
- "I declare the foregoing to be true" without "under penalty of perjury" — rejected; the perjury deterrent phrase is not optional
- Declaration signed but not dated — rejected; date is a required element

**The circuit split on "true and correct" vs. "true and accurate":**
Most circuits accept "true and accurate" or "true and complete" as substantial compliance. A bare "I declare this is true" without "and correct" (or a substantial equivalent) has been rejected by some courts as insufficient. HERALD standard: always use "true and correct" — it is the exact statutory language and eliminates the risk.

---

## The "At the Time of Signing" Rule

A § 1746 declaration must be signed by the declarant. It must be dated **at the time of signing** — a declaration backdated after the fact is defective and potentially fraudulent. The date on the declaration is the legal execution date. In the steward's cases, document dating discrepancies are core evidence — HERALD applies this rule both ways: to the steward's own declarations (ensuring they are properly dated) and to opposing declarations (identifying back-dating as a defect and potential fraud indicator).

## What § 1746 Does Not Cover

| Excluded | Reason |
|---|---|
| Depositions | Explicitly excluded by statute — depositions have their own oath procedure under FRCP 30 |
| Oath of office | Constitutionally and statutorily distinct |
| Oaths before specified non-notary officials | E.g., oaths before a judge, magistrate, or immigration officer — the statute carves these out because those officials have independent authority to administer oaths |
| State court proceedings | § 1746 is federal law; California state court uses CCP § 2015.5 |

---

## Perjury Exposure

A false § 1746 declaration is a federal felony:

- **18 U.S.C. § 1621 (perjury):** Up to 5 years imprisonment for a willfully false statement in a proceeding where an oath is authorized by law.
- **18 U.S.C. § 1001 (false statements):** Up to 5 years for materially false statements in any matter within federal jurisdiction.

The perjury deterrent is the entire enforcement mechanism for § 1746. Because there is no notary and no official witness, the criminal exposure is the only external check on truthfulness. This is why the statute requires the exact phrase "under penalty of perjury" — courts will not imply perjury exposure from softer language.

## Relationship to Other Standards

| Standard | Relationship |
|---|---|
| CAL_CCP_2015_5 | California's parallel provision for state court declarations — same concept, different form |

| FRCP_56_C_4 | Summary judgment declarations — must comply with § 1746 AND must be made on personal knowledge |
| FRE_901 | Authentication — a § 1746 declaration is often the foundation used to authenticate documents under FRE 901(b)(1) |
