## Why § 780 Exists

Credibility determines the outcome of cases where physical evidence alone cannot establish the facts. In family law disputes, criminal cases, administrative appeals, and civil rights litigation, the factfinder must decide who to believe when accounts conflict. Section 780 gives the factfinder permission to use all available logic — every factor that rationally tends to show truthfulness or its opposite.

HERALD's primary function is to generate and document the factual record that allows a factfinder to apply § 780 to the opposing witnesses in the steward's cases. HERALD does not argue credibility — HERALD documents the predicate facts.

## The Prior Inconsistent Statement Factor — § 780(h) — The Bridge to § 1235

Section 780(h) (prior inconsistent statements) is the credibility foundation for California Evidence Code § 1235 (prior inconsistent statements as substantive evidence). The factors work together:

1. § 780(h): The prior inconsistent statement is relevant to the witness's credibility
2. § 1235: The prior inconsistent statement is also substantive evidence of the truth of its contents

Together, these mean that a witness who previously said one thing and now says another has: (a) compromised credibility (§ 780(h)) AND (b) created a competing version of the facts in evidence (§ 1235). This double effect makes prior inconsistent statements extremely powerful in the steward's cases.

**Application:** Christina's prior declarations and testimony in the family law proceedings that contradict her current position are both:
- Impeachment evidence under § 780(h)
- Substantive evidence under § 1235 (of the truth of what she said before)

## Capacity and Opportunity — § 780(c) and (d) — The Personal Knowledge Predicate

Section 780(c) (capacity to perceive) and (d) (opportunity to perceive) are the credibility analogs of FRE 602 (personal knowledge). They ask:
- Could this person have perceived what they claim to have perceived?
- Were they actually in a position to know this?

**Application to unsigned reports:** The unsigned OPD and APD reports describe events. § 780(d) asks: was the reporting officer actually present for all events described in the report? Reports that include secondhand accounts (what Christina told the officer; what dispatch relayed) are making claims about events the officer did not personally perceive. The officer's capacity and opportunity to testify to those events is limited by what the officer actually observed.

## Federal Credibility Parallel

Federal courts use a similar credibility framework under FRE 607-610:
- FRE 607: Any party may attack a witness's credibility
- FRE 608: Evidence of character for truthfulness or untruthfulness
- FRE 609: Prior criminal convictions to attack credibility
- FRE 613: Prior inconsistent statements

California § 780 is broader — it is a single omnibus credibility statute that includes all these factors and more.
