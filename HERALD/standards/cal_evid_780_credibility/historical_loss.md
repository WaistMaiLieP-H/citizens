## The Credibility Battleground in the Steward's Cases

The steward's cases are, at their core, credibility contests. The steward's account of events in the family law proceedings, the June 16, 2023 arrest, the Honeysuckle closing, and the communication fraud is directly disputed by opposing parties. The factfinder must decide who to believe. Section 780 provides the framework for making that decision — and HERALD's job is to document every § 780 factor that supports the steward's credibility and undermines the opposing witnesses'.

## Documented Failure Patterns

### 1. Undocumented bias — the unmade § 780(f) argument

**Pattern:** A custody case turns on the credibility of a social worker whose report is the only evidence of alleged neglect. The parent's attorney fails to develop or present any evidence of the social worker's potential bias — their relationship with the opposing attorney, their history of findings in cases with similar facts, their professional incentives. The court credits the report. Custody is modified against the parent.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** HERALD has documented that Sala Ajaniku (2010 mediator) has no credentials in any public database. A zero-credential mediator whose recommendation changed the custody structure in 2010 is a § 780(f) bias issue — what relationship, incentive, or interest led to that recommendation? The absence of credentials is itself a factor under § 780(c) (capacity to perform the role assigned to her). This was not raised in 2010; it must be raised now as part of the challenge to the family law record.

---

### 2. The prior inconsistent statement left on the table — § 780(h) unused

**Pattern:** A witness at a family law hearing gives testimony that contradicts their prior declaration filed in the same case. Opposing counsel fails to draw the court's attention to the § 780(h) inconsistency. The court credits the live testimony without awareness of the prior statement.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** The family law record spans 16 years. HERALD has identified inconsistencies between Christina's prior declarations and the documented record. Each inconsistency is a § 780(h) credibility factor. Each prior sworn statement that contradicts current testimony is also § 1235 substantive evidence. These must be surfaced systematically — not incidentally, but as a structured component of any further family law or appellate proceeding.

---

### 3. The credibility collapse via § 780(i) — the corroborated contradiction

**Pattern:** A witness testifies that a certain event did not happen. Opposing party has documentary evidence that it did. The court applies § 780(i) — the existence of facts contradicting the testimony — and finds the witness not credible on all related points.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** HERALD's Treasury contradictions map documents four responses to two inquiries that are internally inconsistent. Each response is either: (a) a false statement about the account status, or (b) a correct statement that contradicts the others. A factfinder applying § 780(i) to the Treasury witness will confront a record where the agency's own documents cannot all be true. That is a § 780(i) credibility collapse for the Treasury's position.

---

### 4. The undisclosed interest — § 780(f) in the CalVCB context

**Pattern:** CalVCB staff denies an application based on a "single source" determination — the police report says the crime did not happen in the manner described by the victim. The staff's reliance on the police report without independent investigation is a § 780(f) factor — the agency's institutional interest in managing claim volume creates a structural bias toward denial of close calls. This is not individual staff misconduct; it is systemic bias documented by the pattern of decisions.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** HERALD's CalVCB audit documented 13 FAIL findings. The § 780(f) analysis of CalVCB's institutional bias in applying shortened deadlines and single-source denial is not a direct credibility argument (CalVCB staff are not testifying witnesses at the § 13959 appeal in the same way). But the § 780 framework informs the credibility of the CalVCB determination letter as a public record — a record prepared by an institution with potential bias is less entitled to FRE 803(8) / Cal. Evid. Code § 1280 trustworthiness.

---

### 5. The credibility of the steward — building it proactively

**Pattern (positive):** Pro se plaintiff in a § 1983 case establishes their own credibility through: consistent contemporaneous documentation, prior consistent statements, and detailed personal knowledge testimony that an adversary cannot contradict without attacking the physical record.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** The steward's credibility rests on:
- § 780(g): Prior consistent statements — the steward's contemporaneous records (phone records, scan dates, CalVCB submission dates, Honeysuckle closing documents) corroborate the steward's account at every point
- § 780(d): Opportunity to perceive — the steward was present for the June 16, 2023 events; was the party to the Honeysuckle closing; was the account holder for the phone lines; was the parent at every custody exchange
- § 780(c): Capacity to recollect — the steward's documented record (scan logs, filing dates, correspondence) shows specific, accurate recall, not vague impressions
- § 780(f) (anti-bias): The steward has no institutional interest in any outcome other than the truth of what happened — in contrast to opposing parties with direct financial and legal stakes in the outcome

HERALD documents all four as a structural credibility posture.
