## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

California Evidence Code § 780:

> Except as otherwise provided by statute, the court or jury may consider in determining the credibility of a witness any matter that has any tendency in reason to prove or disprove the truthfulness of his testimony at the hearing, including but not limited to any of the following:
>
> (a) His demeanor while testifying and the manner in which he testifies.
>
> (b) The character of his testimony.
>
> (c) The extent of his capacity to perceive, to recollect, or to communicate any matter about which he testifies.
>
> (d) The extent of his opportunity to perceive any matter about which he testifies.
>
> (e) His character for honesty or veracity or their opposites.
>
> (f) The existence or nonexistence of a bias, interest, or other motive.
>
> (g) A statement previously made by him that is consistent with his testimony at the hearing.
>
> (h) A statement made by him that is inconsistent with any part of his testimony at the hearing.
>
> (i) The existence or nonexistence of any fact testified to by him.
>
> (j) His attitude toward the action in which he testifies or toward the giving of testimony.
>
> (k) His admission of untruthfulness.

## What This Rule Does — Plain Language

**The rule:** In any California court proceeding, the factfinder (court or jury) may use any of the listed factors — or any other logically relevant matter — to decide whether a witness is telling the truth. The list is illustrative, not exhaustive.

**The standard:** "Any matter that has any tendency in reason to prove or disprove the truthfulness of his testimony." This is the California relevance standard applied to credibility — if it logically tends to show the witness is or is not truthful, it is relevant to credibility.

**What it enables:** Every § 780 factor is a tool HERALD can use to evaluate the credibility of opposing witnesses in the steward's cases AND to build and document the steward's own credibility posture.

## The Eleven Credibility Factors

| Factor | Text | HERALD's Use |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Demeanor while testifying | Observe and document opposing witness behavior at hearings |
| (b) | Character of testimony | Internal consistency; specificity vs. vagueness; unusual memory for convenient facts |
| (c) | Capacity to perceive, recollect, communicate | Was the witness actually in a position to know what they say they know? |
| (d) | Opportunity to perceive | Was the witness present? Did they have a clear view/hearing? |
| (e) | Character for honesty or veracity | Prior dishonest acts, prior inconsistent statements, reputation for truthfulness |
| (f) | Bias, interest, or motive | Does the witness gain from this outcome? Is there a relationship affecting objectivity? |
| (g) | Prior consistent statement | A prior statement consistent with testimony — rehabilitates credibility |
| (h) | Prior inconsistent statement | A prior statement inconsistent with testimony — impeaches credibility (and under § 1235, is substantive evidence) |
| (i) | Existence or nonexistence of testified facts | Corroborating or contradicting evidence about what the witness claims happened |
| (j) | Attitude toward the action or giving of testimony | Reluctance, advocacy, hostility, investment in outcome |
| (k) | Admission of untruthfulness | Prior admissions of lying |
