# Layer 1 — Rule
## FRE_602 | Personal Knowledge Requirement — Witness Testimony

**Standard ID:** FRE_602
**Popular Name:** Personal knowledge requirement — witness testimony
**Citizen Owner:** HERALD
**Filed:** 2026-04-12
**Status:** PROPOSED — requires steward review

---

## Statutory Text (Verbatim)

Federal Rule of Evidence 602:

> A witness may testify to a matter only if evidence is introduced sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge of the matter. Evidence to prove personal knowledge may consist of the witness's own testimony. This rule does not apply to a witness's expert testimony under Rule 703.

*(Pub. L. 93–595, § 1, Jan. 2, 1975, 88 Stat. 1934; amended Oct. 1, 1987; Nov. 1, 1988; Dec. 1, 2011.)*

---

## What This Rule Requires — Plain Language

**The rule:** A witness — including a declarant in a § 1746 declaration — can only testify to things they actually know from their own perception. You can testify to what you saw, heard, felt, smelled, or otherwise personally experienced. You cannot testify to what you were told, what you assume, what you believe, or what someone else experienced — unless those things are themselves within your personal knowledge.

**"Sufficient to support a finding" standard:** The court does not require the witness to prove personal knowledge beyond doubt. The threshold is low — enough foundation that a reasonable factfinder could conclude the witness had personal knowledge. The witness's own testimony can satisfy this.

**The expert exception:** FRE 703 allows expert witnesses to base opinions on facts they were not personally present to observe (e.g., medical records, lab reports). FRE 602 does not apply to that expert-opinion testimony. It does apply to lay witnesses and lay declarations.

---

## The Three-Part Test

| # | Question | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Was the witness present to perceive the event? | Must have been there — or received the sensory input directly |
| 2 | Did the witness actually perceive it? | Presence alone is not enough — the witness must have observed/heard/sensed the specific matter |
| 3 | Is there evidence sufficient to support a finding of personal knowledge? | Can be the witness's own testimony; no corroboration required at threshold stage |

---

## What Does NOT Satisfy FRE 602

| Statement Type | Problem |
|---|---|
| "I believe defendant intended to..." | Belief about mental state of another = not personal knowledge of the mental state |
| "I was told by X that..." | Hearsay — personal knowledge of the statement, not of the underlying facts (see FRE 801-807) |
| "Based on information and belief..." | "Information and belief" is the opposite of personal knowledge; this phrase disqualifies the statement |
| "It is my understanding that..." | Understanding derived from sources, not direct observation — not personal knowledge |
| "Records indicate that..." | Records are not the witness's personal knowledge; authentication (FRE 901) + a records exception (FRE 803(6)) is the correct path |

---

## Application to § 1746 and § 2015.5 Declarations

FRE 602 governs the **content** of declarations, not just live testimony. A declaration submitted under 28 U.S.C. § 1746 or CCP § 2015.5:
- Satisfies the **form** requirement of § 1746 or § 2015.5
- Must also satisfy the **content** requirement of FRE 602 (or Cal. Evid. Code § 702 in state court)
- Any sentence in the declaration asserting a fact the declarant did not personally perceive is inadmissible and will be stricken on objection

**HERALD drafting rule:** Every factual sentence in a declaration must be preceded (at least in the drafting review) by the question: "Was this personally observed or experienced by the declarant?" If yes — write it. If no — find the correct vehicle (authenticated record, expert opinion, etc.).
