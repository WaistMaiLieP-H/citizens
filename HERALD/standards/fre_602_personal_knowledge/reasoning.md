# Layer 2 — Reasoning
## FRE_602 | Why Personal Knowledge Is the Foundation of All Testimony

**Standard ID:** FRE_602
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## Why the Personal Knowledge Requirement Exists

FRE 602 codifies the oldest principle in the law of evidence: a witness should speak only to what they know. The rule exists because testimony about facts the witness did not personally observe is unreliable — it is secondhand at best, fabricated at worst. The factfinder cannot evaluate the credibility of testimony about events the witness did not witness, because the testimony is actually about what someone told the witness, filtered through memory, interpretation, and potentially self-interest.

The requirement serves two functions simultaneously:
1. **Reliability gate:** It keeps unreliable testimony out of evidence.
2. **Accountability mechanism:** A witness who testifies to personal knowledge exposes themselves to perjury liability for false testimony. A witness who says "I was told" faces no perjury risk for the underlying fact — they can simply say they were mistaken about what they heard.

---

## "Information and Belief" — The Poison Phrase

The phrase "on information and belief" is the most common violation of FRE 602 in declarations. It means exactly what it says: the declarant has information (from some external source) and believes it. It does NOT mean the declarant personally knows it.

Courts routinely strike "information and belief" statements from declarations on summary judgment and other proceedings. The phrase is a signal that the declarant lacks personal knowledge of the fact being asserted.

**Where this matters in the steward's cases:** Any declaration from an opposing party that contains "on information and belief" assertions about facts they claim support their position — HERALD flags those statements as potentially inadmissible. The lack of personal knowledge is a foundation defect, not a credibility argument.

---

## Personal Knowledge Can Be Established by the Witness's Own Testimony

The rule explicitly allows personal knowledge to be proven by the witness's own testimony — "I saw it," "I was there," "I heard it." No corroboration is required at the threshold stage. This is important for the steward's pro se situation: the steward does not need a second witness to establish the steward's personal knowledge of events the steward directly experienced.

**The steward's core personal knowledge base:**
- The events of June 16, 2023 — the steward was present; all events within personal knowledge
- The phone account history — the steward was the account holder; personal knowledge of account access, changes, unauthorized activity
- The Honeysuckle escrow closing — the steward signed documents; personal knowledge of what was and was not signed, what was and was not present
- The CalVCB application process — the steward submitted the application; personal knowledge of dates, submissions, responses received
- The Treasury inquiry responses — the steward received the letters; personal knowledge of what they said

## The Hearsay Intersection

FRE 602 and the hearsay rules (FRE 801-807) work together but are distinct:

| Situation | FRE 602 Issue | Hearsay Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Witness says: "I saw defendant punch the plaintiff" | No FRE 602 problem — personally observed | No hearsay — not an out-of-court statement |
| Witness says: "Defendant told me he punched the plaintiff" | Personal knowledge of the statement? Yes, if witness heard it. | Hearsay — out-of-court statement offered for truth of the matter (that defendant punched plaintiff) |
| Witness says: "I heard defendant was violent" | FRE 602 problem — no personal knowledge of the underlying claim | Hearsay — out-of-court statement offered for truth |
| Witness says: "The police report says defendant was arrested" | FRE 602 problem unless witness read the report | Hearsay — out-of-court document offered for truth; needs records exception |

**HERALD's rule for declarations:** A declarant has personal knowledge of a statement they directly heard. A declarant does NOT have personal knowledge of the truth of the out-of-court statement's content unless they independently observed the underlying facts.

---

## The "Lay vs. Expert" Distinction at FRE 602's Border

FRE 602 expressly carves out expert testimony under FRE 703. Expert witnesses can base opinions on facts that were not personally observed — laboratory results, medical records, police reports, hypothetical scenarios. Lay witnesses cannot.

In the steward's cases, all factual declarations are lay witness declarations (the steward testifying to what the steward personally experienced). Expert opinions — medical causation for SIRVA, forensic document examination for the unsigned reports — require a separate expert declaration under FRE 702/703. FRE 602 does not block expert testimony, but it does mean the steward cannot personally declare about medical causation or document forensics in a lay declaration.

---

## California Parallel — Cal. Evid. Code § 702

California's parallel to FRE 602 is Evidence Code § 702:

> "Subject to Section 801, the testimony of a witness concerning a particular matter is inadmissible unless he has personal knowledge of the matter."

Same rule. Same principle. For California state proceedings (CalVCB appeal, criminal case, family law), § 702 governs; for federal proceedings (§ 1983 NDCA), FRE 602 governs. HERALD treats them identically in practice — the personal knowledge requirement is the same in both jurisdictions.
