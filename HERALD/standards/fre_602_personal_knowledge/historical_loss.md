# Layer 3 — Historical Loss
## FRE_602 | Cases Lost or Damaged by Personal Knowledge Failures

**Standard ID:** FRE_602
**Filed:** 2026-04-12

---

## The Core Failure Mode

A declaration that lacks personal knowledge foundation for its factual assertions is defective. When the defective portions are stricken, the declaration may no longer support the motion or position it was meant to support. In summary judgment practice — the most common context for § 1746/FRE 602 interaction — a gutted declaration means the moving party has no admissible evidence, and the motion fails.

---

## Documented Failure Patterns

### 1. The "information and belief" summary judgment defeat

**Pattern:** Plaintiff in a civil rights case submits a declaration in opposition to summary judgment containing several paragraphs that begin "Upon information and belief, defendants were aware of..." or "It is my understanding that the policy required..." Defendant moves to strike. Court grants strike. Plaintiff's opposition has no admissible factual assertions. Summary judgment granted for defendant.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** The § 1983 complaint will face a summary judgment motion at some point. Every factual assertion in every opposing declaration must be grounded in personal knowledge. HERALD's declarations for the steward will contain only personally observed facts — events the steward was present for, documents the steward received, statements the steward directly heard.

---

### 2. The pro se declaration with inadmissible hearsay mixed in

**Pattern:** Pro se litigant submits a declaration that mixes personal knowledge ("I was present at the meeting and heard the officer say...") with hearsay ("My attorney told me the department had a policy of...") and conclusions ("The officers acted with malice"). Court strikes the hearsay and the legal conclusions. The personal knowledge portions survive but are insufficient for the relief sought.

**Teaching point:** Personal knowledge testimony survives. The rest is stripped. HERALD drafts declarations with clear demarcation between: (1) personally observed facts [preserved], (2) statements the declarant directly heard being made [FRE 602 OK — personal knowledge of the making of the statement], and (3) opinions about mental states or motivations [only if lay opinion under FRE 701].

---

### 3. The Celotex framework — no personal knowledge = no genuine dispute

**Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986):** The Supreme Court established that on summary judgment, a party cannot create a genuine dispute of material fact by pointing to allegations in the complaint or submitting declarations that lack admissible evidentiary support. A declaration asserting facts without personal knowledge foundation does not create a genuine dispute.

**Why it matters:** This is the foundational case for why FRE 602 compliance in declarations is outcome-determinative on summary judgment. A perfectly § 1746-compliant declaration that violates FRE 602 provides zero evidentiary support under Celotex.

---

### 4. The police report problem — FRE 602 applied to opposing evidence

**Pattern:** Defendant in a § 1983 case relies on a police report to establish facts about the plaintiff. Plaintiff challenges the report — the officer who wrote the report is not testifying, and the report contains statements by the officer that are not within any exception to the personal knowledge requirement because the officer was not present for all events described. Court limits the admissibility of the report to what the officer personally observed.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases offensively:** The APD report #23-004494 (June 16, 2023) contains statements that are themselves the subject of HERALD's witnessed chronology. To the extent the report describes events the officer did not personally observe — statements of other officers, dispatch reports, information provided by Christina — those portions are not personal knowledge testimony and face admissibility challenges. HERALD's chronology documents what the officer actually witnessed vs. what was reported secondhand.

---

### 5. The custody evaluator report without personal knowledge foundation

**Pattern:** A custody evaluator submits a report containing opinions and factual assertions, some of which are based on statements made by one parent (the opposing party) in a private interview — not personal observation by the evaluator. Court finds portions of the report lack personal knowledge foundation.

**Why it matters for the steward's cases:** Social worker and custody evaluator reports in the steward's family law record contain assertions that were not within the evaluator's personal knowledge — they were based on interviews with Christina and with supervised third parties. Under FRE 602 (or Cal. Evid. Code § 702), those portions are not personal knowledge testimony. HERALD flags this as a foundation challenge to the custody evaluation reports in the steward's family law record.
