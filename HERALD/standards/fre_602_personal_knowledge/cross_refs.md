## FRCP 56(c)(4) — Explicit Codification

FRCP 56(c)(4) states:

> "An affidavit or declaration used to support or oppose a motion must be made on personal knowledge, set out facts that would be admissible in evidence, and show that the affiant or declarant is competent to testify on the matters stated."

Three requirements, all rooted in FRE 602:
1. **Personal knowledge** — FRE 602
2. **Admissible in evidence** — FRE 602 + hearsay rules + authentication
3. **Competent to testify** — FRE 601 (competency) + FRE 602

## The Boundaries — What the Steward CANNOT Personally Declare

HERALD flags the following as requiring a different evidentiary vehicle than a lay personal-knowledge declaration:

| Fact to Prove | Why Not Personal Knowledge | Correct Vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| Christina's mental state or intent | Not within steward's personal observation | Circumstantial evidence; lay opinion under FRE 701 about observed behavior |
| Medical causation for SIRVA | Requires expert opinion (FRE 702/703) | Expert declaration from qualified medical professional |
| Forensic document analysis (signature comparison, date manipulation) | Requires expert opinion | Document examiner expert declaration |
| What happened at events the steward was not present for | No personal knowledge | Other witnesses; documentary evidence with proper authentication |
| Whether a criminal defendant "knowingly" acted | Mental state inference | Circumstantial evidence; jury inference |
