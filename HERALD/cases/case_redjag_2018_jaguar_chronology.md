### V. CONTRADICTIONS MAP

**Contradiction 1 — CARFAX VIN mismatch**
The CARFAX provided at point of sale (page 017) is for VIN SAJBF4BV8HCY33371 (2017 Jaguar XF). The vehicle sold bears VIN SAJAM4FV6JCP17944 (2018 Jaguar XE). These are different vehicles. The CARFAX provided cannot and does not describe the vehicle purchased.

**Contradiction 2 — Lender identity discrepancy**
Dealer documents at sale (3/17/2024) name American Credit Acceptance LLC as lienholder and loss payee. DMV registration (processed ~05/01/2024) names AMERICREDIT FINL SVCS INC as lienholder. These are different legal entities. No document in the file explains when or why the lender changed.

**Contradiction 3 — Odometer inconsistency in insurance agreements**
Three Agreement to Furnish Insurance forms signed the same day (3/17/2024) state different odometer readings: 39,900 / 39,900 / 39,000 miles. At least one figure is wrong. The AUTEL scan on 9/10/2024 shows 62,564 miles — meaning approximately 22,564–23,564 miles were added in six months.

**Contradiction 4 — "No repairs promised" vs. three-day free repair**
The Due Bill (page 016) states "No repairs promised," signed 3/17/2024. Three days later, AVA Automotive at the same address performed a motor vehicle inspection and fuse replacement at $0 charge. The suppression of the cost and the same-address repair location suggests the dealer was aware of the condition.

**Contradiction 5 — Denial letter timing (yo-yo)**
The vehicle was delivered 3/17/2024. An AmeriCredit denial letter was issued 4/8/2024 — 22 days later. The buyer had possession of the vehicle with no finalized financing for 22 days.

**Contradiction 6 — Spare tire in road position**
The vehicle was sold for road use. Les Schwab found a spare tire installed in the left-front road tire position 60 days after purchase. No disclosure of this condition appears in any sale document.

**Contradiction 7 — "Stolen/Scrapped" on PCM and TCM**
The Buyer's Guide and sale disclosures do not mention that the vehicle's powertrain control module or transmission control module may be from a stolen or scrapped vehicle. The AUTEL diagnostic report notation (page 053) raises this as a finding.
