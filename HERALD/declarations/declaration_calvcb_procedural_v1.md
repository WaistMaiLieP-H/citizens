I, Michael Vernen Thomas Hartmann, declare as follows:

**1.** I make this declaration based on my personal knowledge. I am competent to testify to the matters stated herein. I reside in Benicia, California.

**2.** I am the claimant in California Victim Compensation Board Application Number A25-10117946.

**3.** On or about [STEWARD: state the date you submitted Application A25-10117946], I submitted an application for victim compensation to the California Victim Compensation Board. That application was submitted in connection with the incident on June 16, 2023, documented in Antioch Police Department Case Report #23-004494, Event #23-033556.

**4.** On August 22, 2025, I received a letter from CalVCB, Form 04015, acknowledging receipt of Application A25-10117946. That letter identified me as the claimant and listed the application number. The "DATE OF CRIME" field in that letter was blank. CalVCB accepted and acknowledged my application without recording the crime date.

**5.** On August 26, 2025, I received two letters from CalVCB issued on the same day. The first, Form 04016, stated that my application was incomplete and requested a Crime Report. That letter gave me ten days to submit the Crime Report. The crime date used in Form 04016 was October 20, 2007. October 20, 2007 is not the date of the crime I reported. The incident I reported occurred on June 16, 2023.

**6.** The second letter received August 26, 2025, Form 04037, denied my emergency payment request. It also used October 20, 2007 as the crime date.

**7.** California Government Code § 13952(c)(2) provides thirty calendar days to respond to a notice of incomplete application. CalVCB's Form 04016 gave me ten days. CalVCB shortened the statutory response period by twenty days.

**8.** [STEWARD: Did you receive Form 04016 promptly, or was there any delay? When did you actually receive it? Did you submit a Crime Report in response? If so, on what date and through what method? Specifically: the online police report system generated three submission records — T25000989 (submitted), T25000995 (submitted and then rejected), and 25-0200794 (approved). Describe what each submission was and what happened with T25000995.]

**9.** On October 8, 2025, I received a denial letter, Form 04036. That letter stated my application was denied because documentation from Antioch Police Department "does not provide sufficient information to verify the victim sustained physical injury as a direct result of the crime." The crime date used in the denial letter was June 16, 2018. June 16, 2018 is not the date of the crime I reported. The crime I reported occurred on June 16, 2023 — five years after the date CalVCB used.

**10.** Across all CalVCB correspondence on Application A25-10117946, the crime date changed three times without explanation: it was blank in the acceptance letter, then October 20, 2007 in the August 26 letters, then June 16, 2018 in the denial. No CalVCB letter ever acknowledged this change or asked me to confirm the crime date.

**11.** I was never asked by CalVCB to supplement my application with photographs, medical records, emergency room records, or any documentation other than the Crime Report. The denial letter stated the denial was based solely on what Antioch Police Department provided. Antioch Police Department Case Report #23-004494 states that Officer Neal photographed injuries and uploaded those photographs to Evidence.com. I was never informed that those photographs were relevant to my application or asked to obtain them.

**12.** [STEWARD: Were you ever contacted by any CalVCB staff member by telephone, email, or in person regarding this application? Were you ever referred to, or offered assistance from, a Victim Witness Assistance Center? Were you ever told about your right to assistance in preparing or supplementing this application?]

**13.** At no point in the twelve pages of CalVCB correspondence I received did any letter identify by name the individual who made the determination at any stage of the process — acceptance, incomplete designation, emergency denial, denial, or Board denial. Every letter was signed by "Customer Service Representative / Victim Compensation Program" or "CalVCB Representative / Appeals Section." No individual at CalVCB was identified as responsible for any outcome.

**14.** On October 8, 2025, along with the denial letter, I received a pre-printed appeal form, Form 06045. My name on that form is misspelled: "Michael Vernen Thomas Hartman." The final "n" in Hartmann is absent. My legal name is Michael Vernen Thomas Hartmann. The form states that the claimant must sign under a certification of truthfulness — but the name on the form does not match my legal name.

**15.** On December 3, 2025, I received a Board denial letter, Form 04435. That letter stated the Board accepted the staff recommendation to deny the application. The crime date in the Board denial was also June 16, 2018 — the same incorrect date used in the October 8, 2025 denial. The Board confirmed the denial of an application evaluated against a crime that did not occur on June 16, 2018. The Board confirmed a denial of a 2018 crime. My application was for a 2023 crime.

**16.** The Board denial stated that I could request reconsideration within sixty days of December 3, 2025. The denial that triggered the reconsideration clock was a denial based on the wrong crime date. A claimant whose application is denied based on the wrong date cannot reasonably be expected to respond to a reconsideration deadline that flows from that wrong-date denial. The denial did not put me on notice that my actual claim — the June 16, 2023 incident — had been evaluated at all. To the extent the sixty-day window has passed, equitable tolling applies because the denial did not accurately describe the decision being made.

**17.** [STEWARD: State what steps you took after receiving the December 3, 2025 Board denial. Did you file any request for reconsideration? Did you contact CalVCB? Did you seek assistance from any advocate or attorney?]
