# Context — Pitchess v. Superior Court / Peace Officer Personnel Records Discovery

## The doctrine in brief

Pitchess v. Superior Court (1974) established that a criminal defendant has a qualified right to discover relevant information from peace officer personnel files — prior complaints of misconduct, excessive force, dishonesty, or fabrication of evidence — when those prior incidents are relevant to the defense.

The doctrine was codified in California Evidence Code §§ 1043-1047 (effective 1978). The procedure has three stages:

**Stage 1 — Written motion with good cause showing (Evid. Code § 1043):**
The moving party files a motion with affidavits establishing:
- The identity of the officer and the records sought
- Good cause — specific facts showing the records are material to the defense
- The relevance of the specific categories sought (excessive force, dishonesty, fabricating reports, etc.)

The good cause showing must be specific, not conclusory. Alleging generally that the officer may have prior complaints is insufficient; the showing must identify a specific defense theory to which the records are material.

**Stage 2 — In camera review by the court (Evid. Code § 1045):**
If the court finds good cause, the personnel file is produced to the court for in camera review — outside the presence of either party. The court reviews the file and identifies what, if anything, should be disclosed.

**Stage 3 — Limited disclosure:**
The court discloses to the defense only what is directly relevant to the stated defense theory. The disclosure is typically limited to the officer's name, the date of the prior incident, and a brief description — not the full internal affairs file.

## Post-SB 1421 — reduced scope of Pitchess motions

SB 1421 (Stats. 2018, Ch. 988, effective January 1, 2019) amended Penal Code § 832.7 to require public disclosure of records relating to:
- Sustained findings of excessive force involving death or great bodily injury
- Sustained findings of dishonesty, false statements, or fabricating evidence
- Sustained findings of sexual assault

**These categories are now publicly accessible through PRA requests — a Pitchess motion is no longer needed to access them.** Pitchess motions are now most important for:
- Non-sustained complaints (allegations that were not found sustained)
- Complaints involving use of force below the § 832.7 disclosure threshold
- Older records from before SB 1421's effective date where files may not have been fully disclosed
- Any category of complaint not covered by § 832.7's mandatory disclosure list

## The Brady-Pitchess sequence

Brady requires the prosecution to disclose impeachment evidence it possesses. Pitchess provides the mechanism to access officer personnel records that may contain Brady material. The operational sequence:

1. Pitchess motion → in camera review → disclosure of relevant prior complaints
2. Brady review — any disclosed Pitchess material that qualifies as Brady/Giglio impeachment material must be disclosed to the defense by the prosecution

Where the prosecution has access to the personnel file but fails to disclose Brady-material records within it, the Pitchess mechanism and the Brady obligation overlap.

## Application to the steward's cases

**APD officers — June 16, 2023:** For any officer involved in the June 16, 2023 incident, a Pitchess motion seeking prior complaints of dishonesty, fabrication of reports, or false testimony would access the full history of complaints — including non-sustained complaints that SB 1421 PRA requests would not reach. The good cause showing: the defense theory that the incident was prearranged and that the officers participated in or facilitated a setup is a specific factual basis for seeking prior complaints of similar conduct.

**OPD officers — any prior contacts:** Pitchess motions for OPD officers involved in any incident where a purported report has been questioned can seek prior complaints of falsifying or fabricating reports — directly material to the authentication challenge.

**Calibration:** Post-SB 1421, start with PRA requests for § 832.7 mandatory-disclosure categories. Use Pitchess for what the PRA cannot reach.
