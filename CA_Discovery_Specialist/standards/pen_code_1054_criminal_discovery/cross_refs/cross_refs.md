# California Criminal Discovery — Chapter 10 — Cross-References

| Standard | Relationship | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| BRADY_V_MARYLAND | IMPLEMENTS | Chapter 10 codifies and extends Brady's constitutional disclosure requirement; Brady is the constitutional floor Chapter 10 cannot fall below; § 1054.1(e) (exculpatory evidence) is the statutory implementation of Brady |
| PEN_832_7_PEACE_OFFICER_RECORDS | SUPPLEMENTS | A peace officer's sustained dishonesty finding (disclosable under § 832.7(b)(1)(C)) is Giglio material — constitutional Brady/Giglio obligation requires prosecution disclosure under § 1054.1(e) independent of Chapter 10's categories |
| GOV_7923_600_CPRA_LE_EXEMPTION | SUPPLEMENTS | CPRA governs public access to LE investigative records; Chapter 10 governs discovery in a pending criminal case; a § 7923.600-exempt record may be discoverable in a criminal case if the prosecution possesses it and it is material |
| CCP_2023_SPOLIATION_SANCTIONS | CROSS_DOMAIN | § 1054.5 provides the criminal enforcement mechanism (preclusion, contempt, continuance); § 2023 provides the civil discovery enforcement mechanism; destruction of evidence by law enforcement implicates § 1054 in criminal cases and § 2023 in parallel civil actions |
| PITCHESS_V_SUPERIOR_COURT | SUPPLEMENTS | Pitchess (Evid. Code § 1043) provides a separate discovery mechanism for peace officer personnel records in pending criminal cases — operates alongside Chapter 10, not within it (§ 832.7(h) confirms Chapter 10 does not supersede Pitchess) |
