# California Criminal Discovery — Chapter 10 — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Pen. Code § 1054–1054.10
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** June 5, 1990 (Proposition 115 — Crime Victims Justice Reform Act)

## The Rule

Chapter 10 of the Penal Code (§§ 1054–1054.10) is the exclusive mechanism for discovery in California criminal cases. No court may order criminal discovery beyond what Chapter 10 authorizes, except as required by the U.S. Constitution (§ 1054.5(a)).

The prosecution must disclose to the defense (§ 1054.1): the names and addresses of all trial witnesses; all defendant statements; all real evidence obtained in the investigation; the existence of felony convictions of any material witness; any exculpatory evidence; and all written or recorded statements of witnesses the prosecution intends to call, including expert reports and scientific test results.

The defense must disclose to the prosecution (§ 1054.3): names and addresses of defense witnesses (other than the defendant); relevant written or recorded statements of those witnesses; any real evidence the defense intends to offer; and expert reports and scientific test results the defense intends to offer.

Both sides must make disclosure at least 30 days before trial, or earlier if ordered by the court (§ 1054.7). The duty to disclose is ongoing — newly discovered material must be disclosed immediately.

Enforcement (§ 1054.5): before seeking court enforcement, the requesting party must first make an informal request and give opposing counsel 15 days to comply. If the 15-day period passes without compliance, the court may order immediate disclosure, contempt, evidence preclusion, continuance, or any other lawful order. Dismissal is available only when required by the U.S. Constitution.

## Key Elements

1. **Exclusivity** (§ 1054.5(a)): Chapter 10 is the ONLY source of criminal discovery rights in California. Courts cannot create discovery obligations outside it except as constitutionally compelled.
2. **Reciprocal disclosure**: Both prosecution (§ 1054.1) and defense (§ 1054.3) have mandatory disclosure obligations — mutual discovery replaced one-way Brady compliance.
3. **30-day advance disclosure** (§ 1054.7): Discovery must occur before trial, not on the day of testimony. Ongoing duty to supplement.
4. **Informal-first enforcement** (§ 1054.5(b)): Court cannot be sought until informal request + 15-day cure period has been attempted.

## What This Means in Practice

If a prosecutor has exculpatory evidence — including evidence bearing on officer credibility — Chapter 10 compels disclosure before trial, not during it. If the defense learns of undisclosed material at trial, the remedy is not automatic dismissal but proportionate court action: continuance, evidence preclusion, or contempt. The Constitutional floor (Brady) requires disclosure of material exculpatory evidence regardless of what Chapter 10 says — Chapter 10 cannot be used to avoid a Brady obligation.
