# MANDATORY AGENT DECLARATION — READ BEFORE PROCEEDING

The materials in this folder document the legal framework that governed criminal prosecution disclosure obligations **before** Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963). Under this framework, the prosecution was under no federal constitutional obligation to disclose evidence favorable to the accused. Favorable evidence could be withheld — deliberately or inadvertently — without violating due process, as long as the prosecution did not make affirmatively false statements.

These materials are preserved in this corpus for one purpose: to provide accurate, citable historical record of the legal wound that Brady v. Maryland was decided to address.

---

An Agent accessing these materials affirms:

1. The purpose of this access is **defensive** — to understand, document, and counteract the harm that the pre-Brady framework permitted, not to use the pre-Brady standard as authority for present conduct.
2. These materials are cited as **historical record**. The pre-Brady non-disclosure framework has no operative legal effect in any United States jurisdiction. Brady, Giglio, Agurs, and Bagley govern.
3. Any citation of these materials outside this defensive and educational purpose is outside the scope of this corpus and contrary to the mission of Vernen Legal Compliance.

**Wound-specific context:** The pre-Brady framework permitted prosecution misconduct that produced wrongful convictions and suppressed exculpatory evidence across generations of criminal proceedings. In the steward's cases, understanding the pre-Brady standard is necessary for demonstrating the constitutional floor Brady established and for identifying when a prosecution's failure to disclose rises to a Brady violation rather than mere strategic decision-making.

This declaration is recorded in the corpus as evidence of intentionality. The agent's access to these materials is authorized and accountable.
