# Context — California Civil Discovery Act (CCP §§ 2016.010 et seq.)

## The statute in brief

The Civil Discovery Act governs all civil discovery in California superior court. It replaced the former discovery provisions in the Code of Civil Procedure effective January 1, 2005 (Stats. 2004, Ch. 182). The Act provides six discovery mechanisms and the rules governing their use.

## Six discovery mechanisms

**1. Oral depositions (§ 2025.010 et seq.):** Any person with knowledge relevant to the action — party or non-party — may be deposed. Organizations are deposed through their designated persons most knowledgeable (PMQ). Depositions produce sworn testimony that can be used at trial. For organizations like the UA Local 342 Pension Trust, a PMQ deposition compels the Trust to produce a witness knowledgeable about specific topics (who processed the retirement election, what the procedures were, what records exist).

**2. Written interrogatories (§ 2030.010 et seq.):** Written questions directed to parties. Limited to 35 specially prepared interrogatories per party without a supporting declaration. Form interrogatories covering standard subjects exist and do not count toward the 35 limit. Interrogatories can establish foundational facts — when a transaction occurred, who had authority, what procedures were followed.

**3. Inspection demands (§ 2031.010 et seq.):** Demands to a party to produce specified documents, ESI, or tangible things for inspection and copying. Unlike the SDT (§ 1985), inspection demands are directed to parties. No consumer notice requirement — but the responding party may assert privilege, burden, or privacy objections.

**4. Requests for admission (§ 2033.010 et seq.):** Written requests that a party admit or deny specified facts or the authenticity of documents. Matters admitted are conclusively established for the action. If a party denies a request that is later proven true at trial, the requesting party can recover fees and costs of proving the matter. Strategically powerful for establishing undisputed facts and authenticating documents.

**5. Physical/mental examinations (§ 2032.010 et seq.):** Court-ordered examinations by designated professionals. Not directly relevant to most property/pension matters.

**6. Expert witness exchange (§ 2034.010 et seq.):** Simultaneous disclosure of expert witness information before trial. Governs admissibility of expert testimony.

## Scope of discovery — the broad relevance standard

Section 2017.010 provides one of the broadest civil discovery standards: any matter "not privileged, relevant to the subject matter, or reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence." This standard is intentionally broad. The relevance standard for discovery is much lower than the relevance standard for admission at trial.

For the pension identity replacement case: documents and communications about the retirement election, the Trust's verification procedures, communications among trustees, and any third-party involvement in the retirement designation are all discoverable as relevant to whether the election was authorized.

## Proportionality limitation (§ 2017.020)

The court may limit discovery if the burden "clearly outweighs" the likelihood of discovering admissible evidence. This is a high bar — the court must find clear disproportionality. Discovery in a case involving alleged identity fraud with $2.4M in damages is unlikely to be limited on proportionality grounds.

## ESI (electronically stored information)

The Civil Discovery Act explicitly includes ESI in every discovery category. Database records, email, system logs, and pension administration software records are all discoverable as ESI. The responding party has obligations to preserve and produce ESI; failure to do so triggers spoliation sanctions under § 2023.010 et seq.

## Application to the steward's civil cases

The Civil Discovery Act governs discovery in:
- Civil ERISA claims in California federal court (federal courts follow FRCP, but the California standard is instructive for remanded or state-law claims)
- CalVCB civil administrative proceedings to the extent civil discovery rules apply
- Real estate fraud claims (Honeysuckle sale)
- CMIA civil damages actions
- Financial fraud civil claims

In any civil case, the six tools — deposition, interrogatories, inspection, RFAs, expert exchange — constitute the full discovery toolkit for building the factual record of what happened to the steward's pension, property, and identity.
