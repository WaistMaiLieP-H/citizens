# Context — California Code of Civil Procedure § 1985 Subpoena Duces Tecum

## The statute in brief

Section 1985 is the primary mechanism for compelling production of documents and records from non-parties in California civil proceedings. The subpoena duces tecum (SDT) requires a non-party witness to produce specified records at a deposition, hearing, or trial — or, via a deposition subpoena for business records under § 2020.410, to produce records without requiring the custodian to appear personally.

## The business records deposition subpoena (§ 2020.410)

The most operationally significant variant for records-intensive litigation is the business records deposition subpoena under CCP § 2020.410. This allows a party to obtain records from a non-party custodian without requiring live testimony. The custodian completes a declaration identifying the records and certifying their authenticity. This streamlines records collection — the requesting party gets authenticated records without scheduling depositions.

## Consumer and employee record protections

Two specific notice requirements govern SDTs directed at institutions holding third-party records:

**§ 1985.3 — Consumer records:** When an SDT is directed to a business holding records about a consumer (bank, medical provider, insurer, credit agency), the consumer must be given advance notice and the opportunity to object. The notice must be served at least 10 days before the SDT compliance date. Failure to provide required consumer notice renders the subpoena unenforceable.

**§ 1985.6 — Employee records:** When an SDT is directed to an employer for records about an employee, the employee must receive notice and the opportunity to object. Same 10-day advance notice requirement.

## Scope and objections

The SDT must describe the documents with "reasonable particularity." An overly broad subpoena can be quashed or limited on motion. The non-party can move to quash or modify the subpoena on grounds including: the demand is unduly burdensome, the documents are privileged (attorney-client, physician-patient, CMIA), or production would violate third-party privacy rights.

For medical records, CMIA's authorization requirements (§ 56.10(b)(3)) work in tandem with § 1985.3 — an SDT for medical records triggers both the § 1985.3 consumer notice requirement and CMIA's protections.

## CMIA disclosure logs — the strategic target

The most important SDT target in cases involving medical records access is the CMIA disclosure log. Under CMIA, providers must maintain a record of every disclosure of a patient's medical information. An SDT to a medical provider requesting the complete CMIA disclosure log for a patient will reveal every party who received that patient's records, the legal basis claimed for each disclosure (treatment, payment, § 56.10(c)(12) probate court investigator, etc.), and the date of each disclosure.

This log is the forensic map of who accessed the records, when, and under what claimed authority.

## UA Local 342 pension records

An SDT directed to the UA Local 342 Pension Trust, as a business holding records about a plan participant, can compel production of:
- The retirement election application (or the record that no such application exists)
- The authorization records supporting the retirement designation
- The date the "retired" status was entered in the database
- All communications with participants regarding the retirement status
- ERISA plan documents governing retirement election procedures

The pension Trust is not a consumer records holder under § 1985.3, so the consumer notice requirements do not apply — but the Trust may assert ERISA preemption for certain records, making federal discovery mechanisms under ERISA § 1132 more appropriate for pension records specifically.

## Application across the steward's cases

SDTs can be directed to:
- OPD records unit — for all records related to specified incident reports (to document that a purported report does not exist)
- Medical providers — for CMIA disclosure logs revealing all third-party disclosures
- Financial institutions — for account records and transaction history
- County recorder — for real estate transaction documents including unsigned documents
- Pension Trust — for retirement election application and authorization records
