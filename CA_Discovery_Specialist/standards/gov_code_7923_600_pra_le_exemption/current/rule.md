# California Public Records Act — Law Enforcement Exemption — Rule

**Authority:** Cal. Gov. Code § 7923.600
**Jurisdiction:** California
**Effective:** January 1, 2023 (reorganization from former Gov. Code § 6254(f)); original exemption in force since 1968

## The Rule

The California Public Records Act (CPRA) gives the public a general right to inspect and copy government records. Section 7923.600 creates a presumptive exemption for law enforcement records: agencies are not required to disclose records of complaints or investigations by the Attorney General, the Department of Justice, the Office of Emergency Services, or any state or local police agency. The exemption also covers intelligence information, security procedures, and investigatory or security files compiled by any state or local agency for correctional, law enforcement, or licensing purposes.

The exemption is not absolute. Three express statutory exceptions limit it: §§ 7924.510, 7924.700, and 7929.610. Most significantly, Penal Code § 832.7(b) — added by SB 1421 (2018) and expanded by SB 16 (2021) — requires public disclosure of specified categories of officer misconduct records regardless of § 7923.600. Those categories (use of force, sexual assault, dishonesty, unlawful arrest, unlawful search, prejudice/discrimination) must be released even though they would otherwise fall within the § 7923.600 exemption.

One additional rule within § 7923.600: customer alarm lists that security companies provide to police at the police agency's request are NOT exempt — they remain subject to CPRA disclosure.

## Key Elements

1. **Presumptive exemption**: Law enforcement investigatory and security files are exempt from mandatory CPRA disclosure by default.
2. **Three express exceptions**: §§ 7924.510, 7924.700, and 7929.610 override the exemption in specific circumstances.
3. **SB 1421 / § 832.7(b) override**: The most consequential carve-back — specific categories of sustained officer misconduct must be disclosed publicly, even though § 7923.600 would otherwise protect those records.
4. **Alarm company customer lists**: Voluntarily provided to police; remain public records subject to CPRA.

## What This Means in Practice

If you submit a CPRA request to a law enforcement agency for investigative records, the default answer is no — § 7923.600 authorizes the agency to withhold them. If your request is specifically for records covered by Penal Code § 832.7(b) (use-of-force incidents causing death or serious injury, sustained officer misconduct findings), the default flips — the agency must produce them within 45 days and cannot hide behind § 7923.600.
