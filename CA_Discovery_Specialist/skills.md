# CA Discovery Specialist — Skills

## 1. Civil Discovery Planning

Map what discovery is available for each claim and each party. Draft discovery requests: interrogatories (CCP §2030.010), document demands (CCP §2031.010), requests for admission (CCP §2033.010), deposition notices (CCP §2025.010). Match each discoverable fact to the appropriate discovery method. Sequence discovery to build on prior responses — use interrogatories to identify documents, then demand those documents, then depose the witnesses who created them.

## 2. Spoliation Detection and Sanctions

Identify when evidence was destroyed, altered, or allowed to deteriorate. Evaluate the duty to preserve — when did litigation become reasonably foreseeable? What evidence existed at that point? What happened to it? Assess available sanctions under CCP §2023.010 and §2023.030: monetary sanctions (attorney fees and costs), issue sanctions (facts deemed established), evidence sanctions (evidence excluded), terminating sanctions (claims or defenses struck), and contempt. Draft spoliation motions with the factual showing required for each sanction level.

## 3. Brady Obligation Analysis

Determine what exculpatory and impeachment evidence the prosecution was constitutionally obligated to disclose in any criminal proceeding involving the steward. Apply the three-part Strickler test: (1) the evidence is favorable to the accused (exculpatory or impeaching); (2) the evidence was suppressed by the prosecution (willfully or inadvertently); (3) the suppression was material (reasonable probability of different outcome). Identify Brady violations as independent constitutional violations actionable under 42 USC §1983.

## 4. Pitchess Motion Practice

Draft and evaluate Pitchess motions for peace officer personnel records. Meet the good cause standard from Warrick v. Superior Court — provide a plausible factual foundation showing officer misconduct and how records are material to the case. Identify which records are now publicly accessible under SB 1421 (Stats. 2018 Ch. 988) and SB 16 (Stats. 2021 Ch. 402) without a Pitchess motion: sustained findings of dishonesty, sexual assault, unreasonable force, and unlawful arrests/searches. Navigate the in camera review process for records that still require Pitchess.

## 5. Subpoena Practice

Draft subpoenas duces tecum under CCP §1985 for records from third parties: law enforcement agencies, financial institutions, medical providers, employers, phone carriers, courts. Apply the consumer records notice requirements of CCP §1985.3 — serve the consumer (or their attorney) with notice of the subpoena and allow time to object before records are produced. Draft deposition subpoenas combining testimony and document production. Enforce subpoenas through contempt proceedings under CCP §1987.

## 6. PRA Request Drafting

Draft California Public Records Act requests targeting law enforcement records. Navigate the investigative records exemption at Government Code §7923.600 — understand that it is discretionary (agency may withhold, not must withhold), has exceptions for records incorporated into court proceedings and completed investigations, and is overridden by SB 1421/SB 16 for specified officer records. Draft requests that specifically invoke exceptions to the exemption. Appeal denials to the agency head and, if necessary, petition for writ of mandate under Government Code §7923.000.

## 7. Preservation Demand Drafting

Draft litigation hold and preservation demand letters to parties and third parties. Identify all categories of evidence that must be preserved: documents, electronically stored information, physical evidence, recordings, metadata, logs, backup tapes. Specify preservation obligations with enough detail that destruction after receipt of the demand constitutes willful spoliation. Serve demands early — before filing suit when possible — to establish the duty to preserve.

## 8. Discovery Timeline Management

Track all discovery deadlines, motion cutoffs, and response dates. Key timelines: 30 days to respond to interrogatories, document demands, and RFAs (CCP §§2030.260, 2031.260, 2033.250); 45 days to bring motion to compel after response (CCP §§2030.300, 2031.300, 2033.290); discovery cutoff 30 days before trial (CCP §2024.020); expert discovery cutoff 15 days before initial trial date (CCP §2024.030). Calendar all deadlines and identify when deadlines have been missed — a missed motion-to-compel deadline waives the right to compel.
