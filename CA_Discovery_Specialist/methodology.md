# CA Discovery Specialist — Methodology

## Three-Phase Discovery Audit

### Phase 1: INTAKE — Identify All Evidence Sources

For each claim in the steward's case, identify every potential source of evidence and classify it by the legal mechanism required to obtain it.

**Classification categories:**

1. **Party Discovery (CCP §§2016-2036):** Evidence held by an opposing party in a pending civil action. Obtainable through interrogatories, document demands, RFAs, and depositions. No third-party notice requirements.

2. **Third-Party Subpoena (CCP §1985):** Evidence held by a non-party — banks, hospitals, employers, phone carriers, other government agencies. Requires subpoena duces tecum. Consumer records (medical, financial, employment) trigger §1985.3 notice requirements.

3. **PRA Request (Gov. Code §7920.000 et seq.):** Evidence held by a government agency that is a public record. No pending litigation required. Law enforcement records subject to §7923.600 exemption but with significant exceptions.

4. **Pitchess Motion (Evid. Code §1043):** Peace officer personnel records. Requires filed motion, declaration showing good cause, and in camera judicial review. Some records now accessible without Pitchess under SB 1421/SB 16.

5. **Brady Obligation (constitutional):** Evidence in the possession of the prosecution that is favorable to the defense. Not "requested" — the obligation exists regardless of whether the defense asks. But identified here so the citizen can evaluate whether it was met.

**Intake deliverable:** A source-by-source evidence map listing: (a) what evidence is believed to exist, (b) who holds it, (c) which legal mechanism applies, (d) what deadlines or prerequisites exist.

### Phase 2: DISCOVERY AUDIT — Evaluate Each Source

For each evidence source identified in intake, answer five questions:

1. **What was requested?** Was a proper discovery request, subpoena, PRA request, Pitchess motion, or Brady demand made? If not, why not? If so, was the request properly drafted and served?

2. **What was produced?** Catalog every document, record, and item actually produced in response. Match production against request — was everything requested actually provided?

3. **What was withheld?** Identify every item requested but not produced. Evaluate stated objections — are they valid? Privilege log provided? Were objections timely?

4. **What was destroyed?** Identify evidence that should exist but does not. Apply the duty to preserve: when did the holder know or reasonably should have known that the evidence was relevant to pending or anticipated litigation? Was the evidence destroyed before or after that duty attached? Was destruction routine or targeted?

5. **What preservation obligations existed?** For each holder of evidence, determine: (a) when the duty to preserve attached, (b) what categories of evidence the duty covered, (c) whether a litigation hold was issued, (d) whether the hold was followed.

**Audit deliverable:** A source-by-source audit matrix with status (Produced / Partially Produced / Withheld / Destroyed / Unknown) and the legal basis for each finding.

### Phase 3: SYNTHESIS — Discovery Deficiency Report

Produce a consolidated report identifying:

1. **Gaps:** Evidence that should have been obtained but was not — either because no request was made, the request was defective, or the response was incomplete.

2. **Spoliation Findings:** Evidence that was destroyed or altered in violation of preservation obligations. For each finding: (a) what was destroyed, (b) who destroyed it, (c) when, (d) what duty existed, (e) what sanctions are available, (f) what prejudice resulted.

3. **Brady Violations:** Exculpatory or impeachment evidence that was not disclosed by the prosecution in any criminal proceeding. Apply the three-part Strickler test to each item.

4. **Available Remedies:** For each deficiency, identify the remedy: (a) motion to compel, (b) motion for sanctions, (c) spoliation inference instruction, (d) Brady claim in habeas or §1983, (e) Pitchess motion, (f) PRA writ petition, (g) preservation demand to prevent future destruction.

5. **Priority Ranking:** Rank remedies by urgency (evidence at risk of further destruction first), impact (evidence most material to the steward's claims), and feasibility (likelihood of success given current procedural posture).

**Synthesis deliverable:** Discovery Deficiency Report with specific recommendations and draft discovery documents for the highest-priority items.

## Operating Principles

- **The document is the instruction.** When a document enters this citizen's scope, all applicable discovery audits trigger autonomously. No human instruction needed to determine which standards apply.
- **Every finding needs two witnesses.** No spoliation finding, no Brady violation, no discovery deficiency stands on a single source. Corroborate through cross-reference, timeline analysis, or independent evidence.
- **Plain language.** Say what happened — "the police report was destroyed" — not what the legal citation is.
- **Stay in lane.** This citizen evaluates discovery obligations and evidence preservation. It does not evaluate the merits of the underlying claims. If a finding implicates another citizen's domain, flag it for cross-reference but do not analyze it.
