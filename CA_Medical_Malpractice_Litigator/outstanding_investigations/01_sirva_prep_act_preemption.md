## The Open Question

Does the PREP Act (42 U.S.C. §247d-6d) preempt a California professional negligence
claim against Walgreens for SIRVA caused by improper injection technique?

The PREP Act provides immunity to "covered persons" for "claims for loss caused by,
arising out of, relating to, or resulting from the administration" of a "covered
countermeasure." COVID-19 vaccines administered under the HHS Secretary's declaration
were covered countermeasures.

**The tension:** The vaccine drug is the covered countermeasure. The injection technique
is a separate act of professional competence. If the needle entered the subacromial
bursa rather than the deltoid muscle, that is a pharmacist's technical error — not
a property of the vaccine itself.

---

## The Split in Authority

**Arguments that PREP Act bars the claim:**
- "Arising out of or relating to" administration language is broad
- Walgreens is a "covered person" (licensed pharmacy)
- HHS has argued in guidance that PREP Act bars most COVID vaccine injury claims
- Federal courts have dismissed some SIRVA claims on PREP Act grounds

**Arguments that PREP Act does NOT bar the claim:**
- PREP Act immunity extends to injuries from the countermeasure itself — adverse
  reactions, immune responses, etc.
- A pharmacist injecting into the wrong anatomical location has committed an act of
  professional negligence independent of the covered countermeasure
- Bruesewitz v. Wyeth (cited for vaccine preemption) was a design-defect case, not
  administration-technique negligence
- State professional negligence claims have traditionally governed pharmacist practice
- The CICP (Countermeasures Injury Compensation Program) is the alternative remedy —
  but CICP coverage for SIRVA from COVID vaccines has been contested
