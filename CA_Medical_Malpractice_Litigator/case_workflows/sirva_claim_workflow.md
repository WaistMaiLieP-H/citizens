## Phase 2 — NCVIA / PREP Act Preemption Analysis

This is the threshold legal question that controls which court and what remedies are available.

**Step 2.1 — Identify the vaccine**
COVID-19 vaccine administered at Walgreens Brentwood approximately November 2021.

**Step 2.2 — Apply PREP Act analysis**
COVID-19 vaccines administered during the public health emergency were covered under the
PREP Act (42 U.S.C. §247d-6d). The CICP (Countermeasures Injury Compensation Program)
is the primary federal no-fault remedy for covered countermeasures.

**Critical question:** Is a SIRVA claim a "loss" caused by a "covered countermeasure"?

The PREP Act's preemption is broad for claims "relating to" covered countermeasures, but:
- **Design defect claims:** preempted (Bruesewitz v. Wyeth extends here by analogy)
- **Administration technique claims:** a significant line of authority holds that improper
  injection technique (not the vaccine itself) is a negligent act outside the covered
  countermeasure — the countermeasure is the vaccine drug, not the injection technique
- A pharmacist who injects too high on the arm (into the bursa) may have committed an act
  of professional negligence outside the PREP Act's immunity

**Step 2.3 — Document injection site specifics**
Obtain the vaccination record's injection-site notation. If it notes subdeltoid or bursa
injection, that is direct evidence of improper technique — the foundation of the
administration-negligence theory.

**Step 2.4 — Research current 9th Circuit / NDCA authority on PREP Act + SIRVA**
As of the 2026 corpus build date, courts are split on whether PREP Act bars SIRVA
administration-negligence claims for COVID vaccines. Research the current state of
NDCA district court orders and any 9th Circuit opinion resolving the split.
