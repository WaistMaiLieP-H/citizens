# SIRVA Doctrine — Current Framework (2026)

**Standard type:** Case-law + regulatory framework (no single statutory text)
**Primary authorities:** Department of HHS Vaccine Injury Table; Bruesewitz v. Wyeth; PREP Act 42 USC §247d-6d; CICP regulations; state-court professional negligence doctrine

## What SIRVA is

**Shoulder Injury Related to Vaccine Administration (SIRVA)** is a medically-recognized injury pattern in which an intramuscular vaccine injection is administered too high on the deltoid — or into the shoulder joint capsule — causing inflammation, bursitis, rotator cuff damage, frozen shoulder, or labral tear. Onset is typically within 48 hours of vaccination. The injury is distinct from routine post-vaccination soreness and is typically persistent, sometimes permanent, and often requires physical therapy, corticosteroid injections, or arthroscopic surgery.

The medical literature first identified SIRVA as a distinct injury pattern in the 2000s, and HHS added it to the Vaccine Injury Table in 2017 (for covered vaccines administered under VICP).

## The three regimes

A SIRVA claim in 2026 sits at the intersection of three regulatory regimes, and the correct choice depends on which vaccine and which administration:

### 1. VICP — Vaccine Injury Compensation Program (NCVIA, 42 USC §300aa-10 et seq.)

- **Applies to:** Vaccines listed on the Vaccine Injury Table by HHS — including most childhood vaccines, flu vaccines administered in many contexts, hepatitis vaccines, etc.
- **Forum:** U.S. Court of Federal Claims (no jury; bench trial before a Special Master)
- **Preemption:** State-court tort claims against vaccine manufacturers for design defect are PREEMPTED (Bruesewitz v. Wyeth, 2011). Manufacturing-defect and failure-to-warn claims may survive.
- **SIRVA treatment:** SIRVA is a Table injury for covered vaccines since 2017. Claims filed within 3 years of onset of the first symptom.
- **No-fault:** VICP is a no-fault system. Claimant need not prove negligence, only that the injury occurred and matches the Table criteria.

### 2. CICP — Countermeasures Injury Compensation Program (PREP Act, 42 USC §247d-6e)

- **Applies to:** COVID-19 vaccines administered under PREP Act authorization. Also applies to other "covered countermeasures" under HHS declarations.
- **Forum:** HHS administrative process (not a court at all). Appeal to federal district court only after HHS final denial.
- **Preemption:** PREP Act §247d-6d provides BROAD immunity for covered persons (manufacturers, administrators, planners) against suit for injuries caused by administration or use of covered countermeasures. Limited "willful misconduct" exception only.
- **SIRVA treatment under PREP Act:** Highly constrained. Most SIRVA claims against COVID-19 vaccine administrators are channeled into CICP, where compensation rates have historically been very low.
- **Filing deadline:** 1 year from vaccination (much shorter than VICP).

### 3. State-court professional negligence

- **Applies to:** Negligent ADMINISTRATION (not the vaccine itself) where PREP Act and NCVIA do not preempt.
- **Examples:** Administration of a non-covered vaccine; administration of a covered vaccine in a manner that constitutes "willful misconduct" under PREP Act; certain pharmacist-administration claims that are carved out by state law.
- **Forum:** California state court under MICRA procedural regime (CCP §340.5, §364, §3333.2 cap).
- **Elements:** Standard professional negligence elements — duty, breach (deviation from standard of care for vaccine administration), causation, damages.

## The critical distinction: vaccine DEFECT vs. vaccine ADMINISTRATION

Bruesewitz v. Wyeth (2011) holds that NCVIA preempts state-court design-defect claims against covered vaccine manufacturers. **But Bruesewitz does NOT preempt claims based on negligent administration.** If the harm is caused by HOW the shot was given (too high, wrong angle, into the joint) — not by WHAT was in the shot — the claim is against the administering provider, not the manufacturer, and is not preempted on a Bruesewitz theory.

This is the pleading doctrine for state-court SIRVA cases: plead the injection was administered improperly, not that the vaccine was defective.

## PREP Act overlay for COVID-19 vaccines

The PREP Act, invoked by HHS declaration in March 2020, grants broad immunity to COVID-19 vaccine administrators for claims arising from "administration or use" of the vaccine. The question whether this immunity reaches SIRVA claims alleging negligent injection technique (not vaccine defect) is contested:

- **HHS position:** SIRVA claims against COVID-19 vaccine administrators should be brought through CICP.
- **Plaintiff position:** Negligent injection technique is not "administration or use" of the countermeasure itself but rather a separate negligent act by the provider; PREP Act immunity should not reach it.
- **Judicial treatment:** Mixed and evolving. Some federal district courts have dismissed SIRVA claims against pharmacists/clinics on PREP Act grounds. Other courts have preserved state-court claims where "willful misconduct" is alleged or where the negligent-technique theory is sufficiently distinct from the countermeasure-administration immunity.

## Why this matters for the steward's 04-22 Shoulder Surgery / SIRVA case

The steward's SIRVA case arises from a COVID-19 vaccine administered at Walgreens Brentwood in approximately November 2021. The critical framing questions:

1. **Which vaccine regime applies?**
   - The COVID-19 vaccine is covered by the PREP Act declaration → CICP is the default channel.
   - HOWEVER, if the claim can be framed as negligent administration technique (improper injection site/angle) sufficiently distinct from "administration of the countermeasure itself," there is a plausible state-court path.

2. **Discovery rule timing.**
   - Under CCP §340.5, the one-year clock runs from discovery of both the injury AND its negligent cause.
   - The steward discovered the SIRVA injury and its connection to the November 2021 vaccine on 2026-03-18.
   - **The §340.5 one-year clock therefore runs to 2027-03-18** for a state-court claim.
   - The CICP one-year clock ran from administration (~November 2021) and is now long expired — unless discovery-rule tolling applies administratively (which CICP generally does not recognize).
   - **Conclusion:** The state-court path is the only viable path, and it requires navigating PREP Act immunity.

3. **Pleading strategy:**
   - Name the administering entity (Walgreens / pharmacist), not the vaccine manufacturer.
   - Plead negligent injection technique (wrong site, wrong angle, improper training, failure to follow CDC/ACIP standards) as professional negligence.
   - Plead "willful misconduct" in the alternative if the facts support it (to invoke the PREP Act immunity exception).
   - Serve CCP §364 90-day notice.
   - File within §340.5 one-year window from 2026-03-18 discovery.

4. **NCVIA alternative:**
   - If for any reason the PREP Act pathway fails, evaluate whether the flu vaccine or any other covered vaccine was administered in the same timeframe that might provide a VICP SIRVA claim as a fallback.
