# Historical Chain — SIRVA Doctrine

## The wound

From roughly the mid-2000s forward, physicians, physical therapists, and orthopedic surgeons began reporting a recurring pattern of patients presenting with persistent shoulder pain, reduced range of motion, and in some cases frozen shoulder or labral tear, all beginning within 48 hours of a routine intramuscular vaccination. The pattern did not match ordinary post-vaccination soreness, which resolves within a few days. It matched a mechanical injury — something damaged by the needle itself, either by injecting above the deltoid muscle belly into the bursa, or by penetrating the shoulder joint capsule entirely.

The medical literature began documenting the pattern in the 2000s. By 2010, case reports were accumulating. In 2011, a retrospective study of claims filed in the Vaccine Injury Compensation Program identified shoulder injuries as a significant and growing category of claims for which no Table injury category existed — meaning claimants had to prove causation in the much-harder "off-Table" track.

The wound was a real, recurring, mechanical injury pattern that had no legal recognition and no compensation pathway proportionate to its frequency.

## The promise

In response to the accumulating evidence, the Department of Health and Human Services added SIRVA to the Vaccine Injury Table in 2017. This meant that claims for SIRVA arising from covered vaccines administered under VICP could be filed on the "Table" track — a presumption of causation if the claimant met the diagnostic criteria and onset-within-48-hours timing.

The Table criteria:
1. No history of pain, inflammation, or dysfunction of the affected shoulder prior to the vaccination.
2. Pain onset within 48 hours of vaccination.
3. Pain and reduced range of motion limited to the shoulder in which the vaccine was administered.
4. No other condition or abnormality present that would explain the symptoms.

SIRVA's addition to the Table was the federal government's formal recognition that this injury pattern was real, mechanical, and compensable.

## The counter-wound — COVID-19 and the PREP Act

When the COVID-19 vaccine rollout began in December 2020, the administering regime was not the same as routine childhood vaccination. HHS had invoked the PREP Act (42 USC §247d-6d, §247d-6e) in March 2020, creating a Countermeasures Injury Compensation Program (CICP) and granting broad immunity to vaccine administrators for injuries caused by "administration or use" of the COVID-19 countermeasures.

The result was a two-track system:
- Non-COVID vaccines administered under VICP/NCVIA: SIRVA is a Table injury; compensation is available in the Court of Federal Claims through a relatively accessible process.
- COVID-19 vaccines administered under PREP Act: SIRVA claims must be filed through CICP; compensation rates are substantially lower; immunity bars most state-court actions; the filing window is 1 year from vaccination.

For COVID-19 SIRVA claimants, the PREP Act and CICP regime imposes three disadvantages:
1. **Shorter filing window** (1 year from vaccination vs. 3 years from onset under VICP).
2. **Lower compensation rates.**
3. **Broader immunity** for administering pharmacies and pharmacists, which forecloses most state-court alternatives.

## The arc

- **2000s:** SIRVA injury pattern begins appearing in medical literature.
- **2010:** VICP claim data begins showing significant off-Table SIRVA claims filed by claimants bearing the burden of proving causation.
- **2011:** Bruesewitz v. Wyeth clarifies that NCVIA preempts state-court design-defect claims against manufacturers but not negligent-administration claims against providers.
- **2017:** HHS adds SIRVA to the VICP Vaccine Injury Table, creating the Table presumption.
- **2020:** PREP Act invoked for COVID-19 response; CICP becomes the channel for COVID-19 vaccine injuries.
- **2021-2024:** COVID-19 SIRVA cases accumulate; most are routed through CICP; a contested minority reach federal district courts under PREP Act immunity analysis.
- **2026 (steward's filing context):** The landscape is mature but the COVID-19 state-court path is narrow and contested.

## The current state

SIRVA doctrine as of 2026 stands on three legs:

1. **Medical/scientific recognition.** SIRVA is an established clinical entity with diagnostic criteria.
2. **Federal compensation framework.** VICP (for covered vaccines) and CICP (for PREP Act countermeasures) provide administrative paths.
3. **State-court residual.** Negligent-administration claims against providers may be viable in state court depending on which regime applies and whether PREP Act immunity reaches the specific claim.

For claimants injured by COVID-19 vaccines, the state-court path requires:
- Framing the claim as negligent injection technique, not vaccine defect
- Naming the administering entity, not the manufacturer
- Navigating PREP Act immunity, typically by pleading willful misconduct in the alternative or by arguing the negligent-technique claim is sufficiently distinct from "administration of the countermeasure itself"
- Meeting the state-court MICRA procedural requirements (CCP §340.5, §364, §3333.2)
