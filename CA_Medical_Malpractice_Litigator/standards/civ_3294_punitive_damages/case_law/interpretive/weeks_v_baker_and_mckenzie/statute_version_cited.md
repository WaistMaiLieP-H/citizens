# Statute Version Cited — Weeks v. Baker & McKenzie

**Case decided:** 1998
**Statute version applicable:** Cal. Civil Code §3294(b) (post-1987 — current operative version)

## The Statutory Text at Issue

§3294(b) as the court applied it:

An employer shall not be liable for damages pursuant to subdivision (a), based upon
acts of an employee of the employer, unless the employer had advance knowledge of the
unfitness of the employee and employed him or her with a conscious disregard of the
rights or safety of others or authorized or ratified the wrongful conduct for which
the damages are awarded or was personally guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice.
**With respect to a corporate employer, the advance knowledge and conscious disregard,
authorization, ratification or act of oppression, fraud, or malice must be on the part
of an officer, director, or managing agent of the corporation.**

## What Weeks Interpreted

Weeks interpreted the phrase "managing agent of the corporation" — which the 1987
Legislature added but did not define. The case fills the definitional gap with the
"substantial discretionary authority over policy decisions" test.

## Current Status

Weeks remains the leading California appellate authority on the "managing agent"
definition under §3294(b). The test has been cited and applied broadly across employment,
medical, and institutional liability contexts.

The 1987 statute is unchanged; Weeks' interpretation of "managing agent" has been
the operative standard since 1998.
