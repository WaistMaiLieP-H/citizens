# Weeks v. Baker & McKenzie — Holding

**Citation:** 63 Cal.App.4th 1128 (1998)
**Court:** Court of Appeal of California, First Appellate District
**Decided:** 1998

## Facts

Rena Weeks, a secretary at the law firm Baker & McKenzie, sued the firm for sexual
harassment and sought punitive damages under §3294(b). The firm argued it could not
be liable for punitive damages because the harassment was committed by a partner
(Martin Greenstein) whose conduct the firm claimed was not authorized or ratified at
the managing agent level.

The central question: what does "managing agent" mean under §3294(b), and did the
firm's conduct in response to Greenstein's harassment constitute authorization or
ratification by a managing agent?

## Holding

**"Managing agent" under §3294(b) means an employee who exercises substantial
discretionary authority over decisions that determine corporate policy — not merely
someone with supervisory authority over other employees.**

The test is whether the employee's decisions "may ultimately determine corporate policy."
A managing agent has authority to make policy-level decisions that bind the corporation,
not just carry out existing policy under supervision. The focus is on the nature and
scope of the employee's authority, not their title.

The court found Baker & McKenzie's managing partners — who decided how to respond to
Weeks' sexual harassment complaints and chose to protect Greenstein rather than
discipline him — were managing agents whose conduct (or inaction) could constitute
ratification of the harassment.

The jury's $3.5 million punitive damages award (later remitted) was upheld on the
managing agent finding.

## Rules Distilled

1. **Managing agent = policy-making authority:** Must exercise substantial discretionary
   authority over decisions that determine corporate policy; supervisory authority over
   employees alone is insufficient
2. **Title does not control:** "Manager" or "supervisor" in title ≠ managing agent;
   the court looks at actual authority and scope of decision-making
3. **Ratification as a path:** If a managing agent (officer, director, or managing agent)
   learns of an employee's malicious/oppressive conduct and consciously chooses not to
   stop or discipline it, that constitutes ratification under §3294(b)(3)
4. **Corporate culture evidence:** Evidence that the corporation had a pattern of ignoring
   or covering up misconduct — which managing agents knew of and perpetuated — is
   admissible to show authorization or ratification
5. **Post-conduct ratification:** Ratification can occur after the wrongful act through
   knowing approval, adoption, or acquiescence

## Significance for Medical Institution Liability

Weeks defines the §3294(b) managing agent test for corporate employer liability in
medical malpractice punitive damages claims:

**Hospital liability path:** To hold a hospital liable for punitive damages for a
physician's or staff member's malicious/oppressive conduct, the plaintiff must show:
- The wrongful conduct was authorized or ratified by a hospital officer, director, or
  managing agent (department chief, medical director, risk management director, or other
  employee with policy-making authority over clinical operations)
- OR the hospital had advance knowledge of the employee's unfitness and consciously
  disregarded that knowledge (§3294(b)(1))

**Systematic pattern evidence:** Where a hospital's risk management or administration
had prior knowledge of a physician's pattern of misconduct and chose not to act, Weeks
allows that institutional non-response to be characterized as ratification by managing agents.

**Medical director / department chair:** These are paradigmatic "managing agents" under
Weeks — they have policy-making authority over clinical operations and physician conduct.
