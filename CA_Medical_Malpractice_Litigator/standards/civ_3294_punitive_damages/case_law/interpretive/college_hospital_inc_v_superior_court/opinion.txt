RELEVANCE TO CIV. CODE § 3294 PUNITIVE DAMAGES:

College Hospital Inc. v. Superior Court establishes the authoritative California definition of "despicable conduct" under Civil Code § 3294(c)(1) and (c)(2) — which is the substantive punitive damages statute.

KEY HOLDINGS FOR § 3294 PURPOSES:

1. MALICE UNDER § 3294(c)(1) — THE DESPICABLE CONDUCT ELEMENT:
"Despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others" satisfies the malice prong. The court defined "despicable" as: conduct so vile, base, contemptible, miserable, wretched, or loathsome that it would be looked down upon and despised by ordinary decent people. 8 Cal.4th at 726.

2. OPPRESSION UNDER § 3294(c)(2):
"Despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person's rights." The same "despicable" standard applies.

3. EMPLOYER LIABILITY — § 3294(b):
A corporation is liable for punitive damages based on employee conduct only when:
- An officer, director, or managing agent had advance knowledge of the employee's unfitness AND employed him/her with conscious disregard of others' rights or safety; OR
- The corporation authorized or ratified the wrongful conduct; OR
- The corporation itself was personally guilty of malice, oppression, or fraud.

4. THE DESPICABLE CONDUCT BAR IS HIGH:
Ordinary negligence, even gross negligence, does not reach despicable conduct. The conduct must fall into a category that "ordinary decent people would despise." This prevents punitive damages from being routinely awarded in medical malpractice cases where the primary theory is substandard care.

---

APPLICATION TO CIVIL CODE § 3294 ANALYSIS:

For punitive damages in any California civil case involving healthcare providers:
- Start with § 3294(c) definitions (malice, oppression, fraud);
- Apply College Hospital's "despicable conduct" definition to assess whether the facts meet the standard;
- For institutional defendants, analyze § 3294(b) employer liability separately;
- For § 425.13 purposes, show substantial probability of meeting all elements.
