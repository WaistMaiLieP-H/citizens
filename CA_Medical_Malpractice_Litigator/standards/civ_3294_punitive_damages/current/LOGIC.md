# Cal. Civil Code §3294 — Punitive Damages
## Current Operative Architecture

### RULE

§3294 authorizes the recovery of punitive damages in non-contract civil actions where
the defendant is guilty of malice, oppression, or fraud. In the medical malpractice
context, §3294 is the substantive standard that §425.13 gates: a plaintiff who clears
the §425.13 procedural motion must still prove §3294's elements at trial to recover
punitive damages.

**Operative text (§3294(a)):**
"In an action for the breach of an obligation not arising from contract, where it is
proven by clear and convincing evidence that the defendant has been guilty of oppression,
fraud, or malice, the plaintiff, in addition to the actual damages, may recover damages
for the sake of example and by way of punishing the defendant."

**Burden of proof: CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE** — heightened standard; more than
preponderance of the evidence; must produce "a firm belief or conviction" in the trier
of fact's mind that the conduct constituting malice, fraud, or oppression occurred.

### DEFINITIONS — §3294(c)

**"Malice" — §3294(c)(1):**
"'Malice' means conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the
plaintiff or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and
conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others."

Two paths:
- **Intentional malice:** Defendant intended to cause the specific harm
- **Despicable conduct + conscious disregard:** College Hospital's "so vile, base,
  contemptible, miserable, wretched or loathsome" standard + willful and conscious
  disregard (not merely negligent; the defendant must know of the risk and consciously
  disregard it)

**"Oppression" — §3294(c)(2):**
"'Oppression' means despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust
hardship in conscious disregard of that person's rights."

Requires: despicable conduct + cruel/unjust hardship + conscious disregard of rights.
Not mere negligence; not mere bad judgment — a deliberate decision to subject someone
to hardship despite knowing their rights.

**"Fraud" — §3294(c)(3):**
"'Fraud' means an intentional misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment of a material
fact known to the defendant with the intention on the part of the defendant of thereby
depriving a person of property or legal rights or otherwise causing injury."

Elements: (1) intentional; (2) misrepresentation, deceit, or concealment; (3) of a
material fact known to defendant; (4) intent to deprive of property or legal rights
or cause injury.

### EMPLOYER/CORPORATION LIABILITY — §3294(b)

For corporate defendants (hospitals, medical groups, Walgreens):
"An employer shall not be liable for damages pursuant to subdivision (a), based upon
acts of an employee of the employer, unless the employer had advance knowledge of the
unfitness of the employee and employed him or her with a conscious disregard of the
rights or safety of others or authorized or ratified the wrongful conduct for which
the damages are awarded or was personally guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice."

**Three employer liability paths:**
1. **Advance knowledge + conscious disregard:** Employer knew the employee was unfit
   and hired/retained them anyway in conscious disregard of others' safety
   (College Hospital pattern: hospital knew employee was a risk to patients)
2. **Authorization or ratification:** Employer authorized the wrongful conduct in
   advance, or ratified it after learning of it
3. **Employer personally guilty:** The entity's own conduct (through officers, directors,
   managing agents) constitutes oppression, fraud, or malice

**"Managing agent" limitation:** Employer is only liable under paths 1-2 if the advance
knowledge, authorization, or ratification was by an "officer, director, or managing
agent" of the corporation — not a line-level employee. This limits punitive damages
against corporations to cases where the wrongdoing was directed, known, or ratified
at a management level.

### MEDICAL MALPRACTICE CONTEXT

**§3294 + §425.13 interaction:**
- §425.13 is the procedural gate: before punitive damages can be included in a pleading,
  the court must find "substantial probability" the plaintiff will prevail under §3294
- §3294 is the substantive standard the plaintiff must ultimately prove at trial
- Clearing §425.13 ≠ establishing §3294 at trial; it only establishes prima facie showing

**Conscious disregard in medical context:**
College Hospital: performing surgery that the surgeon knows (or consciously disregards)
is not clinically indicated, for financial benefit = conscious disregard of patient's
rights and safety = malice under §3294(c)(1)'s despicable conduct path.

**Fraud in medical context:**
A surgeon who misrepresents clinical necessity (tells patient surgery is necessary when
it is not) = §3294(c)(3) fraud: intentional misrepresentation of a material fact
(clinical necessity) known to the surgeon, with intent to cause the patient to undergo
(and pay for) surgery = "depriving a person of ... legal rights" (informed consent)
and "causing injury."

**Oppression in medical context:**
A physician who administers treatment knowing it is contra-indicated, knowing the patient
is vulnerable, and knowing the patient cannot protect themselves from the risk =
oppression: despicable conduct + cruel hardship + conscious disregard of patient's rights.

### SPINE SURGERY FRAUD — §3294 ANALYSIS

**Malice (despicable + conscious disregard):**
- Surgeon performed surgery knowing (or consciously disregarding) that it was not
  clinically indicated per pre-operative diagnostic findings
- Financial motive (maximizing reimbursement) = the mechanism of conscious disregard:
  the surgeon knew the surgery was for financial rather than clinical benefit
- Post-surgical outcome worse than pre-surgical = evidence the claimed clinical
  indication was false
- Financial motive + clinical unsupported procedure = College Hospital despicable
  standard met

**Fraud:**
- Surgeon told patient surgery was necessary = intentional misrepresentation
- Of material fact (clinical necessity) known to surgeon to be false
- With intent to cause patient to proceed with (and pay for) surgery = deprive of
  legal right (informed consent) and cause injury

**Hospital/Employer §3294(b):**
If the hospital had a pattern of allowing this surgeon to perform unnecessary surgeries
(prior Medical Board complaints, billing anomaly patterns, peer review records), the
hospital had "advance knowledge of unfitness" and retained the surgeon in conscious
disregard of patient safety = hospital punitive exposure.

### SIRVA — §3294 ANALYSIS

**Walgreens (§3294(b) employer track):**
If Walgreens knew or had prior reports of injection-related shoulder injuries at that
location (or from that injector) and continued without corrective action = advance
knowledge of unfitness + conscious disregard = §3294(b) employer malice.

**Individual injector (malice or oppression):**
If the injector used a technique known to be contra-indicated for deltoid injection
(injecting too high on the deltoid, into the subdeltoid bursa) and had training to
know this, proceeding with the defective technique = conscious disregard. Whether it
rises to "despicable" depends on the specific facts.

### CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE — TRIAL STANDARD

At §425.13 stage: prima facie case showing (less demanding).
At trial: clear and convincing evidence (heightened).

The distinction matters for strategy: the evidence package that clears §425.13 must be
supplemented with stronger evidence at trial. Expert testimony on the deviation from
standard of care + financial records showing billing motive + outcome evidence will
need to be clear and convincing — not merely persuasive.

**Jury instruction:** CACI 3940-3945 (punitive damages — individual; punitive damages —
entity liability under §3294(b))

### FINANCIAL CONDITION DISCOVERY

Once §425.13 is cleared and punitive damages are pleaded, the plaintiff is entitled to
discover the defendant's financial condition — an otherwise protected category of
information. §3295(c): financial condition information may only be disclosed upon motion
showing good cause + established prima facie case.

For a hospital or medical group: revenue, assets, net worth, profitability of the
specific procedures in question = discoverable after §425.13 is cleared. This
information is critical to calculating a meaningful punitive award.
