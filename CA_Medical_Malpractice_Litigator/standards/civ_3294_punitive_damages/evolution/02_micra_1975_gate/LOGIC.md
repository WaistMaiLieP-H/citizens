# Evolution Stage 02 — MICRA 1975: §425.13 Gate Enacted
## Procedural Gate Added Without Modifying Substantive Standard

### THE WOUND (1975 MICRA Context)

The 1975 MICRA package did not amend §3294's substantive standard. Instead, it created
§425.13 — a procedural gate that required court approval before punitive damages could
even be pleaded in professional negligence actions against health care providers.

The wound being addressed: plaintiffs were including punitive damages claims in medical
malpractice complaints routinely — even without evidence of conduct meeting §3294's
malice/oppression/fraud standard — as leverage for settlement. The mere presence of
a punitive damages claim in a pleading inflated perceived exposure and produced nuisance-
value settlements. Insurers could not easily price this exposure.

### DESIGN RESPONSE (NOT A CHANGE TO §3294 ITSELF)

MICRA's response was procedural, not substantive:
- §3294's "malice, oppression, or fraud" standard remained unchanged
- §425.13 added a procedural filter: you must show prima facie case before pleading
- The two-stage structure emerged: prima facie showing to plead → clear and convincing
  evidence to prove at trial

**Why this matters for §3294 interpretation:**
The §425.13 gate means that every §3294 medical malpractice analysis occurs in two
stages with different evidentiary burdens:
1. §425.13 motion: "substantial probability" = prima facie case; court does not
   resolve credibility; plaintiff presents supporting declarations/records
2. Trial: clear and convincing evidence; jury (or judge) decides

**Strategy implication:** The evidence package built for the §425.13 motion must
be calibrated to survive prima facie scrutiny — but the actual trial burden is higher.
Build more than the minimum §425.13 evidence package.

### PROVENANCE

Stats. 1975, Ch. 2 (MICRA) — enacted §425.13; did NOT amend §3294
§3294 remained in its 1872 codified form through 1975-1987
