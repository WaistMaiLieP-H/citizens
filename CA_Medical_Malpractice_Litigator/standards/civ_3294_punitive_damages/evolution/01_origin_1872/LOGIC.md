# Evolution Stage 01 — Origin 1872 (Field Code)
## Punitive Damages Codified in California Civil Code

### THE WOUND

Before 1872 California courts recognized punitive damages as an equitable remedy in
appropriate cases — following common law tradition. But the standard was common law,
varying by court, with no statutory definition of what conduct warranted punishment.

The Field Code (David Dudley Field's codification project, adopted in California in 1872)
sought to codify all civil law into a comprehensive statutory scheme. Punitive damages
were included as part of this codification effort.

### DESIGN AT ENACTMENT

The 1872 codification established §3294 as a statutory basis for punitive damages where
the defendant had acted with "fraud, malice, express or implied, or oppression." The
original version did not define these terms with the precision that the 1987 amendments
added — it relied on common law understanding of each concept.

**Key design choices:**
- Punitive damages authorized "for the sake of example and by way of punishing the
  defendant" — the dual purposes of deterrence and punishment
- Available only "in addition to the actual damages" — no punitive without compensatory
  award
- Clear and convincing evidence burden — heightened from preponderance; recognized the
  serious nature of punitive awards

**Gap at 1872:** No definition of "malice," "oppression," or "fraud"; no employer
liability limitation; no distinction between corporate and individual defendants.
Courts applied common law definitions with inconsistent results.

### PROVENANCE

Cal. Civil Code §3294 as originally enacted
Stats. 1872 (codification of California Civil Code pursuant to the Field Code project)
