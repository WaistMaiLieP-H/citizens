# Evolution Stage 03 — 1987 Amendment: Definitions + Employer Liability Limitation
## Stats. 1987, Ch. 1498 (AB 3601) — Same Package as §425.13

### THE WOUND

Between 1872 and 1987, §3294's undefined terms — malice, oppression, fraud — had been
applied inconsistently. More critically, corporate employer liability for punitive damages
was expanding dramatically through case law, with some courts holding corporations
vicariously liable for employees' malicious conduct without any requirement that the
malice be authorized or ratified at the management level.

The concern: a single employee acting maliciously could expose a large corporation to
unlimited punitive damages even if management had no knowledge, involvement, or ratification
of the conduct. This created actuarial unpredictability for corporate defendants, particularly
hospitals employing many clinical staff.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1987 AMENDMENTS

AB 3601 (Stats. 1987, Ch. 1498) amended §3294 to add:

**§3294(b) — Employer liability limitation:**
The employer liability limitation established three limited paths to corporate punitive
exposure:
1. Advance knowledge of employee unfitness + conscious disregard
2. Authorization or ratification of wrongful conduct (by officer, director, or managing agent)
3. Employer personally guilty (through officer, director, or managing agent)

**§3294(c) — Definitions:**
Added statutory definitions of malice, oppression, and fraud for the first time:
- Malice: intentional injury OR despicable + conscious disregard
- Oppression: despicable + cruel hardship + conscious disregard
- Fraud: intentional misrepresentation + material fact + intent to deprive/injure

**"Despicable" — the new concept:**
The 1987 amendment introduced "despicable" as the operative modifier for malice
and oppression. College Hospital (1994) later defined "despicable" as conduct "so
vile, base, contemptible, miserable, wretched or loathsome that it would be looked
down upon and despised by ordinary decent people."

### LOGICAL DELTA FROM PRE-1987

| Element | Pre-1987 | Post-1987 |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| "Malice" definition | Common law | Statutory: intentional injury OR despicable + conscious disregard |
| "Oppression" definition | Common law | Statutory: despicable + cruel hardship + conscious disregard |
| "Fraud" definition | Common law | Statutory: intentional misrepresentation + material fact + intent to deprive |
| Corporate employer liability | Common law respondeat superior for punitive | §3294(b): advance knowledge OR authorization/ratification OR personally guilty |
| "Despicable" | Not in statute | New concept; defined by College Hospital (1994) |
| Managing agent requirement | Not required | Required for employer liability through authorization/ratification path |

### SIGNIFICANCE

The 1987 amendments are the current operative version of §3294. Everything from the
1872 codification was either retained (clear and convincing standard, dual purposes)
or superseded by the 1987 definitions. The §3294(c) definitions are the controlling
substantive framework for all California punitive damages analysis.
