# Diff — 1975 Origin → AB 35 (2022) Amendment

## What changed in the text

**Pre-2023 (Original §3333.2(b)):**
> In no action shall the amount of damages for noneconomic losses exceed two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000).

**Post-2023 (AB 35 §3333.2(b)):**
> (1) [Non-death cases] — Tiered amounts starting at $350,000 (2023), escalating $40K/year through 2033, then CPI-U indexed.
> (2) [Death cases] — Tiered amounts starting at $500,000 (2023), escalating $50K/year through 2033, then CPI-U indexed.

## What changed in structure

1. **Single cap → two caps.** Death cases and non-death cases are treated differently for the first time. Death cases receive a higher cap, reflecting the political compromise that wrongful death was the most constrained category under the 1975 statute.

2. **Flat cap → escalating cap.** The static 1975 number is replaced by a mandatory annual increase. Each year of filing moves the cap up until 2033.

3. **Escalation → indexing.** After 2033, the cap automatically indexes to CPI-U. This removes the 47-year erosion pattern that characterized the 1975-2022 regime.

4. **Multi-defendant clarification.** The original §3333.2 was silent on whether the cap applied per-defendant or per-action. Case law resolved it (one cap per provider, plus a separate institution cap under Lathrop v. HealthCare Partners), but the text did not. AB 35 codified the "one cap for all non-institutional providers + one separate cap for all institutional providers" rule.

5. **Death + injury stacking.** The new text expressly addresses the situation where a single course of negligence causes both personal injury and later death. Only the higher of the two caps applies. This resolves a previously ambiguous question.

## What did NOT change

1. **Economic damages remain uncapped.** Medical specials, lost earnings, future care, and other economic losses are not affected by §3333.2 at all. This was true in 1975 and remains true under AB 35.

2. **The definition of "professional negligence"** (§3333.2(e)(3)) is essentially unchanged. A claim based on intentional conduct (battery, fraud, intentional infliction of emotional distress) is not "professional negligence" and is not subject to the cap. This is a critical point for the spine surgery fraud case.

3. **The definition of "health care provider"** is essentially unchanged from the 1975 original.

4. **The cap does not apply to punitive damages.** Punitive damages are authorized under Civ. Code §3294 in MICRA cases (subject to the CCP §425.13 pleading-control rule) and are not capped by §3333.2.

5. **The cap does not apply to prejudgment interest or attorney fees.**

## Why this diff matters for the steward

The steward's SIRVA case (04-22 Shoulder Surgery) will likely be filed in 2026, placing it under the $470,000 non-death cap. This is $220,000 higher than the 1975 legacy cap would have been — a meaningful difference in the damages model but still a material constraint.

The steward's spine surgery fraud case (11-21 Spine Surgery Fraud), if pled as fraud (not professional negligence), may escape the §3333.2 cap entirely under the "professional negligence" definitional limit in §3333.2(e)(3). This is a critical pleading decision — the difference between a capped and an uncapped claim. The Citizen must evaluate the evidentiary support for fraud pleading and the §425.13 punitive-damages pleading motion in tandem.
