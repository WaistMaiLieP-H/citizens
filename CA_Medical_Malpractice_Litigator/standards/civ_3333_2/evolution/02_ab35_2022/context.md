# Context — AB 35 (2022), the First Significant §3333.2 Amendment

## The political setting

By 2022, the MICRA cap had been static for 47 years while inflation had reduced its real value to less than a quarter of the 1975 level. California plaintiffs' advocates mobilized a ballot initiative — the Fairness for Injured Patients Act (FIPA) — which would have adjusted the cap to account for inflation, retroactively applying a compounded CPI-U adjustment to raise the cap to approximately $1.2 million (non-death).

The initiative was on track for the November 2022 ballot and was polling favorably. The medical association and insurance industry faced the likely loss of the static $250,000 cap entirely.

## The negotiation

Consumer Attorneys of California (CAOC), the California Medical Association (CMA), the California Hospital Association (CHA), and insurance industry stakeholders entered direct negotiations in early 2022. The goal was a legislative compromise that both sides could accept in exchange for the plaintiffs' bar withdrawing the initiative.

The resulting bill, AB 35, passed both houses with near-unanimous support and was signed by Governor Gavin Newsom on May 23, 2022. The initiative was withdrawn from the November 2022 ballot.

## The substance of the compromise

Both sides got something:

**Plaintiffs' bar got:**
- Immediate cap increase (from $250K to $350K non-death, $500K death)
- Annual escalators ($40K/year non-death, $50K/year death) through 2033
- Inflation indexing beginning 2034 (ending 47 years of erosion)
- Higher attorney fee sliding scale in B&P §6146 (25% instead of 15% at the top tier), making plaintiff representation more economically viable
- Codification of favorable multi-defendant cap rules

**Providers/insurers got:**
- FIPA initiative withdrawn
- Long-term stability of the MICRA framework
- Cap structure preserved (not abolished)
- Predictable escalation rather than potentially uncontrolled cap removal
- Continued exclusion of economic damages from the cap (the status quo favorable element)

## Effective date

AB 35 took effect January 1, 2023, and applies to actions **filed** or **arbitration demanded** on or after that date, regardless of when the underlying negligence occurred. This is the key operational rule: it is the filing date, not the injury date, that determines which cap applies.

## Current posture (2026)

AB 35's structure is fully in effect. The 2026 caps are:
- Non-death: $470,000 ($350K + $40K × 3)
- Death: $650,000 ($500K + $50K × 3)

No challenge to AB 35 has been mounted. The compromise appears stable for the foreseeable future.

## Why this matters for the steward

The steward's eight medical cases will file under AB 35. The specific 2026 caps apply. For the SIRVA (shoulder surgery) case, which does not involve death, the cap is $470,000. For any death case (none in the current CaseList), the cap would be $650,000.

The Citizen must factor these caps into every damages model, every settlement evaluation, and every trial-plan noneconomic presentation. Importantly, the Citizen must also identify opportunities to plead claims that escape the cap entirely — fraud, battery, intentional infliction, civil rights claims against public hospitals — where the facts support them.
