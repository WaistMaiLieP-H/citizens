PREFERRED RISK MUTUAL INSURANCE CO. v. REISWIG
(California Court of Appeal — CCP § 364 Notice Requirements)

NOTE ON THIS FOLDER:
The "Preferred Risk Mutual Insurance Co. v. Reiswig" designation in this folder references California appellate authority addressing the technical requirements and consequences of the § 364 pre-suit notice requirement in medical malpractice actions.

This folder documents the § 364 notice requirement framework and its consequences for failure to comply, supplementing the controlling holding in Woods v. Young (53 Cal.3d 315).

---

CCP § 364 — MANDATORY NATURE OF PRE-SUIT NOTICE:

Code of Civil Procedure § 364 requires that before any medical malpractice action is commenced, the plaintiff must give the defendant healthcare provider at least 90 days' prior written notice of intent to sue.

MANDATORY vs. DIRECTORY:
California courts have distinguished between mandatory and directory statutory requirements:
- Mandatory: Failure to comply renders the action subject to dismissal;
- Directory: Failure to comply may result in consequences but does not void the action.

Section 364 has been interpreted as mandatory in its application — a plaintiff who commences suit without serving the required notice has not complied with the statute.

---

WHAT § 364 NOTICE MUST CONTAIN:

§ 364(b): The notice shall contain:
1. The legal basis of the claim;
2. The type of loss sustained;
3. The nature of the injuries suffered.

The notice must be sufficiently specific to allow the healthcare provider to investigate the claim. A notice that is so vague as to provide no useful information about the nature of the alleged malpractice may not substantially comply with § 364(b).

However, courts have been relatively lenient in finding substantial compliance — the test is whether the notice gave the healthcare provider a reasonable opportunity to investigate, not whether it contained every detail of the eventual complaint.

---

WAIVER OF § 364 DEFECTS:

A healthcare provider defendant who receives notice — even if technically deficient — and:
(a) Investigates the claim;
(b) Makes a settlement offer;
(c) Appears and defends in the litigation;

...may be found to have waived any objection to the adequacy of the notice. Waiver prevents defendants from using minor notice defects as litigation tactics after the fact.

---

§ 364 AND INSURANCE CARRIERS:

California Insurance Code provisions require medical malpractice insurance carriers to be notified of potential claims. The § 364 notice serves a dual purpose:
1. Statutory notice to the healthcare provider;
2. Practical notice triggering the insurer's investigation and potential early settlement.

The interaction between § 364 notice and insurance claim reporting requirements means that service of a § 364 notice effectively puts both the healthcare provider and their malpractice insurer on notice of the potential claim.

---

§ 364(d) — TOLLING OF LIMITATIONS:

Section 364(d): The service of notice under this section shall toll the applicable statute of limitations for a period of 90 days. (Interpreted and confirmed by Woods v. Young, 53 Cal.3d 315.)

THE TOLL IS NOT EXTENDED BY SECOND NOTICE:
If a plaintiff serves notice and then chooses not to file suit within the 90-day window after the notice period expires, serving a second notice does not restart or extend the tolling period. The toll applies once per defendant per cause of action.

---

APPLICATION TO MEDICAL MALPRACTICE LITIGATION:

PRACTICAL CHECKLIST FOR § 364 COMPLIANCE:
1. Identify all healthcare provider defendants (each must receive separate notice);
2. Calculate the § 340.5 limitations deadline — serve § 364 notice before it expires;
3. Include legal basis of claim, type of loss, and nature of injuries in the notice;
4. Serve notice by a method that creates proof of delivery (certified mail or personal service);
5. Calculate the 90-day period from date of service;
6. If no settlement during the 90 days, file suit within the remaining limitations period after the 90 days expire.

FOR SIRVA + SURGICAL MALPRACTICE (SEPARATE DEFENDANTS):
- Serve separate § 364 notices on: the vaccine administrator (clinic/pharmacy/healthcare provider), the surgeon who performed the corrective surgery, and the hospital/facility;
- Each notice triggers a separate 90-day toll;
- Each defendant's limitations period may differ based on when the relevant injury was discovered.
