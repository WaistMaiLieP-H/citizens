# Preferred Risk Mutual Insurance Co. v. Reiswig (1999)

**Cite:** 21 Cal.4th 208
**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Date:** August 19, 1999
**Opinion by:** Justice Werdegar

## The rule the case stands for

**The §364(d) tolling provision tolls the §340.5 statute of limitations by 90 days regardless of whether the notice of intent was required by §364 to have been served at all.** In other words, if a plaintiff serves a §364 notice within 90 days of the SOL expiration, the SOL is extended — even in circumstances where the plaintiff might have had some theoretical argument that notice was not required.

The case also clarified that §364(d) applies to BOTH the one-year and three-year triggers of §340.5, not just the one-year trigger.

## Plain-language restatement

A plaintiff was uncertain whether CCP §364 applied to her specific factual scenario (there was a defendant classification question). Out of caution, she served §364 notice within 90 days of the limitations deadline. She then filed suit, relying on §364(d)'s tolling to extend the SOL.

The defendant argued that because §364 did not technically apply (the defendant argued), the notice was a nullity and the tolling provision could not extend the SOL. The California Supreme Court rejected the argument. The court held that the tolling provision operates on the act of serving the notice, not on a pre-determination of whether notice was legally required.

## The doctrinal significance

Preferred Risk is important because it gives plaintiffs the benefit of the tolling provision in borderline or disputed cases. The Citizen does not have to be 100% certain that §364 applies to a particular defendant in order to serve notice and get the 90-day extension. Serving notice in uncertainty is a protective move that costs nothing — if §364 applies, compliance is preserved; if §364 doesn't apply, the SOL is still tolled under Preferred Risk.

Three specific takeaways:

1. **Serve in doubt.** If there is any question whether a particular defendant is subject to §364, serve the notice. The tolling benefit attaches regardless.

2. **Tolling applies to both §340.5 triggers.** Both the one-year discovery trigger and the three-year outer limit can be extended by §364(d) when notice is served within 90 days of the applicable trigger.

3. **The tolling is automatic and non-discretionary.** A court cannot refuse to apply the tolling based on some equitable consideration.

## Why this matters for the steward's SIRVA case

For the SIRVA case, there is legitimate uncertainty about whether §364 technically applies to Walgreens as a corporate entity (as distinguished from the individual pharmacist). Under Preferred Risk, the Citizen should serve §364 notice on BOTH Walgreens corporate AND the individual pharmacist regardless of this uncertainty. The tolling benefit applies in either case.

The Citizen should also remember that §364(d) tolling applies to the one-year discovery trigger (currently 2027-03-18 for the SIRVA case). If the Citizen discovers new facts late in the SOL period that support additional claims or defendants, serving supplemental §364 notices for those additional claims/defendants preserves the tolling for those claims even if they are late-discovered.
