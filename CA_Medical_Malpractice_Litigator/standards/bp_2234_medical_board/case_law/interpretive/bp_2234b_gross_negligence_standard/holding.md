# §2234(b) Gross Negligence — Statutory Definition (Anchor Entry)

**Entry type:** STATUTORY ANCHOR — not a case holding
**Primary authority:** Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §2234(b)
**Why this entry exists:** §2234(b) defines "gross negligence" in the statute's own text.
The rule comes directly from the Legislature, not from a court. This entry holds that
statutory definition as the operative rule for corpus purposes.

---

## The Rule

**"Gross negligence"** means **an extreme departure from the applicable standard of care,
whether applied in the same or similar community.**

*(Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §2234(b), verbatim)*

---

## What This Means in Practice

**"Extreme departure"** is a higher threshold than civil malpractice negligence:

- Civil malpractice (MICRA): any deviation from the standard of care
- Medical Board gross negligence: the deviation must be *extreme* — so far below the
  standard as to represent reckless or conscious disregard for patient safety, not merely
  a below-standard clinical judgment call

**"Applicable standard of care"** imports the same-or-similar-community baseline used in
civil malpractice: what a reasonably competent physician of the same specialty would do.
The "extreme departure" threshold is then applied on top of that baseline.

**Single episode sufficiency:** Unlike §2234(c) (repeated negligent acts), a single episode
can support a §2234(b) gross negligence finding if the departure is extreme enough.

---

## Relevance to Corpus Cases

**Spine surgery fraud:** If Dr. Wiseman performed surgery with no genuine medical indication,
that is not a below-standard clinical judgment — it is an extreme departure. Operating on a
patient who did not meet surgical criteria is the paradigm case for §2234(b).

**SIRVA / improper injection technique:** If an administering pharmacist used an injection
site and technique so far below the deltoid-injection standard of care that the needle
entered the subacromial bursa, that extreme technical failure may support §2234(b) in
addition to civil malpractice.
