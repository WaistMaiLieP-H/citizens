GROSS NEGLIGENCE STANDARD UNDER BUSINESS & PROFESSIONS CODE § 2234(b)
(Medical Board of California Disciplinary Standards — Case Law Interpretive)

NOTE: This folder documents the California Medical Board's disciplinary standard for "gross negligence" under Business & Professions Code § 2234(b) through the relevant case law. This is an administrative/disciplinary standard, and the primary authority consists of Medical Board decisions, OAH decisions, and California court decisions reviewing those determinations.

---

BUSINESS & PROFESSIONS CODE § 2234(b) — GROSS NEGLIGENCE:

Section 2234 provides for disciplinary action against a physician's and surgeon's certificate holder for:
"(b) Gross negligence."

STATUTORY DEFINITION — GROSS NEGLIGENCE IN CALIFORNIA MEDICAL BOARD CONTEXT:
California courts and the Medical Board have defined "gross negligence" in the § 2234(b) context as:

An extreme departure from the standard of care which, under similar circumstances, would have been recognized by a reasonable medical practitioner as creating an unreasonable risk of harm to the patient. Gross negligence differs from ordinary negligence in that it involves a more extreme, reckless disregard for the patient's well-being.

The standard is higher than ordinary negligence (§ 2234(c)) but does not require intentional conduct.

---

KEY ELEMENTS OF GROSS NEGLIGENCE UNDER § 2234(b):

1. EXTREME DEPARTURE: The departure from the standard of care must be extreme, not merely substandard. A medical error alone does not constitute gross negligence.

2. OBJECTIVELY RECOGNIZABLE RISK: A reasonable medical practitioner in the same specialty, under similar circumstances, would have recognized the departure as creating an unreasonable risk of harm.

3. SINGLE INCIDENT: Unlike § 2234(c) (repeated negligent acts, which requires a pattern), gross negligence can be established by a single egregious incident.

4. CAUSATION IS NOT REQUIRED FOR DISCIPLINE: For disciplinary purposes under § 2234(b), the Medical Board need not prove that the gross negligence caused actual patient harm. The extreme departure from the standard of care is itself the basis for discipline.

---

EXAMPLES OF CONDUCT HELD TO CONSTITUTE GROSS NEGLIGENCE:

California OAH decisions and Medical Board cases have found gross negligence where:
- A surgeon operated on the wrong site or wrong patient;
- A physician discharged a patient in acute distress without adequate evaluation;
- A prescribing physician provided controlled substances without examination or monitoring;
- A physician falsified or failed to maintain required medical records during an ongoing treatment relationship;
- Post-operative complications were ignored despite clear warning signs.

---

RELATIONSHIP TO CIVIL MALPRACTICE STANDARD:

The Medical Board's § 2234(b) gross negligence standard is not identical to civil malpractice:
- Civil malpractice: ordinary negligence (departure from the standard of care causing injury);
- § 2234(b) discipline: extreme departure from the standard of care (gross negligence);
- The conduct that constitutes § 2234(b) gross negligence is typically also civil malpractice, but the reverse is not always true.

Medical Board findings of gross negligence (even if the physician is not criminally charged) are admissible in civil proceedings and can support civil malpractice claims based on the same conduct.

---

APPLICATION TO MEDICAL FRAUD / SURGICAL MALPRACTICE:

For a case involving surgery performed without adequate patient assessment, on a patient who lacked informed consent, or with clear disregard for post-surgical complications:
1. If the conduct constitutes an extreme departure from the standard of care, § 2234(b) provides a disciplinary pathway;
2. The Medical Board's investigation and findings are separate from the civil case but can provide corroborating evidence;
3. A § 2234(b) finding that the conduct was grossly negligent supports punitive damages in civil litigation (Civil Code § 3294(c)(2) — despicable conduct with conscious disregard for safety).

FOR SIRVA CASES: A botched vaccine injection (incorrect site, incorrect depth, failure to follow vaccination protocol) by a trained healthcare provider who knew or should have known the correct technique is an extreme departure from the standard of care — potentially gross negligence under § 2234(b).
