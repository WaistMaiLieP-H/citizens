# CCP §425.13 Historical Chain — Punitive Damages Gate in Medical Malpractice

## The wound (pre-1988)

Pre-§425.13, punitive damages could be included in a medical malpractice complaint without any
threshold showing. The prayer served primarily as settlement leverage — defendants faced financial-
condition discovery and reputational exposure from the moment a complaint was filed, regardless
of whether the facts supported malice or fraud. The Legislature concluded this was being systematically
abused to force settlements in cases that did not genuinely involve punitive-level conduct.

---

## 1988: §425.13 enacted — pre-pleading gate

Stats. 1987, Ch. 1498 enacted §425.13 as part of a broader MICRA-adjacent package. The gate
required plaintiffs to obtain court permission — supported by evidence — before including a punitive
damages prayer in a professional negligence action against a health care provider.

---

## 1992: Central Pathology — substance-over-label scope test

Central Pathology Service Medical Clinic v. Superior Court (1992) established that §425.13 cannot
be evaded by relabeling a professional negligence claim as an intentional tort. The gate applies
based on the substance of the claim — whether it arises from professional conduct — not the label.
Genuine independent intentional torts remain outside §425.13's scope.

---

## 1994: College Hospital — "despicable" defined; §425.13 standard settled

College Hospital, Inc. v. Superior Court (1994) provided the California Supreme Court's authoritative
interpretation of both §3294's "despicable conduct" standard and §425.13's "substantial probability"
threshold. The prima facie case standard (less than summary judgment; more than notice pleading) is
the governing measure. Financial motive + conscious disregard = despicable.

---

## Current framework (2026)

§425.13 remains a procedural gating requirement in professional negligence actions against health
care providers. The College Hospital standard (prima facie case showing, despicable conduct) and
the Central Pathology scope test (substance-over-label) together define the motion practice.

**The arc:** Punitive prayer abuse (pre-1988) → gate enacted (1988) → scope defined by substance
not label (Central Pathology 1992) → threshold standard settled (College Hospital 1994) → current
stable framework.
