# Statute Version at Time of College Hospital (1994)

**§3294(a) version:** Post-1987 amendment (Stats. 1987, Ch. 1498 — same package as §425.13).
"In an action for the breach of an obligation not arising from contract, where it is proven by
clear and convincing evidence that the defendant has been guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice,
the plaintiff, in addition to the actual damages, may recover damages for the sake of example
and by way of punishing the defendant."

**§3294(c)(1) version (malice definition):**
"'Malice' means conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff or
despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard
of the rights or safety of others."

**Current operative text:** §§3294(a) and (c)(1) are unchanged from 1987. College Hospital's
"despicable" definition controls all §425.13 motion practice and all §3294 punitive damages
analysis in medical malpractice cases.

**Note:** College Hospital is also the primary authority on §3294(c)(1) generally — not just
in the medical malpractice context. The "despicable conduct" definition applies in all §3294
punitive damages cases.
