# Central Pathology Service Medical Clinic v. Superior Court — Holding

**Citation:** 3 Cal.App.4th 181 (1992)
**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District
**Decided:** 1992

## Facts

A patient brought a professional negligence action against a pathology clinic and included a
punitive damages prayer without first filing a §425.13 motion. The clinic moved to strike the
punitive damages allegations. The patient argued that §425.13 did not apply because the conduct
was intentional, not merely negligent.

## Holding

**§425.13 applies to actions "arising out of" professional negligence even when the plaintiff
labels the cause of action as intentional tort**, if the underlying conduct is the same
conduct that forms the professional negligence claim.

The test is substance over label: if the plaintiff's claim — however characterized — is
fundamentally about conduct in the provision of professional health care services, §425.13
applies. Relabeling a negligence claim as "battery" or "intentional infliction" to avoid
§425.13 does not work.

**However:** The Court acknowledged that **genuine** intentional torts independent of the
standard of care are outside §425.13's scope. The issue is whether the complained-of conduct
is professional conduct or independent intentional conduct.

## Rule distilled

§425.13 applies when:
- The action arises out of professional negligence (professional services provided to the patient)
- Even if nominally framed as an intentional tort
- The gate cannot be evaded by relabeling

§425.13 does NOT apply when:
- The claim rests on conduct truly independent of professional services
- Pure fraud unrelated to the quality of clinical care
- Battery based on complete absence of consent (not just insufficient disclosure)

## Significance for spine surgery fraud case

Central Pathology raises the risk that framing the spine surgery as battery/fraud does not
automatically avoid §425.13. The safer approach:

**File the §425.13 motion in parallel with the intentional tort pleading.** If the court
accepts the intentional tort framing: §425.13 moot, punitive prayer stands. If the court
applies Central Pathology and requires §425.13: the motion is ready.

Evidence for the §425.13 motion: expert opinion that surgery was not clinically indicated
(+ financial motive) = conscious disregard satisfying College Hospital's despicable standard.
