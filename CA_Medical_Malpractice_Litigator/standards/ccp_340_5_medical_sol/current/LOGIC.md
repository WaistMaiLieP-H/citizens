# Cal. Code Civ. Proc. §340.5 — Medical Malpractice Statute of Limitations
## Current Operative Architecture

### RULE

§340.5 sets the limitations period for professional negligence actions against health
care providers. It is MICRA's SOL provision and operates as the first gate on every
medical malpractice claim.

**Operative text (§340.5):**
"In an action for injury or death against a health care provider based upon such person's
alleged professional negligence, the time for the commencement of action shall be three
years after the date of injury or one year after the plaintiff discovers, or through the
use of reasonable diligence should have discovered, the injury, whichever occurs first."

**The three-year outer limit:** Absolute except as otherwise provided. No discovery
rule extends the period beyond three years from the date of injury (with exceptions).

**The one-year discovery trigger:** Runs from the date the plaintiff discovered or
through reasonable diligence should have discovered: (1) the injury, and (2) the cause
of the injury.

**Four statutory tolling provisions (§340.5, second paragraph):**
1. **Minor plaintiff:** If plaintiff is under 6 years old at the time of injury, the
   3-year period runs from the 8th birthday. For minors 6 and older, the general rule
   applies (1 year discovery / 3 year outer).
2. **Fraud or intentional concealment:** If defendant fraudulently concealed the
   existence of a cause of action, the time does not begin to run until the cause of
   action is discovered.
3. **Presence of a foreign body:** If a foreign body with no therapeutic or diagnostic
   purpose is negligently left in the body, the time does not run until discovery of
   the foreign body or the date the foreign body should have been discovered.
4. **Legal or physical disability:** Time is tolled during any period in which the
   plaintiff is under a legal disability or mentally incapacitated.

### THE WOUND THIS LAW ANSWERED

Before MICRA (1975), California professional negligence claims against health care
providers were governed by general tort SOL provisions — typically 2-3 years from
discovery under CCP §338 or common law. California courts had applied the discovery rule
broadly: the SOL did not begin to run until the plaintiff discovered the injury AND
its negligent cause.

Medical insurers and the California Medical Association argued that open-ended discovery
tolling made medical malpractice claims unpredictable — a physician could face suit for
an error discovered 10-15 years after the act. Rising malpractice insurance premiums
were cited (whether accurately or not) as driving physicians from California practice.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — MICRA §340.5

The Legislature enacted the 3-year outer limit to provide a finite end point regardless
of discovery. Within that 3-year window, the 1-year discovery rule was retained —
giving plaintiffs who discover their injury promptly a full year to file, but capping
total exposure at 3 years from the date of injury.

**Key design tension:** The 3-year absolute bar means a plaintiff who discovers an
injury on day 1,080 (just after the 3-year mark) is completely time-barred — even if
the discovery was only possible on that date. California courts have applied this rule
strictly in most contexts.

**Exception: fraud and intentional concealment:** If the health care provider
fraudulently concealed the injury or its cause — e.g., altered medical records,
misrepresented the cause of a complication, failed to inform the patient of an adverse
outcome — the 3-year absolute limit is suspended during the concealment period.

### DISCOVERY RULE — TWO-COMPONENT INQUIRY

The one-year discovery period begins when the plaintiff knows or should know:
1. **The injury** — the physical harm or adverse outcome (not merely that something
   went wrong, but that the outcome caused cognizable harm)
2. **The negligent cause** — that the injury was caused by the health care provider's
   negligence, not merely that something bad happened

**Jolly v. Eli Lilly (1988) test:** The plaintiff must suspect (not prove) that the
injury was wrongfully caused. Once a reasonable person in the plaintiff's position would
suspect negligence, the one-year period begins. The plaintiff need not know the specific
acts of negligence — only that an injury may have been wrongfully caused.

**Imputed knowledge:** The one-year period also begins when the plaintiff *should have*
discovered through reasonable diligence. If obvious signs of a surgical error existed
and the plaintiff did not investigate, the SOL may have run even if the plaintiff
subjectively did not know.

### SIRVA APPLICATION

**The SIRVA discovery rule calculation:**
- Date of injury: approximately November 2021 (COVID vaccine injection at Walgreens Brentwood)
- The SIRVA injury (shoulder pain, nerve damage) was immediately apparent — symptoms began
  shortly after injection
- However, the *cause* — negligent injection technique by Walgreens injector — requires
  medical attribution of the shoulder injury to the injection site error rather than
  the vaccine itself
- Discovery date for §340.5 purposes: the date Michael first had reason to suspect the
  shoulder injury was caused by negligent injection rather than a vaccine reaction

**Why discovery rule matters for SIRVA:**
A COVID vaccine recipient who experiences shoulder pain will likely attribute it to
the vaccine (expected side effect) for some period before receiving a diagnosis
identifying the injury as SIRVA (Shoulder Injury Related to Vaccine Administration).
The period between injection (November 2021) and SIRVA diagnosis by Dr. Wiseman
(leading to surgery April 20, 2022) represents the window during which discovery was
not yet complete under the Jolly test.

**SIRVA SOL calculation:**
- Injury: November 2021
- Surgery: April 20, 2022 (Dr. Wiseman — this surgery produced worse outcomes)
- Discovery of negligent cause (SIRVA diagnosis): must be pinpointed from medical records
- 1-year from discovery: runs from SIRVA diagnosis date
- 3-year outer limit: November 2024 (3 years from injection)
- Discovery rule: if SIRVA was not diagnosable until after surgery, discovery period
  may be tolled to the post-surgical period — but must file within 1 year of that
  discovery date AND before November 2024

**Critical deadline:** If SIRVA was first diagnosed in spring 2022 (pre-surgery or at
surgery), the 1-year discovery period ran from spring 2022 → spring 2023. If the SIRVA
diagnosis was only possible after surgery (outcome analysis), discovery runs from
post-surgical period. The 3-year outer limit (November 2024) is the hard backstop.

**Fraud/concealment tolling:** If Walgreens or Dr. Wiseman concealed the SIRVA nature
of the injury (e.g., mischaracterized it as vaccine reaction rather than injection site
error), concealment tolling suspends the §340.5 clock during the concealment period.

### SPINE SURGERY FRAUD APPLICATION

For the spine surgery fraud (Case #11):
- Date of injury: surgery date (2016 or 2017 — confirm from records)
- Discovery of cause: the plaintiff must have suspected the surgery was not clinically
  indicated on or before the date they sought a second opinion or received information
  suggesting the surgery was unnecessary
- The fact that post-surgical outcomes were worse than pre-surgical = immediate notice
  that something went wrong; the further step (the surgery was not indicated in the
  first place) requires medical attribution
- Fraudulent concealment tolling: if the surgeon misrepresented the clinical necessity
  of the surgery or altered pre-operative documentation to make the surgery appear
  indicated, concealment tolling applies

### HRIMNAK ESCAPE AND §340.5

The Hrimnak escape (intentional tort framing — battery/fraud rather than professional
negligence) has SOL implications:

- Professional negligence: §340.5 (1 year discovery / 3 year outer)
- Intentional tort (battery, fraud): CCP §335.1 (2 years from injury) for battery;
  CCP §338(d) (3 years from discovery) for fraud

If the Hrimnak escape applies and the claim is framed as intentional tort, the longer
general tort SOL may apply rather than §340.5. This is another reason the intentional
tort framing is strategically valuable: it potentially buys more time AND removes MICRA
caps simultaneously.

**Caution:** Central Pathology's substance-over-label test means courts will look at the
true nature of the claim. If the court finds the claim is professional negligence despite
intentional tort labeling, §340.5 applies and the shorter SOL controls. File the §425.13
motion in the alternative precisely because of this risk.

### PRE-SUIT NOTICE — CCP §364

§364 requires 90-day pre-suit notice to health care providers before filing a medical
malpractice action. If the defendant is served with notice under §364, the SOL is
tolled for 90 days during the notice period (§364(d)). This creates a 90-day extension
to the §340.5 calculation when notice is timely served.

**Interaction:** If the §340.5 period would expire within the 90-day §364 notice period,
the plaintiff gets the full 90 days after notice to file. Notice must be served BEFORE
the SOL expires (not after).

### FRAUDULENT CONCEALMENT TOLLING — DETAILED

For medical cases, fraudulent concealment requires:
1. The defendant actively concealed the existence of the cause of action
2. The plaintiff did not know and could not reasonably have known of the cause of action
3. The plaintiff acted with reasonable diligence once the concealment was discovered

**Medical record alteration = concealment:**
If a health care provider altered records to misrepresent the clinical course, indication
for surgery, or cause of an adverse outcome, that alteration is fraudulent concealment.
The §340.5 clock does not run during the period of undetectable concealment.

**Evidence of alteration:**
Metadata from electronic health records can establish when entries were made or modified.
VERITAS-0 forensic audit tools apply here: timestamp analysis, metadata extraction,
sequential entry patterns that suggest after-the-fact modification.
