# Historical Chain — Cal. Code Civ. Proc. §340.5 (Medical Malpractice SOL)

## Chain Summary

§340.5's evolution reflects the tension between two competing interests: plaintiff's
access to courts when injury discovery is delayed vs. defendant's interest in finality.
MICRA resolved this by retaining the discovery rule within a 3-year hard cap — and
subsequent case law defined exactly when discovery triggers the clock.

## Stage 1 — Pre-MICRA (Pre-1975)

**Wound:** No specific medical SOL; general personal injury provisions + broad discovery
rule left SOL exposure open-ended; medical malpractice insurance premiums rising.

**State:** Open-ended discovery rule for professional negligence.

## Stage 2 — MICRA Enactment 1975 (Stats. 1975, Ch. 2, 2d Ex. Sess.)

**Wound:** Actuarial unpredictability of medical malpractice exposure; broad discovery
rule theoretically allowed decades of SOL exposure.

**Response:** §340.5 — 1 year discovery + 3 year absolute outer limit; four exceptions.

**Constitutional challenge:** Upheld in American Bank & Trust v. Community Hospital (1984).

## Stage 3 — Discovery Rule Refinement (Jolly 1988 + Foxborough 1994)

**Wound:** Inconsistent application of "discovers or should have discovered" — courts
split on whether specific knowledge or mere suspicion triggered the period.

**Response:**
- Jolly (1988): "reason to suspect" standard; duty to investigate once suspicion arises
- Foxborough (1994): physician reassurance can delay trigger; second opinion = classic
  discovery event; objective reasonable-person standard

## Current State

§340.5 as enacted 1975, interpreted through Jolly (1988) and Foxborough (1994):
- 1 year from "reason to suspect" negligent cause (Jolly)
- 3 year absolute outer limit from date of injury
- Physician reassurance can delay "reason to suspect" trigger (Foxborough)
- Four exceptions: minor, fraud/concealment, foreign body, disability
- §364 pre-suit notice tolls for 90 days during notice period
- Hrimnak escape: intentional tort framing → general tort SOL may apply (CCP §335.1 / §338(d)) instead

## Cases in Chain

1. Jolly v. Eli Lilly & Co., 44 Cal.3d 1103 (1988) — "reason to suspect" discovery trigger
2. Foxborough v. Van Atta, 26 Cal.App.4th 217 (1994) — physician reassurance; second opinion
