# Evolution Stage 02 — MICRA 1975: §340.5 Enacted
## Stats. 1975, Ch. 2 (2d Ex. Sess.) — The Core Statute

### THE WOUND

The California Legislature convened in second extraordinary session in 1975 to address
what was characterized as a medical malpractice insurance crisis. A combination of
rising jury verdicts and investment portfolio losses by insurers had produced dramatic
premium increases. Several insurers threatened to exit the California market.

The Legislature's response was MICRA — Medical Injury Compensation Reform Act —
a package of bills designed to cap insurer exposure from multiple angles simultaneously.
§340.5 was one of seven MICRA provisions acting in concert:
- §340.5: SOL (1 year discovery / 3 year outer)
- §3333.2: Noneconomic damages cap ($250,000)
- §3333.1: Collateral source rule modification
- §425.13: Punitive damages gate
- §6146: Attorney fee cap
- §667.7: Periodic payment of judgments
- §364: Pre-suit notice requirement

### DESIGN RESPONSE — §340.5

**The two-track structure:**
- 1 year from discovery (retained from pre-MICRA law)
- 3 years from injury (new absolute outer limit)
- Whichever comes first is the operative deadline

**Four exceptions preserved:**
1. Minor (under 6): extended to 8th birthday
2. Fraud/concealment: suspended during concealment period
3. Foreign body: suspended until discovery
4. Legal/physical disability: suspended during disability

**The hard cut:** The 3-year absolute limit was the major legislative departure from
pre-MICRA law. Under the old discovery rule, a patient who could not have discovered
the injury within 3 years had no remedy — but the discovery rule might have kept
the SOL open. MICRA cut this off: 3 years from injury regardless of discovery.

### CONSTITUTIONAL CHALLENGE

MICRA's damages cap (§3333.2) and the SOL (§340.5) were both challenged as denying
equal protection and due process. The California Supreme Court upheld MICRA in
American Bank & Trust Co. v. Community Hospital (1984) and subsequent cases:
- Legislature had rational basis for addressing insurance crisis
- Reductions in claimant rights did not violate equal protection when the overall
  MICRA package provided an offsetting benefit (insurance availability)

This constitutional holding is foundational: §340.5 is presumptively valid as enacted.
The constitutional challenge is foreclosed by existing precedent.

### LOGICAL DELTA FROM PRE-MICRA

| Element | Pre-MICRA | Post-MICRA (§340.5) |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|
| Discovery rule | Broad; ran from discovery of injury + cause | Retained; 1 year from discovery |
| Outer limit | None (discovery rule could run indefinitely) | 3 years from date of injury — absolute |
| Minor plaintiff | General disability tolling | Special rule: 8th birthday for under-6 |
| Fraud concealment | Broadly tolled | Preserved but narrowly construed |
| Predictability | Low for insurers | High — 3-year maximum exposure window |

### PROVENANCE

Medical Injury Compensation Reform Act (MICRA)
Stats. 1975, Ch. 2 (2d Extraordinary Session)
Signed by Governor Edmund G. Brown Jr.
Effective: January 1, 1976 for actions accruing on or after that date
§340.5 codified in Code of Civil Procedure
