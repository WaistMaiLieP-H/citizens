# Evolution Stage 03 — Wrongful Death Coordination and Discovery Rule Refinements
## Jolly v. Eli Lilly (1988) and Subsequent Case Law Clarifying Discovery Trigger

### THE WOUND

After §340.5's enactment, courts faced recurring questions about when the 1-year
discovery period was triggered — specifically: did the patient need to know the specific
acts of negligence, or merely that something had gone wrong? How much investigation was
required before "reasonable diligence" was imputed?

Different cases produced inconsistent results: some courts found the period triggered
at injury (when the bad outcome occurred); others required actual knowledge of negligence.
The resulting uncertainty made it difficult to advise clients about filing deadlines.

The California Supreme Court addressed this in Jolly v. Eli Lilly & Co. (1988) and
established a clearer "reasonable suspicion" standard.

### JOLLY V. ELI LILLY — THE SUSPICION STANDARD

**Jolly v. Eli Lilly & Co., 44 Cal.3d 1103 (1988):**

The plaintiff was a DES daughter (exposed to diethylstilbestrol in utero) who later
developed reproductive problems. She knew of her DES exposure and health problems
for several years before filing suit.

**Holding:**
The 1-year discovery period begins when the plaintiff suspects or has reason to suspect
that the defendant caused her injury. "Suspicion" is the trigger — not certainty,
not specific knowledge of the negligent act. The plaintiff need not know the exact
mechanism or have proof of liability; she need only have "reason to suspect" that
someone's wrong caused her harm.

**"Reasonable diligence" after suspicion:**
Once the plaintiff has reason to suspect, she has a duty to investigate. If investigation
would have revealed the cause of action, the period runs from when investigation should
have occurred — not from when the plaintiff actually investigated.

**Medical context application:**
For medical malpractice:
- The period begins when the patient suspects (or should suspect) that the adverse
  medical outcome was caused by negligence — not merely that an adverse outcome occurred
- Adverse outcome + opportunity to investigate = sufficient to trigger the "should suspect" standard
- The patient's knowledge of the specific standard of care violation is not required;
  only suspicion that something was wrongfully done

### WRONGFUL DEATH COORDINATION

For wrongful death claims arising from medical negligence, §340.5 applies rather than
the general wrongful death SOL. The California Supreme Court established that the
beneficiaries' one-year period runs from the date the beneficiaries discovered or should
have discovered that the death was caused by medical negligence — not from the date
of death itself (if discovery is delayed).

This is meaningful for cases where the cause of death was misrepresented in medical
records or certificates: if the true cause of death was concealed, the beneficiaries'
§340.5 clock does not begin until they had reason to suspect the true cause.

### LOGICAL DELTA — DISCOVERY TRIGGER CLARIFICATION

| Element | Pre-Jolly uncertainty | Post-Jolly (1988) |
|---------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Discovery trigger | Inconsistent: actual knowledge vs. injury date | "Reason to suspect" negligence caused harm |
| Diligence requirement | Varied | Duty to investigate once suspicion arises |
| Specific knowledge | Some courts required | Not required — suspicion is sufficient |
| Wrongful death | Inconsistent | Beneficiary discovery rule applies |

### CURRENT EFFECT ON SIRVA

Applying Jolly to SIRVA:
- Patient receives shoulder injection, develops immediate shoulder pain
- Patient attributes pain to vaccine (reasonable — vaccine side effects are expected)
- Patient seeks medical care; initial providers treat as muscle strain or vaccine reaction
- SIRVA diagnosis eventually made (typically requires an orthopedist or physical therapist
  familiar with injection-site injuries)

**Jolly analysis:** At what point did Michael have "reason to suspect" the shoulder
injury was caused by negligent injection rather than the vaccine itself? The answer
controls when the 1-year discovery period began. If SIRVA was not diagnosable from
symptoms alone — if it required specialist examination after failed conservative treatment —
the discovery date may be significantly later than the injection date.
