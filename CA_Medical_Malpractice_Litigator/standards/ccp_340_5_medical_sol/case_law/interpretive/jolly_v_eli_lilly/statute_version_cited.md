# Statute Version at Time of Jolly v. Eli Lilly (1988)

**§340.5 version:** As enacted by MICRA (Stats. 1975, Ch. 2, 2d Ex. Sess., effective
1976-01-01). The discovery rule text of §340.5 — "one year after the plaintiff discovers,
or through the use of reasonable diligence should have discovered, the injury" — was in
its current form when Jolly was decided. The case interpreted this language.

**Current operative text:** §340.5 is unchanged from its MICRA enactment in the
provisions interpreted by Jolly. The "reason to suspect" standard from Jolly remains
the controlling interpretation of the "discovers or through reasonable diligence should
have discovered" language.

**Note on Jolly's product liability context vs. §340.5 medical malpractice:**
Jolly arose from DES product liability, not directly from a §340.5 claim. The California
Supreme Court's discovery rule analysis has been consistently applied to §340.5 medical
malpractice cases because §340.5 uses the identical "discovers or should have discovered"
language. The Jolly standard is controlling for §340.5 despite the product liability
context of the original case.
