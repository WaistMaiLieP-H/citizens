# California Business & Professions Code §6146 — MICRA Attorney Fee Sliding Scale
## Current Framework — Logical Structure

**Standard:** Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §6146
**Build method:** Logical Delta
**MICRA lattice position:** §6146 controls how much of any recovery a plaintiff's attorney
may receive in a medical malpractice case. It directly limits the contingency fee arrangement
and changes the economics of bringing a malpractice case for both the attorney and the client.

---

## Why this statute exists (the wound it answers — MICRA framing)

The 1975 MICRA package argued that plaintiff's attorneys were taking contingency fees of
33–40% of large malpractice verdicts, creating incentives to bring and settle cases regardless
of merit. The fee structure gave attorneys a financial interest in larger recoveries, not
in meritorious cases.

**MICRA's answer:** Impose a declining sliding scale on contingency fees. The attorney gets
a higher percentage of smaller amounts and a progressively smaller percentage of larger amounts.
This reduces the attorney's economic incentive to push for the largest possible verdict (since
the marginal fee return declines) and theoretically makes large fraudulent cases less attractive
to plaintiff's bar.

**The plaintiff bar's argument against:** The sliding scale makes low-value cases less
economically viable for attorneys. A $100K medical malpractice case with 40 hours of work is
only worth $40K maximum in attorney fees (40% of first $100K) — often less than the time cost.
This effectively screens out legitimate small-dollar malpractice cases that attorneys cannot
afford to bring on contingency.

---

## The Current §6146 Sliding Scale

**§6146(a) — Maximum contingency fee in medical malpractice actions:**

| Recovery Range | Maximum Attorney Fee Percentage |
|---|---|
| First $250,000 | 40% |
| Next $250,000 ($250K–$500K) | 33.33% |
| Next $500,000 ($500K–$1M) | 25% |
| Any amount over $1,000,000 | 15% |

**AB 35 (2022) update:** The AB 35 amendment that increased §3333.2's noneconomic cap also
updated the §6146 fee table to reflect the new higher cap levels. The current fee thresholds
above are the post-AB 35 amounts. Prior to 2023, the sliding scale had lower thresholds.

---

## Key Structural Rules

### The "recovered" amount is NET of litigation costs, UNLESS the attorney advanced costs
**§6146(b):** The contingency fee percentage is applied to the "recovered" amount. Typically
this means the net recovery after deducting:
- Court costs
- Expert witness fees advanced by the attorney
- Other litigation expenses

If the attorney did not advance costs (client paid them), the fee applies to the gross recovery.
This creates different economic outcomes depending on who advanced costs.

### The sliding scale applies to ALL forms of recovery
Applies to verdict, settlement, arbitration award — any recovery obtained by the attorney's
services in a medical malpractice case. Cannot be circumvented by structuring settlement as
compensation for a different claim if the underlying injury is medical malpractice.

### Disclosure requirement
**§6146(a):** The attorney must provide the client with a written statement explaining the
fee schedule. Failure to provide the written statement may render the fee agreement void.

### Waiver is not available
The client cannot waive the §6146 sliding scale. It is a mandatory cap, not a default rule.

## How §6146 interacts with §3333.2 and the damages mosaic

**Scenario 1: Professional negligence only claim (§3333.2 cap applies)**
- Noneconomic damages capped at $470K (2026)
- Assume economic damages of $200K (medical bills, lost earnings)
- Total max recovery: $670K
- §6146 fee on $670K:
  - 40% × $250K = $100K
  - 33.33% × $250K = $83.3K
  - 25% × $170K = $42.5K
  - Total fee: ~$225.8K (approximately 33.7% of $670K)

**Scenario 2: Intentional tort (Hrimnak escape — §3333.2 cap does NOT apply)**
- Noneconomic damages uncapped (e.g., $1M+ for willful conduct)
- Full economic damages
- §6146 fee on $1.5M:
  - 40% × $250K = $100K
  - 33.33% × $250K = $83.3K
  - 25% × $500K = $125K
  - 15% × $500K = $75K
  - Total fee: ~$383.3K (approximately 25.6% of $1.5M)

**The Hrimnak strategy doubles as a fee strategy:** Breaking out of MICRA's professional
negligence framework via intentional tort framing not only removes the §3333.2 noneconomic
cap and the §3333.1 collateral source reduction — it also makes the case more economically
attractive to plaintiff's attorneys because the larger potential recovery generates higher
absolute fees even with the declining percentage.

## Application to Michael's cases

### Pro se limitation
Michael is currently acting pro se. §6146 does not constrain him as a pro se litigant —
§6146 governs the attorney-client fee contract, not what the plaintiff may recover directly.
If Michael proceeds pro se, he retains the full recovery without paying attorney contingency fees.

### When retaining counsel
When Michael retains a malpractice attorney, the fee must comply with §6146. The attorney
should provide the written disclosure. Understanding the fee schedule allows Michael to evaluate:
- What the attorney will earn on the expected recovery
- Whether the case economics incentivize the attorney to pursue maximum recovery

### SIRVA case (SOL critical to 2027-03-18)
For the SIRVA case, the time pressure requires prompt engagement with a malpractice attorney.
Understanding §6146 allows Michael to negotiate the retainer with full knowledge of the
mandatory fee cap.
