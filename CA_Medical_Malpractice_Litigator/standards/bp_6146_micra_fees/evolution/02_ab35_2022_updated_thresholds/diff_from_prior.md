# Logical Diff: Stage 01 (1975) → Stage 02 (AB 35, 2022)

## What was unchanged for 47 years

The §6146 sliding scale thresholds were enacted in 1975 and never updated. By 2022, inflation
had made the original thresholds economically irrational — $200K was a large verdict in 1975
but represented a relatively modest medical malpractice recovery in 2022.

## What AB 35 changed

Fee thresholds raised 5× across all tiers. The tier 4 rate increased from 10% to 15%.

**Before AB 35 (1975 thresholds):**
- Tier 4 (over $200K): 10% only
- Large recoveries were heavily penalized in fee terms

**After AB 35 (2023 thresholds):**
- Tier 4 (over $1M): 15%
- Much higher absolute fee dollar amounts on moderate recoveries

## What did NOT change

The mandatory declining structure is unchanged — the fee percentage still falls as recovery
increases. The mandatory disclosure requirement is unchanged. The scope (medical malpractice
cases) is unchanged.

## Net effect

Cases with expected recoveries in the $250K–$1M range are now economically attractive for
plaintiff's attorneys. Cases under $250K (where the attorney earns 40% of a modest sum) may
still be economically marginal if litigation costs are high. The $250K–$750K MICRA-capped
professional negligence case is now the most economically viable tier for plaintiff's bar.
