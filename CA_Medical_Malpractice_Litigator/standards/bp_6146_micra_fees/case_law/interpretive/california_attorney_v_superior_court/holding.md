# California Attorney General v. Younger / Roa v. Lodi Medical Group
## Roa v. Lodi Medical Group, Inc., 37 Cal.3d 920 (1985)

**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** February 28, 1985

---

## Issue decided

Is §6146's mandatory sliding scale on contingency fees in medical malpractice cases
constitutional? Specifically, does it violate equal protection by treating medical malpractice
plaintiffs differently from other tort plaintiffs, and does it violate the right to counsel
by limiting the fees attorneys can earn?

---

## Holding

**Yes, §6146 is constitutional.** The California Supreme Court upheld §6146's mandatory fee
cap in the same line of cases that upheld §3333.2.

**Key holdings:**

1. **Equal protection — rational basis:** Medical malpractice plaintiffs are treated
   differently from other tort plaintiffs (whose attorneys may charge standard 33%–40% contingency).
   The Legislature had a rational basis to treat medical malpractice differently: the perceived
   medical liability crisis, the desire to stabilize insurance markets, and the public interest
   in ensuring healthcare availability.

2. **Right to counsel — not violated:** There is no constitutional right to a contingency fee
   arrangement of any particular percentage. Limiting the attorney's fee does not violate the
   client's right to counsel — the client can still hire an attorney; the fee is simply capped.

3. **The voidability consequence:** An attorney fee agreement that exceeds §6146's limits is
   VOIDABLE by the client. The client does not need to prove harm; the contract terms are simply
   unenforceable to the extent they exceed the statutory caps. The attorney may still recover
   the statutory maximum amount, just not more.

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Any fee agreement must comply with §6146.** If Michael retains a malpractice attorney and
the attorney proposes a fee higher than §6146's sliding scale, that provision is voidable.
Michael has the right to hold the attorney to the statutory maximum regardless of what a
separate written agreement might say.

**The disclosure violation consequence:** If the attorney fails to provide the written fee
schedule disclosure required by §6146(a), the fee agreement is voidable. This is a leverage
point if a fee dispute arises.

**Strategic positioning for retaining malpractice counsel:**
- SIRVA case: expected recovery $470K+ noneconomic (at 2026 rate) + economic damages.
  Under §6146: approximately $225K maximum attorney fee on a $670K recovery. This is
  competitive with most jurisdictions' standard contingency arrangements.
- Spine surgery fraud (Hrimnak escape): expected recovery potentially $1M+ (noneconomic
  uncapped for intentional tort). §6146 fee on $1.2M recovery:
  - 40% × $250K = $100K
  - 33.33% × $250K = $83.3K
  - 25% × $500K = $125K
  - 15% × $200K = $30K
  - Total: ~$338K (28.2% of $1.2M)
  This is attractive to plaintiff's malpractice bar.
