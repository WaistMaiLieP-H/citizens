# §6146 Historical Chain — Business & Professions Code §6146

## The wound (pre-MICRA)

Standard California personal injury contingency fees were 33%–40% with no statutory cap.
In a large malpractice case, attorneys could earn fees in the hundreds of thousands of dollars.
The medical/insurance lobby argued this created overclaiming incentives and contributed to
the 1975 liability insurance crisis.

---

## 1975: MICRA's fee cap

The sliding scale was enacted with modest thresholds appropriate to 1975 recovery amounts:
40% on first $50K, declining to 10% on anything over $200K. The medical lobby got the cap
it wanted; plaintiff's bar argued (correctly, as it turned out) that the low thresholds would
make small-dollar malpractice cases economically unviable for plaintiff's attorneys.

---

## 1985: Constitutional validation (Roa v. Lodi)

California Supreme Court upheld §6146 as constitutional. Confirmed that fee agreements
exceeding the cap are voidable by the client. The constitutional structure has been stable
since 1985.

---

## 1975–2022: 47 years without inflation adjustment

The §6146 thresholds were never adjusted for inflation over 47 years. By 2020, the original
thresholds were economically absurd — $200K was the 1975 threshold for the 10% tier;
by 2020, $200K was a modest settlement in many cases, but attorneys still only earned
10% on amounts above it. Access-to-justice advocates documented that legitimate malpractice
cases in the $100K–$500K range were being turned away by plaintiff's firms because the fee
economics were unworkable.

---

## 2022: AB 35 — threshold modernization

The first update to §6146's thresholds in 47 years. AB 35 raised all thresholds approximately
5× (matching inflation from 1975). The new scale (effective 2023) reflects actual 2023 case
economics. Combined with the §3333.2 noneconomic cap increases, AB 35 represents the first
substantive reform of the MICRA damages framework in nearly half a century.

---

## Current framework (2026)

§6146 is a mandatory, non-waivable sliding scale. The current thresholds (post-AB 35) make
medical malpractice cases in the $250K–$1M recovery range economically viable for plaintiff's
attorneys. Cases framed as intentional tort (escaping MICRA's professional negligence scope)
have no §6146 limitation — the standard contingency rules of the State Bar and the parties'
agreement govern.

**The arc:** 1975 mandatory cap at low thresholds → 1985 constitutional validation → 47 years
unchanged → 2022 AB 35 threshold modernization → current viable-fee landscape.
