# Fein v. Permanente Medical Group (§3333.1 collateral source component)
## 38 Cal.3d 137 (1985)

**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** February 21, 1985
**Note:** Fein was the same case that upheld §3333.2's noneconomic damages cap. The Court
also addressed §3333.1's collateral source modification. This entry documents the §3333.1
holdings separately from the §3333.2 holdings in the civ_3333_2 standard.

---

## Issue decided (§3333.1 component)

Is §3333.1's modification of the collateral source rule — allowing defendants to introduce
evidence of insurance payments — constitutional under equal protection and due process?

---

## Holding

**Yes, §3333.1 is constitutional.** The California Supreme Court upheld §3333.1 as rationally
related to the legitimate government purpose of reducing medical malpractice liability costs.

**Key §3333.1 holdings from Fein:**

1. **Jury instruction standard:** When a defendant introduces collateral source evidence
   under §3333.1(a), the jury must be instructed on how to consider this evidence. The jury
   may reduce the economic damages award by the amount of collateral benefits — but is not
   required to do so. The statute makes collateral source evidence admissible, not mandatory
   deduction.

2. **The anti-subrogation trade (§3333.1(c)):** The Court noted that §3333.1's trade-off —
   collateral source evidence in exchange for elimination of subrogation — was a deliberate
   legislative choice. The Court did not rule on whether this trade was "fair" in any economic
   sense; it upheld it as a rational legislative judgment.

3. **Scope limited to "professional negligence":** §3333.1 applies only to claims "based on
   professional negligence." Claims alleging intentional conduct fall outside the statute.

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**§3333.1 is the MICRA constraint on economic damages** — just as §3333.2 is the constraint
on noneconomic damages. Together they are the two MICRA provisions defendants use most
aggressively to reduce medical malpractice verdicts.

**The Hrimnak escape applies to §3333.1 equally:** The same "professional negligence" scope
limitation that allows §3333.2 cap-escape via intentional tort framing (Hrimnak) ALSO removes
§3333.1's collateral source modification. A fraud/battery/intentional tort claim against a
health care provider:
- Escapes §3333.2's noneconomic cap
- Escapes §3333.1's collateral source modification (plaintiff gets full economic damages
  without reduction for insurance payments)
- Eliminates MICRA's protections for defendants entirely

**Strategic implication for spine surgery fraud (case #11-21):** If the spine surgery was
performed without meaningful informed consent, or based on affirmative misrepresentation of
necessity or of the procedure itself, the intentional tort framing not only recovers full
noneconomic damages (§3333.2 escape) but also retains full economic damages without
§3333.1 reduction. Both MICRA damages provisions disappear together.
