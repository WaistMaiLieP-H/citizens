FACTS:

Lawrence Fein suffered a heart attack while being treated by Permanente Medical Group. He alleged that Permanente's physicians negligently failed to diagnose his heart condition in time. The jury returned a verdict for Fein. On appeal, Permanente challenged the damages award and the constitutionality of MICRA, while Fein cross-appealed challenging MICRA's collateral source modification (§ 3333.1) and noneconomic damages cap (§ 3333.2).

---

HOLDING — CIVIL CODE § 3333.1 (COLLATERAL SOURCE RULE MODIFICATION):

The California Supreme Court upheld Civil Code § 3333.1 against constitutional challenge.

WHAT § 3333.1 DOES:
In medical malpractice cases, § 3333.1(a) allows either party to introduce evidence that the plaintiff has received medical treatment or disability benefits from a collateral source (insurance, workers' compensation, Medicare/Medicaid, Social Security disability).

§ 3333.1(b): If such evidence is introduced, the defendant may also introduce evidence of the premium paid by the plaintiff for the insurance (to show the plaintiff paid for the benefit).

THE EFFECT: The trier of fact considers the collateral benefits received in assessing damages. The plaintiff does not receive a double recovery for losses already compensated by insurance. If the plaintiff received $200,000 in medical expense payments from her insurance company, the jury may reduce the defendant's liability by that amount.

---

CONSTITUTIONAL CHALLENGES REJECTED:

EQUAL PROTECTION: The court held that MICRA's collateral source modification satisfies rational basis review. The Legislature had a rational basis for modifying the collateral source rule in medical malpractice cases specifically — to reduce the cost of medical malpractice judgments, which in turn was intended to reduce malpractice insurance premiums and protect access to medical care.

DUE PROCESS: The modification does not deprive injured patients of a constitutionally protected property interest. Plaintiffs retain the right to recover all uncompensated losses; the modification prevents double recovery for compensated losses only.

---

PRACTICAL EFFECT OF § 3333.1:

PLAINTIFF'S STRATEGY:
In medical malpractice cases, plaintiffs must document all economic losses before trial, including what was and was not paid by insurers. The key calculation:
- Total medical expenses incurred;
- MINUS amounts paid by health insurance (which defendant may introduce under § 3333.1);
- EQUALS the plaintiff's remaining uncompensated medical expenses.

Plaintiffs should also present evidence of:
- Future medical expenses (not yet incurred, so no collateral source offset);
- Loss of future earning capacity (typically not offset by disability benefits);
- Noneconomic damages (pain and suffering — not offset by any collateral source).

SUBROGATION RIGHTS:
If the plaintiff's health insurer paid medical expenses, the insurer may have subrogation rights against the malpractice recovery. Plaintiffs must account for subrogation in calculating net recovery.

---

APPLICATION TO SIRVA / SURGICAL MALPRACTICE:

For a SIRVA case where the patient:
- Received workers' compensation or Medicare/Medicaid for the shoulder injury treatment;
- Received Social Security disability benefits during the period of disability;

Under § 3333.1:
1. The defendant healthcare provider may introduce evidence of these collateral benefits;
2. The jury may reduce the defendant's damages liability by the amount of benefits received;
3. The plaintiff should anticipate this and focus damages presentation on: (a) future unreimbursed medical costs; (b) wage loss not covered by disability; (c) noneconomic damages (unaffected by § 3333.1).
