# Deyo v. Kilbourne
## 84 Cal.App.3d 771 (1978)

**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District
**Decided:** 1978

---

## Issue decided

How does §3333.1's collateral source modification interact with the plaintiff's own insurance
premiums paid — specifically, does the plaintiff's right to introduce premium evidence under
§3333.1(b) meaningfully offset the reduction from collateral source evidence?

---

## Holding

**§3333.1(b) premium evidence is limited in practical utility** for most plaintiffs. The
Court held that:

1. **The premium evidence right is real but narrow:** §3333.1(b) allows the plaintiff to
   introduce evidence of "any amount the plaintiff has paid or contributed to secure the
   right to the benefit." This means total premiums paid, employer contributions, and any
   out-of-pocket contributions to obtain the insurance coverage.

2. **The practical gap:** In most cases, the total benefits received (insurance payments)
   significantly exceed the total premiums the plaintiff personally paid. The jury sees large
   insurance payments on one side and smaller premium contributions on the other — the net
   effect of §3333.1 is almost always a reduction in economic damages.

3. **Jury instruction requirement:** The trial court must instruct the jury that it MAY (not
   must) reduce economic damages by the collateral source amount. The jury has discretion to
   reject the full reduction even after hearing the evidence.

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Planning the economic damages presentation:** In any of Michael's medical malpractice cases
where health insurance (Blue Shield, etc.) paid medical bills, the defense will introduce
§3333.1(a) evidence of those payments. The §3333.1(b) counter is to present:
- Total health insurance premiums paid by Michael over the relevant period
- Any premium contributions by employer (UA Local 342 benefits)
- Co-pays, deductibles, and out-of-pocket costs not covered by insurance

**The jury-instruction argument:** Under Deyo, the jury has discretion to reject the full
§3333.1 reduction. This is a trial advocacy point: the jury should be reminded that the
anti-subrogation benefit (Blue Shield cannot recover its payments) does not come without cost
to Michael (reduced economic damages award). If the full §3333.1 reduction is taken AND the
§3333.2 noneconomic cap is applied, Michael's total recovery may be severely compressed.

**The fraud/battery escape:** If the spine surgery or SIRVA cases can be framed as intentional
tort (not professional negligence), §3333.1 does not apply. Both Fein (§3333.1) and Hrimnak
(§3333.2) confirm that the professional negligence scope limitation removes both MICRA damages
constraints simultaneously for intentional tort claims.
