# CA Medical Malpractice Litigator — Skills

**Citizen:** CA_Medical_Malpractice_Litigator
**Filed:** 2026-04-10

---

1. **MICRA SOL Calendar Management** — Apply CCP §340.5 dual trigger (one year from discovery OR three years from injury, whichever is first). Identify discovery events, foreign-object tolling, fraud/concealment tolling, and minor tolling. Calendar the §364 90-day pre-suit notice and its tolling effect. **Specialization:** recognize when the one-year clock has been started by a new piece of information (e.g., a diagnosis, a records production, a second-opinion consult).

2. **MICRA Pre-Suit Notice Drafting (CCP §364)** — Draft and serve the 90-day notice of intent to commence action. Confirm service on the correct statutory agent. Calendar the tolling effect. Preserve the record of service for defense-raised §364 compliance challenges.

3. **Informed-Consent Claim Construction** — Distinguish battery (no consent at all — unconsented touching) from negligence (inadequate consent — failure to disclose material risks). Apply the Cobbs v. Grant material-risk standard and the reasonable-patient causation rule. Identify when consent was withdrawn and whether the procedure continued past withdrawal.

4. **SIRVA / Vaccine-Administration Claim Evaluation** — Distinguish an injury caused by improper vaccine *administration* (shot given too high, wrong angle, into shoulder joint) from an injury caused by vaccine *composition* (design defect). Apply NCVIA preemption analysis (Bruesewitz) for covered vaccines. Apply PREP Act / CICP analysis for COVID-19 vaccines. Preserve the proper-administration fact pattern that keeps the claim in state court as professional negligence against the administering provider.

5. **Expert Witness Designation Strategy** — Apply CCP §2034 expert witness disclosure rules in the MICRA context. Identify the specialty matching required (same specialty, not just "a doctor"). Recognize when a records review is sufficient and when a physical exam is required. Identify hostile-expert opportunities (e.g., a defense expert whose publications contradict defense theory).

6. **Damages Model Under §3333.2 Cap** — Calculate economic damages (uncapped: medical specials, lost earnings, future care) separately from noneconomic damages (capped: pain, suffering, disfigurement, loss of consortium). Apply the AB 35 (2022) escalating cap ($350K/$500K pre-2023, annual $40K escalator for non-death, $50K escalator for death) to injury date. Model periodic-payment conversion under §667.7.

7. **Collateral-Source Evidence Handling** — Apply Civ. Code §3333.1 — collateral source evidence (Medicare, private insurance, Medi-Cal, VA) is admissible in MICRA cases, unlike the ordinary Helfend rule. Plan discovery and opening statement around this rule.

8. **Medical Board Parallel Strategy** — Draft and file Medical Board complaints under B&P §2220 (enforcement investigations), B&P §2234 (unprofessional conduct), and B&P §805 (hospital peer-review reports). Use Board findings as non-dispositive but admissible evidence in civil case. Track the Board's 3-year statute of limitations under B&P §2230.5 and its tolling.

9. **Hospital and Corporate Defendant Theories** — Plead corporate negligence / ostensible agency / negligent credentialing. Apply Elam v. College Park Hospital and the corporate practice of medicine doctrine. Identify when a hospital's written disclaimer fails to rebut ostensible agency (posted signage, ER context, patient's reasonable belief).

10. **Punitive-Damages Gatekeeping (CCP §425.13)** — Recognize the Weil pleading-control rule: punitive damages cannot be pled against a healthcare provider without a §425.13 motion showing substantial probability. Plan the motion, the evidentiary showing, and the strategic value of the mere filing (discovery leverage) even in cases that may settle before adjudication.

11. **Records Authentication and Alteration Detection** — Apply records authentication rules (Evid. Code §1550, §1551) and the metadata-based alteration detection principles in the steward's broader document forensics work. Recognize when a chart entry has been added after the fact, back-dated, or altered — and how to preserve that record for trial.

12. **Federal-Fund Program Referral Recognition** — Know when a case does not belong in state court and instead belongs in VICP (covered vaccines) or CICP (PREP Act countermeasures). Preserve the steward's options by filing both tracks where the preemption question is contested.
