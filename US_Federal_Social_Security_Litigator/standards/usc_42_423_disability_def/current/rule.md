# SSDI Disability Definition — Inability to Engage in Substantial Gainful Activity — Rule

**Authority:** 42 U.S.C. § 423
**Jurisdiction:** Federal
**Effective:** 1956 (original SSDI); disability definition as currently operative established 1967

## The Rule

To receive Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) benefits under Title II, a claimant must be "disabled" within the statutory meaning. Disability means:

> Inability to engage in any substantial gainful activity by reason of any medically determinable physical or mental impairment which can be expected to result in death or which has lasted or can be expected to last for a continuous period of not less than 12 months.

Three elements must all be present:
1. The claimant cannot engage in any substantial gainful activity (SGA) — not just their specific occupation or their most recent job, but any gainful work that exists in the national economy
2. The inability is caused by a medically determinable impairment — not just the claimant's subjective statement, but a condition established by objective medical evidence (clinical signs, laboratory findings, or diagnostic imaging)
3. The impairment is expected to last at least 12 continuous months or result in death

The impairment must be established by objective medical evidence from acceptable medical sources. Symptoms alone — pain, fatigue, depression reported by the claimant — are not sufficient. There must be a medical diagnosis supported by clinical and laboratory findings.

## Key Elements

1. **Any SGA, not just past work:** The standard is not whether the claimant can do their specific prior occupation but whether any substantial gainful activity exists in the national economy. This makes the step-5 inquiry extremely important.
2. **12-month durational requirement:** The impairment must have lasted or be expected to last 12 months. Conditions that resolve before 12 months do not qualify, even if severely disabling during that period.
3. **Objective medical evidence required:** § 423(d)(2)(B) defines "physical or mental impairment" as one that "results from anatomical, physiological, or psychological abnormalities which can be shown by medically acceptable clinical and laboratory diagnostic techniques."
4. **Claimant bears burden under § 423(d)(5)(A):** The claimant must furnish evidence of disability; however, SSA has an independent duty to develop the medical evidence under 20 C.F.R. § 404.1512 — SSA's failure to develop evidence it knows exists is legal error.
5. **No partial disability:** SSDI is an all-or-nothing determination — there is no partial benefit for partial disability. Either the claimant cannot do any SGA, or they are not disabled.

## What This Means in Practice

The statutory definition sets the threshold. Every step of the five-step sequential evaluation (20 C.F.R. § 404.1520) is an operationalization of this definition — the Listings are impairments presumed to satisfy the "any SGA" standard; RFC is the mechanism for determining what SGA the claimant can still do. A denial must ultimately be traceable back to the statutory definition: the adjudicator must be able to say, supported by substantial evidence, why the claimant can engage in substantial gainful activity despite their medically determinable impairment.
