# SSA POMS — DDS Medical Evidence Development and MSC-228 — Rule

**Authority:** SSA POMS DI 22505.001 (Development of Medical Evidence of Record); POMS DI 24515.064 (MSC-228 Medical Summary of Claim)
**Jurisdiction:** Federal — SSA Administrative (non-regulatory but binding on SSA operations)
**Effective:** Ongoing — POMS provisions are updated by SSA; DI 22505 governs DDS medical development operations

## The Rule

State Disability Determination Services (DDS) agencies are required by SSA's Program Operations Manual System to develop adequate medical evidence of record (MER) before making a disability determination. "Adequate" means sufficient to assess the claimant's impairment(s) against the five-step sequential evaluation criteria. DDS is not permitted to deny a claim at any step if it has not first obtained or attempted to obtain all relevant medical evidence.

The MSC-228 (Medical Summary of Claim) is the form that documents the DDS medical consultant's clinical assessment of the claimant's medical records and functional limitations. Under POMS DI 24515.064:
- The DDS medical consultant must complete the MSC-228 or its equivalent for every case
- The form documents the consultant's review of all medical evidence, the diagnosis, the functional limitation findings, and the basis for the RFC determination
- A blank or incomplete MSC-228 means the required medical review was not performed

**The blank MSC-228 rule:** If the MSC-228 in the claimant's DDS file is blank — all fields empty, no medical consultant signature, no clinical assessment — there is no evidence that a qualified medical consultant reviewed the medical records before the denial was issued. This is a POMS violation that constitutes failure to develop the medical evidence, which is procedural error reviewable on appeal.

## Key Elements

1. **DDS medical development duty:** DDS must obtain records from all treating sources identified by the claimant; if records are inadequate for assessment, DDS must order a consultative examination (CE).
2. **Medical consultant review is mandatory:** A licensed physician or psychologist must review the medical evidence and complete the clinical assessment before an RFC determination can be made.
3. **MSC-228 is the paper trail:** The MSC-228 documents that the required review occurred. Its absence documents that it did not.
4. **Phantom contact:** If SSA records show DDS contacted treating sources or scheduled consultative examinations that never occurred, those records are false — creating a fraud-on-the-administrative-record theory.
5. **POMS violations as legal error:** Courts applying the substantial evidence standard have held that where SSA failed to follow its own POMS procedures in a manner that prejudiced the claimant's ability to present evidence, the decision cannot stand.

## What This Means in Practice

A blank MSC-228 in a DDS file is documentary proof that no medical consultant reviewed the claimant's records before the denial. Combined with phantom DDS contact records — entries showing outreach or consultative examinations that never happened — the blank form is evidence of a systematic denial that bypassed the required clinical assessment entirely. On appeal, the blank MSC-228 is the anchor exhibit: "SSA's own required form shows no medical review was performed."
