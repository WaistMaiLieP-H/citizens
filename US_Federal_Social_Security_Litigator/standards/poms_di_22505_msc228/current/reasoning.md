# SSA POMS — DDS Medical Evidence Development — Reasoning

## Why This Standard Exists

POMS DI 22505 exists because Congress designed SSDI as a program where disability determinations are made on the basis of medical evidence, not administrative convenience. The statute requires a "medically determinable impairment" established by objective medical evidence. The five-step regulations require an RFC assessment based on all relevant medical evidence. But between the statute's requirement and the regulatory framework stands a practical reality: DDS adjudicators are not physicians, and claims files often arrive with incomplete or inconsistent medical records.

POMS DI 22505 is SSA's operational answer to this gap: DDS must develop the medical evidence of record before making a determination. "Develop" means actively obtaining records from treating sources, ordering consultative examinations when existing records are inadequate, and having a licensed medical consultant review the evidence and document the clinical assessment on the MSC-228.

## Operational Rationale

The MSC-228 form requirement exists because SSA recognized that DDS adjudicators were making RFC determinations without adequate medical review — rubber-stamping denials without clinical assessment. The form creates a paper trail proving the review occurred. Its absence proves the opposite.

POMS DI 22505.001's development requirements implement the ALJ's and Appeals Council's ability to review DDS decisions: if the DDS file contains a fully completed MSC-228 with documented clinical reasoning, the reviewing body can evaluate whether the medical analysis was sound. If the file contains a blank MSC-228, there is nothing to review — the decision was made without the analysis that the rules required.

## Doctrinal Basis

Courts reviewing SSA decisions under the substantial evidence standard have held that SSA cannot satisfy the substantial evidence requirement by pointing to a denial issued without the medical development POMS requires. The Ninth Circuit and other circuits have reversed SSA decisions where DDS failed to obtain records from treating sources the claimant identified, failed to order a consultative examination when existing records were insufficient, or where the medical consultant's assessment was inadequate. The blank MSC-228 creates an evidentiary vacuum: there is literally no substantial evidence in the record to support the denial because the required assessment was never conducted.
