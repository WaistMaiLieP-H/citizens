# POMS DI 22505 / MSC-228 — Historical Origin (SSA Administrative)

## Origin Event

**Authority:** Social Security Administration, Office of Disability Programs
**Legal basis:** 42 U.S.C. § 421 (disability determinations by state agencies); 20 C.F.R. § 404.1512 (SSA's duty to develop claimant's complete medical history); 20 C.F.R. § 404.1519a (consultative examinations)
**Nature:** Internal SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) — not a regulation; periodically updated without formal rulemaking

## What Happened

The DDS (Disability Determination Service) medical development framework evolved from the administrative structure created by the 1956 Social Security Act. Congress delegated disability determinations to state agencies operating under federal standards (§ 421). SSA created the POMS to operationalize the statutory requirements into day-to-day DDS procedures.

**The MSC-228 form** originated as the mechanism for ensuring that a licensed physician or psychologist, employed by or contracted to DDS, reviewed the claimant's medical evidence before the DDS made a disability determination. The form creates a paper trail documenting:
- What medical impairments the consultant identified
- Whether any impairment met or equaled a Listing
- What RFC the consultant assessed
- The medical rationale for the assessment

Without the MSC-228 (or its electronic equivalent), there is no record that a medically qualified professional reviewed the evidence before a lay DDS examiner denied the claim.

## Why the Standard Exists

**The foundational problem:** Disability determinations are inherently medical. A lay DDS examiner without medical training cannot assess whether a claimant's degenerative disc disease, rotator cuff tear, or major depressive disorder precludes sustained work activity. Congress recognized this in § 421 by creating a state-federal partnership in which medical consultants are embedded in the DDS process.

**The phantom contact / blank MSC-228 fraud pattern:** Where DDS records show:
1. A log of purported medical contacts (treating physician, hospital, specialist) that the claimant disputes ever occurred
2. A blank MSC-228 with no findings, no RFC, no consultant signature

...the combination proves that the medical development process was not performed. The phantom contact log creates the appearance of compliance; the blank MSC-228 reveals the absence. A determination made without a completed MSC-228 is not merely defective — it is a determination without a medical foundation.

## Historical Loss the Standard Prevents

Prior to POMS medical development requirements, DDS agencies in some states allowed lay examiners to evaluate medical evidence and deny claims without physician review. Congressional oversight hearings in the 1970s and 1980s documented denials made on inadequate records without medical consultant participation.

The regulatory response (20 C.F.R. §§ 404.1512, 404.1519a) and the POMS implementation (DI 22505.001, DI 24515.064) together ensure that:
- The record must be developed before it can be adjudicated
- A licensed professional must review the developed record
- The review must be documented in a form that survives in the administrative record
- Courts can identify, from the administrative record itself, whether the required review occurred

A blank MSC-228 in the file is self-proving evidence of the violation — no extrinsic proof required.

## Case Relevance

**Case #18 — Michael Hartmann SSDI denial:**
The DDS administrative record reportedly contains:
- A phantom contact log showing outreach to treating providers that the claimant disputes
- A blank MSC-228 with no completed fields and no medical consultant signature

If confirmed on production of the administrative record, this establishes a per se violation of POMS DI 22505.001 and DI 24515.064, and a failure of SSA's duty under 20 C.F.R. § 404.1512. The denial was made without the medical foundation required by SSA's own procedures.
