## Key Operative Requirements

**HALLEX I-2-6 — ALJ Hearing Procedures:**

**Notice requirements:** The claimant must receive a Notice of Hearing at least 75 days before the scheduled hearing date. The notice must identify: the time and place of hearing; the issues to be considered; the claimant's right to request subpoenas for witnesses or documents; the claimant's right to representation.

**Hearing opening:** The ALJ must open the hearing on the record, confirm the claimant's identity, confirm the record of exhibits, identify the issues, and advise the claimant of their rights.

**Claimant testimony:** The ALJ must give the claimant a full opportunity to testify about their impairments, their symptoms, the effect of their impairments on their daily activities and ability to work, and any other relevant matters. The ALJ may not cut off testimony without giving the claimant a reasonable opportunity to complete their account.

**Expert witness testimony:** When a medical expert or vocational expert (VE) testifies, the ALJ must: (a) give the claimant advance notice of the expert's anticipated testimony; (b) give the claimant an opportunity to cross-examine the expert; (c) if the ALJ relies on VE testimony, ask the VE whether the testimony is consistent with the Dictionary of Occupational Titles (DOT) or Selected Characteristics of Occupations.

**Vocational expert hypothetical:** The ALJ must pose hypotheticals to the VE that accurately reflect all limitations the ALJ finds credible. If the RFC includes a restriction (e.g., limited to light work; no concentrated exposure to fumes; limited to simple routine tasks), that restriction must be in the hypothetical. A VE's answer to an incomplete hypothetical is not substantial evidence.

**HALLEX I-2-8-18 — Closing the Record:**

After the hearing, the ALJ must: (a) advise the claimant how long the record will remain open for post-hearing submissions; (b) advise the claimant of any outstanding evidence expected in the record; (c) not issue a decision until the record is formally closed. Where the ALJ issues a decision before receiving expected evidence without notifying the claimant and giving an opportunity to object, the procedural right is violated.

**ALJ Written Decision — 20 C.F.R. § 404.953:**

The ALJ's decision must: identify the issues; evaluate the evidence; make findings on each issue including the claimant's RFC, the step at which denial occurred, and the basis for any adverse credibility findings; explain why medical source opinions were accepted or rejected. A boilerplate template decision that does not address the claimant's specific evidence is not a valid decision under § 404.953.
