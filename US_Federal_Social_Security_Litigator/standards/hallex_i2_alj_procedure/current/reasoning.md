# SSA HALLEX — ALJ Hearing Procedure — Reasoning

## Why This Standard Exists

HALLEX I-2-6 exists because the ALJ hearing is the critical procedural juncture in SSA disability adjudication. By the time a claimant reaches the ALJ level, they have been denied at initial application and reconsideration. The ALJ hearing is the first opportunity for a claimant to appear in person, present testimony, cross-examine expert witnesses, and have a decision-maker who must write individualized findings. Without procedural standards governing that hearing, the ALJ's broad discretion could produce outcomes as arbitrary as the pre-1978 adjudicatory chaos.

HALLEX implements the due process requirements recognized in Mathews v. Eldridge (1976) at the hearing level. Notice, opportunity to be heard, and a reasoned written decision are the constitutional minimum. HALLEX operationalizes those minimums with specific procedural requirements: timing of notice, content of the record, format of expert testimony, requirements for the written decision.

## Operational Rationale

The duty to develop the record at the hearing level reflects the recognition that SSDI claimants are by definition impaired — often unable to navigate a complex administrative process on their own or afford qualified representation. Congress did not design SSDI as an adversarial proceeding where the government wins by default if the claimant fails to present sufficient evidence. The ALJ is an independent adjudicator, not an adversary, with an affirmative obligation to obtain the evidence needed to make a fair decision.

The vocational expert hypothetical requirements exist because step-5 analysis — whether jobs exist in the national economy — is inherently a factual question that ALJs are not qualified to answer from their own knowledge. A VE's testimony is the SSA's mechanism for answering that question. The hypothetical must accurately reflect all the claimant's limitations for the VE's answer to have evidentiary weight. If the hypothetical is incomplete, the testimony is unreliable — and a denial based on it is not supported by substantial evidence.

## Doctrinal Basis

HALLEX is not a regulation, but SSA is bound by its own internal rules when those rules create procedural rights and claimants rely on them. Courts have held that HALLEX violations that prejudice a claimant's ability to present their case constitute reversible error requiring remand. The prejudice requirement is modest: if the claimant was denied an opportunity to submit evidence, have an adequate hearing, or receive a decision addressing their impairments, prejudice is generally presumed.
