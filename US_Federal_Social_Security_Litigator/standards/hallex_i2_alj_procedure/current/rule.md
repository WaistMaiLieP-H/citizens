# SSA HALLEX — ALJ Hearing Procedure — Rule

**Authority:** SSA HALLEX I-2-6 (ALJ Hearing Procedures); I-2-8-18 (Closing the Hearing Record)
**Jurisdiction:** Federal — SSA Administrative (non-regulatory but binding on OHO operations)
**Effective:** Ongoing — HALLEX provisions are updated by SSA OHO; I-2-6 governs all ALJ hearings

## The Rule

When a claimant requests a hearing before an Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) after an initial denial and reconsideration denial, the hearing is governed by the SSA Hearings, Appeals and Litigation Law Manual (HALLEX), Chapter I-2. The ALJ hearing is the first opportunity for a full evidentiary record, oral testimony, and cross-examination of vocational experts. HALLEX I-2-6 governs the conduct of the hearing.

**Key HALLEX procedural requirements:**

**Notice:** The claimant must receive written notice of the time, place, and issues of the hearing at least 75 days before the hearing date (20 C.F.R. § 404.938). HALLEX I-2-6-30 governs notice content requirements.

**Right to representation:** The claimant must be informed of the right to representation at the hearing. If unrepresented, the ALJ must take special care to develop the record fully.

**Duty to develop the record:** The ALJ has an affirmative duty to fully develop the record even when the claimant is represented. The ALJ may not deny a claim because the claimant failed to submit evidence the ALJ knew existed and did not obtain.

**Vocational expert testimony:** At step 5, the ALJ poses hypotheticals to a vocational expert (VE) describing a person with the claimant's RFC, age, education, and work experience. The hypothetical must accurately reflect all limitations the ALJ accepts. An incomplete hypothetical that omits accepted limitations is reversible error.

**Closing the record (HALLEX I-2-8-18):** After the hearing, the record must be formally closed before the ALJ issues a decision. The claimant must be given an opportunity to submit post-hearing evidence and written argument. A decision issued before the record is properly closed is procedurally defective.

**Written decision:** The ALJ must issue a written decision explaining the rationale. The decision must address all severe impairments, explain the RFC determination with reference to the medical evidence, and articulate reasons for accepting or rejecting medical source opinions. A boilerplate decision that does not address the claimant's specific evidence is not supported by substantial evidence.

## Key Elements

1. **Due process at the hearing:** HALLEX I-2-6 implements the constitutional due process requirement (Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319 (1976)) in SSA hearings — notice, opportunity to be heard, written decision with reasons.
2. **ALJ's duty to develop:** The ALJ is not an adversary — they have an affirmative duty to develop a full record, including evidence favorable to the claimant.
3. **VE hypothetical must include all accepted limitations:** If the RFC includes a limitation, the VE hypothetical must include it. If the ALJ omits a limitation from the hypothetical, the VE's testimony does not constitute substantial evidence.
4. **HALLEX violations are reviewable:** Courts have held that significant HALLEX violations that prejudice the claimant may constitute reversible error, even though HALLEX is an internal manual not a regulation.

## What This Means in Practice

The ALJ hearing is where most disability cases are won or lost. Procedural errors at the hearing — failure to give adequate notice, failure to develop the record, incomplete VE hypotheticals, boilerplate decision language — are the most common grounds for reversal on § 405(g) review. A claimant who was denied at the ALJ level should examine the hearing record for these procedural failures before framing the federal court appeal.
