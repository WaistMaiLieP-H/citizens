## VE Hypothetical Completeness Rule

The single most frequently reversed SSA error in the Ninth Circuit is an incomplete VE hypothetical at step 5.

**Rule:** The hypothetical must include every limitation the ALJ finds credible.

**Application matrix:**
| ALJ accepts this limitation | Must appear in VE hypothetical |
|---|---|
| Limited to light work (20 lbs occasionally, 10 lbs frequently) | Yes |
| No concentrated exposure to pulmonary irritants | Yes |
| Limited to simple, routine tasks | Yes |
| No more than occasional overhead reaching with right arm | Yes |
| Need to alternate sit/stand every 30 minutes | Yes |

If the RFC includes a restriction and the hypothetical omits it, the VE's answer does not establish what jobs the claimant — with all of their accepted limitations — can perform.

## DOT Consistency Requirement (SSR 00-4p)

When any conflict exists between VE testimony and the DOT, the ALJ must:
1. Ask the VE whether their testimony is consistent with the DOT
2. If the VE says "yes" and the record reveals a conflict, the ALJ must explain the discrepancy
3. If the VE says "no, there is a conflict," the ALJ must obtain a reasonable explanation and accept it or reject it with reasons

Failure to follow SSR 00-4p is a reversible HALLEX violation when the VE is the basis for the step-5 finding.

## Written Decision Requirements (20 C.F.R. § 404.953)

HALLEX operationalizes the § 404.953 written decision requirement:
- The decision must address the claimant's specific impairments, not describe the sequential evaluation process in the abstract
- Each rejected medical opinion must be identified and a specific reason given for the rejection
- Boilerplate credibility findings ("the claimant's statements are not fully consistent with the record") without case-specific reasoning are insufficient

Courts in the Ninth Circuit have reversed on this basis where the ALJ's decision:
- Failed to mention a treating physician's opinion at all
- Used identical boilerplate in multiple cases with no individualization
- Addressed the claimant's impairments in a single conclusory paragraph
