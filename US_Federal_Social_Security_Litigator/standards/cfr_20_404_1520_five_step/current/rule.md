# SSA Five-Step Sequential Evaluation for Disability — Rule

**Authority:** 20 C.F.R. § 404.1520
**Jurisdiction:** Federal
**Effective:** 1978 (43 Fed. Reg. 55349, November 28, 1978); current form reflects subsequent regulatory amendments

## The Rule

When SSA receives a disability claim, it must evaluate it using a mandatory five-step sequential process. The steps must be followed in order. If a determination can be made at any step, the evaluation stops there. The SSA — not the claimant — bears the burden of proof at steps 1, 2, and 4. The claimant bears the burden at step 5.

**Step 1 — Are you doing substantial gainful activity (SGA)?**
If you are currently working and your earnings exceed the SGA threshold ($1,550/month in 2024 for non-blind individuals), you are not disabled regardless of your medical condition. If you are not working at SGA, proceed to Step 2.

**Step 2 — Do you have a severe impairment?**
Your physical or mental impairment must significantly limit your ability to do basic work activities. If your impairment is "not severe" — meaning it has no more than a minimal effect on your ability to work — you are not disabled. If you have a severe impairment, proceed to Step 3.

**Step 3 — Does your impairment meet or medically equal a Listing?**
The Listings of Impairments (20 C.F.R. Part 404, Subpart P, Appendix 1) describe impairments presumed severe enough to prevent any gainful activity. If your condition meets the specific criteria of a listed impairment — or is medically equivalent in severity — you are disabled without further evaluation. If not, proceed to Step 4.

**Step 4 — Can you do your past relevant work?**
SSA determines your Residual Functional Capacity (RFC) — what you can still do despite your impairments. If your RFC allows you to perform any of your past relevant work (work you did in the last 15 years that lasted long enough to learn it), you are not disabled. If you cannot do your past work, proceed to Step 5.

**Step 5 — Can you do any other work?**
Considering your RFC, age, education, and work experience, SSA determines whether jobs exist in significant numbers in the national economy that you can perform. If no such jobs exist, you are disabled. If such jobs exist, you are not disabled.

## Key Elements

1. **Mandatory sequential order:** Steps cannot be skipped or reordered.
2. **SSA bears burden at steps 1, 2, and 4:** The claimant does not need to prove non-severity at step 2 or inability to do past work at step 4 — SSA must affirmatively find those conclusions supportable.
3. **Medical equivalence at step 3:** Meeting a Listing requires either exact match to all criteria or medical equivalence — a combination of impairments equal in severity to a listed impairment qualifies.
4. **RFC is the linchpin between steps 3 and 4:** If a claimant does not meet a Listing, the RFC determination governs steps 4 and 5 — an unsupported RFC is reversible legal error.
5. **Vocational expert at step 5:** ALJs typically use a vocational expert (VE) to identify jobs in the national economy matching the claimant's RFC, age, education, and work experience. Flawed hypotheticals to the VE are reversible error.

## What This Means in Practice

Every SSA disability denial is either a step-2 denial (not severe), a step-3 denial (does not meet a Listing), a step-4 denial (can do past work), or a step-5 denial (can do other work). Identifying the step of denial defines the legal error theory on appeal. A denial that skips a step, applies the wrong standard at a step, or fails to develop the medical evidence needed for a step analysis is procedurally defective and subject to reversal on § 405(g) substantial evidence review.
