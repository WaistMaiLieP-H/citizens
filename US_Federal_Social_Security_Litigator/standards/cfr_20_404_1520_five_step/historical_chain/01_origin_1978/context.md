# CFR 20 § 404.1520 — Historical Origin (1978)

## Origin Event

**Date:** November 28, 1978
**Published:** 43 Fed. Reg. 55349 (Nov. 28, 1978)
**Effective date:** January 1, 1979
**Regulatory authority:** Social Security Administration, pursuant to 42 U.S.C. §§ 405(a), 423(d)

## What Happened

The Social Security Administration promulgated a comprehensive revision to the sequential evaluation process for disability determinations. The 1978 rulemaking codified the five-step framework that had been used informally and inconsistently by DDS agencies and ALJs since the 1960s.

Prior to 1978, the sequential evaluation existed in internal SSA guidance but not in the Code of Federal Regulations. DDS determinations followed varying protocols across states. The 1978 rulemaking brought uniformity, published the evaluation sequence in binding regulatory form, and established the step-by-step burden framework that courts would later use as the basis for judicial review.

## Why the Standard Was Created

**The pre-regulatory problem:** Disability determinations before 1978 were inconsistent across the 50+ state DDS agencies. A claimant denied in one state might have been approved in another applying the same medical evidence under different internal procedures. Congressional oversight hearings in the 1970s documented widespread variation.

**The administrative law dimension:** Without a codified framework, federal courts reviewing SSA decisions had no clear regulatory standard to apply. The "substantial evidence" standard of § 405(g) needed a defined procedural framework to give it operational meaning.

**The 1978 solution:** Codify the five-step process in CFR, making each step a legally reviewable determination. This enabled courts to identify at exactly which step a denial occurred, what burden applied at that step, and whether the record evidence supported the finding.

## Historical Loss the Standard Prevents

Without the codified five-step framework:
- Courts could not identify where the adjudicatory error occurred
- ALJs could deny at "step 5" without actually applying step-5 analysis
- Claimants had no framework to argue which step the ALJ misapplied
- SSA could shift its reasoning on appeal without the framework constraining it

The 1978 codification made denial at each step separately reviewable — a structural protection for claimants that persists today.

## Key 1978 Regulatory Text Notes

The 1978 version introduced the burden framework:
- Steps 1-4: SSA bears the burden of developing the record and applying each step
- Step 5: SSA must show that jobs exist in the national economy; claimant cannot be denied at step 5 without affirmative evidence of available work

This burden allocation was the significant legal advance of the 1978 rulemaking. Prior to codification, burden allocation was disputed and inconsistently applied.

## Evolution After 1978

- **1991:** Amended to clarify RFC assessment procedures and consultative examination requirements
- **2000:** SSR 00-4p issued (DOT consistency requirement for VE testimony)
- **2017:** 20 C.F.R. § 404.1520c replaced § 404.1527's treating physician hierarchy with a "supportability and consistency" framework for claims filed on or after March 27, 2017
- **Ongoing:** The five-step framework itself (§ 404.1520(a)-(f)) has remained structurally unchanged since 1978; amendments have been to subordinate provisions
