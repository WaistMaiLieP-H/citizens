## Predicate Offense Cross-References

### 18 USC § 1347 — Healthcare Fraud
**Relationship:** Criminal statute that runs parallel to FCA civil liability. A provider who submits false claims to Medicare or Medi-Cal violates both § 1347 (criminal, DOJ prosecution) and § 3729 (civil, qui tam). FCA recovery and criminal prosecution are not mutually exclusive — DOJ routinely pursues both tracks simultaneously.
**Governing Citizen:** CA_Criminal_Law_Specialist (criminal track), DELATOR (civil qui tam track)
**Cross-case trigger:** Any CMS-1500 claim for a procedure that was unnecessary, falsely coded, or never performed.

---

### 18 USC § 1001 — False Statements to Federal Agencies
**Relationship:** False statements made to SSA (disability determinations), DOL (pension filings), or CMS (Medicare enrollment/claims) that are also used to obtain federal funds constitute dual violations: § 1001 (criminal false statement) and § 3729(a)(1)(B) (civil false record or statement material to a false claim).
**Governing Citizen:** CA_Criminal_Law_Specialist (criminal), DELATOR (civil)
**Cross-case trigger:** Any SSA contact, DDS report, or DOL Form 5500 entry containing material falsehoods.

---

### 26 USC § 7623(b) — IRS Whistleblower Program
**Relationship:** Tax fraud that accompanies FCA violations — false W-2s, unreported income, false pension deductions — routes to the IRS Whistleblower Program in parallel with the FCA filing. The § 7623(b) threshold is: tax, penalties, and interest exceed $2M AND taxpayer annual income exceeds $200K in at least one tax year. Awards: 15–30% of collected proceeds.
**Governing Citizen:** DELATOR (SKILL-DEL-003: irs-whistleblower-claim)
**Cross-case trigger:** False W-2 filings, false pension contribution records, unreported income above $200K.

---

### 15 USC § 78u-6 — SEC Whistleblower (Dodd-Frank § 922)
**Relationship:** If fraud involves publicly traded entities, securities, or material misstatements in SEC filings (Form 10-K, proxy, 8-K), the SEC Whistleblower Program pays 10–30% of sanctions exceeding $1M. Requires original information voluntarily provided to SEC. Coordinates with FCA where the defendant is a publicly traded corporation that defrauded both federal programs and its shareholders.
**Governing Citizen:** DELATOR (adjacent scope)
**Cross-case trigger:** Any defendant that is a publicly traded company with SEC reporting obligations.

---

### Cal. Government Code §§ 12650–12656 — California False Claims Act
**Relationship:** Parallel state qui tam for Medi-Cal fraud and other California state program fraud. Filed in California Superior Court. CFCA relator share: 15–33%. Seal period mirrors federal FCA. California Attorney General (DHCS for Medi-Cal) reviews during seal period. Filing both federal FCA and CFCA simultaneously maximizes intervention probability and relator recovery.
**Governing Citizen:** DELATOR (SKILL-DEL-004: ca-fca-qui-tam-filing)
**Cross-case trigger:** Any Medi-Cal billing, DHCS submission, or California state grant/contract fraud.

---

### ERISA §§ 406, 409 — Prohibited Transactions and Fiduciary Breach
**Relationship:** UA Local 342 pension is an ERISA-governed multi-employer Taft-Hartley plan. False contribution records, false advancement records submitted to the plan, and failure to fund properly are ERISA violations. DOL Form 5500 is the federal reporting document — if false entries appear on Form 5500, those are false records submitted to the DOL, triggering § 3729(a)(1)(B) liability.
**Governing Citizen:** US_Federal_Financial_Fraud_Litigator (ERISA track), DELATOR (FCA false-record track for Form 5500 entries)
**Cross-case trigger:** Any UA Local 342 contribution record, advancement record, or Form 5500 filing.

---

### 31 USC §§ 3801–3812 — Program Fraud Civil Remedies Act (PFCRA)
**Relationship:** Administrative analog to the FCA for smaller claims (under $150,000 per claim). Agencies can impose civil penalties of up to $5,000 per false claim administratively without court action. Runs concurrently with FCA for SSA, DOL, and HHS false submissions. Relator does not receive a share under PFCRA — this is an agency-initiated remedy — but PFCRA liability corroborates FCA materiality (shows government treats violation as actionable).
**Cross-case trigger:** SSA disability determination fraud, DOL benefit claim fraud under $150K.

---

### Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens, 529 US 765 (2000)
**Relationship:** Supreme Court held that a relator (private party) has Article III standing to bring a qui tam suit because the government has partially assigned its claim to the relator. DELATOR applies this at standing analysis. Key holding: injury-in-fact is the government's injury; the partial assignment gives the relator standing even though the relator did not personally suffer the financial loss from the false claim.
**Cross-case significance:** Confirms DELATOR's standing even where the steward was harmed as a victim but not the direct treasury payee.

---

### United States ex rel. Hendow v. University of Phoenix, 461 F.3d 1166 (9th Cir. 2006)
**Relationship:** Ninth Circuit's leading case on implied false certification in the Ninth Circuit (pre-Escobar). Established that a defendant who submits claims impliedly certifying compliance with program requirements violates the FCA when it knows those requirements are violated. Supports DELATOR's theory for Medicare Advantage claims.
**Jurisdiction:** Ninth Circuit (governs federal filings in California district courts including NDCA)

---

### United States v. Bourseau, 531 F.3d 1159 (9th Cir. 2008)
**Relationship:** Ninth Circuit held that Medicare cost reports are "claims" under the FCA. Every annual Medicare cost report submitted by a participating provider certifying compliance with program requirements is a potential FCA false claim if the provider knew it was noncompliant.
**Cross-case trigger:** Any Medicare-participating provider with annual cost report obligations.
