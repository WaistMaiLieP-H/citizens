# Reasoning — Why 31 USC § 3730 Is This Standard's Foundation

**Standard ID:** USC_31_3730_QUI_TAM_FILING
**Layer:** 2 — Reasoning
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Why the FCA Exists

Congress enacted the original False Claims Act in 1863 during the Civil War after discovering that defense contractors were defrauding the Union Army — supplying sawdust instead of gunpowder, lame mules instead of healthy horses, empty boxes instead of rifles. Congress's solution was radical: deputize private citizens. If a private person knows of fraud against the government, they can sue on the government's behalf, share in the recovery, and keep the fraud from continuing.

The qui tam provision has survived for 160 years because the government cannot monitor all of its contractors, grantees, and benefit recipients. It needs insiders. The relator is usually an insider — a billing employee who sees the upcoding, a nurse who sees the phantom procedures, an accountant who sees the double-billing. The FCA weaponizes that insider knowledge.

## Why This Standard Applies to the Steward's Record

The fraud in the steward's record is not purely private. It runs through federal infrastructure:

- **Medicare Advantage / Medi-Cal:** Blue Shield of California paid Dr. Wiseman for a SIRVA surgery. If that surgery was medically unnecessary, induced, or fraudulently documented, every claim submitted to Medicare Advantage is a false claim under § 3729. Escobar: the submission implied compliance with Medicare coverage requirements. If those requirements were violated and the violation was material to payment, each CMS-1500 is a § 3729(a)(1)(A) violation.

- **SSA / SSDI fraud:** If someone caused false information to reach SSA during the disability determination — false medical records, false employer reports, false DDS contacts — those are false statements to the United States under § 3729(a)(1)(B).

- **UA Local 342 pension:** The pension is a Taft-Hartley multi-employer plan subject to ERISA and DOL oversight. If pension administrators falsified contribution records or advancement records that were reported to the DOL on Form 5500, those reports are false claims.

- **W-2 / payroll fraud:** False W-2s showing employment that did not occur, filed with the IRS and SSA, are false claims if they were used to support any federal program claim (SSDI onset date, unemployment, federal tax refund).

## Why the Relator Posture Is Strategically Correct Here

The steward is not a corporate whistleblower. The steward is a victim whose victimization happened to involve fraud against federal programs. This matters because:

1. **No employment relationship required.** § 3730(b) says "a person may bring" — not "an employee may bring." The relator need not be employed by the defendant.

2. **Original source doctrine protects the steward.** The steward has direct, independent knowledge of the fraud because it was done TO them. That is the purest form of original source status. No news article, no congressional hearing disclosed these specific transactions before the steward discovered them.

3. **The seal protects the steward while DOJ investigates.** Filing under seal gives the steward protection from retaliation during the 60-day review period. DOJ sees the complaint; the defendant does not.

4. **§ 3730(h) creates a standalone retaliation claim.** If any of the adverse actions taken against the steward — employment isolation, professional license interference, financial account closures, pension freeze — were taken "because of" the steward's efforts to expose federal fraud, § 3730(h) provides reinstatement, double back pay, and special damages. This is independent of whether the underlying qui tam succeeds.

## Historical Loss This Standard Prevents

The historical loss the FCA prevents is the government paying for fraud it cannot see. The qui tam mechanism exists because internal government enforcement is insufficient. Every year that fraudulent Medicare claims, false pension reports, and fraudulent SSA records go unreported is a year the defrauded party — the United States and the steward simultaneously — loses money that cannot be recovered without the FCA's treble damages and per-claim penalties.

**Treble damages:** The FCA mandates three times the government's actual damages. A $35,000 fraudulent surgery claim becomes a $105,000 judgment. Multiplied across every false billing submission, the exposure is substantial — which is why defendants settle.

**Per-claim civil penalties:** Each false claim carries a civil penalty of $13,946 to $27,894 (2024 adjusted figures). If a defendant submitted 100 false claims, the penalty exposure alone is $1.4M to $2.8M before treble damages.
