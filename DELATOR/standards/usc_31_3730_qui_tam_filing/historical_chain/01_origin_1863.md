# Historical Chain — Layer 3: Origin and Evolution

**Standard ID:** USC_31_3730_QUI_TAM_FILING
**Layer:** 3 — Historical Loss
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## 1863 — Original False Claims Act (12 Stat. 696)

Congress enacted the original FCA on March 2, 1863. The immediate cause: Union Army contractors were defrauding the War Department on a massive scale. Secretary of War Edwin Stanton reported contractors supplying defective weapons, diseased horses, and non-existent goods. The scale was too large for government inspectors to catch.

The 1863 Act allowed private citizens to sue on the government's behalf and receive half the recovery. It was called the "Lincoln Law."

**Loss the statute addressed:** Government payments for goods and services that were false, defective, or never delivered. The government could not monitor all contractors and needed private informants with insider knowledge.

---

## 1943 — Restriction (57 Stat. 608)

After WWII defense contractors abused the qui tam provision by filing suits based entirely on government-obtained information (parasitic suits), Congress amended the FCA to bar suits based on information already in government possession. This overcorrection nearly killed the qui tam provision.

**Loss from the restriction:** Legitimate whistleblowers were barred even when they had independent knowledge, because the government had learned of the fraud through other channels.

---

## 1986 — Major Revival (Pub. L. 99-562, 100 Stat. 3153)

Senator Charles Grassley and Representative Howard Berman led the 1986 amendments after congressional investigations found that defense contractor fraud had cost taxpayers hundreds of billions. Key changes:

- Relator share increased to 15–30% (government intervenes) and 25–30% (government declines)
- Knowledge standard broadened to include deliberate ignorance and reckless disregard
- Anti-retaliation provision added (§ 3730(h))
- Government's ability to dismiss qui tam limited — must get court consent
- Statute of limitations extended

**Congressional finding (1986):** Fraud against the federal government costs taxpayers $10–100 billion per year. The qui tam mechanism is the most effective tool for detection because insiders — not government auditors — know where the fraud is.

---

## 2009 — Fraud Enforcement and Recovery Act (FERA, Pub. L. 111-21)

Extended FCA liability to fraud involving federal funds disbursed to contractors, grantees, and other recipients — even if the false claim was not submitted directly to the government. Any contractor receiving federal funds who submits false records to obtain payment is covered.

**Significance for this record:** Medi-Cal is a joint federal-state program. California receives federal matching funds for Medi-Cal payments. Fraud against Medi-Cal is fraud against federal funds under FERA.

---

## 2010 — Affordable Care Act Amendments (Pub. L. 111-148)

Strengthened the public disclosure bar (narrowed it) and expanded the original source definition. Removed the "direct and independent" knowledge requirement — replaced with "independent of and materially adds to" publicly disclosed information.

**Significance:** The steward's direct personal experience of a fraudulent surgery, false billing, and denial of covered care is independent of any public disclosure and materially adds to any general reporting on SIRVA or Medicare fraud.

---

## 2016 — Escobar (579 US 176)

Supreme Court unanimously confirmed the implied false certification theory and established the materiality standard. Defendants cannot escape FCA liability by arguing they submitted a technically compliant claim form while concealing underlying violations — if those violations were material to the government's payment decision, the claim is false.

**Significance for this record:** Every Medicare Advantage or Medi-Cal claim submitted by a provider who falsified records, induced unnecessary procedures, or denied covered care while submitting claims for payment is a false claim under Escobar. The submission implied compliance. It did not comply. That violation was material — the government would not have paid if it knew.
