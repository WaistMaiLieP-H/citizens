# §1983 Evolution: Pre-1979 → Pub. L. 96-170 (1979)

## What Changed

Two additions:

### 1. "or the District of Columbia" added to the "under color of" clause
- **Before:** "of any State or Territory"
- **After:** "of any State or Territory or the District of Columbia"
- **Effect:** Extended §1983 to cover D.C. officials acting under color of D.C. law. Before this amendment, the District of Columbia was arguably neither a "State" nor a "Territory" for §1983 purposes, creating a gap in coverage for D.C. residents.

### 2. New sentence added at end
- **Added:** "For the purposes of this section, any Act of Congress applicable exclusively to the District of Columbia shall be considered to be a statute of the District of Columbia."
- **Effect:** Clarified that D.C.-specific federal legislation counts as "D.C. law" for §1983 purposes. Because D.C. is governed in part by Acts of Congress, this was necessary to close the definitional gap — without it, a D.C. official acting under a D.C.-specific Act of Congress could argue they were acting under "federal" law, not "D.C." law, and escape §1983 liability.

## Why This Amendment Was Enacted

The District of Columbia had home rule under the District of Columbia Self-Government and Governmental Reorganization Act of 1973 (Pub. L. 93-198), but many D.C. laws were still enacted by Congress rather than the D.C. Council. The 1979 amendment ensured that D.C. residents had the same §1983 remedies as residents of the 50 states — regardless of whether the offending official was acting under a D.C. Council enactment or a D.C.-specific Act of Congress.

This was the first time Congress amended the substantive text of §1983 since the original 1871 enactment. The text had sat unchanged for 108 years.
