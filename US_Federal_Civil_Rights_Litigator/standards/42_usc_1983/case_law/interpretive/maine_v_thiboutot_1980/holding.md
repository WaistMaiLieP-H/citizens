# Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980)

## The Case

Lionel and Joline Thiboutot were welfare recipients in Maine. They alleged that Maine's Department of Human Services had calculated their welfare benefits incorrectly under the federal Social Security Act — a federal statute, but not a civil rights statute. They sued the state officials under §1983.

The question was whether §1983's phrase "rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution **and laws**" covered all federal statutes, or only civil rights statutes.

## What the Court Held

Justice Brennan, writing for the majority:

1. **The phrase "and laws" in §1983 means all federal laws, not just civil rights laws.** The plain text does not limit "laws" to a particular category. If Congress had intended to restrict §1983 to civil rights violations, it would have said so.

2. **Section 1983 provides a cause of action for the deprivation of rights secured by any federal statute, not just the Constitution or civil rights statutes.** This vastly expanded §1983's reach — from a purely constitutional-rights remedy to a general vehicle for enforcing federal statutory rights against state and local officials.

## The Rule Thiboutot Stands For

Section 1983 covers violations of **any** federal law, not just the Constitution and not just civil rights statutes. A state official who violates a federal statute — welfare law, education law, environmental law, healthcare law, any federal statute that creates individual rights — can be sued under §1983.

## Why This Case Matters to the Evolution Chain

This case gave operational meaning to the two words "and laws" that were added to §1983 during the 1874 Revised Statutes recodification. The original 1871 text protected rights "secured by the Constitution of the United States." The recodification added "and laws." For over a century, the implications of that addition were largely unexplored. Thiboutot settled it: "and laws" means what it says.

The 1874 codifiers' quiet expansion — from constitutional rights to constitutional-and-statutory rights — became one of the most consequential features of §1983 more than a hundred years later. Every §1983 claim based on a federal statutory violation (Medicaid, IDEA, Title IX enforcement against state actors, Social Security, etc.) traces its viability to this two-word addition and this case.

## Statute Version the Court Was Reading

42 USC §1983 (post-1979 D.C. amendment, pre-1996 judicial immunity amendment). The "and laws" language the Court interpreted was in the statute from 1874 onward.
