# 42 U.S.C. § 1983 — Civil Action for Deprivation of Rights

**Standard ID:** USC_42_1983_CIVIL_RIGHTS_ACTION
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or usage, of any State or Territory or the District of Columbia, subjects, or causes to be subjected, any citizen of the United States or other person within the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party injured in an action at law, suit in equity, or other proper proceeding for redress, except that in any action brought against a judicial officer for an act or omission taken in such officer's judicial capacity, injunctive relief shall not be granted unless a declaratory decree was violated or declaratory relief was unavailable.

*(42 U.S.C. § 1983; R.S. § 1979; Pub. L. 96–170, § 1, Dec. 29, 1979; Pub. L. 104–317, title III, § 309(c), Oct. 19, 1996)*

---

## Elements of a § 1983 Claim

To state a claim under § 1983, the plaintiff must show:

1. **The defendant is a "person"** — natural persons and local government entities (municipalities, counties) qualify. States and state agencies do not (Eleventh Amendment immunity). (*Will v. Michigan Dept. of State Police*, 491 U.S. 58 (1989).)

2. **Action under color of state law** — the defendant acted under authority or apparent authority of state or local law, regulation, custom, or policy. Private parties generally cannot be sued under § 1983 unless they act in concert with or at the direction of state actors.

3. **Deprivation of a federal right** — the conduct deprived the plaintiff of a right secured by the Constitution or federal law. The right must be individually enforceable — not every federal statute creates a § 1983 right.

4. **Causation** — the defendant's action caused the deprivation.

---

## Key Limitations and Defenses

**Qualified immunity:** Individual government officers are shielded from § 1983 liability unless they violated a "clearly established" right that a reasonable person would have known. (*Harlow v. Fitzgerald*, 457 U.S. 800 (1982).) The "clearly established" standard is demanding — the officer's conduct must violate a prior case with "materially similar facts."

**Absolute immunity:** Judges (for judicial acts), prosecutors (for prosecutorial functions), and legislators (for legislative acts) have absolute immunity from § 1983 claims.

**No respondeat superior for municipalities:** A municipality cannot be held liable solely because it employs someone who violated constitutional rights. Municipal liability requires proof that an official policy or custom caused the violation (*Monell v. Dept. of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658 (1978)).

**Statute of limitations:** § 1983 does not have its own limitations period. Courts apply the forum state's general personal injury statute of limitations — in California, two years under CCP § 335.1.

**Exhaustion:** In prison cases, the Prison Litigation Reform Act (42 U.S.C. § 1997e) requires exhaustion of available administrative remedies before filing suit.

---

## Remedies

- **Damages** — compensatory, nominal, and punitive (against individual defendants; not against government entities)
- **Injunctive relief** — against ongoing constitutional violations
- **Attorney fees** — mandatory to prevailing plaintiffs under 42 U.S.C. § 1988(b)

---

## Plain English

Section 1983 is the primary federal statute for suing state and local government officials who violate constitutional rights. A police officer who uses excessive force, a school administrator who suspends a student in violation of due process, a county official who retaliates against a whistleblower — all are potential § 1983 defendants. The statute does not work against federal officers (see Bivens doctrine) or the State itself (Eleventh Amendment). The key obstacle is qualified immunity, which protects individual officers unless the right they violated was "clearly established."
