# Historical Chain — 42 U.S.C. § 1985

**Standard ID:** USC_42_1985_CONSPIRACY_CIVIL_RIGHTS
**Layer:** 3 — Historical Chain
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Origin — Reconstruction Era

**1871 — Ku Klux Klan Act (42nd Congress, Ch. 22)**
Section 2 of the Ku Klux Klan Act, enacted April 20, 1871, created civil liability for conspiracies that deprive persons of equal protection of the laws. The statute was enacted in direct response to documented Klan violence: masked conspiracies of private individuals terrorizing freedmen, preventing them from exercising rights guaranteed by the Fourteenth Amendment, and preventing state officials from enforcing those rights.

The Klan Act is the source of the current § 1985 in its entirety. The Revised Statutes codified it at R.S. § 1980 (1874).

**1874 — Revised Statutes § 1980**
The Enforcement Acts were consolidated into the Revised Statutes of the United States. The conspiracy-to-deprive-of-equal-protection provision was codified at R.S. § 1980.

---

## Modern Codification

**1948 — Title 42, U.S.C. § 1985**
The Judicial Code of 1948 recodified R.S. § 1980 at 42 U.S.C. § 1985, its current location. The text was not substantively amended at that time.

---

## Judicial Construction — The Critical Narrowing

**1951 — *Collins v. Hardyman*, 341 U.S. 651**
The Supreme Court initially adopted a narrowing construction: § 1985(3) required state action, limiting its reach to conspiracies involving government actors. This effectively gutted the statute's application to private conspiracies.

**1971 — *Griffin v. Breckenridge*, 403 U.S. 88**
The Court unanimously overruled *Collins* and held that § 1985(3) reaches private conspiracies — no state action is required. The Court imposed the class-based animus requirement to ensure the statute had constitutional grounding: Congress had power to reach private conduct under the Thirteenth Amendment (abolishing slavery) and the privileges and immunities clauses. Without the class-based animus requirement, the statute would create a general federal tort for private conspiracies without constitutional basis.

*Griffin* remains the foundational § 1985(3) case.

---

## Key Subsequent Developments

**1985 — *United Brotherhood of Carpenters v. Scott*, 463 U.S. 825**
The Court refused to extend § 1985(3) to conspiracies motivated by economic or commercial animus rather than class-based discriminatory animus. Plaintiffs cannot bootstrap § 1985(3) as a general conspiracy statute; the class-based animus element is not merely prefatory but is an essential element.

**1987 — *Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic*, 506 U.S. 263 (1993)**
The Court held that opposition to abortion is not animus against women as a class for § 1985(3) purposes — the animus must be directed at the plaintiff as a member of a protected class, not at an activity in which the class disproportionately participates.

---

## Current Status

The statute remains in its 1871 form, as judicially construed by *Griffin* (1971) and its progeny. No significant statutory amendment has occurred since codification. The class-based animus requirement and the private-conspiracy reach are settled law.
