KUSH v. RUTLEDGE
460 U.S. 719 (1983)
United States Supreme Court
Decided: April 4, 1983

---

FACTS:

Native American football players at the University of Arizona brought a § 1985(1) claim against coach Frank Kush and university officials, alleging a conspiracy to intimidate witnesses and obstruct justice in connection with a lawsuit filed against Kush. The plaintiffs argued the conspiracy targeted them based on their race.

The threshold question was whether the first two subsections of § 1985 — § 1985(1) (preventing federal officers from performing duties) and § 1985(2) (obstructing justice in federal courts) — required a showing of racial or class-based discriminatory animus, as § 1985(3) did under Griffin v. Breckenridge (1971).

---

HOLDING:

Justice Marshall wrote for a unanimous Court.

§ 1985(1) and § 1985(2) [FIRST CLAUSE] DO NOT REQUIRE CLASS-BASED ANIMUS:
Griffin v. Breckenridge had held that § 1985(3) requires proof of class-based discriminatory animus to state a claim. But Griffin's animus requirement was specific to § 1985(3) — it was not a general requirement for all three subsections.

§ 1985(1) — Preventing Federal Officers:
Section 1985(1) protects federal officers from conspiracies to prevent them from discharging their official duties. No class-based animus is required. The protection is based on the officer's status as a federal officer, not on membership in any protected class.

§ 1985(2) — Obstructing Justice in Federal Courts [First Clause]:
The first clause of § 1985(2) — protecting litigants, witnesses, and jurors from intimidation in FEDERAL courts — does not require class-based animus. The text protects anyone involved in federal judicial proceedings.

§ 1985(2) — SECOND CLAUSE — STATE COURTS:
The second clause of § 1985(2), covering conspiracies to obstruct justice in STATE courts, DOES require class-based discriminatory animus under Griffin (because it mirrors § 1985(3)'s equal-protection concern).

THE KUSH STRUCTURAL READING OF § 1985:
| Subsection | Subject Matter | Animus Required? |
|---|---|---|
| § 1985(1) | Conspiracies against federal officers | NO |
| § 1985(2) first clause | Federal court proceedings | NO |
| § 1985(2) second clause | State court proceedings | YES (class-based) |
| § 1985(3) | Depriving persons of equal protection | YES (class-based) |

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Kush resolves a critical structural question for § 1985 claims:

1. **§ 1985(2) — Federal court obstruction**: If witnesses have been intimidated, communications have been intercepted, or evidence has been suppressed in connection with federal proceedings (the § 1983 complaint, or any federal court case), § 1985(2) first clause applies WITHOUT needing to prove the conspiracy was motivated by class-based animus. Proving a conspiracy to intimidate a witness in a federal proceeding is enough.

2. **SIM swap / phone interception as § 1985(2) conspiracy**: If Ryan McClaran and affiliated actors intercepted communications to prevent witnesses from providing testimony in federal proceedings, that conduct falls under the no-animus-required § 1985(2) first clause.

3. **Combination with § 1985(3)**: Claims against the same defendants may proceed under both § 1985(2) (for federal court obstruction — no animus needed) and § 1985(3) (for equal-protection conspiracy — animus based on parent-class or sex discrimination required).
