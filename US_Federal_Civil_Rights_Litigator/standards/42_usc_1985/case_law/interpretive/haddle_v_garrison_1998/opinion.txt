HADDLE v. GARRISON
525 U.S. 121 (1998)
United States Supreme Court
Decided: December 14, 1998

---

FACTS:

Michael Haddle, an at-will employee at Healthmaster Inc., was allegedly fired in retaliation for complying with a federal grand jury subpoena and cooperating with federal agents investigating Healthmaster for Medicare fraud. Haddle sued under 42 U.S.C. § 1985(2), alleging that his employer and others conspired to obstruct justice in a federal judicial proceeding by getting him fired to prevent his cooperation.

The Eleventh Circuit dismissed the complaint, holding that § 1985(2) requires that the conspirators cause injury to the plaintiff's "person or property" and that loss of at-will employment — which the plaintiff had no vested legal right to — was not an injury to "property" cognizable under § 1985.

The Supreme Court unanimously reversed.

---

HOLDING:

Chief Justice Rehnquist wrote for a unanimous Court.

§ 1985(2) DOES NOT REQUIRE INJURY TO A "CONSTITUTIONALLY PROTECTED PROPERTY INTEREST":
The Eleventh Circuit's reasoning — that at-will employment is not "property" because there is no legal entitlement to continued at-will employment — was wrong.

Section 1985(2) protects "any party or witness in any court of the United States" from conspiracies that "injure [them] in [their] person or property on account of" their attendance at or testimony in a federal judicial proceeding.

"The term 'property' as used in § 1985(2) should be given its natural meaning and should not be confined to constitutional property interests."

"At-will employment, though precarious by nature, is still 'property' in the colloquial sense of an asset or interest that has economic value. The wrongful deprivation of that employment is an injury to 'property' within § 1985(2)'s meaning."

WHAT § 1985(2) PROTECTS — THE HADDLE HOLDING:
Section 1985(2) creates a federal cause of action when:
1. Two or more persons conspire
2. To deter, by force, intimidation, or threat, any party or witness in any court of the United States from attending such court or testifying freely in any matter pending therein, OR to injure any party or witness on account of his attendance or testimony
3. And the conspiracy causes actual injury to the plaintiff's person or property

The "injury" element is satisfied by any tangible economic harm — including loss of at-will employment.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Haddle is critical for § 1985(2) claims arising from economic retaliation against witnesses or parties in federal proceedings:

1. **Employment retaliation for federal court participation**: Any at-will employee fired (or constructively discharged) because of actual or threatened testimony in federal proceedings — including grand jury proceedings, federal civil rights cases, or federal criminal investigations — has a § 1985(2) claim.

2. **Non-employment economic harm**: Following Haddle's broad reading of "property," harm to other economic interests (loss of business, destruction of credit, loss of professional licenses, interference with pension benefits) as retaliation for federal court participation satisfies the "injury to property" element.

3. **SIM swap / communication disruption**: If actors cut off a witness's ability to communicate (by taking over their phone number or intercepting their communications) to prevent their participation in federal proceedings, that is both a § 1985(2) obstruction AND an injury to property (economic harm from disrupted communications).

4. **Federal grand jury context**: The SIM swap pattern beginning circa 2018, if timed to prevent coordination with federal investigators or testimony in federal proceedings, is precisely the conduct § 1985(2) was enacted to address.
