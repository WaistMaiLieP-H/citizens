# 42 U.S.C. § 12132 — ADA Title II (Discrimination by Public Entities)

**Standard ID:** USC_42_12132_ADA_TITLE_II
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

Subject to the provisions of this subchapter, no qualified individual with a disability shall, **by reason of such disability, be excluded from participation in or be denied the benefits of the services, programs, or activities of a public entity, or be subjected to discrimination by any such entity.**

*(42 U.S.C. § 12132; Pub. L. 101-336, title II, § 202, July 26, 1990, 104 Stat. 337)*

---

## Elements of an ADA Title II Claim

To establish a violation of § 12132, the plaintiff must show:

1. **The plaintiff has a disability** as defined by the ADA — a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more major life activities (42 U.S.C. § 12102);

2. **The plaintiff is a "qualified individual"** — meets the essential eligibility requirements of the service, program, or activity with or without reasonable modification;

3. **The defendant is a "public entity"** — any state or local government, any department, agency, special purpose district, or other instrumentality of a state or local government (42 U.S.C. § 12131(1));

4. **The plaintiff was excluded, denied benefits, or subjected to discrimination by reason of disability.**

---

## "Public Entity" — Scope

ADA Title II covers:
- All state government agencies and departments;
- All local government agencies and departments (cities, counties, districts);
- Courts (including state court systems);
- Public hospitals, public transit systems, public schools;
- Law enforcement agencies.

ADA Title II does **not** cover the federal government (which is covered by § 504 of the Rehabilitation Act).

---

## Reasonable Modification Requirement

A public entity must make reasonable modifications to its policies, practices, and procedures when necessary to avoid discrimination against individuals with disabilities — unless the modification would fundamentally alter the nature of the service, program, or activity. (*PGA Tour, Inc. v. Martin*, 532 U.S. 661 (2001); 28 C.F.R. § 35.130(b)(7).)

---

## Physical Accessibility — Program Access Standard

Public entities are not required to make every facility accessible. They must ensure that each program, service, and activity is accessible "when viewed in its entirety." (*28 C.F.R. § 35.150.*) For structural changes, public entities with 50+ employees must complete a transition plan to bring facilities into compliance.

---

## Relationship to Sovereign Immunity — *Tennessee v. Lane*

States have Eleventh Amendment immunity from private suits in federal court as a general rule. However, in *Tennessee v. Lane*, 541 U.S. 509 (2004), the Supreme Court held that Congress validly abrogated state sovereign immunity under Title II as applied to access to courts. The Court's broader holding — whether Title II abrogates immunity in all contexts — remains subject to circuit-by-circuit analysis depending on the constitutional right underlying the claim.

---

## Intentional Discrimination and Deliberate Indifference

For compensatory damages (as opposed to injunctive relief), plaintiffs must generally show intentional discrimination — deliberate indifference to the plaintiff's federally protected rights. Deliberate indifference requires:
- Knowledge that a harm to a federally protected right is substantially likely; and
- Failure to act despite that likelihood.

Negligent failure to accommodate generally does not support a compensatory damages award.

---

## Interaction with § 504 Rehabilitation Act

ADA Title II and § 504 of the Rehabilitation Act (29 U.S.C. § 794) are closely aligned and often pled together:
- Both prohibit disability discrimination by government entities;
- § 504 requires federal financial assistance; Title II requires only that the defendant be a public entity;
- Both provide a private right of action and attorney fee recovery;
- The legal standards are essentially the same for program accessibility and employment discrimination.

---

## Plain English

ADA Title II bars state and local governments from discriminating against people with disabilities in any of their services, programs, or activities — courts, jails, transit systems, schools, and every other government function. Unlike Title I (which covers employment) or Title III (which covers private businesses), Title II focuses entirely on government. If a public entity makes its programs inaccessible or refuses to make reasonable modifications for a disabled person, it violates Title II. In most cases, Title II and § 504 of the Rehabilitation Act are pled together because the standards overlap almost entirely.
