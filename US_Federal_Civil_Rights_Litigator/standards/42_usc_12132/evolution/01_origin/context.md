# 42 USC §12132 — Origin Context

## What happened

The Americans with Disabilities Act was signed into law on July 26, 1990 (Pub. L. 101-336, 104 Stat. 327). Title II, Section 202 — codified at 42 USC §12132 — prohibits disability discrimination by public entities (state and local governments).

## The wound

Before the ADA, persons with disabilities were systematically excluded from public services, programs, and activities by state and local governments. Inaccessible buildings. Exclusionary policies. Institutionalization. People were shut out of the basic functions of government — not because they could not participate, but because the government refused to let them.

Congress found that "historically, society has tended to isolate and segregate individuals with disabilities" and that "discrimination against individuals with disabilities persists in such critical areas as... access to public services."

## Why this law exists

The ADA was modeled on the Civil Rights Act of 1964, extending that framework to disability. Title II specifically targets state and local government discrimination. Before this, the only federal disability rights law was Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act (1973), which only covered programs receiving federal funding. Title II closed that gap: ALL public entities, whether they receive federal money or not.

## How it is enforced

Title II is enforceable through its own enforcement mechanism (§12133, which incorporates the remedies of the Rehabilitation Act). It is also enforceable through 42 USC §1983, per case law — meaning individuals can bring suit directly against state and local officials for violations, just as they can for other federal rights.
