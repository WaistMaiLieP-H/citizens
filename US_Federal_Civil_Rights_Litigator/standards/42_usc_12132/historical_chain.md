# 42 USC §12132 — Historical Chain

## Origin

The disability rights movement fought for decades before the ADA existed. The first federal disability rights law was Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (29 USC §794). Section 504 said: if you take federal money, you cannot discriminate against people with disabilities. That was the principle. But it only applied to federally funded programs — leaving out every state and local government function that did not receive federal dollars.

The ADA extended the principle to ALL public entities and private employers. Title II, Section 202 — codified at 42 USC §12132 — is the one-sentence guarantee:

> No qualified individual with a disability shall, by reason of such disability, be excluded from participation in or be denied the benefits of the services, programs, or activities of a public entity, or be subjected to discrimination by any such entity.

That is the entire statute. One sentence. It covers every state and local government service, program, and activity in the country.

## The legislative path

- **1973:** Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act — first federal disability rights law, limited to federally funded programs
- **1988:** Fair Housing Amendments Act extended disability protections to housing
- **1990-07-26:** ADA signed by President George H.W. Bush (Pub. L. 101-336, 104 Stat. 327)
- **1990–present:** §12132 has never been amended. 36 years of unchanged text.

## Enforcement

§12132 is enforced through two channels:

1. **The ADA's own enforcement mechanism** — §12133 (42 USC §12133) incorporates the remedies, procedures, and rights of Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act.
2. **42 USC §1983** — case law confirms that Title II rights are enforceable through §1983 as well, meaning individuals can sue state and local officials directly for disability discrimination.

Key case law on enforcement and scope:

- **Olmstead v. L.C., 527 U.S. 581 (1999):** The Supreme Court held that unjustified institutional isolation of persons with disabilities is discrimination under Title II. States must provide community-based services when appropriate.
- **Tennessee v. Lane, 541 U.S. 509 (2004):** Congress validly abrogated state sovereign immunity under the Fourteenth Amendment for Title II claims involving access to courts.
- **Barnes v. Gorman, 536 U.S. 181 (2002):** Punitive damages are not available under Title II (or Section 504), because the enforcement is modeled on Spending Clause legislation.

## Connection to the steward's case

Any state or local government entity that excludes or discriminates against persons with disabilities in their services, programs, or activities violates §12132. This includes courts, law enforcement agencies, social services, and every other function of state and local government.
