# Barnes v. Gorman, 536 U.S. 181 (2002)

## The Case

Jeffrey Gorman, a paraplegic, was arrested by Kansas City police. Officers transported him in a van not equipped for wheelchair users, causing him to fall from his wheelchair. His urine bag broke, causing him to sit in his own urine during transport. He sued the city under ADA Title II and §504 of the Rehabilitation Act and was awarded compensatory and punitive damages.

## What the Court Held

Justice Scalia, writing for the majority:

1. **Punitive damages are NOT available in private suits under Title II of the ADA or §504 of the Rehabilitation Act.** These statutes are Spending Clause legislation — the federal government conditions the receipt of federal funds on compliance with nondiscrimination requirements. Punitive damages are not available in Spending Clause cases because the recipient of funds could not have agreed to bear liability for punitive damages by accepting the funds.

2. **The remedies available under Title II and §504 are those available under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964** — which provides for compensatory damages and injunctive relief but not punitive damages.

## The Rule Barnes Stands For

**ADA Title II and Rehabilitation Act §504 do not permit punitive damages.** Remedies are limited to compensatory damages, injunctive relief, and attorney's fees.

## Citation

Barnes v. Gorman, 536 U.S. 181 (2002). Vote: 9-0 on the holding (various concurrences on reasoning). Majority: Scalia, J.
