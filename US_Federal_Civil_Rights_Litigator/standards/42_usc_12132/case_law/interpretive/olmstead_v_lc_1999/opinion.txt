OLMSTEAD v. L.C. ex rel. ZIMRING
527 U.S. 581 (1999)
United States Supreme Court
Decided: June 22, 1999

---

FACTS:

L.C. and E.W. were two women with mental disabilities (mental retardation and mental illness) voluntarily admitted to Georgia Regional Hospital, a state psychiatric hospital. Their treatment professionals determined they were ready for community-based treatment, but Georgia kept them institutionalized because appropriate community placements were not immediately available. Both women sought release to community settings under Title II of the ADA, 42 U.S.C. § 12132, which prohibits public entities from discriminating against disabled persons.

The District Court ordered Georgia to place the women in community settings within reasonable time. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed. The Supreme Court affirmed in part.

---

HOLDING:

Justice Ginsburg wrote for a plurality (joined in full by three justices; with a fifth justice concurring in the judgment on a narrower ground).

UNJUSTIFIED INSTITUTIONALIZATION IS DISCRIMINATION UNDER § 12132:

"Unjustified isolation ... is properly regarded as discrimination based on disability."

"[S]tate-run programs that unnecessarily institutionalize disabled persons, when community placement is appropriate and available within reasonable time, discriminate in violation of Title II."

THE OLMSTEAD INTEGRATION MANDATE:
Under ADA Title II (§ 12132) and the DOJ's integration regulation (28 C.F.R. § 35.130(d)):

1. A public entity must provide services to disabled persons in the most integrated setting appropriate to their needs.
2. Unjustified segregation of disabled persons in institutions, when community placement is (a) appropriate, (b) not opposed by the individual, and (c) achievable within reasonable time, violates § 12132.
3. "Reasonable time" is measured against a "comprehensive, effectively working plan" for placing individuals in community settings.

THE "EFFECTIVELY WORKING PLAN" DEFENSE:
States are not required to immediately place every institutionalized person in the community. A state that has a comprehensive, effectively working plan for placing persons in community settings, moving those on waiting lists at a reasonable pace, is not in violation — even if placement is not immediate.

BUT: A plan that amounts to "wait indefinitely" does not satisfy the defense.

---

TWO LIMITING PRINCIPLES (Ginsburg plurality):

1. A state is not required to provide community-based services when the individual does not want them.
2. A state is not required to provide community placements that require a "fundamental alteration" of its programs (picking up Southeastern/Davis).

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Olmstead is the operational mandate for § 12132 claims involving institutionalization or segregation of disabled persons. It applies to:

1. **Conservatorship / involuntary placement**: If a disabled adult is inappropriately institutionalized or placed in a restrictive setting against their wishes when community-based alternatives exist, Olmstead is the substantive hook.

2. **Mental health diversion programs**: Diversion programs that limit access to community-based treatment (§ 1001.36) may implicate the integration mandate.

3. **Olmstead planning requirements**: Government entities with ADA obligations must have functioning plans for community integration. A failure to have or follow such a plan supports a § 12132 claim.
