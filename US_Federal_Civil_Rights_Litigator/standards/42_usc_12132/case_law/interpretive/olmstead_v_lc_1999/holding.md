# Olmstead v. L.C., 527 U.S. 581 (1999)

## The Case

L.C. and E.W. were women with mental disabilities confined in a Georgia state psychiatric hospital. Their treatment professionals determined they were ready for community-based care, but Georgia kept them institutionalized. They sued under ADA Title II, arguing that unjustified institutionalization is discrimination.

## What the Court Held

Justice Ginsburg, writing for the plurality:

1. **Unjustified institutional isolation of persons with disabilities is a form of discrimination under Title II of the ADA.** When a state's treatment professionals determine that community placement is appropriate, the patient's needs can be met in a community setting, and the placement can be reasonably accommodated, continued institutionalization violates Title II.

2. **States must provide community-based services for persons with mental disabilities when: (a) the state's treatment professionals determine community placement is appropriate, (b) the affected person does not oppose transfer, and (c) the placement can be reasonably accommodated, taking into account the state's resources and the needs of others with disabilities.**

3. **The "fundamental alteration" defense is available** — states need not make changes that would fundamentally alter their service system. But routine assertions of cost are not enough; the state must show a concrete impact on its ability to serve other disabled individuals.

## The Rule Olmstead Stands For

**The ADA's integration mandate requires states to provide community-based services instead of institutional confinement when treatment professionals approve, the individual consents, and reasonable accommodations are possible.** Unjustified segregation of persons with disabilities is discrimination.

## Citation

Olmstead v. L.C. ex rel. Zimring, 527 U.S. 581 (1999). Vote: 6-3. Plurality: Ginsburg, J.
