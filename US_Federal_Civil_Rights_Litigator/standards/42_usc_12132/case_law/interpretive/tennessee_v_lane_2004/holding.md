# Tennessee v. Lane, 541 U.S. 509 (2004)

## The Case

George Lane was a paraplegic who had to crawl up two flights of stairs to reach a second-floor courtroom in a Tennessee courthouse that had no elevator. He was arrested for failure to appear when he refused to crawl again. Beverly Jones, a court reporter, was unable to access courthouses for her work. They sued Tennessee under ADA Title II.

Tennessee argued sovereign immunity under the Eleventh Amendment barred the suit.

## What the Court Held

Justice Stevens, writing for the majority:

1. **Congress validly abrogated state sovereign immunity for Title II claims involving access to the courts.** The right of access to courts is a fundamental constitutional right under the Due Process Clause. Congress's abrogation of immunity for Title II claims that implicate this right is a congruent and proportional response to a documented pattern of disability discrimination.

2. **The validity of Title II's abrogation is assessed as applied to particular constitutional rights, not as a wholesale facial challenge.** The Court did not decide whether Title II validly abrogates sovereign immunity in all contexts — only that it does so for claims involving access to courts.

## The Rule Lane Stands For

**ADA Title II validly abrogates state sovereign immunity for claims involving the fundamental right of access to courts.** States can be sued for money damages when they deny disabled persons access to judicial proceedings.

## Citation

Tennessee v. Lane, 541 U.S. 509 (2004). Vote: 5-4. Majority: Stevens, J.
