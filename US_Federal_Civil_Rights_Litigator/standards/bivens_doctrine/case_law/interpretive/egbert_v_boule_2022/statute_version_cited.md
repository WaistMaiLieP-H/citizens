# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Egbert v. Boule, 596 U.S. 482 (2022)
**Statute at issue:** No statute — question was whether to extend Bivens to new contexts (Fourth Amendment excessive force by Border Patrol; First Amendment retaliation)
**Doctrinal stage:** Near-total closure of Bivens expansion
**Evolution stage:** `../../bivens_doctrine/evolution/01_origin/` (Bivens remains the doctrinal origin; Egbert effectively terminates extension)

The Court declined to extend Bivens to either claim. Justice Thomas's majority opinion reformulated the test: a court must ask whether there is "any reason to think that Congress might be better equipped to create a damages remedy." If so, no Bivens action. This formulation is so broad that it effectively forecloses all new Bivens contexts. Combined with Ziglar (2017), which had already introduced the "new context" and "special factors" analysis, Egbert completed the doctrinal closure: the three contexts recognized before 1980 (Fourth, Fifth, Eighth Amendments) are the only surviving Bivens actions.
