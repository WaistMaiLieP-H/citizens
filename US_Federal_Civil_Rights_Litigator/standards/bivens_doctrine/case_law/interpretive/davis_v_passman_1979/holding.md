# Davis v. Passman, 442 U.S. 228 (1979)

## The Case

Shirley Davis was a deputy administrative assistant to U.S. Congressman Otto Passman. He fired her because of her sex — he stated explicitly that he preferred a male assistant. Davis sued Passman directly under the Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause, seeking damages for gender discrimination.

## What the Court Held

Justice Brennan, writing for the majority:

1. **A Bivens-type damages remedy is available for Fifth Amendment gender discrimination by a federal official.** The Due Process Clause of the Fifth Amendment protects against gender discrimination by the federal government (just as the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment protects against state gender discrimination).

2. **Davis had no alternative remedy** — Title VII did not cover congressional employees at the time. Without a Bivens remedy, she would have no means of vindicating her constitutional right.

## The Rule

**The second recognized Bivens context: the Fifth Amendment's guarantee of equal protection (as incorporated through due process) supports an implied damages action against a federal official who engages in gender discrimination.** One of only three surviving Bivens contexts after Ziglar v. Abbasi (2017) and Egbert v. Boule (2022).

## Citation

Davis v. Passman, 442 U.S. 228 (1979). Vote: 5-4. Majority: Brennan, J.
