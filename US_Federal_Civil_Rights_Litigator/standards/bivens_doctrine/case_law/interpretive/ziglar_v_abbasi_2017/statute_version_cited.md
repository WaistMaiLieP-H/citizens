# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Ziglar v. Abbasi, 582 U.S. 120 (2017)
**Statute at issue:** No statute — question was whether to extend Bivens to post-9/11 detention conditions (Fifth Amendment substantive due process)
**Doctrinal stage:** Beginning of near-total Bivens closure
**Evolution stage:** `../../bivens_doctrine/evolution/01_origin/` (Bivens remains the doctrinal origin; Abbasi begins the narrowing)

Justice Kennedy's majority opinion introduced the two-step framework that now governs all Bivens claims: (1) Is this a "new context" — does the case differ in any meaningful way from the three recognized Bivens contexts? (2) Are there "special factors counselling hesitation" — any reason to think Congress is better positioned to authorize a damages remedy? The Court found the post-9/11 detention context was new and presented special factors (national security, executive policy, congressional inaction). This framework, combined with Egbert v. Boule (2022), effectively closed Bivens to any further extension.
