# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14 (1980)
**Statute at issue:** No statute — implied constitutional cause of action under the Eighth Amendment (cruel and unusual punishment)
**Doctrinal stage:** Third recognized Bivens context
**Evolution stage:** `../../bivens_doctrine/evolution/01_origin/` (Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 1971)

Carlson extended Bivens to the Eighth Amendment. Marie Green, a federal prisoner, died from asthma after prison officials refused to treat her condition. The Court held a Bivens remedy was available even though the Federal Tort Claims Act (FTCA) provided an alternative remedy — because FTCA was not an "equally effective" substitute (no punitive damages, no deterrence of individual officers). This is the third and final surviving Bivens context. After Ziglar and Egbert, the Court has refused to extend Bivens to any new context.
