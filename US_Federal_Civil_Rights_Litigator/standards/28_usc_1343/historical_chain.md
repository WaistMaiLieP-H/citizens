# 28 USC §1343 — Historical Chain

## The need

The 1871 KKK Act created a federal cause of action (§1) and placed it in the federal courts. The jurisdictional grant and the cause of action were one provision. When the Revised Statutes separated the substance from the jurisdiction in 1874, the jurisdictional provisions scattered across several sections.

In 1948, Congress enacted Title 28 of the United States Code into positive law, consolidating the scattered jurisdictional provisions into a clean section: 28 USC §1343.

## The structure

§1343(a)(1)–(2): jurisdiction over §1985 conspiracies and §1986 failure-to-prevent claims
§1343(a)(3): jurisdiction over deprivation of constitutional or equal-rights statutory rights under color of state law — the primary §1983 jurisdictional hook
§1343(a)(4): jurisdiction over civil rights actions generally — broadest net

## Amendment history

- 1948: Title 28 enacted into positive law (62 Stat. 932)
- 1954: Technical amendment (68 Stat. 1241)
- 1957: Voting rights expansion (Civil Rights Act of 1957, 71 Stat. 637)
- 1979: DC extension (Pub. L. 96-170, same bill that amended §1983)

## Modern relevance

§1343 is the specific jurisdictional statute cited in every federal civil rights complaint alongside the general federal-question jurisdiction at 28 USC §1331. The steward's §1983 complaint cites both.
