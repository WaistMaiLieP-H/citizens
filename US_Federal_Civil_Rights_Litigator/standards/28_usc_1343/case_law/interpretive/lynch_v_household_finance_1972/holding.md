# Lynch v. Household Finance Corp., 405 U.S. 538 (1972)

## The Case

Lynch challenged a Connecticut garnishment statute that allowed a creditor to seize a debtor's wages without prior notice or hearing. She sued under §1983, arguing the garnishment violated due process. The question was whether 28 USC §1343(3) — which grants jurisdiction over actions to redress the deprivation of rights "under color of State law" — encompasses property rights or is limited to personal liberty rights.

## What the Court Held

Justice Stewart, writing for the majority:

1. **Section 1343(3) jurisdiction is not limited to "personal liberty" rights — it encompasses property rights as well.** The distinction between "personal" and "property" rights is artificial and unworkable. The Fourteenth Amendment protects both, and §1343's jurisdictional grant covers both.

2. **The right to due process before deprivation of property is a right "secured by the Constitution" within the meaning of §1343(3).** A state garnishment statute that allows seizure without hearing deprives the debtor of a constitutional right that §1343 gives the federal courts jurisdiction to redress.

3. **The artificial personal/property distinction had been used by lower courts to deny jurisdiction over property-related §1983 claims, forcing plaintiffs into state court.** This frustrated §1983's purpose — providing a federal forum for constitutional violations by state actors.

## The Rule

**28 USC §1343(3) grants federal jurisdiction over §1983 claims involving both personal liberty rights and property rights. The personal/property distinction is rejected.**

## Why This Case Matters

Lynch eliminated a jurisdictional barrier that had channeled property-related §1983 claims back to state court. After Lynch, federal courts have jurisdiction over any §1983 claim — whether the right violated is personal liberty, property, due process, equal protection, or any other constitutional guarantee. This completed the jurisdictional framework that began with the 1871 Act's original venue provisions.
