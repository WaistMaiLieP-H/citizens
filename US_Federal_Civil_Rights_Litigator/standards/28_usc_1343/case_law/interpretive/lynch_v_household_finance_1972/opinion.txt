LYNCH v. HOUSEHOLD FINANCE CORP.
405 U.S. 538 (1972)
United States Supreme Court
Decided: March 23, 1972

---

FACTS:

Sylvester Lynch, a Connecticut resident, challenged the constitutionality of Connecticut's wage garnishment procedures. Household Finance Corporation had obtained a prejudgment garnishment of Lynch's wages under state law without notice or a hearing. Lynch brought a federal civil rights action under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 seeking to enjoin enforcement of Connecticut's garnishment statute as a deprivation of property without due process, invoking federal jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1343(3).

The threshold question was whether § 1343(3) — which granted district courts jurisdiction over civil rights suits — reached claims protecting property rights as well as personal liberty rights. The District Court dismissed for lack of subject matter jurisdiction, holding that § 1343(3) reached only personal liberty claims, not property claims.

---

HOLDING:

The Supreme Court reversed unanimously. Justice Stewart wrote for the Court.

SECTION 1343(3) COVERS PROPERTY RIGHTS:

"There is no distinction between personal liberties and proprietary rights in terms of their constitutional protection. The Framers recognized the inseparability of personal and economic freedom."

"Section 1343(3) grants jurisdiction over claims that a statute deprives a person of any right, privilege or immunity secured by the Constitution and laws. Property rights are constitutional rights. The right not to have property taken without due process is as much a right secured by the Constitution as is the right to speak freely or to be free from unreasonable searches."

THE UNITY OF CONSTITUTIONAL RIGHTS:

The Court rejected the lower court's distinction between "personal" and "proprietary" rights:

"The dichotomy between personal liberties and property rights is a false one. Property does not have rights. People have rights. The right to enjoy property without unlawful deprivation, no less than the right to speak or the right to travel, is in truth a 'personal' right, whether the 'property' in question be a welfare check, a home, or a savings account."

JURISDICTION IS PROPER:

Where a plaintiff alleges that a state statute deprives them of property without constitutional due process, § 1343(3) grants subject matter jurisdiction in federal district court. A § 1983 plaintiff need not allege deprivation of personal liberty — deprivation of property rights secured by the Fourteenth Amendment suffices.

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Lynch is the anchor holding that federal jurisdiction under § 1343(3) reaches property deprivation claims under § 1983. For cases involving:
- Unauthorized sale or seizure of real property (Honeysuckle)
- Wage garnishment or asset freezes without due process
- Government-facilitated theft of financial accounts
- Benefits termination without adequate hearing (SSA/CalVCB)

Lynch confirms federal subject matter jurisdiction is proper when the claim is that a state actor deprived the plaintiff of property without constitutionally required process.
