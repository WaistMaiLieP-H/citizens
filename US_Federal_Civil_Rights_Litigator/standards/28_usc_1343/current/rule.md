# 28 U.S.C. § 1343 — Civil Rights and Elective Franchise (Federal Jurisdiction)

**Standard ID:** USC_28_1343_CIVIL_RIGHTS_JURISDICTION
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text (current)

**(a)** The district courts shall have original jurisdiction of any civil action authorized by law to be commenced by any person:

**(1)** To recover damages for injury to his person or property, or because of the deprivation of any right or privilege of a citizen of the United States, by any act done in furtherance of any conspiracy mentioned in section 1985 of Title 42;

**(2)** To recover damages from any person who fails to prevent or to aid in preventing any wrongs mentioned in section 1985 of Title 42 which he had knowledge were about to occur and power to prevent;

**(3)** To redress the deprivation, under color of any State law, statute, ordinance, regulation, custom or usage, of any right, privilege or immunity secured by the Constitution of the United States or by any Act of Congress providing for equal rights of citizens or of all persons within the jurisdiction of the United States;

**(4)** To recover damages or to secure equitable or other relief under any Act of Congress providing for the protection of civil rights, including the right to vote.

**(b)** For purposes of this section—

**(1)** the District of Columbia shall be considered to be a State; and

**(2)** any Act of Congress applicable exclusively to the District of Columbia shall be considered to be a statute of the District of Columbia.

*(28 U.S.C. § 1343; June 25, 1948, ch. 646; Sept. 3, 1954; Pub. L. 85-315, Sept. 9, 1957; Pub. L. 96-170, Dec. 29, 1979)*

---

## Function in the Civil Rights Framework

Section 1343 is a **jurisdictional statute** — it grants federal district courts original subject-matter jurisdiction over the substantive civil rights claims arising under:

| § 1343 Subsection | Underlying Substantive Claim |
|------------------|------------------------------|
| § 1343(a)(1) | § 1985 conspiracy claims (damages) |
| § 1343(a)(2) | § 1986 failure-to-prevent claims |
| § 1343(a)(3) | § 1983 color-of-law civil rights claims |
| § 1343(a)(4) | Any federal civil rights statute |

---

## Relationship to 28 U.S.C. § 1331 (Federal Question Jurisdiction)

Section 1343 was historically important because early case law restricted § 1331 (federal question jurisdiction) to cases involving "a substantial dollar amount in controversy." Civil rights claims often involved nominal or small damages — below the amount-in-controversy threshold — so plaintiffs could not always use § 1331. Section 1343 provided an independent jurisdictional basis without an amount-in-controversy requirement.

Since 1976, § 1331 has had no amount-in-controversy requirement. In modern practice, § 1343 and § 1331 are largely co-extensive for civil rights cases. Both statutes can provide the jurisdictional hook, and courts rarely distinguish between them.

---

## Practical Significance

- § 1343(a)(3) provides federal jurisdiction for every § 1983 claim as a matter of course.
- § 1343(a)(4) provides federal jurisdiction for ADA Title II claims (42 U.S.C. § 12132), Rehabilitation Act § 504 claims (29 U.S.C. § 794), and other federal civil rights statutes.
- The statute does not create substantive rights — it only provides the court's authority to hear them. The substantive right must arise from another statute.

---

## Plain English

Section 1343 is the courthouse door for federal civil rights cases. It tells the federal district court it has the power to hear the case — but the underlying right being enforced must come from § 1983, § 1985, the ADA, the Rehabilitation Act, or another civil rights statute. Without § 1343 (or § 1331), a federal civil rights claim filed in federal court lacks a jurisdictional basis even if the substantive right is valid.
