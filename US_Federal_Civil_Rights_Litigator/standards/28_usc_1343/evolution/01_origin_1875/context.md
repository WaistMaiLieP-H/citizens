# 28 USC §1343 — Origin Context

## What this statute does

28 USC §1343 is the jurisdictional statute for federal civil rights cases. It gives federal district courts original jurisdiction over civil rights actions — including actions under §1983, §1985, and §1986.

## Why it exists separately from §1983

The 1871 KKK Act §1 (now §1983) originally contained both the substantive cause of action AND the jurisdictional grant in a single provision. The 1874 Revised Statutes separated them: the cause of action stayed in R.S. §1979 (now §1983), and the jurisdictional grant was codified separately in R.S. §1977 and related provisions. When Title 28 was enacted into positive law in 1948 (62 Stat. 932), the civil rights jurisdictional provisions were consolidated into 28 USC §1343.

## Structure

- **§1343(a)(1):** Jurisdiction over §1985 conspiracy claims
- **§1343(a)(2):** Jurisdiction over §1986 misprision-of-conspiracy claims
- **§1343(a)(3):** Jurisdiction over deprivation of constitutional or equal-rights statutory rights under color of state law — this is the jurisdictional hook for §1983 claims
- **§1343(a)(4):** Jurisdiction over civil rights actions generally — broadest grant
- **§1343(b):** DC treated as a state (added 1979, Pub. L. 96-170, same act that added DC to §1983)

## Practical significance

In modern practice, §1343 is largely redundant with federal-question jurisdiction under 28 USC §1331 (which covers "all civil actions arising under the Constitution, laws, or treaties of the United States"). §1331 was amended in 1980 to remove the amount-in-controversy requirement, making it broad enough to encompass virtually all §1983 claims. But §1343 remains independently operative, and litigants routinely cite both §1331 and §1343 as jurisdictional bases for civil rights complaints.

## Source

R.S. §§ related to jurisdiction under civil rights titles; consolidated as 28 USC §1343 in the 1948 Title 28 enactment (62 Stat. 932). Amended 1954 (technical), 1957 (voting rights), 1979 (DC extension).
