# 29 U.S.C. § 794 — Rehabilitation Act § 504 (Nondiscrimination Under Federal Grants)

**Standard ID:** USC_29_794_REHAB_ACT_504
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text — Core Prohibition (§ 794(a), current)

No otherwise qualified individual with a disability in the United States, as defined in section 705(20) of this title, shall, **solely by reason of her or his disability, be excluded from the participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under** any program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance or under any program or activity conducted by any Executive agency or by the United States Postal Service.

*(29 U.S.C. § 794; Pub. L. 93-112, title V, § 504, Sept. 26, 1973; as amended through Pub. L. 113-128 (2014) and Pub. L. 114-95 (2015))*

---

## Elements of a § 504 Claim

To state a claim under § 794, the plaintiff must show:

1. **The plaintiff has a disability** as defined under the Rehabilitation Act (29 U.S.C. § 705(20));

2. **The plaintiff is "otherwise qualified"** — able to meet the essential eligibility requirements of the program or activity with or without reasonable accommodation;

3. **The plaintiff was excluded from participation, denied benefits, or subjected to discrimination solely by reason of disability**; and

4. **The defendant receives federal financial assistance** (for programs receiving federal funding) OR is an Executive agency or the U.S. Postal Service.

---

## "Solely by Reason of Disability" — The Causation Standard

The "solely by reason of" language in § 504 is a higher causation standard than the ADA's "because of" or "on the basis of" language in some subsections. However, courts have generally aligned § 504 and ADA Title II causation analysis given that § 794(d) incorporates ADA employment standards for employment claims.

---

## Covered Entities — Federal Financial Assistance

Section 794(b) broadly defines "program or activity" to include all operations of:
- State and local government departments, agencies, and instrumentalities;
- Colleges, universities, and educational institutions;
- Private organizations receiving federal assistance (if principally providing education, health care, housing, or social services, or if receiving direct program assistance);
- Any entity established by two or more covered entities.

If **any part** of an entity receives federal financial assistance, the nondiscrimination requirement may extend to the **entire entity** (*Grove City College v. Bell*, 465 U.S. 555 (1984) was legislatively reversed by the Civil Rights Restoration Act of 1987, which restored the "whole entity" coverage standard codified in § 794(b)).

---

## Standards Applied — Employment Claims

For employment discrimination claims, § 794(d) adopts the standards of ADA Title I (42 U.S.C. §§ 12111 et seq.) and related ADA provisions. This harmonizes Rehabilitation Act and ADA employment analysis.

---

## Reasonable Accommodation

The requirement that the plaintiff be "otherwise qualified" means the question is always: could the plaintiff meet the program's essential requirements with reasonable accommodation? An entity covered by § 504 must make reasonable modifications unless doing so would cause an undue hardship or fundamentally alter the nature of the program.

---

## Relationship to ADA Title II (42 U.S.C. § 12132)

| Feature | § 504 Rehabilitation Act | ADA Title II |
|---------|--------------------------|--------------|
| Enacted | 1973 | 1990 |
| Coverage trigger | Federal financial assistance OR federal agency | Public entity (state/local government) |
| Standard | "Solely by reason of" disability | "By reason of" disability |
| Private right of action | Yes | Yes |
| Attorney fees | Yes (§ 505) | Yes (§ 1988) |

In practice, disability discrimination claims against public entities are typically brought under both § 504 and ADA Title II simultaneously. § 504 may reach federally-funded private entities that ADA Title II does not cover.

---

## Plain English

Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act prohibits any federally-funded program — including state agencies, hospitals, schools, and transit systems receiving federal money — from discriminating against disabled individuals. If the government gives an entity federal dollars, that entity must accommodate disabled people and cannot exclude them solely because of their disability. Section 504 predates the ADA by 17 years and remains important because it covers federally-funded private organizations that ADA Title II (which covers government entities) does not reach.
