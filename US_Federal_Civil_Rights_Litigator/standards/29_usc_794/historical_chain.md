# 29 USC §794 — Historical Chain

## The foundation

Section 504 was the foundation. It proved the principle: disability discrimination by federally funded entities is illegal and remediable. Before Section 504, there was no federal civil rights protection for people with disabilities. After Section 504, the question was no longer whether disability discrimination was wrong — the question was how far the protection would extend.

## What Section 504 built

The ADA (1990) extended that principle to ALL public entities and private employers, regardless of whether they received federal funding. Section 504 covered federally funded programs. The ADA covered everything else.

## How they work together now

Section 504 and ADA Title II now operate in parallel:
- **Section 504** covers programs and activities receiving federal financial assistance.
- **ADA Title II** (42 USC §12132) covers all public entities — state and local governments and their instrumentalities — regardless of federal funding.

A state agency that receives federal funds is covered by both. A local government program funded entirely by state money is covered by ADA Title II but not Section 504. In practice, most public entities are covered by both statutes, and courts apply them together.

## Enforcement

Both Section 504 and ADA Title II are enforceable through 42 USC §1983 when the violation is committed by a state or local actor under color of law. Section 504 also supports a direct private right of action (implied from the statute and confirmed by case law).

## Connection to the steward's case

Any federally funded state or local program that discriminates against persons with disabilities violates Section 504. This includes courts, law enforcement agencies, social services, medical facilities, and any other program that accepts federal money. When a state actor uses a federally funded program to discriminate against a person with a disability, that is both a Section 504 violation and a potential §1983 claim.
