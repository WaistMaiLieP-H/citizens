# Section 504 — 2014 Amendment (P.L. 113-128, WIOA §412)

## What Changed

The Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act of 2014 (WIOA) amended Section 504 as part of a comprehensive modernization of workforce and rehabilitation programs. The key changes:

1. **Updated terminology.** "Handicapped individual" was replaced throughout the Rehabilitation Act with "individual with a disability," completing a language modernization that had been underway since the ADA (1990). Section 504 itself had already been amended to use "individual with a disability" in earlier amendments, but WIOA cleaned up remaining references across the Act.

2. **Broader definition of disability.** WIOA aligned the Rehabilitation Act's definition of disability with the ADA Amendments Act of 2008 (ADAAA), which had broadened the ADA's definition of disability in response to *Toyota Motor Manufacturing v. Williams* (2002) and *Sutton v. United Air Lines* (1999). The ADAAA instructed that "disability" should be construed broadly, that mitigating measures (eyeglasses, medication, prosthetics) should not be considered in determining disability, and that "substantially limits" does not require a significant restriction on a major life activity.

3. **Strengthened competitive integrated employment provisions** in the broader Rehabilitation Act (not Section 504 specifically, but contextually relevant).

## Why It Matters

The 2014 amendment ensured that Section 504's definition of "individual with a disability" matches the broadened ADA definition. This closed a gap where someone might qualify as disabled under the ADA (post-ADAAA) but not under Section 504 (if still using the narrower pre-ADAAA definitions).

## Impact

More people qualify as "individuals with disabilities" under Section 504 after WIOA aligned the definitions. The mitigating-measures and substantially-limits reforms from ADAAA now apply to Section 504 claims as well.
