# Section 504 — 1988 Amendment (P.L. 100-259, Civil Rights Restoration Act)

## What Changed

The Civil Rights Restoration Act of 1987 (enacted March 22, 1988) redefined "program or activity" in Section 504 to mean the entire institution — not just the specific program receiving federal funds. If a university's physics department receives an NSF grant, the entire university is covered by Section 504, not just the physics department.

## Why It Happened

*Grove City College v. Bell*, 465 U.S. 555 (1984), held that Title IX's prohibition applied only to the "program or activity" directly receiving federal funds. The same interpretation was applied to Section 504, Title VI, and the Age Discrimination Act. This gutted disability discrimination enforcement: a hospital receiving Medicare funds for its cardiac unit could discriminate against disabled people in its maternity ward, because the maternity ward was a different "program."

Congress spent four years fighting over the fix. President Reagan vetoed it. Both chambers overrode the veto — the first civil rights veto override since the override of Andrew Johnson's veto of the Civil Rights Act of 1866.

## The Text

The Restoration Act defined "program or activity" to encompass:
- An entire corporation, partnership, or other private organization if it receives federal assistance
- An entire state or local government entity
- An entire college, university, or other post-secondary institution

## Impact

The program-specific loophole was closed. Any institution that receives any federal funds is covered by Section 504 across all its operations. This restored Section 504 to the scope Congress originally intended in 1973 and the 1977 regulations had assumed.
