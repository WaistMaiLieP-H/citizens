# Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 — Origin

## What it is

Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (Pub. L. 93-112, 87 Stat. 394). The first federal disability civil rights law. Signed September 26, 1973.

## The wound it was written to heal

Before Section 504, there was no federal prohibition against disability discrimination in federally funded programs. People with disabilities were excluded from schools, hospitals, transit, and employment funded by federal money — with no legal remedy. If your local school received federal funds and refused to admit your child because of a disability, there was no federal law that said they could not do that.

## Where the idea came from

Section 504 was modeled on Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, which prohibited race discrimination in federally funded programs. The same framework — if you take federal money, you cannot discriminate — was extended to disability. One principle, applied to a new class of people the law had ignored.

## The sentence that changed everything

> "No otherwise qualified individual with a disability... shall, solely by reason of her or his disability, be excluded from the participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance."

One sentence. It meant that every school, hospital, transit authority, social service agency, and government program that accepted a single dollar of federal funding was now legally prohibited from discriminating against people with disabilities.

## How it became real

The law passed in 1973, but the implementing regulations were not signed until 1977. HEW Secretary Joseph Califano delayed signing the regulations. Disability rights activists responded with the 504 Sit-In — occupying the HEW regional office in San Francisco beginning April 5, 1977. The occupation lasted until April 28, 1977 — 25 days. It was the longest occupation of a federal building in United States history.

Califano signed the regulations on April 28, 1977. The sit-in worked. The regulations made Section 504 enforceable.

## Why this matters

Section 504 proved a principle: disability discrimination by entities that accept federal money is illegal, and the federal government will enforce that prohibition. Every federal disability rights law that followed — the ADA, the IDEA, the Fair Housing Amendments Act — built on the foundation that Section 504 laid down.
