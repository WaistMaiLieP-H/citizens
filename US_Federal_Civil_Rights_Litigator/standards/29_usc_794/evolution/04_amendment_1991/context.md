# Section 504 — 1991 Amendment (P.L. 102-166, Civil Rights Act of 1991)

## What Changed

The Civil Rights Act of 1991 aligned Section 504's employment discrimination standards with ADA Title I. Before 1991, Section 504 had its own body of employment discrimination case law — including the foundational *Southeastern Community College v. Davis* (1979) standard. The 1991 amendment provided that the standards used to determine employment discrimination under ADA Title I would also apply to Section 504 employment claims.

## Why It Happened

The ADA was enacted in 1990. Section 504 (1973) and ADA Title I (1990) now covered overlapping territory in employment discrimination. An employer receiving federal funds was subject to both statutes. The 1991 Act ensured consistency: the same standards, defenses, and remedies would apply whether the claim was brought under Section 504 or ADA Title I.

## Key Effects

1. **Undue hardship defense** from ADA Title I applies to Section 504 employment claims
2. **Reasonable accommodation** requirements from ADA regulations apply to Section 504
3. **Essential functions** analysis from ADA Title I applies
4. Compensatory and punitive damages available under Section 504 employment claims (though subject to caps)

## What Didn't Change

Section 504's coverage of programs and activities beyond employment — education, healthcare, government services, transportation — was not affected by the 1991 amendment. The alignment was specifically for employment discrimination standards.

## The Practical Impact

Before 1991, a disability discrimination employment case required choosing between two statutes with potentially different standards. After 1991, the standards converged. This simplified litigation but also imported ADA Title I's limitations into Section 504's employment context.
