# Section 504 — 1978 Amendment (P.L. 95-602, §120)

## What Changed

The Rehabilitation, Comprehensive Services, and Developmental Disabilities Amendments of 1978 made two critical changes to Section 504:

1. **Explicit regulatory authority.** Federal agencies were given clear statutory authority to issue regulations implementing Section 504. Before 1978, the 504 regulations (signed under protest after the sit-in) rested on implied authority. Congress made the authority explicit and directed the Attorney General to coordinate enforcement across all federal agencies.

2. **Remedies defined.** The amendment specified that the "remedies, procedures, and rights" of Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 would apply to Section 504 violations. This was critical because Title VI had an established enforcement apparatus — administrative complaints, fund cutoffs, and private right of action. By incorporating Title VI's machinery, Congress gave Section 504 real teeth.

## Why It Happened

The 1977 sit-in had forced the regulations, but the legal foundation was shaky. The 1973 statute was a single sentence with no enforcement mechanism. The 1978 amendment gave the regulatory structure legal certainty. It also reflected Congress's recognition that Section 504 needed more than a prohibition — it needed an enforcement infrastructure.

## The Promise Strengthened

If you take federal money and discriminate against people with disabilities, there is now an explicit statutory basis for the regulations that will be used to enforce the prohibition against you. The enforcement is not implied — it is commanded.
