# Section 504 — Current State (last substantive amendment 2014)

## Pointer

The current text of 29 USC §794 is at `../../current/`. Last substantive amendment was WIOA (P.L. 113-128, 2014). A minor conforming amendment in 2015 (P.L. 114-95, ESSA) updated cross-references but did not alter the substance.

## The Full Evolution Arc

| Stage | Year | Event | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 1973 | Rehabilitation Act §504 | One-sentence prohibition — no enforcement mechanism |
| 02 | 1978 | P.L. 95-602 | Regulatory authority + Title VI remedies |
| 03 | 1988 | Civil Rights Restoration Act | Institution-wide coverage restored (overturned Grove City) |
| 04 | 1991 | Civil Rights Act of 1991 | Employment standards aligned with ADA Title I |
| 05 | 2014 | WIOA | Disability definition aligned with ADAAA broad standard |
| 06 | Current | Unchanged since 2014 | Full text reflects all amendments |

## What the Evolution Shows

Section 504 started as the smallest possible statement — one sentence, no enforcement, no regulations. Every amendment added enforcement infrastructure:
- 1978: the legal authority to enforce
- 1988: the scope to cover whole institutions
- 1991: consistency with the ADA employment framework
- 2014: the broadest definition of who qualifies for protection

The story of Section 504 is an expansion story. Unlike §1983 (where the Court has narrowed enforcement through qualified immunity and Monell limitations), Section 504 has gotten stronger with each congressional action. The sit-in of 1977 set the tone: this law will be enforced, and Congress will keep strengthening the enforcement tools.

## Relationship to ADA Title II (42 USC §12132)

Section 504 and ADA Title II overlap significantly for public entities. Key differences:
- **§504** requires federal financial assistance; **ADA Title II** covers all public entities regardless of funding
- **§504** has no employee-count threshold; **ADA Title I** requires 15+ employees (but §504 employment claims are not subject to this limit)
- **§504** reaches federal contractors via Section 503 companion provision
- In practice, plaintiffs plead both whenever a public entity receives federal funds
