# Southeastern Community College v. Davis, 442 U.S. 397 (1979)

## The Case

Frances Davis, who had a serious hearing disability, applied to Southeastern Community College's nursing program. The college rejected her, concluding that her hearing disability made it unsafe for her to participate in clinical training. Davis sued under §504 of the Rehabilitation Act.

## What the Court Held

Justice Powell, writing for a unanimous Court:

1. **Section 504 does not require an institution to make "fundamental alterations" in its programs to accommodate a disabled person.** "Otherwise qualified" means qualified despite the disability — the individual must be able to meet the program's requirements, with reasonable accommodations, without requiring the program to change its essential nature.

2. **Davis was not "otherwise qualified" because she could not safely perform essential clinical functions.** The college's determination that her hearing disability created safety risks in clinical settings was legitimate, and §504 did not require the college to redesign its clinical program.

3. **Reasonable accommodation does NOT mean lowering standards or eliminating essential requirements.** It means modifications that do not impose undue hardship and do not fundamentally alter the program.

## The Rule

**Section 504 requires reasonable accommodations but not fundamental alterations.** An individual must be "otherwise qualified" — able to meet essential requirements with reasonable modifications — to be protected.
