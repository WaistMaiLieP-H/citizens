# Alexander v. Choate, 469 U.S. 287 (1985)

## The Case

Tennessee proposed reducing the number of annual inpatient hospital days covered by Medicaid from 20 to 14. Disabled Medicaid recipients sued under §504, arguing the reduction disproportionately affected people with disabilities (who statistically require more hospital days) and therefore constituted disability discrimination.

## What the Court Held

Justice Marshall, writing for a unanimous Court:

1. **Section 504 can reach policies with discriminatory effects on people with disabilities, not just intentional discrimination.** The statute does not require proof of discriminatory intent in every case. Facially neutral policies that disproportionately burden disabled persons may violate §504.

2. **However, the challenged Medicaid reduction did not violate §504.** The 14-day limit applied equally to all Medicaid recipients. Disabled persons were not denied "meaningful access" to the Medicaid program — they received the same hospital benefit as everyone else. The fact that they might need more hospital days as a statistical matter did not make the neutral reduction discriminatory.

3. **The touchstone is "meaningful access."** Section 504 requires that disabled persons receive meaningful access to the benefit that the government program provides — not that the program be redesigned to meet their greater needs.

## The Rule

**Section 504 reaches disparate-impact discrimination but requires denial of "meaningful access" to the program.** A neutral policy that applies equally does not violate §504 merely because disabled persons are statistically more affected by it.

## Citation

Alexander v. Choate, 469 U.S. 287 (1985). Vote: 9-0. Majority: Marshall, J.
