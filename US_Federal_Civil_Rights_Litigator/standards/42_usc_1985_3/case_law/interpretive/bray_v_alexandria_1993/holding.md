# Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic, 506 U.S. 263 (1993)

## The Case

Operation Rescue organized blockades of abortion clinics in the Washington, D.C., metropolitan area. The clinics and their patients sued under §1985(3), alleging a conspiracy to deprive women seeking abortions of their right to equal protection — specifically, that the blockades targeted women as a class.

## What the Court Held

Justice Scalia, writing for the majority:

1. **Opposition to abortion does not constitute "class-based, invidiously discriminatory animus" against women.** The protesters' animus was directed at the practice of abortion, not at women as a class. To hold otherwise would mean that any group defined by its engagement in a contested activity (drug users, gun owners, gamblers) would constitute a protected "class" under §1985(3).

2. **The "class-based animus" requirement from Griffin means animus directed at the class qua class — at people because of who they are, not because of what they do.** Race is the paradigm: the Klan targeted Black citizens because they were Black, not because of any activity they engaged in.

3. **Section 1985(3)'s "deprivation of equal protection" language requires an intent to deprive persons of a right guaranteed against private impairment.** Not every constitutional right qualifies — only those protected against private interference (e.g., the right to travel, the Thirteenth Amendment right to be free from badges and incidents of slavery). The right to abortion (as it existed pre-Dobbs under Roe v. Wade) was a right against government interference, not against private interference.

## The Rule Bray Stands For

Section 1985(3) does not reach conspiracies motivated by opposition to an activity — only those motivated by discriminatory animus against a class of persons defined by an immutable or defining characteristic. The "class" must be analogous to race — the paradigm the statute was enacted to address.

## Why This Case Matters

Bray narrowed §1985(3) significantly by limiting both what counts as a "class" and what counts as a right that §1985(3) protects. The practical effect is that §1985(3) primarily reaches race-based conspiracies and a narrow set of other classifications (national origin, potentially religion). Gender-based conspiracies remain viable after Bray but require showing animus against women as women, not against a contested activity that predominantly affects women.
