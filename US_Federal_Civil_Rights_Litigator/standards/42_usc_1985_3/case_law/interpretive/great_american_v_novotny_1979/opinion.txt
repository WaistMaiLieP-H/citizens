GREAT AMERICAN FEDERAL SAVINGS & LOAN ASSOC. v. NOVOTNY
442 U.S. 366 (1979)
United States Supreme Court
Decided: June 11, 1979

---

FACTS:

Thomas Novotny, a male employee at Great American Federal Savings & Loan, was fired after he supported female coworkers in their sex discrimination complaints under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. He sued under 42 U.S.C. § 1985(3), arguing a conspiracy to deprive him of equal protection by retaliating against him for supporting Title VII claims.

The question: can § 1985(3) be used as an alternative remedial vehicle for conduct that constitutes a Title VII violation? Can a plaintiff bypass Title VII's administrative exhaustion requirements and go straight to federal court via § 1985(3)?

---

HOLDING:

Justice Stewart wrote for the Court (7-2).

§ 1985(3) CANNOT BE USED TO ENFORCE RIGHTS CREATED SOLELY BY TITLE VII:
"Section 1985(3) may not be invoked to redress violations of Title VII."

The reasoning: Title VII created a comprehensive remedial scheme with specific procedures (EEOC charge, administrative exhaustion, 90-day right-to-sue letter). Congress intended Title VII to be the exclusive mechanism for enforcing the rights it created. If § 1985(3) could be invoked directly to enforce Title VII rights, plaintiffs could circumvent Congress's carefully constructed administrative scheme.

"[A] deprivation of a right created by Title VII cannot be the basis for a cause of action under § 1985(3)."

THE DISTINCTION — CONSTITUTIONAL RIGHTS vs. STATUTORY RIGHTS UNDER § 1985(3):
Section 1985(3) reaches conspiracies to deprive persons of "equal protection of the laws" or "equal privileges and immunities under the laws." After Novotny:

- Conspiracy to deny constitutional rights (race discrimination violating equal protection, interference with federal travel rights) → § 1985(3) available
- Conspiracy to deny rights existing only by virtue of a federal statute that has its own remedial scheme → § 1985(3) NOT available (must use the statute's own remedy)

The right must be "secured by" the Constitution or laws in a way that § 1985(3) was designed to enforce — meaning constitutional rights or rights that the 1871 Congress intended the statute to protect.

---

NOVOTNY'S SCOPE:

Novotny applies when:
1. The right at issue was created by a statute enacted after 1871
2. That statute provides its own comprehensive remedial scheme
3. Allowing § 1985(3) to bypass the statutory scheme would undermine Congress's intent

Novotny does NOT prevent § 1985(3) claims where:
1. The right violated is constitutional (not merely statutory)
2. The conspiracy involves race/class-based animus under Griffin
3. The plaintiff has exhausted statutory remedies and separately alleges constitutional conspiracy

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Novotny is a limiting case requiring that the rights pled under § 1985(3) be traceable to the Constitution (or pre-1871 civil rights statutes), not solely to later-enacted federal statutes.

For pleading purposes:
1. **Frame § 1985(3) claims around constitutional rights**: right to equal protection, right to parent-child family association (constitutional), right to access federal courts — not around Title VII, ADA, or other statutory rights that have their own exclusive administrative schemes.
2. **Parallel claims**: The fact that the conduct also violates Title VII (or another statute) does not make § 1985(3) unavailable — it just means § 1985(3) must be independently premised on constitutional rights, not solely on the statutory violation.
3. **The § 1985(2) route**: If the conspiracy involved obstruction of federal court proceedings, § 1985(2) (Kush/Haddle) remains available without the Novotny problem because § 1985(2) protects rights in federal proceedings — a category untouched by Novotny.
