# Great American Federal Savings & Loan Assn. v. Novotny, 442 U.S. 366 (1979)

## The Case

Novotny, a member of a savings and loan's board of directors, alleged that other board members conspired to retaliate against him for advocating equal treatment of women employees. He sued under §1985(3), claiming the retaliation was a conspiracy to deprive him of equal protection.

The question was whether §1985(3) could be used to enforce rights created by Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

## What the Court Held

Justice Stewart, writing for the majority:

1. **Section 1985(3) does not create substantive rights — it only provides a remedy for the deprivation of rights that exist independently.** The plaintiff must identify a right protected by some other source of law (the Constitution, a federal statute, the right to travel) that the conspiracy aims to deprive.

2. **Section 1985(3) cannot be used as a vehicle to enforce Title VII rights.** Title VII has its own comprehensive enforcement scheme (administrative exhaustion through the EEOC, specific procedures, specific remedies). Allowing §1985(3) to bypass that scheme would circumvent Congress's carefully designed enforcement framework.

## The Rule

**Section 1985(3) is a remedial statute, not a rights-creating statute.** The plaintiff must identify an independently existing right. And §1985(3) cannot be used to enforce rights under statutes that have their own exclusive enforcement schemes.

## Citation
