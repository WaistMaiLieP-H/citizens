# United Brotherhood of Carpenters v. Scott, 463 U.S. 825 (1983)

## The Case

Non-union workers at a construction site in Texas alleged that union members conspired to intimidate and assault them to drive them off the site. They sued under §1985(3), claiming a conspiracy to deprive them of equal protection based on their non-union status.

## What the Court Held

Justice White, writing for the majority:

1. **Section 1985(3) does not reach conspiracies motivated by economic or commercial animus.** The "class-based, invidiously discriminatory animus" required by Griffin v. Breckenridge means racial or similar animus — not economic rivalry, labor disputes, or commercial competition.

2. **Non-union workers are not a "class" entitled to §1985(3) protection.** The statute was designed to combat racial discrimination and analogous forms of invidious discrimination. Labor union disputes, however violent, are not the kind of class-based animus the statute targets.

## The Rule

**Economic, commercial, and labor-related animus is not "class-based, invidiously discriminatory animus" under §1985(3).** The statute reaches racial animus and analogous forms of identity-based hatred, not economic disputes.

## Citation
