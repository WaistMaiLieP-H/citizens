# 42 U.S.C. § 1985(3) — Conspiracy to Deprive Persons of Equal Protection

**Standard ID:** USC_42_1985_3_CONSPIRACY_EQUAL_PROTECTION
**Layer:** 1 — Rule
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Filed:** 2026-04-20

---

## Statutory Text — § 1985(3) (current)

If two or more persons in any State or Territory conspire or go in disguise on the highway or on the premises of another, for the purpose of depriving, either directly or indirectly, any person or class of persons of the equal protection of the laws, or of equal privileges and immunities under the laws; or for the purpose of preventing or hindering the constituted authorities of any State or Territory from giving or securing to all persons within such State or Territory the equal protection of the laws; or if two or more persons conspire to prevent by force, intimidation, or threat, any citizen who is lawfully entitled to vote, from giving his support or advocacy in a legal manner, toward or in favor of the election of any lawfully qualified person as an elector for President or Vice President, or as a Member of Congress of the United States; or to injure any citizen in person or property on account of such support or advocacy; in any case of conspiracy set forth in this section, if one or more persons engaged therein do, or cause to be done, any act in furtherance of the object of such conspiracy, whereby another is injured in his person or property, or deprived of having and exercising any right or privilege of a citizen of the United States, the party so injured or deprived may have an action for the recovery of damages occasioned by such injury or deprivation, against any one or more of the conspirators.

*(42 U.S.C. § 1985(3); R.S. § 1980)*

---

## Elements of a § 1985(3) Claim

*Griffin v. Breckenridge*, 403 U.S. 88 (1971), establishes the four required elements:

1. **A conspiracy** — two or more persons acting together with a common purpose;

2. **Purpose of depriving of equal protection or equal privileges and immunities** — the conspiracy must be motivated by a racial or other class-based, invidiously discriminatory animus;

3. **An overt act in furtherance of the conspiracy** — at least one conspirator must take an act advancing the conspiracy's purpose;

4. **Injury to person or property OR deprivation of a right or privilege** — the plaintiff must have suffered actual harm.

---

## Class-Based Animus Requirement — The Critical Limitation

The Supreme Court in *Griffin* held that § 1985(3) requires proof of "some racial, or perhaps otherwise class-based, invidiously discriminatory animus." This is not a statute of general conspiracy law — it reaches only conspiracies motivated by discriminatory hostility toward a protected class.

**Recognized classes under § 1985(3):**
- Race (*Griffin v. Breckenridge*)
- National origin
- Sex (recognized in most circuits)
- Disability (recognized in some circuits)
- Political affiliation (split in circuits; recognized in some)

**Political animus vs. class-based animus:** Conspiracies motivated by hostility toward a political party are generally not actionable under § 1985(3). The animus must be toward the person as a member of a protected class — not merely because of their political views.

---

## Private Conspiracies — No State Action Required

Unlike § 1983 (which requires "color of law"), § 1985(3) reaches **purely private conspiracies** when the conspiracy aims at depriving persons of equal protection based on class-based animus. *Griffin*, 403 U.S. at 96. This is a significant expansion beyond § 1983's reach.

However, for privately-conducted conspiracies to be actionable, the target must be a right that Congress has the power to protect against private interference — typically rights that are constitutional in character or rooted in federal statutes protecting equal rights.

## § 1985 Subsections Overview

| Subsection | Prohibits |
|-----------|-----------|
| § 1985(1) | Conspiring to prevent federal officers from performing duties |
| § 1985(2) | Conspiring to obstruct justice in federal courts or intimidate witnesses/jurors |
| § 1985(3) | Conspiring to deprive any person or class of equal protection (primary civil rights subsection) |

---

## Interaction with § 1986

Section 1986 creates liability for persons who know a § 1985 conspiracy is about to be committed, have the power to prevent it, and fail to act. A § 1986 claim can only be brought if a § 1985 violation occurred.

---

## Plain English

Section 1985(3) allows a private civil lawsuit against two or more people who conspire — outside of any government role — to deprive a person of equal protection based on race, national origin, or another protected class. Unlike § 1983, you do not need a government actor. But you must show the conspiracy was motivated by class-based hatred, not merely political disagreement or personal animosity. The classic case is a private group that conspires to use violence or intimidation to deprive a racial minority of civil rights.
