# §1985 Evolution: 1871 Act §2 → Revised Statutes §1980 (1874)

## What Changed

The 1874 Revised Statutes separated the civil action (damages) from the criminal penalties.

### Criminal penalties removed
- **1871:** The original Section 2 made conspiracy a "high crime" punishable by $500–$5,000 fine and/or 6 months to 6 years imprisonment.
- **1874:** The criminal penalties were codified separately (R.S. §§5508, 5509). R.S. §1980 preserved only the civil cause of action.

### "Within any State or Territory" → "in any State or Territory"
Minor phrasing change. No substantive effect.

### Venue provisions removed
- **1871:** Specified prosecution "in the proper district or circuit court of the United States" with cross-reference to the 1866 Civil Rights Act.
- **1874:** Venue provisions separated to jurisdictional statutes (same pattern as §1983's recodification).

### Conspiracy categories preserved unchanged
The substantive conspiracy definitions — including the critical "go in disguise upon the public highway or upon the premises of another" language and the equal protection/privileges/immunities language — were preserved verbatim.

## The Key Fact

**42 USC §1985 has never been textually amended.** The text that exists today is, in substance, the 1874 recodification of the 1871 Act's Section 2 civil action provision. Every change in §1985's scope and meaning has come from judicial interpretation, not legislative amendment.
