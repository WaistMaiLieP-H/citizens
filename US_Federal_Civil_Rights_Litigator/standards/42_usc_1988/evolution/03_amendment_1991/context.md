# 42 USC §1988 — 1991 Amendment (P.L. 102-166, §113)

## What Changed

The Civil Rights Act of 1991 added subsection (c) to §1988. For the first time, expert witness fees could be included as part of attorney's fees in certain civil rights actions. Specifically, §1988(c) authorized courts to include expert fees as part of the attorney's fee in actions under 42 USC §1981 (equal rights under the law) and 42 USC §1981a (damages in intentional employment discrimination cases).

## Why It Happened

The Civil Rights Act of 1991 was Congress's response to a series of Supreme Court decisions that narrowed civil rights protections in the employment context. The most significant was *Wards Cove Packing Co. v. Atonio* (1989), which shifted the burden of proof in disparate impact cases back to plaintiffs, and *Patterson v. McLean Credit Union* (1989), which limited §1981 to contract formation (not post-formation conduct).

Expert witnesses are critical in employment discrimination cases — statisticians to prove disparate impact, industrial psychologists to prove discriminatory culture, economists to calculate lost wages and benefits. Before 1991, expert fees were not recoverable under §1988. In *West Virginia University Hospitals v. Casey* (1991), the Supreme Court held that §1988(b) did not authorize recovery of expert fees — only attorney's fees.

Congress responded within months by adding §1988(c) as part of the broader 1991 Act.

## The Text Added

> In awarding an attorney's fee under subsection (b) in any action or proceeding to enforce a provision of sections 1981 or 1981a of this title, the court, in its discretion, may include expert fees as part of the attorney's fee.

## Impact

Expert fees became recoverable in §1981 and §1981a actions — but notably NOT in all §1983 actions. This created an asymmetry: employment discrimination plaintiffs proceeding under §1981 could recover expert fees, but §1983 plaintiffs suing for police misconduct or due process violations could not. This gap persists today.

## The Promise Extended — With a Hole

Congress said: experts are necessary to prove civil rights claims, so experts should be funded by fee-shifting. But it only fixed the gap for employment discrimination, not for all civil rights litigation. The selective fix reflected the political reality of 1991 — the Act was primarily about employment, not §1983.
