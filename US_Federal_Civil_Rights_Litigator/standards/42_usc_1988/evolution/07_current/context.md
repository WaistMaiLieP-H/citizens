# 42 USC §1988 — Current State (unchanged since 2000)

## Pointer

The current text of §1988 is at `../../current/`. It has not been amended since the RLUIPA addition in 2000 (P.L. 106-274).

## The Full Evolution Arc

| Stage | Year | Event | Impact on §1988 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 1976 | Civil Rights Attorney's Fees Awards Act | Created §1988(b) — fee-shifting for civil rights actions |
| 02 | 1988 | Civil Rights Restoration Act | Broadened scope of underlying statutes (no §1988 text change) |
| 03 | 1991 | Civil Rights Act of 1991 | Added §1988(c) — expert fees in §1981/§1981a actions |
| 04 | 1993 | RFRA | Added RFRA to §1988(b) fee list (limited after Boerne 1997) |
| 05 | 1994 | VAWA | Added §13981 to §1988(b) (dead letter after Morrison 2000) |
| 06 | 2000 | RLUIPA | Added RLUIPA to §1988(b) — last amendment |
| 07 | Current | No change since 2000 | Three subsections: (a) choice of law, (b) fees, (c) expert fees |

## What the Evolution Shows

The story of §1988 is the story of Congress repeatedly trying to keep the economic engine of civil rights enforcement running while the Supreme Court repeatedly narrows the underlying substantive rights. Congress adds fee-shifting for RFRA — Court strikes down RFRA as to states. Congress adds fee-shifting for VAWA — Court strikes down VAWA's civil remedy entirely. Congress adds fee-shifting for RLUIPA — Court upholds RLUIPA. The pattern is: Congress insists that civil rights enforcement must be economically viable; the Court decides which civil rights survive.

The core provision — §1988(b) fees for §1983 actions — has never been textually threatened. It is the single most important structural guarantee in federal civil rights enforcement: the promise that if you vindicate a constitutional right, the government pays your lawyer.
