# 42 USC §1988 — 1994 Amendment (P.L. 103-322, §320201(a))

## What Changed

The Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act of 1994 added the Revised Statutes §1979 (42 USC §1983) enforcement provisions for hate crimes under 42 USC §13981 (the Violence Against Women Act's civil rights provision) to the list of statutes covered by §1988(b) fee-shifting.

Specifically, the 1994 Act created a new federal civil rights remedy for gender-motivated violence (42 USC §13981) and ensured that prevailing plaintiffs under this provision could recover attorney's fees via §1988(b).

## Why It Happened

The Violence Against Women Act (VAWA) recognized that gender-motivated violence is a civil rights violation, not merely a state criminal matter. The civil remedy in §13981 allowed victims of gender-motivated violence to sue their attackers in federal court for compensatory and punitive damages. Adding §13981 to §1988(b) made the economic logic work — just as the 1976 Act had done for §1983.

## Note on Subsequent Invalidation

In *United States v. Morrison*, 529 U.S. 598 (2000), the Supreme Court struck down §13981's civil remedy as exceeding Congress's Commerce Clause and §5 enforcement powers. The §1988 reference to §13981 remains in the statute text but is a dead letter — there are no actionable §13981 claims after Morrison.

## The Pattern

RFRA (1993): Congress adds to §1988, Court narrows. VAWA civil remedy (1994): Congress adds to §1988, Court strikes down entirely. Both times, the fee-shifting mechanism was in place but the underlying substantive right was invalidated by the Court. The §1988 amendments survive as statutory fossils — testaments to Congress's intent that courts and the Court frustrated.
