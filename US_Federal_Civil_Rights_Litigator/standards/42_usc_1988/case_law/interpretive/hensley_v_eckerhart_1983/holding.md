# Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424 (1983)

## The Case

Patients at a Missouri state hospital sued officials under §1983 alleging constitutional violations in their treatment and conditions of confinement. The plaintiffs prevailed on some claims but lost on others. They sought attorney's fees under §1988(b). The question was how to calculate the fee when the plaintiff prevailed on some claims but not all.

## What the Court Held

Justice Powell, writing for the majority:

1. **The lodestar method is the starting point for calculating §1988 fees.** The lodestar is the number of hours reasonably expended on the litigation multiplied by a reasonable hourly rate. This product is presumed to be the reasonable fee.

2. **The court should exclude hours spent on claims that are distinct and unsuccessful.** If the plaintiff's unsuccessful claims are unrelated to the successful claims, the hours spent on the unsuccessful claims should be excluded. But if all claims arise from a "common core of facts" or are based on "related legal theories," the court should focus on the overall relief obtained rather than parsing hours claim by claim.

3. **The most critical factor is the degree of success obtained.** A fully prevailing plaintiff should recover a fully compensatory fee. A plaintiff who achieves only partial or limited success should receive a reduced fee commensurate with the results achieved.

## The Rule Hensley Stands For

**The lodestar (reasonable hours × reasonable rate) is the presumptive §1988 fee.** Adjustments (upward or downward) are permitted but must be supported by specific evidence. The degree of success obtained is the most important factor in determining reasonableness.
