HENSLEY v. ECKERHART
461 U.S. 424 (1983)
United States Supreme Court
Decided: May 16, 1983

---

FACTS:

Plaintiffs were involuntarily committed mental patients at the Fulton State Hospital in Missouri. They brought a class action under § 1983 against hospital officials for unconstitutional conditions of confinement. They prevailed on some claims and lost on others. The district court awarded attorney's fees under § 1988, computing the fee based on the number of hours times a reasonable hourly rate (the "lodestar"), and declining to reduce the fee for the unsuccessful claims.

The Eighth Circuit affirmed. The Supreme Court affirmed the legal framework but remanded for reconsideration of the fee amount.

---

HOLDING:

Justice Powell wrote for the Court.

THE LODESTAR METHOD — THE MANDATORY STARTING POINT:
"The most useful starting point for determining the amount of a reasonable fee is the number of hours reasonably expended on the litigation multiplied by a reasonable hourly rate. This calculation provides an objective basis on which to make an initial estimate of the value of a lawyer's services."

Step 1: Reasonable hours × Reasonable hourly rate = Lodestar
Step 2: Adjust upward or downward based on results obtained

RESULTS OBTAINED — THE KEY MODIFIER:
"The results obtained" is the most important factor in determining the reasonableness of the fee after calculating the lodestar.

If the plaintiff obtained "excellent results," the lodestar needs no reduction — the fee award should be the full lodestar.

If the plaintiff obtained only partial or limited success, the fee must be reduced:
"Where the plaintiff achieved only partial or limited success, the product of hours reasonably expended on the litigation as a whole times a reasonable hourly rate may be an excessive amount."

THE RELATED vs. UNRELATED CLAIMS RULE:
- Where claims involve a "common core of facts" or are based on "related legal theories," fees for all time spent may be awarded even if some claims fail.
- Where unsuccessful claims are "distinctly different" from the successful claims and involve different facts and legal theories, hours spent solely on the failed claims should be excluded.

---

THE HENSLEY FRAMEWORK (summary):

1. Start: Calculate lodestar (hours × rate)
2. Ask: Are unsuccessful claims distinct from successful claims, or do they share a common core of facts?
   - Related: No automatic reduction; consider overall success level
   - Unrelated: Exclude hours spent on unrelated, unsuccessful claims
3. Adjust: Reduce lodestar if only partial success; full fee if excellent results
4. Document: Time must be documented with sufficient detail to allow judicial review; excessive, redundant, or otherwise unnecessary hours should be excluded

---

APPLICATION TO STEWARD'S CASES:

Hensley is the lodestar framework that will govern any § 1988 attorney's fee application:

1. **Document every hour**: Time records showing hours spent on each category of work are required. Inadequate documentation results in reduction or denial.

2. **Claim selection matters**: Winning on all claims supports full lodestar. Winning on some, losing on others requires the Hensley adjustment analysis. Strategic tip: if some claims are long shots, they should be pleaded as alternative theories that share common facts with the core claims — making them "related" under Hensley and avoiding the fee-reduction risk.

3. **Pro se litigant note**: Pro se plaintiffs generally cannot recover attorney's fees under § 1988 because they did not pay an attorney (Kay v. Ehrler). However, if an attorney is later retained or if the steward's professional experience creates a different argument, this should be analyzed separately.

4. **Excellent results goal**: The strategic objective is to win on the core constitutional claims so that "excellent results" support full lodestar without Hensley reduction.
