# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Christiansburg Garment Co. v. EEOC, 434 U.S. 412 (1978)
**Statute at issue:** 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-5(k) (Title VII fee provision), applied by analogy to § 1988
**Version the court was reading:** Original 1976 enactment of § 1988 (P.L. 94-559); Title VII fee provision as enacted 1964/amended 1972
**Evolution stage:** `../../42_usc_1988/evolution/01_origin_1976/`

Although this case technically arose under Title VII's fee provision, the Court's asymmetric standard was immediately adopted for § 1988 by legislative history and subsequent decisions. A prevailing plaintiff should ordinarily recover fees unless special circumstances would render an award unjust. A prevailing defendant, however, may recover fees only if the plaintiff's claim was "frivolous, unreasonable, or without foundation." This asymmetry ensures civil rights plaintiffs are not deterred from bringing good-faith claims by fear of fee liability.
