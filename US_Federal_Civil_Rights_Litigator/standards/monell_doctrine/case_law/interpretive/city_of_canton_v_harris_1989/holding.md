# City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378 (1989)

## The Case

Geraldine Harris was arrested and brought to the Canton, Ohio police station. She slumped to the floor several times, was incoherent, and was clearly in medical distress. The officers left her on the floor for an hour. She was eventually taken to a hospital, where she was diagnosed with emotional ailments. She sued the city under §1983, alleging a failure to train officers to recognize and respond to medical emergencies.

## What the Court Held

Justice White, writing for the majority:

1. **A municipality can be held liable under §1983 for failure to train its employees when the failure amounts to "deliberate indifference" to the rights of persons with whom the employees come into contact.** This is a Monell policy-or-custom theory: the failure to train is the "policy" that caused the violation.

2. **"Deliberate indifference" requires showing that the need for training was so obvious that the failure to train reflects a deliberate or conscious choice.** The municipality must have actual or constructive notice that its training program is inadequate and likely to result in constitutional violations.

3. **A single incident of excessive force or medical neglect is generally insufficient to establish deliberate indifference** — unless the violation was a "highly predictable consequence" of the failure to train.

## The Rule

**A municipality is liable under Monell for failure to train when the failure reflects "deliberate indifference" to constitutional rights — meaning the need for training was obvious and the municipality consciously chose not to provide it.**
