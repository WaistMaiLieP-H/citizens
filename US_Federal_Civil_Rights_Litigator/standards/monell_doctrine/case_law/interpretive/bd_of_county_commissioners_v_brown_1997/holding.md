# Board of County Commissioners v. Brown, 520 U.S. 397 (1997)

## The Case

Jill Brown was pulled over by Reserve Deputy Stacy Burns of the Bryan County, Oklahoma Sheriff's Office. Burns used excessive force during the stop — he grabbed Brown from her vehicle and threw her to the ground, causing severe permanent knee injuries requiring multiple surgeries. Burns had a criminal record including guilty pleas to assault and battery, resisting arrest, and public drunkenness. The county had hired him despite this record. Brown sued the county under §1983, arguing the sheriff's decision to hire Burns despite his violent criminal history was a "policy" that caused her injuries.

## What the Court Held

Justice O'Connor, writing for the majority:

1. **A municipality's single hiring decision can constitute a "policy" for Monell purposes** — but the plaintiff must prove that the hiring decision reflected deliberate indifference to the risk that the specific constitutional violation at issue would follow.

2. **Proving deliberate indifference in a single-hire case requires showing that the officer's background made the specific violation a "plainly obvious consequence" of the hiring decision.** It is not enough to show the officer was unfit generally; the plaintiff must connect the officer's known propensity to the particular type of harm that occurred.

3. **This is a harder standard than City of Canton's failure-to-train test.** Where a pattern of violations can establish notice for training claims, a single hire must demonstrate that this specific hiring decision, given what was known, was so obviously wrong that it reflected a deliberate choice to endanger constitutional rights.

## The Rule

**Municipal liability for a single hiring decision requires proof that the officer's known background made the specific constitutional violation a "plainly obvious consequence" of hiring — mere negligence in screening is not enough. The connection between the hire and the harm must be direct and predictable.**

## Citation

Board of County Commissioners of Bryan County v. Brown, 520 U.S. 397 (1997). Vote: 5-4. Majority: O'Connor, J.
