# Statute Version Cited

**Case:** Connick v. Thompson, 563 U.S. 51 (2011)
**Statute at issue:** 42 U.S.C. § 1983 (via Monell municipal liability doctrine)
**Version the court was reading:** Current version (post-1979 amendment)
**Evolution stage:** `../../42_usc_1983/evolution/06_current/`

John Thompson spent 18 years in prison — 14 on death row — because New Orleans prosecutors suppressed exculpatory blood evidence. He sued the District Attorney's office under § 1983, arguing a failure to train prosecutors on their Brady obligations. The Court reversed the $14 million jury verdict. Justice Thomas held that a single Brady violation by a single prosecutor does not prove "deliberate indifference" to training needs — the DA's office could assume prosecutors knew their Brady duties from law school and bar admission. A failure-to-train claim requires a pattern of similar violations, absent extraordinary circumstances where the need for training is "so patently obvious."
