Monell Municipal Liability Doctrine — Case-Law Standard

This is a case-law standard, not a statutory provision. The Monell doctrine has no statutory
text of its own — it is a body of judicial interpretation that defines when municipalities
and other local government bodies can be held liable under 42 USC §1983.

Core rule (Monell v. Dept of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978)):
A municipality is liable under §1983 only when the constitutional violation results from:
  (1) An official policy adopted by the municipality's legislative body; OR
  (2) An act or decision by an official whose acts "may fairly be said to represent
      official policy"; OR
  (3) A custom or practice so widespread and persistent that it carries the force of law,
      even though not formally adopted.

NO RESPONDEAT SUPERIOR: A municipality cannot be held liable under §1983 solely because
it employs someone who committed a constitutional violation. The violation must be caused
by the municipality's own policy, custom, or deliberate choice.

This distinguishes Monell from California's Gov. Code §815.2, which DOES impose respondeat
superior (vicarious liability for employee acts within scope of employment). A plaintiff
bringing both §1983 and Bane Act claims has a harder path to entity liability under §1983
(must show policy/custom) than under state law (must show scope of employment).
