# Section 1983 Complaint Workflow

## Intake → Analysis → Drafting → Filing

---

### Phase 1: INTAKE — Identify the Constitutional Violation and the State Actor

**Required elements for ANY §1983 claim:**
1. **A person** (defendant — individual officer, municipality, or both)
2. **Acting under color of state law** (badge, authority, government position)
3. **Deprived** the plaintiff
4. **Of a right secured by the Constitution or laws** (4th, 5th, 8th, 14th Amendment; or federal statute per Thiboutot)

**Intake questions:**
- What constitutional right was violated? (excessive force = 4A; due process = 14A; equal protection = 14A)
- Who committed the violation? (name, badge number, agency, position)
- Were they acting with state authority when they did it? (on duty, using government power, invoking official position)
- What are the facts? (date, time, location, witnesses, injuries, documentation)

---

### Phase 2: ANALYSIS — Evaluate Viability

**Qualified Immunity Screen (Harlow/Pearson/Anderson):**
- Was the right "clearly established" at the time of the conduct?
- Is there a prior case with sufficiently similar facts?
- If not, is the violation so obvious it falls under Taylor v. Riojas "obvious clarity"?
- Recommendation: proceed, proceed with QI risk noted, or decline

**Municipal Liability Screen (Monell/Canton/Connick):**
- Is there an official policy that caused the violation?
- Is there a custom or practice? (pattern of similar violations)
- Is there a failure to train amounting to deliberate indifference?
- Was the decision made by a final policymaker?

**Damages Assessment:**
- Compensatory damages (medical bills, lost wages, pain and suffering)
- Punitive damages (available against individual officers, not municipalities)
- Attorney's fees under §1988 (lodestar × degree of success)
- Nominal damages (if right violated but no compensable harm)

**Statute of Limitations:**
- §1983 borrows the state personal injury SOL (California: 2 years, CCP §335.1)
- Federal tolling rules apply (discovery rule, equitable tolling)
- Government Tort Claims Act does NOT apply to §1983 (Felder v. Casey)

---

### Phase 3: DRAFTING

**Complaint structure (FRCP Rule 8(a) + Twombly/Iqbal plausibility):**

1. **Caption** — court, parties, case number
2. **Jurisdictional statement** — 28 USC §1343(a)(3), 28 USC §1331
3. **Parties** — plaintiff (with standing), defendants (individual + entity)
4. **Factual allegations** — chronological, specific, plausible (not conclusory)
5. **Claims for relief:**
   - Count 1: §1983 against individual defendants (constitutional violation + under color of law)
   - Count 2: §1983 Monell against municipal defendant (policy/custom/failure to train)
   - Count 3: State-law claims (Bane Act, negligence, etc.) — supplemental jurisdiction under 28 USC §1367
6. **Prayer for relief** — compensatory damages, punitive damages (individual only), injunctive relief, declaratory relief, attorney's fees under §1988, costs

**Parallel state claims to consider:**
- Bane Act (§52.1) — no QI, respondeat superior via §815.2
- Ralph Act (§51.7) — if hate-motivated violence
- Tort Claims Act compliance — for state claims only (NOT required for §1983)

---

### Phase 4: FILING

**Pre-filing checklist:**
- [ ] Venue: NDCA (Oakland division for Alameda/Contra Costa)
- [ ] IFP application if proceeding pro se (28 USC §1915)
- [ ] Civil cover sheet (JS 44)
- [ ] Summons for each defendant
- [ ] Service plan (FRCP Rule 4 — 90 days)
- [ ] If Bane Act included: Government Tort Claim filed? If not, federal claims survive but state claims may be barred.

**Post-filing:**
- Serve all defendants within 90 days
- Monitor for anti-SLAPP motion (if state claims in federal court — applicability varies by circuit)
- Prepare for qualified immunity motion at summary judgment stage
- Fee petition preparation begins at case opening (contemporaneous time records)
