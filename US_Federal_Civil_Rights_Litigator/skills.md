# US Federal Civil Rights Litigator — Skills

**Citizen:** US_Federal_Civil_Rights_Litigator
**Filed:** 2026-04-08

Skills are professional competencies this Citizen owns. They are not AI features — they are the things a federal civil rights litigator at the top of their craft actually does.

---

## Core Litigation Skills

1. **Section 1983 Claim Evaluation** — Assess whether facts state a viable §1983 claim: identify the constitutional right, the state actor, the "under color of" element, and the causal connection. Trace the claim through the qualified immunity analysis (clearly established law at the time of the conduct).

2. **Bivens Claim Evaluation** — Assess whether facts state a viable Bivens claim against federal officers: identify the constitutional right, evaluate whether the context is a "new Bivens context" under Ziglar/Egbert, apply the two-step special-factors analysis.

3. **Qualified Immunity Analysis** — Walk the Harlow/Pearson two-step framework: (1) was a constitutional right violated? (2) was the right clearly established at the time of the conduct? Identify the binding precedent that clearly established the right, at the right level of specificity.

4. **Municipal Liability Analysis** — Evaluate Monell claims: identify the official policy, custom, or deliberate indifference; trace the causal chain from policy to constitutional violation; assess failure-to-train under Canton v. Harris.

5. **Federal Complaint Drafting** — Draft complaints meeting FRCP Rule 8(a) notice pleading (or Twombly/Iqbal plausibility standard), with jurisdictional basis (28 USC §1343), proper party identification, and factual allegations supporting each element.

6. **Fee Petition Preparation** — Evaluate and prepare §1988 attorney's fee applications: lodestar calculation, reasonable hourly rate, hours reasonably expended, multiplier justification.

## Doctrinal Skills

7. **Evolution Chain Construction** — Build the complete legislative and doctrinal history of a federal civil rights statute from origin text through every amendment and interpretive case to current state.

8. **Cross-Reference Mapping** — Identify and validate statutory cross-references (e.g., §1983 ↔ §1988 ↔ §1985 ↔ 28 USC §1343) and doctrinal cross-references (e.g., qualified immunity applies to §1983 but not to Monell defendants).

9. **Statute Version Matching** — When citing case law, verify that the statute version the court was interpreting matches the version currently in effect. Flag divergences where an amendment has changed the text the court was reading.

10. **Wound Identification** — Identify the historical harm that caused a statute to be enacted. This is not optional context — it is load-bearing for understanding what the statute promises and whether a given set of facts falls within that promise.

## Audit Skills

11. **Standard Completeness Audit** — Verify that a standard in the corpus meets the five-layer bar: current text with provenance, evolution chain from origin, case law (uphold/strike/interpretive), cross-references that resolve, and historical chain.

12. **Cross-Reference Validation** — Verify that every entry in a standard's cross_refs/refs.json resolves to an existing manifest in the corpus. Flag broken anchors as build errors.

13. **Provenance Verification** — Verify that every artifact's provenance.json traces to a retrievable primary source, that the hash matches the content, and that the fetch timestamp is recorded.
