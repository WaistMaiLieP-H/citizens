# 15 USC §1692 — Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA)
## Current Framework — Logical Structure

**Standard:** 15 USC §§1692–1692p (Fair Debt Collection Practices Act)
**Build method:** Logical Delta

---

## Why this statute exists (the wound it answers)

Before 1977, the debt collection industry was effectively unregulated at the federal level.
Congressional findings documented: (1) abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt collection practices
by many debt collectors, (2) these practices contributing to personal bankruptcies, marital
instability, job losses, and invasion of individual privacy, and (3) existing laws inadequate
to protect consumers. Documented pre-1977 practices included:
- Calling at 3am, calling workplaces after being told to stop
- Falsely claiming to be attorneys or government officials
- Threatening arrest, garnishment, or legal action collectors had no intention or ability to take
- Publishing lists of "debtors" who refused to pay
- Using profane, abusive, or threatening language
- Communicating with third parties (employers, neighbors, relatives) to embarrass debtors
- Contacting represented debtors directly, bypassing their attorneys

The wound: the power imbalance between institutional collectors (who did this professionally,
at scale) and individual consumers (who faced collection rarely, did not know their rights,
and were vulnerable to intimidation). 15 USC §1692(a)-(e).

---

## Who is a "debt collector" — the definitional gate

**Covered:** Any person who regularly collects debts owed to another. 15 USC §1692a(6).
Includes third-party collection agencies, collection attorneys, debt buyers who purchase
defaulted accounts.

**NOT covered by FDCPA (but covered by state analogs like Rosenthal):**
- Original creditors collecting their own debts (banks, credit card companies collecting
  in their own name for debts they extended)
- Government employees collecting government debts in the course of employment

**ChexSystems / debt collection application for Michael:** Third-party debt collectors who
purchased or were assigned Michael's accounts from original creditors are "debt collectors"
under FDCPA. Any collection attorney who sent demand letters is a "debt collector." The
original bank or credit card company is NOT a debt collector under FDCPA (it is under
California's Rosenthal Act — see cross-ref).

**"Consumer debt" requirement:** FDCPA only covers debts incurred for personal, family, or
household purposes. Business debts are not covered. All of Michael's documented debts
(auto loans, credit accounts, medical debts) are consumer debts.

## The Six Core Prohibitions

### Prohibition 1 — Communication restrictions: §1692c
**Rule:**
- May not contact consumer at unusual or inconvenient times or places; presumption: before 8am
  or after 9pm local time is inconvenient
- May not contact consumer at workplace if collector knows the employer prohibits it
- If consumer is represented by an attorney, collector must communicate only with the attorney
- Consumer may send written cease-communication demand; collector must stop all contact except
  to notify of a specific action (filing suit, ceasing collection)

**Application:** If Michael sent written cease-communication demands that were ignored, each
subsequent contact is a §1692c violation.

---

### Prohibition 2 — Harassment and abuse: §1692d
**Rule:** No conduct whose natural consequence is to harass, oppress, or abuse any person.
Specific prohibitions include: threats of violence or harm, use of obscene or profane language,
publishing a list of consumers who allegedly refuse to pay, causing a telephone to ring
repeatedly with intent to annoy or harass, placing calls without meaningful disclosure of
collector's identity.

---

### Prohibition 3 — False or misleading representations: §1692e
**Rule:** No false, deceptive, or misleading representation or means in connection with
collection of a debt. Specific prohibitions include:
- Falsely representing character, amount, or legal status of debt
- False representation that collector is an attorney or government official
- Threatening action not legally available (e.g., threatening criminal prosecution when only
  civil collection is available)
- Using false, deceptive, or misleading name (e.g., implying government affiliation)
- Failing to disclose in initial communication that information is being used to collect a debt
  ("mini-Miranda" requirement)

**Application for Michael:** If collectors misrepresented the legal status of debts (claiming
debts were legally valid when they resulted from identity theft or fraud), that is §1692e(2)(A).
If collectors threatened legal action they could not or did not intend to take, that is §1692e(5).

---

### Prohibition 4 — Unfair practices: §1692f
**Rule:** No unfair or unconscionable means to collect or attempt to collect a debt. Includes:
collecting amounts not authorized by the agreement or permitted by law, depositing a postdated
check before the date on the check, soliciting a postdated check, communicating with the
consumer by postcard, using envelopes or symbols revealing debt collection purposes.

---

### Prohibition 5 — Validation notice requirement: §1692g
**Rule:** Within 5 days of initial contact, collector must send written notice containing:
(1) the amount of the debt, (2) the name of the creditor to whom the debt is owed,
(3) notice that the consumer may dispute the debt in writing within 30 days, (4) notice that
upon request the collector will verify the debt, and (5) notice that upon request the collector
will provide the name of the original creditor if different.

**30-day dispute right:** If consumer disputes the debt within 30 days, the collector must
cease collection until it provides verification. Failure to cease = §1692g violation.

**Application for Michael:** Any debt collector who failed to send the validation notice, or
who continued collection after receiving a written dispute, has independent §1692g violations.

---

### Prohibition 6 — Debt validation: §1692g (continued)
**Verification upon dispute:** Collector must provide verification of the debt (a copy of the
judgment or verification of the amount) and cease collection until provided.

**FDCPA identity theft application:** When Michael disputes a debt as resulting from identity
theft, the collector must cease collection and verify. Continuing collection of a debt known
to result from identity theft = §1692e(2)(A) (false representation of legal status) +
§1692f (unfair means) + §1692g (failure to verify and cease).

## Damages

### §1692k — Civil liability
**Individual action:**
- Actual damages (financial loss, emotional distress with evidence)
- Statutory damages up to $1,000 per lawsuit (not per violation) regardless of actual damages
- Attorney fees + costs (mandatory if successful, not discretionary)

**Class action:**
- Statutory damages up to the lesser of $500,000 or 1% of the debt collector's net worth

**Bona fide error defense:** Collector not liable if violation was not intentional and resulted
from a bona fide error notwithstanding maintenance of procedures reasonably adapted to avoid
any such error.

**One-year SOL:** Actions must be brought within 1 year of the date of the violation. §1692k(d).

## FDCPA + FCRA compound claim

When a debt collector reports a disputed debt to a CRA while the §1692g dispute is pending,
that reporting is:
- Potentially §1681s-2(b) FCRA furnisher violation (reporting inaccurate/disputed information)
- §1692e(2)(A) FDCPA false representation of the character or legal status of a debt
- §1692e(8) FDCPA communicating credit information known to be false or which should be known
  to be false

This compound claim is available when collectors report to ChexSystems or credit bureaus
while disputes are pending.
