# Rosenthal v. Great Western Financial Securities Corp.
## 14 Cal.4th 394 (1996)

**Court:** California Supreme Court
**Decided:** July 29, 1996
**Note:** This case interprets the California Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act
(Civil Code §§1788–1788.33), NOT the federal FDCPA directly. Included here because:
(1) the Rosenthal Act is the FDCPA's California analog that covers original creditors;
(2) this case establishes the standard for FDCPA/Rosenthal compound claims in California;
(3) the doctrinal analysis of "debt collector" scope under Rosenthal bears directly on
understanding the FDCPA's complementary structure in California federal practice.

---

## Issue decided

Does the California Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act apply to original creditors
(not just third-party collectors), and what standard governs the "debt collector" definition?

---

## Holding

**Yes.** The Rosenthal Act applies to original creditors collecting their own debts — this
is the primary distinction from the federal FDCPA, which covers only third-party collectors.
California Civil Code §1788.2(c) defines "debt collector" to include any person who
collects consumer debts in the regular course of business, regardless of whether the debt
was originally owed to them.

**The compound federal-state claim:** When a California consumer is subjected to collection
conduct that violates both FDCPA and the Rosenthal Act:
- The federal FDCPA claim covers: third-party collectors, collection attorneys (Heintz)
- The California Rosenthal Act claim covers: original creditors + third-party collectors
- The consumer can bring both claims simultaneously in federal court with supplemental jurisdiction

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Original creditor gap filled by Rosenthal.** If a bank, credit card company, or original
lender (not a collection agency) engaged in abusive or deceptive collection practices against
Michael, the federal FDCPA does not reach them — but California's Rosenthal Act does.
See CA_Consumer_Protection_Litigator / civ_1788_rosenthal for the full Rosenthal standard.

**Cross-reference for compound claims:** Any collection case in California should plead both
FDCPA (against third-party collectors) and Rosenthal Act (against all collectors including
original creditors). The Rosenthal Act also allows recovery of punitive damages under Civil
Code §1788.30(b) — which the federal FDCPA does not.
