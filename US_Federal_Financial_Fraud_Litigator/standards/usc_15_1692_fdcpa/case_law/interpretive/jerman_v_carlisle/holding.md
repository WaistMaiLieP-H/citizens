# Jerman v. Carlisle, McNellie, Rini, Kramer & Ulrich LPA
## 559 US 573 (2010)

**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** April 21, 2010
**Vote:** 7-2

---

## Issue decided

Does the bona fide error defense under §1692k(c) protect a debt collector from liability for
legal errors — mistakes about what the FDCPA requires — or only for clerical and factual errors?

---

## Holding

**Legal errors are NOT protected by the bona fide error defense.** The bona fide error defense
under §1692k(c) only protects debt collectors from liability for unintentional violations
caused by clerical, factual, and procedural errors (e.g., a computer glitch that sends a
letter twice, a transposed account number). It does not protect a debt collector who
incorrectly interprets the law and acts on that incorrect legal interpretation.

**The text of §1692k(c):** "A debt collector may not be held liable in any action brought under
this subchapter if the debt collector shows by a preponderance of evidence that the violation
was not intentional and resulted from a bona fide error notwithstanding the maintenance of
procedures reasonably adapted to avoid any such error."

**The Court's reasoning:** The phrase "procedures reasonably adapted to avoid any such error"
implies preventable operational errors — the kind addressed by compliance procedures, staff
training, quality control. Legal mistakes (wrong interpretation of the statute) cannot be
avoided by operational procedures; they require legal advice. Congress did not intend to
immunize debt collectors who adopt the wrong legal interpretation of FDCPA and follow it
consistently.

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Institutional policy ≠ bona fide error defense.** If a debt collector had an institutional
policy of sending collection notices without the mini-Miranda disclosure (§1692e(11)), or
routinely continuing collection after receiving written disputes (§1692g), and justified this
by claiming they interpreted the statute differently — that is a legal error, not a bona fide
operational error. Jerman forecloses the defense.

**Combining with Safeco (FCRA willfulness):** Jerman + Safeco together create a consistent
framework across consumer protection statutes: institutional policies based on objectively
unreasonable interpretations of consumer protection law are willful/intentional violations, not
protected errors. A debt collector who violated FDCPA and reported to a CRA under an incorrect
legal interpretation faces both FDCPA statutory damages (up to $1,000) and FCRA willful
violation damages (up to $1,000 + punitive) on the credit reporting component.
