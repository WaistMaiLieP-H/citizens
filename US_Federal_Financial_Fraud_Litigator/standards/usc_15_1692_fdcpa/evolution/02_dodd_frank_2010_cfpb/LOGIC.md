# FDCPA — Evolution Stage 02: Dodd-Frank 2010 + CFPB Regulation F 2021

**Key events:**
- Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act, Pub. L. 111-203 (2010)
- CFPB Regulation F, 12 CFR Part 1006, effective November 30, 2021

---

## The wound this stage answered

**The enforcement gap (pre-2010):** FDCPA enforcement was split between the FTC and state
attorneys general. The FTC had limited resources and could not issue substantive rules on FDCPA
without going through notice-and-comment rulemaking that took years. Consumer advocates
documented that many large collectors violated the FDCPA systematically because the $1,000
statutory damages cap made class actions the only economically viable enforcement mechanism.

**The digital gap (pre-2021):** The 1977 FDCPA was written for telephone and letter collection.
By the 2010s, collectors were using email, text messages, social media direct messages, and
online portals to contact debtors. The statute said nothing about these channels. Courts split
on whether email contacts were covered, how the "unusual or inconvenient" standard applied to
digital communications, and whether displaying debt information on a debtor's public social
media profile violated the statute.

---

## Dodd-Frank 2010 — enforcement transfer, not substantive change

**What changed:** Enforcement authority transferred from FTC to newly created Consumer
Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB). CFPB received authority to:
- Issue substantive rules under FDCPA via notice-and-comment (§1692l(d))
- Supervise large debt collectors (over $10M annual receipts) directly
- Bring enforcement actions with full CFPB remedial authority

**What did NOT change:** The six core prohibitions, the damages structure, the private right
of action, the one-year SOL, and the definitional scope all remained identical to the 1977 text.

---

## CFPB Regulation F 2021 — the digital update

**What changed via rulemaking:**
- **Electronic communication rules:** Collectors may use email and text messages if consumer
  has provided the address/number for debt collection purposes AND the message includes
  opt-out mechanism. If consumer opts out, no further electronic contacts.
- **7-in-7 call frequency cap:** Collector may not place more than 7 phone calls to a consumer
  about a specific debt within 7 consecutive days. After a conversation occurs, must wait 7 days
  before calling again about that debt.
- **Opt-out for digital:** Consumer may opt out of electronic communications. Opt-out must
  be honored within 10 days.
- **Electronic validation notices:** Collectors may send validation notice electronically
  with specific disclosures.

**What Regulation F did NOT change:** All six core §1692d/e/f/g prohibitions remain unchanged.
The new electronic rules supplement (not replace) the prohibition framework.

---

## Logical shift from Stage 01 to Stage 02

| Dimension | 1977 origin | Post-2021 |
|---|---|---|
| Enforcement authority | FTC | CFPB (with rulemaking power) |
| Electronic communications | Not addressed | Opt-in model for email/text; 7-in-7 call cap |
| Core six prohibitions | Enacted | Unchanged |
| Damages structure | $1,000 statutory + actual + fees | Unchanged |
| Private right of action | Enacted | Unchanged |

---

## Application to Michael's cases

The 2021 Regulation F electronic rules are relevant if debt collectors contacted Michael via
email or text in connection with disputed debts. Each electronic contact after a valid opt-out
is an independent FDCPA violation. The 7-in-7 call cap is relevant if collectors called
repeatedly about the same debt in the same week. These are independent violations supplementing
the classic §1692d/e/f/g claims.
