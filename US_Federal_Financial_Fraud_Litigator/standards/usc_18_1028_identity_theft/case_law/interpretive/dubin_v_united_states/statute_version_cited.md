# Statute Version Cited — Dubin v. United States (2023)

The Dubin court construed 18 USC §1028A as in force in 2023 — unchanged from the 2004 ITPEA enactment and identical to the version applied in Flores-Figueroa (2009).

**Relevant statutory language:**
> Whoever, during and in relation to any felony violation enumerated in subsection (c), knowingly transfers, possesses, or uses, without lawful authority, a means of identification of another person shall, in addition to the punishment provided for such felony, be sentenced to a term of imprisonment of 2 years.

**Evolution stage applicable:** 03_ithpa_2004

**Version cited by the court:** 18 USC §1028A as in force June 2023 (identical to 2004 enactment text).

**Interpretive interaction with Flores-Figueroa:** Dubin and Flores-Figueroa stand together. Flores-Figueroa narrowed §1028A by requiring actual knowledge of a real person. Dubin narrowed it further by requiring the identity use to be at the crux of the fraud. Both decisions applied the identical statutory text but interpreted different aspects of its scope.
