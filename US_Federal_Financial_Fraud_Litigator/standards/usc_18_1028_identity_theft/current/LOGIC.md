# 18 USC §1028 — Current Logical Framework (2026)

**Purpose of this document:** Structural knowledge base of the statute as it currently operates. Not a verbatim replica. A map of the logic the Citizen uses when evaluating an identity-theft fact pattern.

## The statute's operational structure

§1028 prohibits a set of specific acts involving identification documents, authentication features, and means of identification. The statute organizes its prohibitions by ACT, not by victim harm. This is the first critical logic point: §1028 is a conduct statute, not a results statute. A defendant can violate it without successfully stealing anything, if the prohibited conduct (making a false ID, possessing a stolen means of identification, transferring identification features) is proved.

## The three categorical objects of protection

The statute protects three categories:

1. **Identification document** — a document made or issued by, or under the authority of, a governmental entity and primarily used to identify an individual (driver's licenses, birth certificates, passports, SSN cards, military IDs). The original 1982 category.

2. **Authentication feature** — holograms, watermarks, magnetic strips, biometric templates, encrypted identifiers built into an ID document. Added in 2003–2004 to track how IDs became more technologically complex.

3. **Means of identification** — ANY name or number that can be used, alone or in conjunction with other information, to identify a specific individual: SSN, date of birth, biometric data, unique electronic identification number, routing number. This is the broadest category and the one added in 1998 by ITADA.

The three-category structure allows the statute to reach traditional forged-document cases (category 1), physical-feature counterfeiting cases (category 2), and pure-data identity theft (category 3).

## The eight prohibited acts

The statute enumerates eight types of prohibited conduct. The Citizen applies them by mapping facts to conduct:

1. Producing an identification document/authentication feature/means of identification without lawful authority
2. Transferring such a document/feature/means knowing it was stolen or produced without authority
3. Possessing with intent to use unlawfully, or to transfer unlawfully, five or more such documents/features
4. Possessing an identification document (other than one authorized to be produced by the lawful issuer) with intent to defraud the United States
5. Producing, transferring, or possessing a document-making implement or authentication feature with intent to produce a false ID
6. Possessing an identification document or authentication feature that is or appears to be one issued by the United States government
7. Knowingly transferring or using a means of identification of another person without lawful authority with intent to commit or aid any unlawful activity that violates federal law or constitutes a felony under state or local law — **this is the core modern identity theft prohibition added by ITADA in 1998**
8. Trafficking in false or actual authentication features for use in identification documents, document-making implements, or means of identification

The Citizen's job with a new fact pattern is to identify which of the eight maps to the conduct alleged, and then to evaluate the elements (interstate nexus, knowledge, intent, value thresholds).

## The companion statute: §1028A aggravated identity theft

§1028A is a sentencing enhancement. When someone commits certain enumerated felonies (wire fraud, mail fraud, bank fraud, and others) AND "knowingly transfers, possesses, or uses, without lawful authority, a means of identification of another person" during and in relation to that felony, a MANDATORY TWO-YEAR CONSECUTIVE sentence attaches. The two years runs after the sentence for the predicate felony — it cannot be concurrent and cannot be waived.

The leverage of §1028A in criminal negotiation is enormous: a defendant facing a potential five-year sentence on the underlying felony knows a §1028A charge means seven years minimum. This is why §1028A charges are routinely used by USAOs as the pressure point in plea negotiations.

## The civil use of a criminal statute

§1028 is a criminal statute — it does not create a private right of action. But it has two civil uses:

1. **Civil RICO predicate.** §1028 is listed in 18 USC §1961(1)(B) as a RICO predicate offense. A plaintiff who can show a pattern of §1028 violations (two or more within 10 years) by an enterprise that injured the plaintiff's business or property can plead civil RICO under §1964(c) with treble damages and attorney fees.

2. **Criminal referral leverage.** A well-prepared §1028 criminal referral package — sent to USPIS, FBI, IRS-CI, or the USAO — creates investigative pressure that often moves civil defendants to settle. The Citizen builds referral packages as part of the civil strategy, not as an alternative to it.

## Interstate nexus

§1028 requires one of several interstate nexuses to be constitutionally within federal jurisdiction:
- The production, transfer, possession, or use was in or affecting interstate or foreign commerce
- The document/feature/means was transported in the mail in the course of the offense
- The document or feature is one issued by the United States

For a SSN-based identity theft case, the "issued by the United States" prong applies — no interstate commerce showing is needed. This is important for Treasury Securities and SSN cases: the federal nexus is automatic.

## Why the logic matters for the steward's cases

The steward's Treasury Securities and SSN-theft matters map to §1028(a)(7) (the modern identity theft prohibition), with the "issued by the United States" nexus satisfied automatically because SSNs are federal-government identification. The structural logic points to:

1. Criminal referral package documenting the §1028(a)(7) elements
2. Civil RICO predicate for a §1964(c) action if a pattern can be shown
3. FCRA parallel claims for the credit-reporting downstream effects

The three-category structure (document / feature / means of identification) also matters for the compound-identity theory in the Hillberg case: a compound identity like HILLBERGMANN is a "means of identification" under category 3 if the name is used to identify a specific individual. Traditional category-1 thinking would miss this framing.
