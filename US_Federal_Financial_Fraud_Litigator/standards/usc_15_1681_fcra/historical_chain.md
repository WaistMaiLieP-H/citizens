# FCRA Historical Chain — 15 USC §§1681–1681x

## The wound (pre-1970)

Credit bureaus operated as closed creditor-serving networks. Consumer files were assembled,
maintained, and disclosed without consumer knowledge, access, or any right to dispute. The
consumer was the subject of the file but had no legal relationship with the bureau. Errors,
stale information, and misidentification (similar names, similar SSNs) could permanently block
consumers from credit, employment, and housing with no remedy. Bureau files were proprietary.
The consumer could not see their own file.

The injury: systematic denial of economic participation based on unverifiable, uncontestable
private information. The consumer was invisible inside the system that controlled their life.

---

## Stage 1: The 1970 promise — FCRA origin

Congress flipped the obligation. Credit bureaus became Consumer Reporting Agencies with duties
running to consumers. The core 1970 promise:
- You may see your own file (§1681g)
- We must follow reasonable procedures to be accurate (§1681e(b))
- You may dispute errors and we must reinvestigate (§1681i)
- We cannot keep adverse information forever (§1681c obsolescence)
- We can only share your file for permitted purposes (§1681b)
- If you're denied because of our report, you'll be told so (§1681m)

**The 1970 structural gap:** No furnisher duty. No identity theft mechanism. No free report
access. Reinvestigation period not specified (no 30-day clock). Largely unknown to consumers
until a denial triggered their rights.

**Consumer Credit Reporting Reform Act of 1996 (CCRAA), Pub. L. 104-208:**
- Added §1681s-2(b) furnisher duty — the third structural duty
- Specified 30-day (or 45-day) reinvestigation deadline in §1681i
- Strengthened adverse action notice requirements

---

## Stage 2: The 2003 sharpening — FACTA

By 2003, identity theft was epidemic (documented surge after ITADA 1998 awareness). The 1970
structure had no answer for the identity thief's account. Reinvestigation under §1681i depended
on the furnisher — who could claim the account was "verified" even when it was fraudulent.
Consumers were locked in a loop: dispute → furnisher verifies → tradeline stays → dispute again.

FACTA broke the loop with §1681c-2: consumer's identity theft report overrides furnisher
verification. Block first. Four business days. No furnisher veto.

FACTA also created the free annual report mechanism (§1681j) — consumers could now
self-monitor before adverse actions, not after.

**The 2003 structural gap still unresolved:** FACTA did not address standing requirements for
cases where the inaccuracy existed but no third-party disclosure had yet occurred. That question
would be answered by the courts (Spokeo 2016, TransUnion 2021).

---

## The standing evolution (judicial layer, 2016–2021)

**Spokeo (2016):** A bare procedural FCRA violation without concrete harm does not confer
Article III standing. Raised the floor for plaintiff pleading: must show the inaccuracy was
disclosed to someone and caused real harm (or realistic risk of harm).

**TransUnion (2021):** Tightened Spokeo — disclosure to a third-party subscriber is the
concrete-injury line for credit report accuracy claims. Used defamation-by-publication
analogy: the injury happens when the false report is disclosed, not when it is merely
maintained in the file. Non-disclosed class members lack standing.

**Net effect of standing evolution on Michael's cases:**
The standing decisions do not threaten Michael's claims because:
1. ChexSystems actively discloses its reports to banks when they run inquiries
2. Bank denials of account opening are documented adverse actions = concrete harm
3. Every inquiry run = a disclosure event = a standing anchor

---

## Current framework (2026)

The statute as currently operative reflects the 1970 base + 1996 CCRAA amendments + 2003
FACTA additions + 2010 Dodd-Frank (transferred enforcement to CFPB, did not alter private
rights). The judicial standing overlay from Spokeo/TransUnion applies to all FCRA claims.

**The chain:**
1970 opacity wound → FCRA accuracy/dispute structure (1970) → furnisher duty added (1996) →
identity theft block added (2003) → standing gated by concrete-injury doctrine (2016, 2021)

**The gap that remains:** FACTA's blocking mechanism only works after an identity theft report
is filed. Before filing, or where the CRA contests the block, the consumer remains exposed.
The statute has no automatic monitoring or preventive mechanism — it is reactive throughout.
