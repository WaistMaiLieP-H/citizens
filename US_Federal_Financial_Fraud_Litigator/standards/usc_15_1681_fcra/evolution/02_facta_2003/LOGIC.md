# FCRA — Evolution Stage 02: FACTA 2003
## Fair and Accurate Credit Transactions Act, Pub. L. 108-159 (2003)

**Enacted:** December 4, 2003
**Effective:** Staggered — most provisions effective June 2004 (FTC implementation)

---

## The new wound FACTA answered

By 2003, the ITADA (1998) had named identity theft as a federal crime and built the criminal law
around it. But FCRA had no corresponding civil mechanism. The gap was structural:

1. **Fraudulent tradelines persisted:** A thief opens a credit card in the victim's name, defaults,
   and the account goes to collections. The CRA reports it. The victim disputes it under §1681i.
   The CRA asks the original furnisher (the credit card company) to investigate. The furnisher has
   no incentive to admit it issued a fraudulent account — it reports "verified." The tradeline stays.

2. **No blocking right:** Pre-FACTA, a consumer's only tool was §1681i reinvestigation, which
   depended on the furnisher's cooperation. If the furnisher did not cooperate, the inaccuracy
   stayed — even if the consumer could prove identity theft.

3. **Free report access:** Consumers could not get their free report without going through a
   multi-step process. Many did not know their files existed until denied credit or employment.

4. **Specialty CRAs not understood as covered:** Medical information bureaus, check reporting
   agencies (like ChexSystems), insurance CLUE databases — all were CRAs under the original 1970
   definition, but consumers rarely knew these files existed or how to access them.

---

## The FACTA logical additions

### New structure 1 — Identity Theft Block: §1681c-2 (THE KEY ADDITION)

**Command:** A CRA must block any information in a consumer's file that the consumer identifies
as having resulted from identity theft. Block must occur within 4 business days of receiving:
- Proof of identity (ID showing the consumer is who they say)
- A copy of an identity theft report (FTC IdentityTheft.gov report or equivalent law enforcement report)
- Identification of the specific information to be blocked
- A statement that the information resulted from identity theft

**Logical move:** This breaks the furnisher-cooperation dependency. The consumer does not need
the thief's creditor to admit fraud. The consumer files an identity theft report, submits it to
the CRA, and the CRA is obligated to block — regardless of what the furnisher claims.

**Notice to furnisher:** After blocking, the CRA must promptly notify the furnisher. The furnisher
may then investigate on its own and challenge the block, but the default is block first.

**Rescission limits:** CRA may rescind a block only if: (1) the consumer misrepresented material
facts; (2) the consumer obtained goods, services, or money with the blocked information; or
(3) the consumer agreed that the information should not be blocked. Rescission requires notice.

**Application to Michael's portfolio:** The Treasury Securities identity theft (4 contradictory
government responses, bonds in Michael's name belonging to "another individual") and SSN theft
are the primary candidates. If ChexSystems entries resulted from identity-theft-related account
fraud, §1681c-2 blocking is available once Michael files an identity theft report with FTC.

---

### New structure 2 — Free Annual Report: §1681j(a)
**Command:** Each of the three nationwide CRAs must provide one free consumer report per year
upon request. Created AnnualCreditReport.com process.

**Logical move:** Removed the economic barrier to self-monitoring. Before FACTA, consumers had
to pay for their own reports.

---

### New structure 3 — Fraud Alerts: §1681c-1
**Command:** Consumer may place a 90-day initial fraud alert, a 7-year extended alert (for
identity theft victims), or an active duty alert in their file. Creditors who receive a flagged
report must use reasonable procedures to verify the consumer's identity before extending credit.

**Application:** Extended fraud alert (7 years) is available to Michael once an identity theft
report is filed. This requires every creditor receiving his file to verify identity — a structural
brake on further fraudulent account openings.

---

### New structure 4 — Truncation of account numbers: §1681c(g)
**Command:** Receipts from electronic transactions may not include more than the last 5 digits
of a card number or the expiration date. Prevents skimming from receipt data.

---

### New structure 5 — Summary of rights (§1681g amendment)
CRAs must now include a "Summary of Rights" with each report, including instructions for disputing
information and the identity theft block mechanism.

---

## What FACTA did NOT change

- The four structural duties from the 1970 statute (accuracy, reinvestigation, furnisher duty,
  permissible purposes) are unchanged in their core commands.
- §1681s-2(b) furnisher duty (added by the Consumer Credit Reporting Reform Act of 1996, not FACTA)
  remained unchanged.
- Damages structure (§1681n/§1681o) unchanged.
- The SOL under §1681p unchanged (2 years from discovery, 5 years from violation).

## Logical shift from Stage 01 to Stage 02

| Dimension | 1970 origin | After FACTA 2003 |
|---|---|---|
| Identity theft blocking | No mechanism | §1681c-2: CRA blocks within 4 days on identity theft report |
| Free report access | Paid or adversity-triggered | One free annual report per nationwide CRA |
| Fraud alert | None | 90-day, 7-year, active duty tiers |
| Furnisher power over disputed identity theft | Determinative — furnisher verifies = stays | Consumer identity theft report overrides; block first |

## Why this stage matters for Michael's cases

FACTA § 1681c-2 is the most powerful tool in Michael's FCRA arsenal because it does not depend
on the cooperation of whoever reported the fraudulent data. The Treasury SSN theft and ChexSystems
fraud entries can be blocked by filing an identity theft report with the FTC and submitting it
to the relevant CRA. The CRA must block within 4 business days. Any subsequent failure to block,
wrongful rescission, or continued reporting after block = §1681n willful violation.
