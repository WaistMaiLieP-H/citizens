# Logical Diff: Stage 01 (1970) → Stage 02 (FACTA 2003)

## What the 1970 structure could not do

**Problem:** Fraudulent tradeline opened by identity thief. Consumer disputes under §1681i.
CRA asks furnisher. Furnisher says "verified." No block available. Tradeline stays.

**The dependency gap:** The 1970 reinvestigation mechanism could only succeed if the furnisher
admitted error or could not produce documentation. A furnisher that actively maintained the
fraud (or simply refused to engage) could defeat any §1681i dispute.

---

## What FACTA added to break the dependency

**§1681c-2 identity theft block:** Consumer submits identity theft report → CRA blocks
within 4 business days, regardless of what the furnisher claims.

**Key logical move:** Consumer's sworn identity theft report is sufficient — the furnisher
no longer has veto power over the accuracy of what appears in the consumer's file when
identity theft is the claim.

---

## What stayed the same

The four structural duties are unchanged:
- §1681e(b) accuracy: same
- §1681i reinvestigation: same (30-day deadline added in 1996, not altered by FACTA)
- §1681s-2(b) furnisher duty: same (added 1996, not altered by FACTA)
- §1681n/§1681o damages: same

FACTA adds identity theft-specific tools on top of the existing structure — it does not
replace or modify the base duties.

---

## Operational consequence for Michael's cases

| Claim type | Pre-FACTA path | Post-FACTA path |
|---|---|---|
| ChexSystems inaccuracy (non-ID-theft) | §1681i dispute → CRA reinvestigates | Same path (no change) |
| ChexSystems entry from identity theft | §1681i dispute → furnisher verifies → stays | §1681c-2 block → CRA must block within 4 days |
| Treasury SSN theft downstream CRA reporting | No blocking mechanism | §1681c-2 block on FTC identity theft report |
| Fraud alerts | None | 7-year extended fraud alert available |
