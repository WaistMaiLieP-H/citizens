# Safeco Insurance Co. of America v. Burr
## 551 US 47 (2007)

**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** June 4, 2007
**Vote:** 9-0 (unanimous on core willfulness holding)

---

## Issue decided

What does "willfully" mean under §1681n? Does willful require intentional/knowing violation,
or does it extend to reckless disregard?

---

## Holding

**Willful** under §1681n encompasses both:
1. Intentional violations (knowing the conduct is unlawful), and
2. Reckless disregard of a statutory duty.

**Recklessness defined:** A company acts in reckless disregard of FCRA requirements if it
acts under an objectively unreasonable reading of the statute's requirements — that is, if
the company's interpretation of the law "is not just clearly wrong, but objectively unreasonable
in light of the statute's plain language and authoritative guidance."

**Negligent is not willful:** Mere negligence — acting under a reading that was wrong but
not objectively unreasonable — triggers §1681o (actual damages only), not §1681n.

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Willfulness is achievable against institutional defendants.** Safeco establishes that a
CRA or furnisher that has an established policy or practice that is objectively unreasonable
under FCRA's plain text can be found to have willfully violated the statute — even without
proving the company's employees consciously knew they were violating the law.

**ChexSystems willfulness theory:** If ChexSystems has a policy of:
- Refusing to delete entries after dispute without meeting the §1681i standard
- Maintaining entries it cannot verify because deletion is economically costly
- Treating identity theft blocking requests as not triggering §1681c-2

...any of these policies, if objectively unreasonable under the statute's plain command,
constitutes willful violation. Statutory damages of $100–$1,000 per violation + punitive
damages become available.

**Institutional pattern evidence:** Safeco's recklessness standard opens the door to
discovery of the defendant's internal policies, training materials, and institutional
dispute-handling practices — evidence of pattern willfulness supports punitive award.

---

## Downstream effect

Safeco remains the controlling standard for §1681n willfulness as of 2026. Courts apply
the "objectively unreasonable" test uniformly. The case makes FCRA claims against large
CRAs with standardized (but FCRA-noncompliant) policies far more viable than if willful
required proof of actual knowledge of illegality.
