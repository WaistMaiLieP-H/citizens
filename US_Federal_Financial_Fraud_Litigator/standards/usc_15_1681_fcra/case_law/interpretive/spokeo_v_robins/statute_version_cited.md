# Statute Version at Time of Spokeo v. Robins (2016)

**FCRA version:** 15 USC §§1681–1681x as amended through Dodd-Frank 2010

The Court addressed §1681e(b) (accuracy duty) and the §1681n damages provisions as they existed
in the post-Dodd-Frank consolidated statute.

**The constitutional provision at issue** was not FCRA's text per se but Article III of the
Constitution's "case or controversy" standing requirement. Spokeo does not interpret or limit
FCRA's statutory private right of action — it interprets the constitutional floor that any
plaintiff must clear, regardless of what the statute says.

**Effect on current statute:** FCRA's text is unchanged by Spokeo. The private right of action
under §1681n/§1681o remains intact. Spokeo limits WHO can bring that action — only plaintiffs
who have suffered concrete harm, not merely a bare procedural violation.

**Post-Spokeo FCRA landscape:** TransUnion v. Ramirez (2021) tightened the standing requirement
further (see that case entry). Together they establish that the concrete-injury gate must be
cleared for each class member individually, not just the named plaintiff.
