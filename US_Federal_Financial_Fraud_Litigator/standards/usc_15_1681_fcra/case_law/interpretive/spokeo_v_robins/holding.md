# Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins
## 578 US 330 (2016)

**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** May 16, 2016
**Vote:** 6-2 (Ginsburg and Sotomayor dissenting)
**On remand from:** Ninth Circuit (reversed and remanded; Ninth Circuit later found standing on remand)

---

## Issue decided

Does the violation of a statutory right under FCRA, standing alone without any concrete harm,
satisfy Article III's injury-in-fact requirement?

---

## Holding

**No.** A bare procedural violation of FCRA — one divorced from any concrete harm — does not
automatically satisfy Article III standing's injury-in-fact requirement.

**The two-element test:** Injury-in-fact requires both:
1. **Concrete** (real, not abstract; not merely a technical statutory violation), AND
2. **Particularized** (affecting the plaintiff specifically, not a generalized grievance)

**Key clarification:** Congress can elevate intangible harms to legally cognizable injuries by
enacting statutes like FCRA, but Congress cannot invent an injury where none exists. A risk of
real harm is sufficient, but the harm must be real — not just "a risk of harm of an entirely
made-up risk."

**Spokeo's facts:** Spokeo published an inaccurate "people profile" about Robins — wrong age,
marital status, education, employment. The Court remanded for the Ninth Circuit to determine
whether the specific inaccuracies (wrong zip code, marital status) created any concrete risk of
harm (e.g., affected employability), or whether they were "mere procedural violations" with no
real-world consequence.

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Spokeo is a standing gate, not a merits gate.** It does not require a dollar figure of harm.
It requires the inaccuracy to have been used — or to create a realistic risk of use — in a way
that affects the consumer.

**Strong standing facts:**
- ChexSystems reported adverse data → bank denied account opening → Michael could not access
  banking services. This is concrete harm. Spokeo does not threaten standing.
- CRA report disclosed to employer → employment denied → economic harm. Concrete.
- Inaccurate file accessible to subscribers who pull it → realistic risk of downstream use.
  The Ninth Circuit on remand found this sufficient.

**Weak standing facts (be aware):**
- Inaccuracy exists in file but was never disclosed to any third party and no denial resulted.
  This is the Spokeo problem. Must document every disclosure and every adverse action.

**Practical requirement:** For each ChexSystems and credit report inaccuracy, document:
1. The specific inaccuracy
2. Who pulled the report (which bank, employer, or creditor)
3. What adverse action resulted (account denial, credit denial, employment denial)

This chain converts a bare procedural FCRA violation into a concrete injury that survives Spokeo.
