# Statute Version at Time of TransUnion v. Ramirez (2021)

**FCRA version:** 15 USC §§1681–1681x (post-Dodd-Frank consolidated, unchanged as to the
sections at issue from FACTA 2003 version)

The specific FCRA sections before the Court:
- §1681e(b): accuracy duty — the basis for the inaccuracy claim (OFAC name-match overlay)
- §1681i: reinvestigation — TransUnion failed to correct after notice
- §1681n: willful violation damages — the class sought statutory + punitive damages

**The constitutional limitation:** Like Spokeo, TransUnion does not modify FCRA's text. It
imposes an Article III overlay — Congress cannot grant standing by statute to plaintiffs who
have not suffered a concrete injury as defined under constitutional case-or-controversy doctrine.

**Effect on individual plaintiffs (vs. class action):** TransUnion's standing analysis was
most consequential for the class action context — the 6,332 non-disclosed class members lost
standing as a group. For an individual plaintiff like Michael, who can document specific
ChexSystems disclosures and specific account-opening denials, TransUnion does not present
a standing barrier. The named plaintiff in Ramirez had standing throughout.

**Current operative law:** As of 2026, Spokeo + TransUnion together form the standing framework
for FCRA claims. Both decisions remain good law.
