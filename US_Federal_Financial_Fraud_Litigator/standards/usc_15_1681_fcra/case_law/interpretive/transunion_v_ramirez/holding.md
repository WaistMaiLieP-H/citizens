# TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez
## 594 US 413 (2021)

**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** June 25, 2021
**Vote:** 5-4 (Thomas, Breyer, Sotomayor, Kagan dissenting)

---

## Issue decided

In a class action under FCRA, do all class members have Article III standing when (1) TransUnion
incorrectly labeled 8,185 class members as potential terrorists/drug traffickers on an OFAC alert
overlay in their credit files, but (2) only 1,853 of those class members had their inaccurate
files actually disclosed to third-party businesses?

---

## Holding

**No.** Only the 1,853 class members whose reports were actually disclosed to third-party
businesses suffered concrete harm sufficient for Article III standing. The remaining 6,332
class members — whose inaccurate files existed in TransUnion's database but were never sent
to anyone — lacked standing.

**Key rule:** "An injury in law is not an injury in fact. Only those plaintiffs who have been
concretely harmed by a defendant's statutory violation may sue that private defendant over that
violation in federal court." 594 US at 426.

**The line:** A credit file with an inaccuracy = potential harm only. A credit file with an
inaccuracy that was actually disclosed to a third-party creditor, employer, or landlord = concrete
harm (analogous to common-law defamation by publication, which requires publication to a third party).

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**TransUnion refines and tightens Spokeo.** The downstream-disclosure requirement is now explicit:
inaccuracy in file + actual disclosure to third party = standing. Inaccuracy in file, never
disclosed = no standing in federal court.

**Strong standing facts for Michael:**
- ChexSystems is a specialty CRA whose entire business is disclosing its files to subscribing
  banks. Every time a bank ran a ChexSystems inquiry on Michael — when he applied to open a
  checking or savings account and was denied — that is a disclosure to a third party. Standing.
- Credit reports disclosed to creditors, employers, or landlords who then took adverse action:
  standing (and adverse action notice claim under §1681m).

**Documentation requirement TransUnion creates:**
For every inaccuracy claimed, Michael must be able to document:
1. The specific inaccuracy
2. The date(s) ChexSystems or other CRA disclosed the report to a subscriber
3. The subscriber (bank name or creditor name)
4. The adverse action taken (account denial, credit denial, etc.)

This documentation converts abstract injury into the concrete-injury standard TransUnion requires.

**ChexSystems report request strategy:** File a §1681g request for full ChexSystems file +
complete disclosure log showing every subscriber who pulled his report and when. This disclosure
log is the standing evidence.

---

## Operational note

TransUnion's majority opinion explicitly uses defamation-by-publication as the historical
analogue for credit report disclosure. A credit report disclosed to a third party has the same
legal injury structure as a defamatory statement published to a third party — both require
publication/disclosure to someone other than the plaintiff to constitute the actionable harm.
This analogy is useful in briefing: the FCRA plaintiff is the equivalent of a defamation plaintiff
asserting publication to a third party.
