# Evolution Stage 03 — Doe v. Chao (2004)
## 540 U.S. 614 (2004) — "Actual Damages" Requirement Clarified

### THE WOUND

From 1975 to 2004, federal circuits split on whether a plaintiff bringing a §552a(g)(1)(C)
claim (failure to maintain accurate records causing adverse determination) needed to prove
specific actual monetary damages, or whether the adverse determination itself — the loss
of a benefit, privilege, or right — was sufficient to establish damages.

The Fourth Circuit and others held the adverse determination alone sufficed; the fact
of wrongful denial was the injury. The D.C. Circuit held specific monetary loss must be
shown. Courts could not agree on what Congress meant by "actual damages" in §552a(g)(4).

This uncertainty meant the Privacy Act functioned differently across circuits: a denied
Social Security claimant in Virginia (Fourth Circuit) could recover on the wrongful denial
alone; the same claimant in D.C. had to prove specific dollar amounts.

### HOLDING

The Supreme Court resolved the split in Doe v. Chao, 540 U.S. 614 (2004):

A plaintiff bringing a §552a(g)(1)(C) claim must prove **actual damages** to recover.
The adverse determination is not itself sufficient — the plaintiff must show monetary harm
caused by the violation.

**What this means:**
- An individual who suffered a wrongful denial of benefits based on false agency records
  must show the dollar value of the lost benefits (or other concrete harm) to recover under
  §(g)(1)(C)
- The adverse determination is the gateway — it proves the violation harmed the plaintiff —
  but the plaintiff still must establish the damages amount

**What the Court did NOT decide:**
- Whether mental or emotional distress alone constitutes "actual damages" (circuit split continues)
- Whether the $1,000 minimum in §(g)(1)(D) requires no actual damage proof (general agreement:
  §(g)(1)(D) provides a floor without requiring proven monetary harm if willfulness shown)

### LOGICAL DELTA

| Element | Pre-Doe v. Chao | Post-Doe v. Chao |
|---------|-----------------|------------------|
| §(g)(1)(C) damages | Circuit split: adverse determination alone (4th Cir.) vs. monetary proof required (D.C. Cir.) | Resolved: must prove actual damages |
| Emotional distress | Some courts allowed as "actual damages" | Still unsettled; circuits continue to diverge |
| §(g)(1)(D) floor | $1,000 minimum | Unchanged; willfulness + privacy invasion = $1,000 minimum without needing to prove monetary harm |

### STRATEGIC SIGNIFICANCE FOR SSA CASE

Under Doe v. Chao:
- The denied disability payments ARE the actual damages — SSA's false contact records caused
  denial → denial = loss of SSI/SSDI payments → those payments are measurable actual damages
- No need to rely on emotional distress theory (though it may supplement)
- Calculate: monthly benefit amount × months of wrongful denial = actual damages base
- §552a(g)(4) provides attorneys' fees and costs on top of actual damages when plaintiff prevails

The SSA case is stronger under Doe v. Chao's framework than the pre-Chao adverse-determination-
alone theory — because the specific lost benefit payments are computable and directly causally
linked to the false contact records.
