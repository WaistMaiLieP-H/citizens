# Evolution Stage 02 — Computer Matching and Privacy Protection Act of 1988
## Pub. L. 100-503, 102 Stat. 2507

### THE WOUND

Between 1975 and 1988, federal agencies increasingly used computer matching — the bulk
comparison of two or more systems of records to identify individuals appearing in multiple
databases for program integrity purposes. HHS matched welfare rolls against Social Security
earnings records. DOD matched employee databases against welfare databases. OPM matched
federal employment records against benefit rolls.

These matches operated entirely outside the 1974 Act's transparency and due process
protections. A match that "found" an individual in two conflicting systems could trigger
adverse action — suspension of benefits, termination of employment — before the individual
had any opportunity to challenge the underlying data or the matching methodology.

A Government Accountability Office study found over 2,000 computer matching programs
operating by 1986. None required advance notice to individuals. Many used data
comparisons that, if challenged, would reveal the underlying records as inaccurate
(different SSNs, name variants, data entry errors across systems).

### DESIGN RESPONSE

The 1988 amendments added a new subchapter: the Computer Matching and Privacy Protection
Act (CMPPA), codified at §552a(o) through §552a(u).

**Key additions:**

§552a(o) — **Matching Agreements:** Agencies must execute written matching agreements
before conducting computer matches. Agreements must specify: purpose, legal authority,
justification, records to be used, procedures for notifying matched individuals, and
procedures for verifying matches before adverse action.

§552a(p) — **Verification:** Before adverse action based on a computer match, the agency
must independently verify the match and provide the individual with notice and opportunity
to respond. 30-day response period required before action can be taken.

§552a(u) — **Data Integrity Boards:** Each agency with a matching program must establish
a Data Integrity Board to oversee matching agreements and review program compliance.

### LOGICAL DELTA

| Element | Pre-1988 | Post-1988 |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Computer matching | Entirely unregulated | Requires written agreement + verification |
| Adverse action from match | Immediate, no notice | 30-day notice + opportunity to respond |
| Oversight | None | Data Integrity Boards |
| Individual remedy | §(g) civil action | §(g) civil action + §(p) pre-action verification right |

### APPLICATION TO SSA/DDS PATTERN

The SSA/DDS phantom contact records are not computer matching records — they are
records individually entered by DDS examiners. However, the 1988 CMPPA amendments
established the principle that **adverse determinations based on data that has not been
independently verified violate the Act's design.** This principle reinforces the
§(g)(1)(C) claim: DDS using unverified phantom contact entries as the basis for
denial = failure to maintain accurate records + adverse determination.
