# Quinn v. Stone — Holding

**Citation:** Quinn v. Stone, No. 90-0412, 1991 WL 236996 (D.D.C. Oct. 31, 1991)
**Court:** U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia
**Decided:** 1991
**Status:** District court — persuasive, not binding; cited for "intentional or willful" standard formulation

## NOTE ON CITATION

This entry documents the "intentional or willful" standard as articulated in Quinn v. Stone
and the line of cases applying it. The Albright v. United States formulation (Albright v.
United States, 631 F.2d 915 (D.C. Cir. 1980)) is the controlling circuit-level articulation
and should be the primary citation. Quinn v. Stone is included here as a secondary reference
to the standard's application in the D.C. district court context.

The more authoritative statement is Albright: "intentional or willful" means the agency
acted with knowledge that its action was wrongful or with reckless disregard for whether
the action was wrongful — not merely negligent, not merely mistaken.

## Standard Distilled from Albright v. United States, 631 F.2d 915 (D.C. Cir. 1980)

"Intentional or willful" under §552a(g)(1)(D):
1. Agency knew the act was wrongful — had actual knowledge that the disclosure,
   use, or maintenance of records violated the Privacy Act, and proceeded anyway; OR
2. Agency acted with reckless disregard for whether the act was wrongful — did not
   investigate, ignored obvious Privacy Act implications, proceeded without regard for
   the legal constraint

**NOT sufficient:**
- Negligent mistake — incorrect data entered without knowing it was wrong
- Clerical error — transcription error, system glitch
- Good faith reliance on an exception that doesn't apply

## Application to SSA/DDS Pattern

If DDS examiners entered contact dates knowing no contact occurred — or if supervisors
entered contacts to paper the file without any actual contact attempt — the conduct
satisfies the intentional prong: the examiner knew no contact was made and entered
a false date anyway.

If the false entries resulted from a systematic practice of papering files without
actual contact attempts (pattern within DDS), the reckless disregard prong is available:
DDS policy required contact attempts; examiners routinely skipped attempts and entered
false dates; supervisors knew of the practice; the recklessness is institutional.

Either path supports the §552a(g)(1)(D) $1,000 minimum floor + fees + costs.

## Significance

The Albright/Quinn standard matters because it determines whether the plaintiff must
show the agency specifically targeted them, or merely that the agency's conduct was
institutionally reckless. In cases involving systematic false documentation (not
isolated clerical error), the reckless-disregard prong is typically easier to establish.
