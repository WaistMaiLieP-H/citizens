# 18 USC §§1961-1968 — Civil RICO: Current Logic

## The civil RICO architecture

RICO (Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act) was enacted primarily as a criminal
statute but contains one of the most powerful private civil remedies in federal law: §1964(c)'s
treble damages and mandatory attorney fees. For a civil plaintiff, RICO converts ordinary fraud
claims into a federal case with triple the actual damages and shifts attorney fees.

## The four elements of a §1964(c) civil RICO claim

**1. A "person"** (any individual or entity) engaged in:

**2. A "pattern of racketeering activity"** — at minimum two predicate acts from §1961(1)'s list,
committed within 10 years of each other, that are related and continuous (H.J. Inc. test):
- **Related:** Acts have the same or similar purposes, results, participants, victims, or methods
- **Continuous:** Either a closed-ended pattern (multiple acts over substantial time period) or
  open-ended pattern (acts that by their nature project into the future)

**3. Through the conduct of:**

**4. An "enterprise"** — any individual, partnership, corporation, association, or other legal
entity, or any union, group of individuals associated in fact (Boyle v. United States, 2009: an
associated-in-fact enterprise need not have any formal structure; a group acting together for a
common purpose suffices).

## Predicate acts relevant to Michael's portfolio

§1961(1)(B) lists all federal criminal offenses punishable by imprisonment for more than one year
that qualify as racketeering acts. The most important:
- **18 USC §1343 (wire fraud)** — listed directly; each interstate electronic communication in
  furtherance of a scheme is a separate predicate act
- **18 USC §1341 (mail fraud)** — listed directly; each mailing is a separate act
- **18 USC §1028 (identity theft)** — listed directly in §1961(1)(B) (added 2002)
- **18 USC §1028A** — listed in §1961(1)(B) (added 2004)
- **18 USC §1344 (bank fraud)** — listed directly

For Michael's portfolio: **two §1343 wire fraud acts** (each email/electronic transmission in the
Treasury fraud, SIM swap, crypto scheme, or Hillberg compound identity operation) constitute a
two-act RICO pattern, provided they satisfy the H.J. Inc. relatedness + continuity test.

## The "enterprise" requirement (Reves' operation-or-management test)

It is not enough that an enterprise exists and the predicate acts were committed in connection with
it. Under **Reves v. Ernst & Young** (1993), a defendant must have **conducted or participated in
the conduct of the enterprise's affairs** through the pattern of racketeering.

The "operation or management" test: the defendant must have had some part in directing the
enterprise's affairs — not merely performing services for the enterprise.

**Application:** If the same actors committed the identity theft, the wire fraud, the SIM swap,
and the Hillberg account manipulation, they were conducting the enterprise's affairs, not providing
peripheral services. The enterprise is the associated-in-fact group coordinating these activities.

## §1964(c) civil remedy

**Private right of action:** Any person injured in their business or property by reason of a §1962
violation may sue in federal district court.

**Treble damages:** Automatic tripling of actual damages. Not discretionary. If actual damages =
$1M (crypto + Treasury + equity + surgery out-of-pocket), civil RICO recovery = $3M.

**Mandatory attorney fees:** "including a reasonable attorney's fee." No loser-pays discretion;
fees are mandatory on a successful RICO plaintiff.

**Standing requirement:** Injury "in business or property" — must be concrete economic loss.
Personal injury alone is not sufficient for RICO standing. Michael's cases satisfy this: financial
losses are property injuries (theft of money, deprivation of equity, interference with banking
access, crypto loss, surgery cost).

## The proximate cause requirement — Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp. (1992)

RICO requires proximate cause between the racketeering activity and the plaintiff's injury. Direct
injury = strong RICO claim. Indirect injury = weaker (standing issues). Michael as the direct
target of identity theft, wire fraud, and account takeover = direct injury. No proximate cause
barrier.

## Pattern — the H.J. Inc. test in detail

**H.J. Inc. v. Northwestern Bell Telephone Co.** (1989): "Pattern" requires more than two predicate
acts. Requires relatedness AND continuity.

**Closed-ended continuity:** A series of related predicate acts extending over a "substantial
period of time." Courts have found 1-2 months insufficient; 1+ years is typically sufficient.

**Open-ended continuity:** A shorter time period, but the predicate acts by their nature threaten
future similar conduct (an ongoing criminal enterprise that would continue but for the lawsuit).

**For Michael's cases:**
- The SIM swap, identity theft, and financial fraud pattern appears to span at least 2018–2022 (4+
  years) — well within closed-ended continuity
- The enterprise continues: blocked banking access, Treasury dispute ongoing, crypto losses unresolved
- Open-ended continuity argument: the enterprise has not been dismantled; it continues to impact
  Michael's access to financial services

## Association-in-fact enterprise (Boyle v. United States, 2009)

An enterprise does not need organizational formality. A group of individuals acting together for a
common criminal purpose — even without defined roles, hierarchy, or rules — is an enterprise.

For Michael's cases: if Ryan McClaran, Christina Cerretani, family members, and associated actors
acted together across the SIM swap, identity theft, financial fraud, and legal-system manipulation
— they formed an associated-in-fact enterprise. Each actor need not have committed all predicate
acts; they need only have participated in the enterprise's affairs.

## RICO vs. ordinary fraud: when to use it

**Use civil RICO when:**
- Multiple predicate acts across a period of years
- The same actors appear across multiple fraud schemes
- You need treble damages to make federal litigation economically viable
- You need mandatory attorney fees (cannot find contingency counsel otherwise)
- The pattern of conduct ties together claims that would otherwise be fragmented

**Civil RICO limitations to address:**
- 4-year SOL (Agency Holding Corp. v. Malley-Duff, 1987): runs from when plaintiff discovered
  or reasonably should have discovered the RICO injury
- Proximate cause requirement (Holmes)
- Operation-or-management test (Reves) — must show defendants directed the enterprise
- Pattern requires more than isolated acts
- RICO does not compensate personal injury (only business/property injury)
