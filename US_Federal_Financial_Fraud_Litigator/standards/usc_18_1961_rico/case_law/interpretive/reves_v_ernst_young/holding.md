# Reves v. Ernst & Young — Holding (Operation-or-Management Test)

**Citation:** 507 US 170 (1993)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** February 24, 1993
**Author:** Blackmun, J. (7-2)

## Facts

An Arkansas cooperative sold notes to investors. Ernst & Young (then Arthur Young) audited the
cooperative and allegedly produced false audit reports that concealed the cooperative's insolvency,
enabling it to continue selling notes and defrauding investors. When the cooperative failed,
investors sued Ernst & Young under civil RICO §1964(c), alleging E&Y's false audit reports were
predicate wire fraud acts and that E&Y "conducted" the enterprise (the cooperative) through the
pattern of racketeering.

## Holding

**The "operation or management" test:** A defendant "conducts" or "participates" in the conduct
of an enterprise's affairs under §1962(c) only if the defendant played **some part in directing
those affairs** — not merely performing services for the enterprise.

**Ernst & Young did not "conduct" the cooperative's affairs.** Ernst & Young was an outside
accounting firm providing professional services. Even though it knew about the fraud and its
false audit reports enabled it, it was not directing the enterprise — it was serving it.

**The rule:** §1962(c) requires the defendant to have had "some degree of direction over the
enterprise's affairs." Facilitating, enabling, or even knowingly assisting the enterprise is
not sufficient. The defendant must be in the position of directing the criminal enterprise
or participating in its management, not just providing services to it.

## Rule distilled

**Operation-or-management test:**
- Sufficient: Enterprise officers/directors, managers, co-conspirators who coordinate the scheme,
  associates who run specific arms of the operation
- Insufficient: Outside service providers (lawyers, accountants, consultants) who facilitate but
  do not direct; employees who perform specific tasks under direction without managing the enterprise

**The question to resolve:** Were there any defendants who should be excluded from §1962(c) under

## Note on §1962(a) and §1962(b) alternatives

Reves applies primarily to §1962(c) ("conduct or participate"). §1962(a) (investing racketeering
proceeds into an enterprise) and §1962(b) (acquiring an enterprise through racketeering) have
different language and different standards. If the Reves operation-or-management test is a barrier
for some defendants under §1962(c), consider §1962(a) or §1962(d) (conspiracy to violate RICO —
which has a lower threshold for participants who agreed to the scheme even if they did not personally
direct it).
