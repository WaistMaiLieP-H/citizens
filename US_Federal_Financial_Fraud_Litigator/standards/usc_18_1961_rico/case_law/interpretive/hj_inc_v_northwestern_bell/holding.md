# H.J. Inc. v. Northwestern Bell Telephone Co. — Holding

**Citation:** 492 US 229 (1989)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** June 26, 1989
**Author:** Brennan, J. (8-1 in result; 5-4 on rationale)

## Facts

Northwestern Bell telephone customers alleged the telephone company had bribed members of the
Minnesota Public Utilities Commission over several years to obtain favorable rate decisions.
Each bribe was a predicate act (bribery under state law, listed in §1961(1)(A)). The Eighth Circuit
dismissed, holding the predicate acts did not constitute a "pattern" because they were part of a
single scheme and not separate episodes.

## Holding

The Supreme Court reversed and established the "pattern" standard:

**Pattern of racketeering activity requires:**
1. **Relatedness:** The predicate acts must be related — sharing "the same or similar purposes,
   results, participants, victims, or methods of commission, or [be] otherwise interrelated by
   distinguishing characteristics."
2. **Continuity:** The predicate acts must amount to or pose a threat of continued criminal activity.
   Two forms:
   - **Closed-ended continuity:** A series of related predicate acts extending over a "substantial
     period of time." The Court declined to define "substantial" precisely but noted it is a
     requirement — short-term fraud with two acts does not satisfy it.
   - **Open-ended continuity:** Acts that, by their nature, project into the future with a threat
     of repetition, OR conduct that is a regular way of doing business by the enterprise.

**Two predicate acts are necessary but not sufficient.** "While two acts are necessary, they may
not be sufficient."

## Rule distilled

**Relatedness + Continuity test:**
- Related: same/similar purpose, victim, method, participants
- Continuous: substantial closed-ended period OR open-ended threat of future conduct

**Single scheme / single victim / short period:** Can still satisfy pattern if the other
elements are present (open-ended threat; nature of enterprise; regular business practice).

## Significance for Michael's portfolio

**Relatedness: satisfied.** The predicate acts across Michael's cases share the same victim
(Michael Hartmann), the same participants (Ryan McClaran + associated actors), the same method
(electronic communications to commit identity theft and wire fraud), and the same purpose
(systematic extraction of Michael's financial assets and suppression of his recovery).

**Continuity: satisfied both ways.**
- Closed-ended: 2018 (SIM swap) through 2022 (SIRVA discovery) = 4-year closed period
- Open-ended: ChexSystems denial + Treasury dispute ongoing = enterprise continues

The H.J. Inc. test is met on Michael's facts as described. The strongest response to a pattern
challenge is the multi-year timeline and the fact that the predicate acts were the enterprise's
regular method of operation (ongoing surveillance, SIM maintenance, coordinated account access).
