# Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co., Inc. — Holding

**Citation:** 473 US 479 (1985)
**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** July 1, 1985
**Author:** White, J. (5-4)

## Facts

Sedima and Imrex had a business relationship. Sedima alleged Imrex had committed mail and wire
fraud (predicate acts) in connection with their joint venture. Sedima brought a civil §1964(c)
RICO action. The Second Circuit held: no prior criminal conviction of the predicate acts means
no civil RICO; and the injury must be a "racketeering injury" — a harm peculiar to organized
crime's corrupting influence, not ordinary business fraud losses.

## Holding

The Supreme Court reversed both limitations:

**1. No prior criminal conviction required.** §1964(c) does not by its terms require a prior
criminal conviction for the predicate acts. The civil proceeding can establish the predicate acts
as part of the civil RICO case.

**2. No "racketeering injury" requirement.** The "injured in his business or property by reason
of a violation of section 1962" language requires only that the injury be to business or property
caused by the §1962 violation — not a distinctive organized-crime-type injury.

**3. Scope:** "RICO is to be read broadly" and "liberally construed to effectuate its remedial
purposes." If the statute has expanded beyond organized crime, Congress has the tools to narrow it.

## Rule distilled

Civil §1964(c) RICO is available:
- Without any prior criminal conviction of the predicate acts
- For any injury to business or property
- In any context — not limited to organized crime or infiltration of legitimate business
- Brought before any criminal proceeding or in the absence of any criminal case

## Significance for Michael's portfolio

Sedima removes the two most common defenses raised against civil RICO:
1. "You need a criminal conviction first" → False after Sedima
2. "This isn't really organized crime, just ordinary fraud" → Irrelevant after Sedima

The civil RICO case stands on its own against any defendant whose pattern of predicate acts
caused injury to Michael's business or property, regardless of whether the FBI has investigated
or criminal charges have been filed.
