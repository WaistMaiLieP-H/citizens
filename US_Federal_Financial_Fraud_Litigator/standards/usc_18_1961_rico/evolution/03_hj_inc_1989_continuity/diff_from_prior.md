# Civil RICO — Diff: Pre-H.J. Inc. vs. Post-H.J. Inc.

## Textual change: NONE
The statute was not amended. H.J. Inc. is a judicial interpretation of the word "pattern."

## Logical architecture change

| Dimension | Post-Sedima (1985-1988) | Post-H.J. Inc. (1989-present) |
|---|---|---|
| Two acts sufficient for pattern? | Circuit split — some said yes | No — two acts necessary but not sufficient |
| Relatedness requirement | Implicit or contested | Explicit — acts must be related by purpose/method/victim/participants |
| Continuity requirement | Contested | Explicit — closed-ended (substantial period) or open-ended (ongoing threat) |
| Single isolated scheme | Some courts excluded | May or may not satisfy — depends on continuity analysis |
| Pattern standard | Unresolved after Sedima | Resolved: relatedness + continuity (flexible, case-by-case) |

## What did NOT change after H.J. Inc.

1. §1964(c) private right confirmed by Sedima — still fully available
2. No prior criminal conviction required — still not required
3. No organized-crime requirement — still not required
4. Treble damages — still automatic on successful RICO claim
5. Mandatory attorney fees — still mandatory
6. Predicate act list — still the same §1961(1) list

## Operational implication

H.J. Inc. raises the floor for RICO claims (can't win with two isolated acts) but does not
eliminate civil RICO for sustained fraud patterns. For Michael's cases where the predicate
acts span 4+ years with the same victim, same actors, and same methods, the H.J. Inc.
standard is met: related (same purpose, victim, method) + continuous (4-year closed period
+ ongoing open-ended threat from ChexSystems denial and unresolved Treasury dispute).
