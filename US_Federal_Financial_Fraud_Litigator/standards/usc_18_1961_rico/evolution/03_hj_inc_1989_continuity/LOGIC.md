# Civil RICO — H.J. Inc. (1989): Pattern = Relatedness + Continuity

## The wound this decision answered

Post-Sedima, defendants argued that "pattern of racketeering activity" should require more than
two predicate acts — otherwise any fraud involving two communications (two emails, two phone calls)
would automatically satisfy the pattern requirement, converting every ordinary fraud into a RICO
claim. Plaintiffs countered that Congress said "pattern" means "at least two acts" — nothing more.

Lower courts were all over the map. Some required the predicate acts to constitute separate
criminal schemes. Others required them to form a "nexus." The circuit split was severe.

## The H.J. Inc. holding: relatedness + continuity

**H.J. Inc. v. Northwestern Bell Telephone Co., 492 US 229 (1989):**

A "pattern" requires:
1. **Relatedness:** The predicate acts must have the same or similar purposes, results,
   participants, victims, or methods of commission, OR be otherwise interrelated by distinguishing
   characteristics, AND not be isolated events.

2. **Continuity:** Either:
   - **Closed-ended:** A closed period of repeated conduct over a "substantial" time period
     (roughly 1+ years; courts have rejected 1-2 months as insufficient)
   - **Open-ended:** Acts that by their nature project into the future (ongoing criminal enterprise
     that will continue unless stopped; need not be long-running if the threat is inherent)

## The practical framework for Michael's cases

**Relatedness:** The predicate acts are related if they target the same victim (Michael), use the
same method (identity theft + wire fraud + financial account manipulation), have the same purpose
(systematic extraction of Michael's financial assets and blocking of his economic recovery), and
involve overlapping participants (Ryan McClaran + associated actors across multiple schemes).

**Closed-ended continuity:** If the pattern of fraud runs from approximately 2018 (SIM swap onset)
through 2022 (crypto/SIRVA/spine surgery/Treasury disputes), that is a 4-year closed-ended pattern
— well above the "substantial period" threshold.

**Open-ended continuity:** The enterprise continues to impact Michael through the ongoing ChexSystems
banking denial, the unresolved Treasury dispute, and the continued blocking of financial access.
The acts threaten continued criminal conduct.

## What H.J. Inc. did NOT resolve

The Court acknowledged that "pattern" is a concept that "is not self-defining" and requires
case-by-case development. The test is flexible, not mathematical. This creates both opportunity
(a flexible test that good facts can satisfy) and risk (defendants can always argue no pattern).

## Diff from prior stage (post-Sedima, pre-H.J. Inc.)

| Dimension | Pre-H.J. Inc. | Post-H.J. Inc. |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern requirement | Circuit split; some courts said two acts = pattern | Two acts + relatedness + continuity |
| Short-duration fraud | Potentially RICO | Not RICO if no substantial continuity or open-ended threat |
| Long-duration fraud | Unclear | Closed-ended continuity: yes, if substantial period |
| Ongoing enterprise | Unclear | Open-ended continuity: yes, if nature of acts threatens future conduct |
| Single scheme | Some circuits: excluded | Not categorically excluded; continuity determines |
