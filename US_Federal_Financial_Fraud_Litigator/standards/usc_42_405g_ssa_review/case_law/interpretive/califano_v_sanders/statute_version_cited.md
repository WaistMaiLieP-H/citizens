# Statute Version Cited — Califano v. Sanders

**Case:** Califano v. Sanders, 430 U.S. 99 (1977)
**Decided:** 1977
**Statutes interpreted:** 42 U.S.C. §405(g), 42 U.S.C. §405(h), 5 U.S.C. §702 (APA general review)

---

## Version of §405(g) in Effect at Decision Date

**Stage in evolution chain:** `02_amendment_1956_disability_program`

By 1977, the Social Security Act's judicial review provisions had been in place with
the §405(h) exclusivity clause since the 1939 amendments. The 1956 disability program
amendments added Title II disability benefits — the program type at issue in Califano —
but did not materially change the §405(g)/(h) judicial review structure.

The Court in Califano was interpreting the then-current text of §405(g)'s "final
decision" requirement and §405(h)'s bar on other judicial review routes. That text
was substantially the same as the 1935-origin text for purposes of the jurisdictional
question decided.

---

## Statutory Text at Issue (1977 version, relevant portions)

**42 U.S.C. §405(g) (1977):** "Any individual, after any final decision of the Secretary
made after a hearing to which he was a party...may obtain a review of such decision by
a civil action commenced within sixty days..."

**42 U.S.C. §405(h) (1977):** "The findings and decision of the Secretary after a
hearing shall be binding upon all individuals who were parties to such hearing...No
findings of fact or decision of the Secretary shall be reviewed by any person, tribunal,
or governmental agency except as herein provided."

---

## Why This Case Maps to Stage 02

The disability program was added in 1956 (stage 02), making Title II disability
benefits a cognizable claim under §405(g). Califano's holding — that constitutional
claims challenging SSA disability determinations survive §405(h)'s exclusivity clause —
applies directly to Title II disability claims. The 1983 reform crisis (stage 03) had
not yet occurred when Califano was decided.
