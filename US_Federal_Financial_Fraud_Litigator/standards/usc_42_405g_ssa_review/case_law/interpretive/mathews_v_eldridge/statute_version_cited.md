# Statute Version Cited — Mathews v. Eldridge

**Case decided:** 1976
**Statute version applicable:** 42 U.S.C. §405(g) (pre-1983 amendments; substantively the same for judicial review purposes)

## The Constitutional Analysis

Mathews addressed the Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause requirements, not just
the statutory framework of §405(g). The constitutional analysis established:
- SSA's process (with §405(g) federal court review) = constitutionally sufficient
- The adequacy of process depends on whether it was actually provided

## Current Relevance

The Mathews balancing test applies to any argument that additional process should
have been provided. For phantom contact fraud, the argument is not that Michael
needed more process — he needed the process he was legally entitled to (actual
contact by SSA, not fabricated contact records).

The 1983 amendments did not change the fundamental Mathews framework. The three-factor
test remains the operative constitutional standard for evaluating SSA process adequacy.
