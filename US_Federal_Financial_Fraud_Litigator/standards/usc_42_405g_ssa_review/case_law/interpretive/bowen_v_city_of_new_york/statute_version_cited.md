# Statute Version Cited — Bowen v. City of New York, 476 U.S. 467 (1986)

## Version Applied

**42 U.S.C. §405(g)** as amended by the Social Security Disability Benefits Reform Act of 1984,
Pub. L. 98-460 (September 19, 1984), and prior Social Security Amendments of 1983,
Pub. L. 98-21 (April 20, 1983).

## Relevant Text at Time of Decision (1986)

The Court applied the version of §405(g) containing the 60-day filing deadline running from
"the date of mailing of notice of such decision or determination." The Court specifically
addressed the "60 days after the mailing of notice" limitation and the "good cause" extension
provision under which the Secretary could extend the time for filing.

The 1984 amendments had modified the disability evaluation process but did not alter the
§405(g) filing deadline or the court's authority to review disability determinations. The
Court noted that Congress, in creating this administrative review scheme, did not intend
to bar review where the agency's own conduct prevented the claimant from satisfying the
jurisdictional prerequisites.

## Equitable Tolling Principle

The Court held that equitable tolling applies to the §405(g) 60-day deadline when:

1. The SSA has pursued a policy of deliberately concealing information from claimants
2. The concealment deprives claimants of the ability to know they have been denied benefits
3. The claimant's failure to file within 60 days is directly caused by the government's concealment

**The statute version does not contain an express equitable tolling provision.** The Court
derived equitable tolling from:
- General equitable principles applicable to federal limitations periods
- The rule that the government cannot benefit from its own wrongdoing
- Constitutional due process concerns about meaningful access to judicial review

## Post-Decision Status

The §405(g) 60-day deadline language has remained substantively unchanged since the 1986
Bowen decision. Equitable tolling under Bowen is established precedent. Congress has not
legislatively overridden the equitable tolling holding.

## Application to Phantom Contact Fraud

The phantom contact records in the case file (9/18/2019, 3/23/2020, 12/17/2020) constitute
exactly the type of SSA concealment that Bowen addresses: false records substituted for
genuine administrative contact that deprived the claimant of the ability to understand the
basis for the disability determination and challenge it within the 60-day window. The
equitable tolling clock begins running from the date the phantom contacts were discovered,
not from the date of the original SSA notice.
