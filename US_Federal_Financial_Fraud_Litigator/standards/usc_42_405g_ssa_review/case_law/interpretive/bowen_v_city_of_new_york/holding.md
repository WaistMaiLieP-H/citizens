# Bowen v. City of New York — Holding

**Citation:** Bowen v. City of New York, 476 U.S. 467 (1986)
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Decided:** May 27, 1986
**Author:** Powell, J.

## Facts

In the early 1980s, SSA implemented a secret internal policy directing adjudicators
to apply stricter standards for evaluating mental impairments in disability cases than
the law required — and to deny claims accordingly. This policy was not disclosed to
claimants. Thousands of claimants had their disability benefits denied or terminated
under the secret policy without knowledge that the policy existed.

The City of New York and other plaintiffs sought a class action challenging the denials
and argued that equitable tolling of the §405(g) 60-day filing deadline should apply
because the claimants did not know (and could not have known) about the secret policy
that caused their denials.

## Holding

**Equitable tolling of the §405(g) 60-day filing deadline is available where SSA
has concealed from claimants material information about the basis for their denials.**

The Court held:
1. The §405(g) 60-day deadline is not jurisdictional in the same sense as a statute
   of limitations bar — it is a limitations period that may be equitably tolled
2. Where the government's own conduct prevented timely filing — including where the
   government concealed the basis for its actions — equitable tolling applies
3. SSA's secret policy constituted concealment that justified class-wide equitable
   tolling of the §405(g) deadline
4. Claimants could not reasonably have discovered the basis for challenging their
   denials when SSA concealed the policy that caused those denials

## Rules Distilled

1. **§405(g) 60-day deadline is tolled by government concealment:** When SSA conceals
   material information affecting a claimant's ability to challenge a denial, the
   60-day deadline is equitably tolled
2. **Class action under §405(g) is permissible:** Systematic SSA policy violations can
   be challenged through class actions; individual claimants need not each exhaust
   administrative remedies for class-wide challenges
3. **Secret agency policy = grounds for tolling:** An agency's application of undisclosed
   or concealed policies that affect claimants' rights automatically triggers equitable
   tolling because claimants cannot challenge what they don't know about
4. **Government cannot benefit from own wrongdoing:** The equitable principle that a
   party cannot benefit from concealment it caused applies to SSA just as to private parties
5. **Administrative exhaustion may be waived or tolled for systematic violations:**
   When the agency's own conduct makes the administrative process inadequate or unavailable,
   exhaustion requirements are relaxed

## Significance for Phantom Contact Fraud

Bowen v. City of New York is the most directly applicable precedent for the phantom
contact fraud equitable tolling argument:

**Concealment analog:** SSA's phantom contact records (9/18/2019, 3/23/2020, 12/17/2020)
are false records concealing from Michael that: (a) real contact never occurred;
(b) his disability claim was being processed, adjudicated, or abandoned based on
fake communications; and (c) he had the right to challenge what was actually happening.

Michael could not know the basis for challenging SSA's actions because SSA's own records
falsely reflected successful contact. Just as the Bowen plaintiffs could not challenge
a secret policy they didn't know existed, Michael could not challenge a false process
he didn't know was occurring.

**The equitable tolling argument:**
- SSA maintained false contact records that concealed the fraudulent interference
  with Michael's disability claim
- Michael could not reasonably discover the phantom contact fraud until he obtained
  SSA records showing the false dates
- The §405(g) 60-day deadline (if it has run) is equitably tolled from the date of
  discovery of the phantom contact fraud
- Bowen directly supports this argument: government concealment = tolling
