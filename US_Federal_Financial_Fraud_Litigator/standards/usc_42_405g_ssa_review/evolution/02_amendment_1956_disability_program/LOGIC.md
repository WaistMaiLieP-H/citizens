# Evolution Stage 02 — 1956 Amendment: Disability Insurance Added

### THE WOUND

The 1935 Social Security Act provided retirement benefits but no disability insurance.
By the mid-1950s, millions of workers suffered disabling conditions that prevented
them from working before reaching retirement age. Without disability benefits, these
workers faced poverty with no social safety net while they were unable to work.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1956

Social Security Amendments of 1956 (Pub. L. 84-880, Aug. 1, 1956) added Social
Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) to the program:

**Disability definition:** "Inability to engage in any substantial gainful activity
by reason of any medically determinable physical or mental impairment which can be
expected to result in death or which has lasted or can be expected to last for a
continuous period of not less than 12 months."

**Evaluation process:** DDS (Disability Determination Services) state agencies were
created to evaluate disability claims on behalf of SSA. This created the federal-state
joint administration structure where state DDS agencies make initial and reconsideration
determinations, and federal ALJs hear appeals.

**§405(g) extended to disability:** The judicial review provision (§405(g)) was extended
to apply to SSDI disability determinations — same administrative exhaustion requirements,
same substantial evidence standard of review.

### SIGNIFICANCE

The 1956 addition of SSDI created the current system Michael is navigating:
- Michael's disability claim is an SSDI claim based on the inability to work
- The DDS/SSA phantom contacts document fraudulent interference in this system
- The §405(g) federal court review path was established in 1956 for exactly this type
  of administrative denial challenge

### PROVENANCE

Social Security Amendments of 1956, Pub. L. 84-880 (Aug. 1, 1956)
Added: SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance) to Title II of the Social Security Act
Extended §405(g) judicial review to disability determinations
