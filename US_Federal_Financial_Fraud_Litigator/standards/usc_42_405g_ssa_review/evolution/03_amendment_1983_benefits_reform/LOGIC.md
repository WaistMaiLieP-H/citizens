# Evolution Stage 03 — 1983 Social Security Amendments: Process and Judicial Review Clarified

### THE WOUND

By the late 1970s and early 1980s, the Reagan Administration had initiated a mass
termination of disability benefits — SSA was reviewing existing disability cases and
terminating benefits for hundreds of thousands of recipients. Federal courts were
receiving large volumes of §405(g) appeals and applying inconsistent standards.

A critical problem emerged: SSA was terminating benefits without substantial evidence
supporting the termination — it was placing the burden on recipients to prove continuing
disability at each review rather than requiring SSA to establish changed medical
circumstances. Bowen v. City of New York (1986) addressed a related procedural problem.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1983

Social Security Amendments of 1983 (Pub. L. 98-21, April 20, 1983):

**Clarified §405(g) review framework:**
Confirmed that federal courts apply a "substantial evidence" standard of review — the
court reviews the administrative record but does not substitute its own judgment for
the Commissioner's on questions of fact.

**Continuing disability review (CDR) reforms:**
After the Reagan-era mass terminations created severe hardship and massive federal
court litigation, Congress and SSA reformed the CDR process. The 1983 amendments
established procedural protections for continuing disability reviews.

**Equitable tolling codification:**
The 1983 amendments and subsequent case law (particularly Bowen v. City of New York,
1986) established that equitable tolling applies to the §405(g) 60-day deadline
where SSA's own conduct prevented timely filing.

### CURRENT OPERATIVE VERSION

The current 42 U.S.C. §405(g) reflects the 1983 amendments and subsequent case law
development. The substantial evidence standard and the equitable tolling doctrine are
the operative framework.

### PROVENANCE
