# Evolution Stage 01 — Origin 1935: Social Security Act

### THE WOUND

The Social Security Act of 1935 created the Old-Age Insurance program (retirement).
The Act provided for administrative determination of benefits but did not initially
include an explicit provision for federal court review of administrative decisions.
Without a judicial review provision, denial of benefits could only be contested
through internal administrative channels — and the government's administrative
decisions were effectively unreviewable.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1935

The Social Security Act (Pub. L. 74-271, Aug. 14, 1935) originally established:
- SSA as an independent federal agency
- Administrative determination process for benefits
- §205(g) providing limited judicial review: "Any individual, after any final decision
  of the Administrator made after a hearing to which he was a party, may obtain a review
  of such decision..."

The original §205(g) (now 42 U.S.C. §405(g)) established the federal court review
mechanism from the beginning of the program. However, the scope and standards of
review developed through case law over subsequent decades.

**Initial scope:** Old-age retirement benefits only; no disability benefits in 1935.

### PROVENANCE

Social Security Act, Pub. L. 74-271 (Aug. 14, 1935)
Original codification at 42 U.S.C. §405(g) (§205(g) of the Act)
