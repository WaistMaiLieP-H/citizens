# Evolution Stage 01 — Origin 1996: Telecommunications Act §222

### THE WOUND

Before 1996, carriers had no federal obligation to protect customer call records or
account information. The market for CPNI was essentially unregulated — carriers sold
call records to direct marketers, shared them with affiliates, and disclosed them
to anyone willing to pay for "look-up" services.

The practice of obtaining CPNI through social engineering ("pretexting") was widespread:
private investigators would call carriers posing as the customer and request call records.
This was legal under existing law. The phone records of executives, celebrities, and
ordinary citizens were for sale.

The Telecommunications Act of 1996 (Pub. L. 104-104) included §702, which added
47 U.S.C. §222 to the Communications Act — creating the statutory duty to protect CPNI.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1996

**47 U.S.C. §222:** Carriers must protect CPNI confidentiality. Customer approval is
required before CPNI can be used for most marketing or shared with third parties.

**Initial implementing rules (1999):** FCC adopted initial CPNI rules under the statutory
mandate, creating the Part 64, Subpart U framework.

**Key limitation:** The initial rules did not yet establish specific authentication
requirements for how carriers must verify identity before disclosing CPNI or making
account changes. The authentication gap would be filled by the 2007 order.

### PROVENANCE

Telecommunications Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-104, §702 (Feb. 8, 1996)
Added: 47 U.S.C. §222 to the Communications Act of 1934
FCC initial implementing regulations (1999)
