# FCC CPNI Enforcement History — Pattern of SIM Swap / Carrier Authentication Failures

**Type:** Regulatory enforcement history (not a single case; documented enforcement pattern)
**Agency:** Federal Communications Commission, Enforcement Bureau
**Relevance:** Establishes industry knowledge of SIM swap / CPNI authentication vulnerabilities

## Summary of FCC CPNI Enforcement Pattern

### Background

The FCC has documented a consistent pattern of carrier CPNI failures related to:
1. Pretexting (social engineering to obtain CPNI through false identity claims)
2. SIM swap fraud (redirecting a customer's phone number to an attacker's SIM)
3. Port-out fraud (fraudulently porting a number to another carrier)
4. Insider access (carrier employees accessing CPNI for unauthorized purposes)

This enforcement history establishes that:
- The vulnerability of carrier authentication systems to SIM swap and pretexting has
  been publicly known since at least 2006-2007
- Carriers including AT&T were on actual notice of these vulnerabilities through prior
  enforcement actions, industry notices, and FCC proceedings
- FCC rules specifically requiring stronger authentication (FCC 07-22) have been in
  effect since 2007

### Key Enforcement Actions and FCC Proceedings

**2007 — HP Pretexting Response (FCC 07-22):**
FCC adopted enhanced CPNI rules specifically in response to the HP pretexting scandal.
The 2007 order required carriers to use shared secrets/passwords for authentication —
prohibiting reliance on biographical information alone. This put all carriers, including
AT&T, on notice that authentication through biographical data was inadequate.

**2012 — FCC CPNI Certification Violations:**
Multiple carriers fined for failure to file required annual CPNI certifications.
Demonstrates ongoing FCC oversight of CPNI obligations.

**2015 — AT&T $25 Million CPNI Settlement:**
Documented above. Established AT&T was aware of and responsible for call center
authentication failures enabling unauthorized CPNI access.

**2018-2020 — SIM Swap Victim Complaints:**
Class action litigation and FCC complaints from SIM swap victims against multiple carriers
(AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon) grew significantly during this period. Consumer advocacy groups
filed FCC complaints. The FCC began tracking SIM swap incidents as a distinct category.

**2023 — FCC Declaratory Ruling on SIM Swap (FCC 23-67):**
The FCC specifically addressed SIM swap fraud as a CPNI violation, requiring carriers to:
- Notify customers before executing SIM swaps
- Implement additional verification for SIM port requests
- Allow customers to lock their SIM against port-outs

## What the Enforcement Pattern Establishes for Civil Litigation

**Industry knowledge:** The FCC's sustained enforcement pattern (2007-2023) demonstrates
that SIM swap and authentication fraud vulnerabilities were well-known to the carrier
industry. AT&T cannot claim ignorance.

**Standard of care:** The FCC rules (47 C.F.R. §§64.2010-64.2011) define the minimum
standard of care for carrier authentication. Violations of these rules = negligence per se
evidence.

**AT&T-specific knowledge:** AT&T specifically paid $25M in 2015 for authentication
failures enabling unauthorized CPNI access. AT&T had actual knowledge of the vulnerability
and an obligation to remediate.

**Timeline for SIM swap attack (~2018 forward):** The attack on Michael Hartmann
allegedly began approximately 2018. By that time:
- FCC 07-22 authentication rules had been in effect for 11 years
- AT&T had settled its 2015 CPNI enforcement action and committed to enhanced authentication
- FCC complaints about SIM swap fraud were documented and increasing

AT&T's failure to prevent the SIM swap after all of this notice is the foundation of
the negligence claim.
