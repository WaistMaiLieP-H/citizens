# In re AT&T Inc. — FCC CPNI Enforcement (2015)

**Citation:** In the Matter of AT&T Inc., FCC Consent Decree (April 8, 2015); $25 million settlement
**Agency:** Federal Communications Commission, Enforcement Bureau
**Category:** FCC enforcement action (not a court decision; administrative)
**Status:** Consent decree / settlement; AT&T admitted no wrongdoing but paid $25 million

## Facts

The FCC Enforcement Bureau investigated AT&T following reports that call center
employees at AT&T's vendors — located in Mexico, Colombia, and the Philippines —
had provided unauthorized access to customers' personal information, including:
- Customer account information
- Social Security Numbers (last four digits used as CPNI authentication)
- Account PINs and passcodes
- Call records and CPNI

The employees provided this information to third parties who used it to unlock
AT&T phones (making them usable on other carriers) and for identity fraud purposes.
Hundreds of thousands of customers were affected.

The FCC found that AT&T violated 47 U.S.C. §222 and 47 C.F.R. §64.2010 by failing
to adequately protect CPNI and failing to supervise its call center vendors.

## Settlement / Enforcement Result

AT&T agreed to pay $25 million to resolve the investigation and to implement a
compliance plan including:
- Enhanced authentication procedures
- Supervision of overseas call centers
- Employee training on CPNI obligations
- Annual CPNI compliance certifications

This was (at the time) the largest FCC CPNI enforcement action.

## Rules Established / Confirmed

1. **Carrier responsibility for vendor agents:** Carriers are responsible for their
   call center vendors' CPNI violations — the duty to protect CPNI extends to all
   agents handling customer information
2. **Biographical authentication is insufficient:** Using SSN last four alone (which
   was how authentication was bypassed in this case) is inadequate CPNI protection
   under FCC 07-22's authentication rules
3. **Oversight duty:** Carriers must actively supervise third-party vendors who handle
   CPNI — failure to supervise = carrier CPNI violation
4. **Scale of exposure:** $25 million enforcement action demonstrates the seriousness
   of CPNI violations; individual customer damages can be significant

## Significance for AT&T SIM Swap Liability

The 2015 enforcement action establishes that AT&T has known since at least 2015
that:
- Its authentication procedures were being exploited to gain unauthorized access to CPNI
- Third parties could bypass AT&T's CPNI protections through social engineering
- AT&T had an obligation to implement stronger authentication

If AT&T failed to implement adequate SIM swap authentication safeguards AFTER the
2015 enforcement action put it on actual notice of these vulnerabilities, AT&T's
negligence in the SIM swap attack is strengthened — AT&T cannot claim ignorance
of SIM swap/authentication fraud after paying $25 million for exactly this type
of CPNI failure.
