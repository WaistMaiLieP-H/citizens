# Statute Version Cited — In re AT&T Inc. (2015 CPNI)

**Enforcement action:** 2015
**Rules violated:** 47 U.S.C. §222 + 47 C.F.R. §64.2010 (FCC 07-22 authentication rules, effective 2007)

## The Rules at Issue

**47 U.S.C. §222(a):** "Every telecommunications carrier has a duty to protect the
confidentiality of proprietary information of, and relating to, other telecommunication
carriers, equipment manufacturers, and customers, including telecommunications carriers
reselling telecommunications services provided by a telecommunications carrier."

**47 C.F.R. §64.2010(a):** Authentication requirement — carriers must use customer-
selected passwords, PINs, or other shared secrets for online and telephone access to
CPNI. Readily available biographical information alone is insufficient.

## AT&T's Admitted Inadequacy

The consent decree settlement reflected AT&T's call center authentication procedures
did not adequately protect against insider misuse of biographical authentication
information. This is the same class of vulnerability exploited in SIM swap attacks —
an attacker who knows the target's biographical information (SSN, DOB) can defeat
inadequate authentication protocols.

## Current Rule Status

Both 47 U.S.C. §222 and 47 C.F.R. §64.2010 remain in effect. The FCC's 2023
SIM swap declaratory ruling (FCC 23-67) has further strengthened carrier duties
specifically for SIM swap scenarios.
