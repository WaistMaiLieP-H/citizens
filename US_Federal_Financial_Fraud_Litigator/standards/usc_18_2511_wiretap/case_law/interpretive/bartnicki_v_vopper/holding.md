# Bartnicki v. Vopper — Holding

**Citation:** Bartnicki v. Vopper, 532 U.S. 514 (2001)
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Decided:** May 21, 2001
**Author:** Stevens, J.

## Facts

Gloria Bartnicki was a teachers' union negotiator. An unknown person intercepted and
recorded a cell phone call between Bartnicki and the union president discussing
contentious negotiations. The recording was provided to a radio commentator (Vopper)
who broadcast portions of it. Bartnicki sued Vopper under §2511 for disclosing the
intercepted communication.

The First Amendment defense: Vopper had not done the intercepting; he received a
recording from someone else and broadcast it as a matter of public concern.

## Holding

**1. §2511(1)(c) prohibits disclosure of intercepted communications:**
The Wiretap Act prohibits not only interception but also the intentional disclosure
of intercepted communications. A party who did not intercept but knowingly disclosed
an intercepted communication is liable under §2511(1)(c).

**2. First Amendment creates a narrow exception for public concern:**
When (a) the disclosing party played no part in the illegal interception, (b) the
information was lawfully obtained (received from the interceptor), and (c) the
information involves a matter of public concern, the First Amendment protects
disclosure even of illegally intercepted communications.

**3. The exception is narrow:**
The public concern exception does NOT apply to private communications without
public interest. Bartnicki's case was a close call because the content involved
public negotiations. For purely private communications — personal conversations,
financial information, private medical discussions — there is no public concern
defense and §2511 applies fully to disclosure.

## Rules Distilled

1. §2511 covers disclosure as well as interception — you need not be the interceptor
   to face liability
2. Knowing disclosure of intercepted communications = §2511(1)(c) violation
3. First Amendment public concern exception = narrow; applies when: no participation
   in interception + lawfully obtained + matter of public concern
4. For private communications (financial, medical, personal): no First Amendment
   defense; §2511 applies fully to unauthorized disclosure

## Significance for SIM Swap

**Bartnicki confirms disclosure liability:**
Any person who received intercepted communications from the SIM swap attacker and
disclosed them — to attorneys, courts, family members as part of the conspiracy —
faces §2511(1)(c) liability. The disclosure liability extends the chain:
not just the attacker who ported the number, but each person in the chain who
knowingly disclosed the intercepted content.

**For Michael's case:**
If intercepted calls were shared among conspiracy participants (Christina, Ryan
McClaran, others) who used the content to coordinate legal filings, benefit denials,
or other adverse actions — each act of disclosure = separate §2511 violation.
$10,000 per disclosure event.

**Private communications exception:**
Michael's communications about disability benefits, financial accounts, medical care,
and legal proceedings are entirely private — no public concern. No First Amendment
defense is available to anyone who intercepted or disclosed them.
