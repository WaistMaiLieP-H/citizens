# Historical Chain — 18 U.S.C. §2511 (Wiretap Act / ECPA Title I)

## Chain Summary

The Wiretap Act evolved from a narrow prohibition on voice wiretapping (1968) to a
comprehensive framework covering all electronic communications in transit (1986). The
key evolution is the ECPA's addition of "electronic communication" — which brings SMS,
email, and all digital communications within the prohibition. Van Buren (CFAA) and
the stored/transmitted distinction create the boundary with CFAA and the SCA.

## Stage 1 — Title III Origin 1968 (Pub. L. 90-351)

**Wound:** Private wiretapping was a commercial industry; no effective federal prohibition
on private party interception; no civil remedy for victims.

**Response:** Prohibition on intentional interception of wire/oral communications;
law enforcement exception with court order; §2520 civil remedy ($10K minimum + fees).

**Gap:** Electronic communications (email, cellular) not covered.

## Stage 2 — ECPA 1986 (Pub. L. 99-508)

**Wound:** Electronic communications (cellular calls, email, pager) unprotected by 1968 Act.

**Response:** Extended Wiretap Act to "electronic communications"; created Stored
Communications Act (§§2701-2712) for stored communications; established interception
vs. storage distinction.

**Current effect:** All SMS in transit, all calls in transit, all email in transit = covered.

## Key Case Law in Chain

1. Bartnicki v. Vopper, 532 U.S. 514 (2001) — disclosure liability; narrow First
   Amendment public concern exception; private communications fully protected
2. Jewel v. NSA, 673 F.3d 902 (9th Cir. 2011) — carrier maintenance exception narrow;
   standing for §2511 civil plaintiffs; scope analysis

## Current State

§2511 as amended through ECPA (1986):
- Covers wire, oral, and electronic communications
- Prohibits interception (§2511(1)(a)) and disclosure (§2511(1)(c))
- Civil remedy §2520: greater of actual damages or $10,000 minimum (or $100/day)
  + punitive + attorneys' fees
- Exceptions: law enforcement (court order); one-party consent; provider maintenance
  (narrow — normal course of business + necessary to service)
- California CIPA parallel: Cal. Penal Code §632 — all-party consent state; §637.2 civil
  remedy ($5,000/violation or 3× actual)
- SOL: 2 years from discovery
