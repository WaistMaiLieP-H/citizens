# Evolution Stage 01 — Origin 1968
## Title III of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act

### THE WOUND

Before 1968 federal law on wiretapping was in disarray. The Federal Communications Act
of 1934, §605, prohibited the interception and divulgence of wire communications —
but courts had read it narrowly. The Supreme Court's Katz v. United States (1967)
had just held that the Fourth Amendment protected reasonable expectations of privacy
in communications, requiring a warrant for law enforcement wiretapping.

But Katz addressed government conduct — it did not create a private civil remedy for
private party wiretapping. And private party wiretapping was widespread: businesses
tapped competitor phone lines, suspicious spouses hired private investigators who
tapped phones, organized crime used wiretaps for surveillance and extortion.

The legislative history of Title III documented that private wiretapping had become
a commercial industry. There was no effective federal prohibition with teeth — and
no civil remedy for victims.

### DESIGN AT ENACTMENT (1968)

**Title III** — codified at 18 U.S.C. §§2510-2520 — established:

1. **Prohibition:** Intentional interception of wire or oral communications without
   authorization = federal crime (§2511)
2. **Law enforcement exception:** Federal and state law enforcement may intercept
   communications pursuant to a court order (§2518 procedures)
3. **Consent exception:** One party to the communication may consent; provider
   exception for service providers
4. **Civil remedy (§2520):** Any person whose communications are intercepted may sue
   for actual damages + statutory damages + attorneys' fees

**Gap at origin:** Title III covered "wire communications" (voice over wire) and
"oral communications" (in-person conversations with reasonable expectation of privacy).
It did NOT cover electronic communications — fax, computer data, email did not yet exist
at the drafting stage and were not anticipated.

### PROVENANCE

Title III of the Omnibus Crime Control and Safe Streets Act of 1968
Pub. L. 90-351, 82 Stat. 197 (June 19, 1968)
