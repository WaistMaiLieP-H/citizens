# Logical Diff: Stage 01 (1952) → Stage 02 (1987–1988)

## What the 1952 statute did NOT address

The 1952 wire fraud statute said nothing about "honest services." Federal courts read the
"scheme to defraud" language expansively over 35 years to cover corruption, bribery, and
employee self-dealing — the argument being that depriving a public or private employer of
loyal, honest services was a form of "defrauding" them of something of value.

## McNally's cut (1987)

The Supreme Court read the legislative history and held: "scheme to defraud" protects property,
not intangible rights to honest conduct. Everything built on the honest-services theory over
35 years was wiped out in one decision.

## Congress's counter-move (1988)

§1346 was a one-sentence legislative override: "honest services" is within "scheme to defraud."
Fast passage — part of the Anti-Drug Abuse Act of 1988 omnibus bill.

## Skilling's narrowing (2010)

§1346, read broadly, would be unconstitutionally vague (what counts as "honest services"?).
The Court saved the statute by narrowing: only quid-pro-quo bribery and kickbacks are covered.
Pure self-dealing is not.

## Net change for property fraud cases (Michael's cases)

No change. The entire McNally → §1346 → Skilling arc is about the honest-services branch of
§1343. The property-fraud branch — the original §1343 core — was untouched throughout. All
financial fraud schemes targeting money, accounts, bonds, and cryptocurrency proceed on the
property-fraud theory and are unaffected by the honest-services evolution.
