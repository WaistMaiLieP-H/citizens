# Kelly v. United States
## 590 US 391 (2020)

**Court:** United States Supreme Court
**Decided:** May 7, 2020
**Vote:** 9-0 (unanimous)

---

## Issue decided

Does wire fraud require that the scheme be directed at depriving the victim of money or
property? Or does it cover schemes that use fraudulent pretexts to exercise government
regulatory authority over traffic or infrastructure?

---

## Holding

**The scheme must target property.** Wire fraud under §1343 (and mail fraud under §1341)
requires that the object of the scheme be money or property. A scheme that obtains an
exercise of government regulatory authority by fraud — even if that regulatory action harms
people — does not constitute wire/mail fraud because "regulatory power" is not property.

**Facts:** New Jersey officials orchestrated the "Bridgegate" scandal — they fraudulently
told Port Authority officials that a traffic study justified lane closures on the George
Washington Bridge, when the real purpose was political retaliation. The scheme:
- Caused massive traffic jams harming commuters
- Wasted Port Authority labor (employees diverted to the pretextual "study")
- Deprived Fort Lee residents of access

**Court's analysis:** The government argued that the Port Authority's labor and resources
were "property" fraudulently obtained. The Court rejected this: the officials did not obtain
the labor to enrich themselves; the labor was an incidental cost of executing the regulatory
(traffic management) decision they fraudulently caused. The "object" of the scheme was
regulatory action (closing the lanes), not property. Wire fraud does not cover schemes where
property loss is merely an incidental byproduct of obtaining regulatory power through fraud.

---

## The Kelly rule stated simply

Wire fraud = scheme whose PURPOSE is to obtain money or property.
Wire fraud ≠ scheme whose PURPOSE is regulatory action (even if property is incidentally used
or lost in executing that regulatory action).

---

## Why this case matters for Michael

**Kelly does not threaten Michael's wire fraud claims.** Kelly applies when the object of the
scheme is government regulatory authority rather than money or property. In Michael's cases:

- **Treasury Securities fraud:** The scheme's purpose was to obtain Michael's bonds (property)
  and/or prevent Michael from accessing them. That is a property-object scheme.
- **Crypto fraud (#21):** The purpose was to take cryptocurrency (property). Clear property object.
- **ChexSystems / account fraud:** The purpose was to open accounts and access financial
  services (property, money). Property object.
- **Hillberg UIT:** The purpose was to access or control financial instruments under a
  compound identity (property). Property object.

None of these are "regulatory authority" schemes. Kelly is inapplicable. The wire fraud theory
for all of Michael's financial fraud cases survives Kelly.

**Kelly's importance:** It confirms the outer boundary of §1343 — it does NOT extend wire
fraud to all fraudulent conduct in government contexts. This matters in government-agency
cases (like the Treasury responses) where the argument might be that the fraud was "just"
an administrative irregularity rather than a property scheme.
