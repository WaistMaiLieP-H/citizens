# Statute Version at Time of Kelly v. United States (2020)

**§1343 version:** 18 USC §1343 current consolidated (post-1988 amendments; same "scheme to
defraud" language as 1952 origin, with enhanced penalty for financial institution).

The Court applied §1343's "money or property" phrase — the same phrase that has been in the
statute since its 1952 enactment. Kelly did not create a new interpretation; it clarified
the outer boundary of what "property" means: government regulatory power is not property,
even if exercising it fraudulently costs the government or others real resources.

**The current framework after Kelly:**
- Property: money, tangible assets, intangible business information (Carpenter), contractual
  rights, financial instruments, cryptocurrency = all property for §1343
- Not property: regulatory authority, government policy decisions, administrative power
  (even if these have economic value or cause economic harm when exercised)

**Application:** Every financial account, financial instrument, SSN-based entitlement (bonds,
tax refunds), and cryptocurrency wallet qualifies as property. Kelly does not affect the
analysis of any of Michael's cases.
