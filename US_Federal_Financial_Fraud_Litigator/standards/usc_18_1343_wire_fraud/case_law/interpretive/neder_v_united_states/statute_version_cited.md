# Statute Version at Time of Neder v. United States (1999)

**§1343 version:** 18 USC §1343 post-1988 (with "affecting a financial institution" enhanced
penalty clause; with §1346 honest services definition in companion section).

The Court interpreted §1343's "scheme to defraud" language, specifically whether "defraud"
imported a materiality requirement from the common law. The 1988 amendments to §1343 (adding
the financial institution enhancement) did not alter the core "scheme to defraud" language.

**The interpretive principle:** When Congress uses a common-law term without defining it,
courts presume Congress intended to incorporate the common-law meaning and all its embedded
elements. "Defraud" at common law required a material misrepresentation — therefore §1343
requires materiality even though the word does not appear in the statute.

**Current operative law:** Neder remains the controlling standard. The materiality element
applies to all §1343 prosecutions. It is built into jury instructions in all federal circuits.
No subsequent amendment has modified or removed this element.
