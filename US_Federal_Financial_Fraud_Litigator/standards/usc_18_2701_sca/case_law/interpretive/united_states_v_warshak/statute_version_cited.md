# Statute Version Cited — United States v. Warshak

**Case decided:** 2010
**Statute version applicable:** 18 U.S.C. §§2703(b), 2701 (post-PATRIOT Act version; substantively unchanged from 1986 for email content provisions)

## §2703(b) — The Provision Held Unconstitutional

The court addressed §2703(b), which allowed government access to "the contents of a
wire or electronic communication" in storage for more than 180 days with:
- A government entity may require disclosure by the provider using a court order (not warrant)
- Must give subscriber notice

The court held: for email content, this lower process (court order vs. warrant) violates
the Fourth Amendment — a probable cause warrant is required regardless of storage duration.

## Constitutional Floor vs. Statutory Framework

Warshak does not hold that the SCA is unconstitutional generally — only that its
application in requiring less than a warrant for email content violates the Fourth
Amendment. The SCA continues to operate; government must simply obtain a warrant.

## Civil Action Under §2707

The civil remedy provision (§2707) was not before the court. But Warshak's holding
establishes the constitutional weight of stored communications privacy — a private
actor who accesses stored email "without authorization" (§2701) is violating rights
of equivalent constitutional magnitude to what the government cannot do without a warrant.

This supports punitive damages in civil §2707 cases involving intentional unauthorized
email access via SIM swap account takeover.
