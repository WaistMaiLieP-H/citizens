# Evolution Stage 01 — Origin 1986: ECPA Title II

### THE WOUND

In 1986, Congress enacted ECPA in response to the growing gap between the original
federal Wiretap Act (Title III, 1968) and the emerging landscape of electronic
communications. The 1968 Wiretap Act was designed for telephone wiretapping — real-time
interception of voice communications on wire lines.

By 1986, two new categories of communications existed that the 1968 Act did not address:
1. **Electronic communications in transit:** Email, electronic bulletin boards, and data
   communications transmitted electronically but not over wire telephone lines
2. **Stored communications:** Email sitting on a server, voicemail waiting to be
   retrieved, data files stored by service providers

The wound: these new categories were effectively unprotected. The Supreme Court's
third-party doctrine (Smith v. Maryland, 1979) potentially meant that communications
stored with service providers had no Fourth Amendment protection — the user had
"shared" them with the provider.

Congress chose to create statutory protection for stored communications rather than
rely on constitutional doctrine.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1986

**Title II of ECPA created the Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. §§2701-2711):**

**§2701 — Prohibited acts:** Intentional unauthorized access to stored electronic
communications is prohibited (criminal penalties and civil liability).

**§2702 — Voluntary disclosure:** Service providers may not voluntarily disclose
the contents of stored communications to government or third parties except in
limited circumstances.

**§2703 — Required disclosure:** Government access to stored communications requires
warrants, court orders, or subpoenas depending on the type and age of communication.

**§2707 — Civil action:** Private right of action for victims of §2701 violations;
recovery of actual damages, statutory minimum, punitive damages, and attorneys' fees.

**Key design choice — tiered age-based protection:**
- Communications stored <180 days = warrant required for government access
- Communications stored >180 days = court order or subpoena with notice (lower bar)
(This age-based distinction has been heavily criticized and may be constitutionally
infirm after Warshak — see case law.)

### PROVENANCE

Electronic Communications Privacy Act of 1986, Pub. L. 99-508, Title II
18 U.S.C. §§2701-2711 (original numbering; §2712 added later)
100 Stat. 1860 (Oct. 21, 1986)
