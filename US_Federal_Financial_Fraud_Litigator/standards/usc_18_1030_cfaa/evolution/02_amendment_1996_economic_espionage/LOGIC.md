# Evolution Stage 02 — 1996 Amendments + Civil Cause of Action
## National Information Infrastructure Protection Act of 1996

### THE WOUND

Between 1984 and 1996 the internet transformed from a government/university network
to a commercial and consumer platform. The 1984 Act's narrow scope left the vast
majority of computers — commercial servers, personal computers, corporate networks —
entirely outside federal protection.

By the mid-1990s the pattern of computer-related crimes had also expanded:
- Corporate espionage via computer intrusion
- Financial account access via modem/dial-up
- Denial of service attacks on commercial websites
- Theft of proprietary information from corporate computers

The 1984 Act did not cover these. And even for covered computers, the Act was
criminal-only — victims could not sue their attackers.

### DESIGN RESPONSE — 1996 AMENDMENTS

The National Information Infrastructure Protection Act of 1996 (Pub. L. 104-294):

**1. Expanded "protected computer" definition:**
Added the critical "affecting interstate or foreign commerce or communication" prong —
§1030(e)(2)(B). In the internet era, any connected computer affects interstate commerce.
This made the CFAA effectively universal.

**2. Civil cause of action — §1030(g):**
Added the private right of action: any person who suffers damage or loss may bring
a civil action against the violator for compensatory damages and injunctive/equitable
relief, subject to the $5,000 loss threshold.

**3. Strengthened criminal penalties:**
Increased maximum sentences; added categories of offenses.

### LOGICAL DELTA

| Element | Pre-1996 | Post-1996 |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Protected computer | Gov + financial institution only | Any computer affecting interstate commerce |
| Civil cause of action | None | §1030(g) — $5,000 loss threshold |
| Scope | Narrow (NORAD + banks) | Effectively universal |
| Penalties | Criminal only | Criminal + civil |

### SIGNIFICANCE

The 1996 "affecting interstate commerce" expansion is the definitional foundation for
all modern CFAA civil claims. Every internet-connected device is a "protected computer."
Every internet-connected account service (email, banking, SSA portal, TreasuryDirect)
is a protected computer system.

For the SIM swap / digital intrusion theory: the accounts accessed via the hijacked
phone number (email, SSA portal, financial accounts) are all hosted on computers that
affect interstate commerce = protected computers = CFAA applies.
