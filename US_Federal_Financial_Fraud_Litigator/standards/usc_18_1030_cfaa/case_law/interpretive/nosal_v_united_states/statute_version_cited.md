# Statute Version — United States v. Nosal (2012, 2016)

**§1030(a)(2) version:** As amended through 2008 (Identity Theft Enforcement Act).
The operative language was in its current form for both Nosal I (2012) and Nosal II (2016).

**Van Buren supersession note:**
- Nosal I's "exceeds authorized access" scope-based reasoning: adopted by Van Buren (2021)
- Nosal II's "without authorization" credential-sharing reasoning: preserved by Van Buren

**Current operative text of §1030(a)(2) (as interpreted post-Van Buren):**
"[W]hoever ... intentionally accesses a computer without authorization or exceeds
authorized access, and thereby obtains ... (C) information from any protected computer"
is subject to criminal penalties.

**"Without authorization" (§1030(e)(6)) — NOT defined in statute; defined by courts:**
Accessing a computer when the person has not been granted permission to access it.
Credential-sharing case: borrowing another's credentials to access a system you are
not permitted to access = "without authorization" (Nosal II).

**"Protected computer" — §1030(e)(2)(B):**
Any computer "used in or affecting interstate or foreign commerce or communication"
— universally applicable to all internet-connected computers post-1996.
