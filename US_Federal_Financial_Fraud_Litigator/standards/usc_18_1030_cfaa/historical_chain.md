# Historical Chain — 18 U.S.C. §1030 (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act)

## Chain Summary

The CFAA expanded from a narrow criminal statute protecting only government and bank
computers (1984) to a universal framework covering all internet-connected computers
(1996). The civil action was added in 1996. Van Buren (2021) then narrowed the
"exceeds authorized access" prong to scope-based (not purpose-based) violations —
but left "without authorization" cases fully intact.

## Stage 1 — Origin 1984 (Pub. L. 98-473)

**Wound:** No federal statute specifically prohibited unauthorized computer access;
existing fraud statutes required wire/mail; computers as an industry were new.

**Response:** Criminal-only; narrow scope (government + financial institutions).

**Gap:** Commercial computers excluded; no civil action.

## Stage 2 — 1996 Expansion (Pub. L. 104-294)

**Wound:** Internet commercialization made narrow 1984 scope obsolete; no civil remedy.

**Response:** "Affecting interstate commerce" = universal protected computer definition;
§1030(g) civil action added with $5,000 loss threshold.

## Stage 3 — PATRIOT Act 2001 + Van Buren 2021

**Wound (2001):** Post-9/11 critical infrastructure concerns; loss vs. damage gap in
civil action; circuit split on "exceeds authorized access" scope vs. purpose.

**Response (2001):** PATRIOT Act expanded loss definition; aggregation provisions.

**Wound (2021):** Circuit split on "exceeds authorized access" — purpose-based theory
in some circuits produced overbroad criminalization of ordinary employee behavior.

**Response (Van Buren 2021):** Supreme Court adopted scope-based test; purpose-based
"exceeds authorized access" theory eliminated. "Without authorization" prong unaffected.

## Current State

§1030 as amended through 2008, interpreted through Van Buren (2021) and Nosal (2012/2016):
- Protected computer: any internet-connected computer
- "Without authorization": no permission at all — SIM swap, credential theft = covered
- "Exceeds authorized access": access files/systems outside permission scope (Van Buren)
- Civil action §1030(g): $5,000 loss threshold; compensatory + injunctive/equitable
- Loss includes account access response costs, benefit denial, remediation
- SOL: 2 years from discovery

## Cases in Chain

1. United States v. Nosal, 676 F.3d 854 (9th Cir. 2012) (en banc) (Nosal I) —
   scope-based "exceeds authorized access"; adopted by Van Buren
2. United States v. Nosal, 844 F.3d 1024 (9th Cir. 2016) (Nosal II) —
   credential-sharing = "without authorization"; survives Van Buren
3. Van Buren v. United States, 593 U.S. 374 (2021) — "exceeds authorized access"
   definitively scope-based; "without authorization" prong unaffected
