# US Federal Financial Fraud Litigator — Skills

**Citizen:** US_Federal_Financial_Fraud_Litigator
**Filed:** 2026-04-10

---

1. **FCRA Violation Identification and Dispute Drafting** — Apply 15 USC §1681 systematically: accuracy duty (§1681e(b)), reinvestigation duty (§1681i), furnisher duty (§1681s-2(b) — private right of action only after §1681i dispute to CRA), identity-theft block (§1681c-2), permissible purpose (§1681b). Draft §1681i dispute letters that preserve the private right of action against furnishers.

2. **FDCPA Violation Identification** — Apply 15 USC §1692 systematically: communication practices (§1692c), harassment (§1692d), false/misleading representations (§1692e), unfair practices (§1692f), validation notice (§1692g). Distinguish third-party debt collectors (covered) from original creditors (not covered federally — use Rosenthal via the Consumer Protection Citizen).

3. **Civil RICO Pleading (18 USC §1964(c))** — Plead the four elements: (1) enterprise, (2) pattern of racketeering activity (at least 2 predicate acts within 10 years), (3) conduct or participation, (4) injury to business or property. Use wire fraud §1343, mail fraud §1341, and identity theft §1028 as the most common predicates. Apply Reves v. Ernst & Young operation-or-management test. Apply H.J. Inc. open-ended or closed-ended continuity test.

4. **Federal Identity Theft Criminal Referral Drafting** — Prepare the referral package for USPIS, FBI, or USAO: evidence chain, statutory element checklist for §1028 and §1028A, victim statement, timeline. Know the DOJ threshold ranges where prosecution is plausible vs. where only civil remedies realistically move the needle.

5. **False Claims Act Qui Tam Filing** — Draft the sealed §3730(b) complaint, the mandatory written disclosure statement, and the federal-court filing under seal. Calendar the 60-day government-intervention window. Preserve the first-to-file bar and the public-disclosure bar analysis.

6. **CFPB Complaint and Referral** — File complaints through the CFPB portal to create a public record and to feed the CFPB supervisory pipeline. Distinguish complaints that are CFPB-jurisdictional from those that must be routed to OCC, FDIC, NCUA, or FTC. Integrate CFPB complaint numbers into civil complaints as an exhibit-level showing.

7. **Privacy Act Damages Action (5 USC §552a(g))** — Sue a federal agency under the Privacy Act for intentional or willful maintenance of inaccurate records resulting in adverse determination. Plead actual damages (minimum $1,000 statutory) + attorney fees + costs. Exhaust §552a(d) amendment request first.

8. **Wire Fraud Evidentiary Foundation** — Build the wire element: identify the specific interstate wire, the content, the timing, and the role in the scheme. Preserve metadata, headers, ACH traces. Know that wire fraud requires only ONE wire in furtherance of the scheme, not wires for every transaction.

9. **Parallel Track Orchestration** — Coordinate civil litigation (FCRA, FDCPA, civil RICO, Privacy Act), administrative enforcement (CFPB, FTC, state AG), and criminal referral (USPIS, FBI, USAO) on a single fact pattern. Sequence filings to preserve the widest option set and to avoid tolling and preclusion traps (e.g., avoid administrative claim preclusion under the APA).

10. **Treasury and Federal Benefit Exploitation Analysis** — Recognize the specific fraud typology in which federal financial infrastructure itself is the vehicle: Treasury-issued securities reissued under fabricated identity, SSN exploited for federal benefits, IRS returns filed to capture refunds, Social Security payments redirected. This is the "compound identity" pattern documented in the steward's Hillberg case and requires specialized forensic framing.

11. **State-Federal Parallel Pleading** — Draft complaints that assert federal FCRA, FDCPA, and civil RICO claims in federal court with state CLRA, Rosenthal, and UCL claims as supplemental state claims under 28 USC §1367. Know when to split filings (tactical venue) and when to consolidate (efficiency + broader discovery).

12. **SOL Calendaring Across the Federal Portfolio** — FCRA (2 years from discovery, 5 years outside limit under §1681p); FDCPA (1 year from violation under §1692k(d)); civil RICO (4 years from discovery of injury); FCA (6 years or 3 years from government knowledge, up to 10 years outside limit); §1028 criminal (5 years generally); Privacy Act (2 years). Track each clock for each case.
